# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Danny Lopez (Republican)

Danny Lopez, a Carmel Republican first elected in 2024, is an executive vice president at Pacers Sports & Entertainment who previously held roles in the Daniels, Pence and Holcomb administrations; the House Republican caucus lists him on the Judiciary and Ways and Means committees [3].

- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on Senate Bill 1, the 2025 property-tax law, which passed the House 65-29 [4].
- April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the final version of the 2025-27 state budget (HEA 1001), adopted by the House 66-27 [5].
- December 5, 2025: Was one of the Republicans who voted no on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [6].
- 2025: Sponsored Senate Enrolled Act 176, which lets high school students enroll in state-accredited nursing programs and adds two members to the State Board of Nursing, according to the caucus [7].
- 2026 session: The caucus says he authored legislation folded into HEA 1406 (redevelopment and venture capital tax credits) and HEA 1277 (home health services and Medicaid rates), authored HEA 1033 (governance changes for Marion County Superior Court), and sponsored SEA 15 (Foster Care Youth Bill of Rights) and SEA 160 (masks during crimes as a sentencing factor and a new malicious littering offense) [8].
- 2026: Co-authored HEA 1210, which raises the property tax deduction for totally disabled veterans to 100% of assessed value and converts several veteran deductions to credits [9].

## This race

- House District 39 lies entirely within Hamilton County and includes parts of Carmel and Westfield [3, 10].
- Lopez won the seat in 2024 after longtime Rep. Jerry Torr retired; Wikipedia reports he received 53.7% of the vote that year [10, 11].
- In the May 5, 2026 primaries, Lopez had no Republican opponent after challenger Jiamain "Billy" Qian was removed by the Indiana Election Commission under the party-affiliation statute, which Qian conceded he did not meet; Gramlich won the Democratic nomination [12, 13, 11].
- No debates or candidate forums for this race were found.
- Where they differ: Gramlich's campaign emphasizes public school funding, utility and child care costs, and returning data-center decisions to local governments, while Lopez's campaign emphasizes expanding educational options, public safety funding and keeping tax rates low [14, 15].

## Danny Lopez

Rep. Danny Lopez, the Republican incumbent seeking a second term, is a Pacers Sports & Entertainment executive and former state government official [3].

Campaign website: https://www.lopezforindiana.com/

### Background

- Executive vice president for community and external affairs and corporate communications at Pacers Sports & Entertainment; previously chief of staff and senior vice president at Strada Education Network, according to the caucus [3].
- Held senior roles in the administrations of Govs. Mitch Daniels, Mike Pence and Eric Holcomb and served as state director for U.S. Sen. Dan Coats [3, 16].
- Holds a bachelor's degree from Florida International University and a master's from Georgia State University [3].
- Lives in Carmel [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Was unopposed on the May 5, 2026 Republican primary ballot after the Indiana Election Commission upheld a challenge to the candidacy of Jiamain "Billy" Qian in February 2026 [12, 11].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- His campaign calls for focusing on early learning and literacy, directing more resources to classrooms rather than administrative offices, and expanding parental choice in education options [15].

**Public safety**

- His campaign supports state resources for cities and counties to recruit, train and retain police and fire officers and stronger penalties for those who threaten or attack police [15].
- Said the 2026 court-reform law and related measures "will help keep Carmel and Westfield the safe and strong communities they have become" [8].

**Economy & jobs**

- His campaign supports keeping state government lean and tax rates low, and sending more infrastructure money to local governments with flexibility in how it is spent [15].
- On the 2026 tax-credit law, said, "The economic incentives we put in place will keep wages rising and investments in our communities flowing" [8].

**Property taxes**

- Voted for the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) and co-authored the 2026 veterans' property-tax law, saying veterans have "earned meaningful support from the state they served" [4, 9].

**Health care**

- His campaign supports continued funding for community mental health centers and mobile crisis units and expanding county drug courts [15].

**Veterans**

- His campaign calls for more resources for Veteran Services Officers, expanding veterans courts and recognizing occupational licenses for veterans who move to Indiana [15].

**Local control**

- The caucus says he worked to preserve local control for cities including Carmel and Westfield over housing and community-character decisions in 2026 legislation [8].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder.
- No statements by Lopez about Gramlich were found; his campaign website does not mention an opponent [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he will work to ensure Carmel and Westfield's "voice is heard loud and clear in the General Assembly," citing local control and continued investment in local amenities [10].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Carmel Mayor Sue Finkam and Westfield Mayor Scott Willis are co-chairs of his re-election campaign [10].
- The caucus reports he was named a 2026 Legislative Champion by the Indiana Technology & Innovation Association [3].

## Lindsay Gramlich

Lindsay Gramlich, the Democratic nominee, is president and CEO of Girls Inc. of Greater Indianapolis and a first-time candidate focused on school funding and living costs [13].

Campaign website: https://www.lindsaygramlich.com/

### Background

- President and CEO of Girls Inc. of Greater Indianapolis; previously worked in several roles at the Children's Museum of Indianapolis [13].
- Holds a bachelor's degree from DePauw University and a Master of Public Affairs from Indiana University; is a graduate of the Stanley K. Lacy and Mitch Daniels leadership fellowships [18].
- Named to the Indianapolis Business Journal's 2026 Forty Under 40 list and served as co-chair of community outreach for the 2026 Final Four local organizing committee [13].
- Lives in Carmel [18].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced her candidacy on November 14, 2025, and won the Democratic nomination in the May 5, 2026 primary [18, 13].
- After the primary she said Democratic turnout had more than doubled the 2024 primary total, calling those "clear signs that this Statehouse seat is truly winnable" [13].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Says, "I'll fight to fully fund our public schools," and pledges resources for teachers and staff [14].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says costs "are simply too high right now" and pledges to "take on predatory utility rate hikes" [14].

**Data centers**

- Says data centers should be "properly regulated" and that control should be returned to local governments [14].

**Health care**

- Pledges to bring down health care costs and lists health care access, including mental health resources, among her priorities [14, 18].

**Child care**

- Pledges to work to expand access to affordable child care [14].

**Reproductive rights**

- Lists reproductive rights as a campaign priority and says she is running for "a future that looks forward—not backward—on women's rights" [14].

**Government accountability**

- Lists restoring transparency and accountability in state government and "restoring the checks and balances our founders envisioned" among her goals [13, 14].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements by Gramlich about Lopez's record were found; she has said "Hoosiers should demand more from their elected officials" and that national Democrats see "our potential to flip this seat" [13, 19].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Her stated priorities are fully funding public schools, lowering the cost of living and restoring transparency and accountability in state government [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by Run for Something in February 2026 [20].
- The Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee listed her race among its top ten races for July 2026 [19].
- Her campaign website lists support from groups including the DLCC, the American Federation of Teachers, the Indiana AFL-CIO, UAW Region 2B, Mental Health Now and Moms Demand Action's "Gun Sense Candidate" designation [14].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign finance totals were found.
- Primary vote totals were not found; the race listing does not show a Democratic primary opponent.
- Unverified (search snippet of the Indiana Capital Chronicle voter guide, which returned 403): that she blames local funding cuts on SEA 1, wants to balance local funding with stabilizing property tax rates, and opposes tax breaks for data centers.
- Unverified (search snippet only): that she does not support eliminating property taxes but says the system has major problems.
- Her campaign website lists Public Safety and Environment & Energy as priorities but the page read gave no specific positions.
