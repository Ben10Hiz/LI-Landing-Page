# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Ed Clere (Independent)

Ed Clere of New Albany has held House District 72 since 2008, winning every election as a Republican [3]. On Jan. 30, 2026 he said he would leave the Republican Party, not seek a 10th term, and run for New Albany mayor in 2027 as an independent; House Republicans then removed him from their caucus [3, 4].

- Why the seat is open: Clere said "the Republican Party has lost its way, and I can no longer be a part of it," and called the 2025 redistricting push "an example of toxic Washington politics making their way into Indiana" [3]. Speaker Todd Huston said Clere would no longer caucus with House Republicans but would keep his committee assignments [4]. The Floyd County Republican Party said he had voted against Republican values; Clere called that "a very dishonest attempt to rewrite history" [4].
- Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [5], and on the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [6]; voted no on HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025), one of 12 House Republicans to do so [7, 3]. A bomb threat against Clere and his home on Dec. 10, 2025 was under State Police investigation [3].
- Authored Indiana's 2015 syringe-services law after the Scott County HIV outbreak and the 2019 law joining the Nurse Licensure Compact; chaired the Public Health Committee from 2012 to 2015 [3]. In 2026 he sponsored legislation to extend the syringe-exchange law's sunset [3].

## This race

- House District 72 includes most of Floyd County, including New Albany [4, 8].
- Open seat: Rep. Ed Clere, first elected in 2008 as a Republican, is not running after leaving the Republican Party to run for New Albany mayor as an independent in 2027 [3, 4].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Darrell Neeley 2,315 votes (54.19%), Shawn Carruthers 1,957 (45.81%) [8]. Democratic primary: Michele Henry 2,248 (52.16%), Cory Cochran 2,062 (47.84%) [8].
- Debates and forums: Neeley and Carruthers debated at a Floyd County Republican Party event before the primary [9]. iVoterGuide reports that Henry took part in a MADVoters Indiana panel on Sept. 5, 2026 [10]. No general-election debate between Neeley and Henry was found.
- In an April 2026 LPM interview, all four primary candidates said they would have voted against the 2025 congressional redistricting bill [11].
- Key differences: Henry emphasizes utility rates, data-center costs, affordable housing and concerns about school-voucher spending [11]; Neeley emphasizes stewardship of tax dollars, listening to residents and bringing state jobs and projects to Southern Indiana [9, 11].

## Darrell Neeley

Darrell Neeley is a retired Navy commander who served 33 years and is now an associate minister in New Albany [12]. He won the May 2026 Republican primary with 54% [8].

Campaign website: https://www.neeleyforindiana.com/

### Background

- Served 33 years in the U.S. Navy, starting in the enlisted ranks and retiring as a commander [12]; says he designed and oversaw $300 million budgets [9].
- Associate minister at Howard Chapel Missionary Baptist Church in New Albany; member of the VFW Post 1693 honor guard, American Legion Post 28 and the New Albany Elks [12].
- New Albany High School graduate; undergraduate degree from Auburn University; MBA and master's in human resource management from Webster University [12].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced Feb. 2, 2026 [12]; won the May 5 Republican primary over former Floyd County commissioner Shawn Carruthers by 358 votes [8].

### Positions & plans

**Taxes & spending**

- Says the next representative should "keep in mind the people's wages" and be a good steward of tax money [9].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says Southern Indiana must be included "in the discussions when it comes to the job opportunities, the state monies and projects" [9].

**Housing**

- Says Floyd County, and New Albany especially, lacks affordable housing and asks what the state can do to encourage more [11].

**Data centers**

- Says data centers should not be a burden on taxpayers, and praised Gov. Mike Braun's efforts to lower utility costs [11].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said he would have voted against the 2025 congressional redistricting bill, and that Indiana leaders should do what is right for Indiana [11].

### On the current officeholder

- At a Republican debate he said the Floyd County districts need to earn respect at the Statehouse by bringing "true conservatism to the House" [9]. No direct statements about Clere were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he would listen to residents and "ensure that [people] have that voice, and they have somebody that listens" [11].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- At his announcement he listed support from Floyd County Commissioners Jason Sharp and Frank Loop, Clerk Danita Burks, Recorder Lois Endris, Auditor Diana Topping, Council President Danny Short and other local officials [12]. iVoterGuide lists an NRA-PVF endorsement [13]. No fundraising totals were verified.

### What we couldn't verify

- His campaign website lists no issue positions; no questionnaire answers were found.
- Unverified (search snippets only): he ran unsuccessfully for New Albany City Council in 2023.
- No campaign finance reports were reviewed.

## Michele (Shelly) Henry

Michele Henry is a New Albany-area employment and labor lawyer of about 25 years and a Floyd County Democratic precinct chair [11]. She won the May 2026 Democratic primary with 52% [8].

Campaign website: https://michelefor72.com/

### Background

- Employment attorney for about 25 years who represents plaintiffs and labor unions [11, 14].
- Has served on the local and national boards of Americans United for Separation of Church and State; serves on the boards of Waterfront Botanical Gardens and the Women Lawyers Association; volunteers with Access Justice [11, 14].
- First run for office; she said she decided to run because "our country is at a precipice" [11].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Feb. 12, 2026 with an endorsement from Nichole Jones, who had ended her own campaign for the seat [14]; won the May 5 Democratic primary over Cory Cochran, 2,248 votes to 2,062 [8].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Supports limiting utility companies' ability to "increase rates indiscriminately," calling energy "a fundamental human necessity" [11].

**Data centers**

- Wants more research on data-center siting and wants their costs "entirely borne by the company," not offset by tax credits and sales-tax exemptions [11].

**Housing**

- Says affordable housing is a real problem and that state business incentives should go toward housing that is affordable to residents below average income [11].

**Education**

- Has raised concerns about spending on Indiana's Choice Scholarship (voucher) program, now open regardless of income [11].

**Health care**

- Lists rising costs of health care, heating and food as her top everyday concerns [14].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said she would have voted against the 2025 redistricting bill, and that "I do not feel obligated to a national party to do the right thing for people in District 72" [11].

### On the current officeholder

- Said she wants a state legislature where "common sense prevails" [14]. No direct statements about Clere were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says her campaign is "here to make Indiana a better place for working people" and to ensure taxpayer money improves quality of life for Indiana residents [8].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by former candidate Nichole Jones; her campaign said it raised more than $6,000 in its first two weeks [14]. iVoterGuide lists endorsements from the Indiana AFL-CIO, IBEW Local 369 and Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates [10].

### What we couldn't verify

- Her campaign website has little issue detail.
- Her nickname is spelled "Shelly" on the ballot and "Shelley" in an LPM candidate list.
- No campaign finance reports were reviewed.
