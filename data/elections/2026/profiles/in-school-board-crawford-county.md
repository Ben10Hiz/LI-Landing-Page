# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The district: Crawford County Community School Corp. covers all of Crawford County, with East, South and West Crawford elementary schools, Crawford County Middle School, Crawford County High School and Crawford County Virtual Academy; its administration office is south of Marengo [24, 25]. Wikipedia lists enrollment of about 1,340 students, without a year [26]. Levi Carmichael became superintendent July 1, 2026 [27].
- The board has seven trustees: four chosen by district and three at-large, with the at-large members living in residential districts A, B and C but elected countywide [28, 29]. The board redrew the four district boundaries in March 2023 after the 2020 Census; District 1 is Liberty 1 and 2 and Whiskey Run 1; District 2 is Jennings 1 and 2, Whiskey Run 2 and the Harrison County part of Milltown; District 3 is Patoka 1 and 2 and Sterling 1 and 2; District 4 is Boone, Johnson 1 and 2, Ohio 1 and 2, Sterling 3 and Union 1 and 2 [28]. The three at-large seats were last elected in 2024 [30].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: the district website lists four trustees whose terms end Dec. 31, 2026 — board president Wayne Carothers, Lance Stroud, Cliff Kerce and Sherry Newton — and three whose terms end in 2028: Jerry Hanger Jr., Craig Adams and Karen Sheller [24]. Newton was appointed in 2023 to fill a vacancy [29]. The state list shows races only for Districts 1, 2 and 4, each electing one member; it lists no District 3 race [6]. Of the four members whose terms end, only Kerce appears to be on the ballot, in District 4 [6, 24].
- Party labels: under the 2025 state law allowing school board candidates to list a party, the District 1 and 2 candidates are listed as Republicans and both District 4 candidates as Democrats [6].
- Superintendent turnover (Dec. 2025–July 2026): Superintendent Brandon Johnson submitted his resignation, effective June 30, 2026, on Dec. 16, 2025; trustee Karen Sheller cast the only vote against accepting it [31]. Johnson then took an indefinite leave, with the high school principal as acting superintendent, and former superintendent Mike Key was hired as interim on a 4-1 vote on Feb. 5, 2026 [32, 33]. On Jan. 20 the board voted to open the search to outside candidates, with Lance Stroud the only trustee opposed [25]. On April 27 the board hired Carmichael, a former district teacher and coach, on a 6-1 vote with Kerce opposed, under a three-year contract at $130,000 a year [34].
- Budget (2026): at a Sept. 17, 2026 hearing on an advertised $18.1 million 2027 budget, Carmichael said the state's property-tax caps limit operations revenue and that falling enrollment is reducing education-fund money; adoption was expected Oct. 20 [35]. The board also approved $1.02 million in extra 2026 spending authority and moved money from operations back to the education fund to cover the rest of the year [35]. In December 2025 the board voted 7-0 to notify all administrators that their contracts might not be renewed for 2026-27 [31].
- Career center (2025–2026): the district is developing a career and technical education center at the former English Elementary School, backed by a $250,000 Community Foundation of Crawford County grant [31]. In September 2026 Carmichael said there were 'hard questions' about operating the site and that the district had talked with companies about partnerships or shared use, adding that this was not a decision to sell the building or end the program [35].
- Facilities and safety: the board awarded a $6.46 million contract in May 2025 for a severe-weather safe room at the middle and high schools, funded by a $6 million FEMA grant, and Carmichael said in September 2026 it should be operational soon [36, 35]. The district's school resource officer program costs $200,000, with an $86,000 state grant covering part [35]. A new wastewater treatment plant and an HVAC project at the middle/high school are also in progress [35].
- Academics: in April 2025 then-superintendent Johnson said attendance was below the state average and test scores had not returned to pre-pandemic levels; the district studied a four-day student week, with a board vote planned for June 2025 [37, 36]. Carmichael reported in September 2026 that 88% of students passed IREAD, about the state average [35].
- Forums: this guide found no 2026 candidate forum, questionnaire or news coverage of these races.

## Clifford Kerce — District 4

Appears to be the incumbent: trustee Cliff Kerce, whose term ends Dec. 31, 2026, is on the current board; listed as a Democrat [24, 6].

### Background

- Trustee Cliff Kerce has served on the board since at least 2023 [28, 24].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed on the state candidate list as a Democratic candidate for District 4, one of two candidates [6].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- In December 2025 Kerce made the motion to notify administrators their contracts might not be renewed, saying, 'We've got a couple of months to analyze our finances and see what we can need and afford' [31].
- At the Sept. 17, 2026 meeting he said of the career center at the former English Elementary, 'I've been asking for a year for a budget, and there's not been a single budget provided as to what it's going to cost to put the building together or what it's going to cost to operate it' [35].

**Teachers & staff**

- In January 2026 he favored opening the superintendent search beyond internal candidates, saying, 'I think we look for the best applicant, whether it's here in Crawford or a surrounding county or in the state or wherever' [32]. He made the Jan. 20 motion that opened the search externally and hired a consultant [25].
- On hiring Mike Key as interim superintendent, he said 'for where we're at now, I think this is the best option to keep us going' [33]. When the consultant said the previous superintendent's pay was low for the area, Kerce said he thought the district was comparable to surrounding counties and asked for the data [33].
- He cast the only vote against hiring Levi Carmichael as superintendent on April 27, 2026 [34].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member [24]. In September 2026 he pressed for cost estimates on the career center; board president Wayne Carothers said no vote had been taken to stop the project and that he still supported it [35].
