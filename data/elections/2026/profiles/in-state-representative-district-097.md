# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Justin Moed (Democratic)

Justin Moed has represented House District 97 since 2012 and serves as House Democratic Whip and ranking minority member of the Public Policy Committee [3, 4]. His caucus highlights his work on Indianapolis road funding, homelessness and housing, and utility costs [3].

- Committees: Public Policy (ranking minority member), Local Government, and Agriculture and Rural Development; also House Democratic Whip [3]. Before running for office he worked at the Statehouse as a doorkeeper and later senior policy analyst [3, 4].
- 2025: Long sought a change to the state road-funding formula, filing a bill for five years running; backed HB 1461 after it was amended to add $50 million for Marion County secondary streets if the city matches it, saying the current system leaves Indianapolis "short-changed" [5].
- Housing and homelessness: created the Low-Barrier Shelter Task Force in 2022, which recommended a low-barrier shelter; in October 2025 he celebrated the groundbreaking of the city's Housing Hub, which includes Indianapolis' first low-barrier shelter [6].
- 2026: Authored HB 1213 to remove the 7% sales tax from household utility bills [7] and HB 1435 on landlord-tenant matters, which was referred to the Judiciary Committee without further action [8].
- Voted no on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [9]; the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [10]; and HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [11].

## This race

- House District 97 is in Marion County and includes downtown and parts of southwest Indianapolis, according to Mirror Indy [4, 3].
- In 2024 Moed won with 58% of the vote against Republican Stephen Whitmer (39%) and Libertarian Mark Renholzberger (about 3%) [12].
- May 5, 2026 Democratic primary: Moed 1,820 votes (67.3%), Sarah Shydale 886 (32.8%) [13, 4]. No Republican filed for the seat [4, 13]; Renholzberger was nominated by the Libertarian Party at its March 2026 convention [14].
- Key differences: Moed's stated priorities include road funding, utility costs and homelessness [3, 7]; Renholzberger has not published issue positions that could be found [15, 16].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Justin Moed

Justin Moed, the incumbent and House Democratic Whip, is seeking an eighth term [3, 4]. He won a contested Democratic primary in May with about 67% of the vote [13].

### Background

- Butler University graduate; owns and manages an urban farm; appointed to the IUPUI Board of Advisors, White River State Park Commission and State Tourism Council, according to his caucus [3].
- Lives on the south side of Indianapolis [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary over Sarah Shydale, an accountant for the Indiana Department of Child Services, 67.3% to 32.8% [13, 4].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Said of HB 1213: "You can't opt out of electricity or water. You need them," and that the state should not get "windfall profits" in sales-tax revenue when utility rates rise [7].

**Transportation**

- Said the road-funding formula "is imbalanced for high-population centers like Indianapolis" because it does not consider lanes or traffic [5].

**Housing**

- Said the Housing Hub shelter "will fill a critical gap in Indy's support services" [6].
- Authored a 2026 landlord-tenant bill (HB 1435) [8].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Moed about his Libertarian opponent were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- His caucus describes his focus as strengthening neighborhoods and helping constituents navigate state government [3].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed in the primary by the Indiana Farm Bureau's AgELECT PAC, according to Wikipedia's race page [13]. No fundraising figures were verified.

## Mark A Renholzberger

Mark Renholzberger, a supply-chain and production-scheduling professional, is the Libertarian nominee, running for this seat for the third time [17, 14]. He received about 3% of the vote in 2024 [12].

### Background

- Master scheduler at Aero Industries since 2023; earlier held supply chain, operations and chemical engineering roles going back to 1982, according to BallotReady [17].
- Holds a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering from Rose-Hulman Institute of Technology and an MBA from Indiana University, according to WFYI's 2024 voter guide [18].

### Path to the ballot

- Named among the Libertarian Party of Indiana's General Assembly nominees at its March 20-22, 2026 convention in Fort Wayne [14].
- Previously ran for House District 97 in 2020 and 2024 and for Indianapolis City-County Council District 18 in 2023 [17, 13].

### On the current officeholder

### How they say they'd run the office
