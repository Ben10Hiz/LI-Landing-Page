# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Jennifer Meltzer (Republican)

Jennifer Meltzer, a Shelbyville attorney and former deputy attorney general, has represented House District 73 since November 2022 [3, 4]. The House Republican caucus lists her as vice chair of the Courts and Criminal Code Committee [3].

- Committees: Courts and Criminal Code (vice chair), Judiciary, and Local Government, per the caucus's member page [3].
- 2023: Her first law, HEA 1286, requires coroners to test for xylazine, a veterinary sedative found mixed into street drugs, in suspected overdose deaths [5].
- 2024: Authored HEA 1203, which made possessing xylazine without a prescription a crime and set penalties for dealing it, effective July 1, 2024 [6]. She said the law was "a strong step in the right direction to help law enforcement get 'tranq' off of our streets" [6].
- Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [7], and yes on the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [8]. Was one of the Republicans who voted no on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 on Dec. 5, 2025 [9].

## This race

- House District 73 covers portions of Bartholomew, Decatur, Jennings and Shelby counties in southeastern Indiana, per the House Republican caucus [3].
- Meltzer is seeking a third full term; she won 76.9% of the vote in 2024 [10].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Meltzer 3,675 votes (66.6%), Edward Comstock 1,844 (33.4%) [10]. A.J. Miller was the only Democratic candidate [10].
- Key differences: Miller's campaign lists labor, infrastructure, education, natural resources, public health and legalizing cannabis as issues [11]; Meltzer's campaign site lists opposition to abortion, support for gun rights, school choice and reducing regulation [12].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Allen J. (A.J.) Miller

A.J. Miller is a technical writer from Greensburg making his first run for office [13]. He was the only Democrat in the May primary [10].

Campaign website: https://millerfor73.com/

### Background

- Works as a technical writer producing repair documentation, and says this is his first run for office [13].
- Grew up on a small farm outside Pendleton; lives in Greensburg [13].
- Lists membership in the Indiana Rural Summit [14].

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only Democratic candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [10].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Says "Everyday Hoosiers continue to struggle while those in power benefit from our struggle" and that he would work to even the playing field [11].

**Infrastructure**

- Calls for repairing existing infrastructure first and then pursuing long-term preventative solutions, with technology as part of that work [11].

**Education**

- Supports better educational opportunities, more support for teachers and more support for the trades [11].

**Environment**

- Says natural resources should be prioritized and protected from excessive industrial development [11].

**Health care**

- Says he would pursue policies aimed at a healthier Indiana [11]. Supports legalizing and regulating cannabis, citing economic growth and health benefits [11].

### On the current officeholder

- Says he started the campaign "as a way to take a principled stand that no incumbent should run unopposed" [14]. His campaign site does not mention Meltzer by name; he says lawmakers have ignored public testimony [13].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he wants to bring "Better Outcomes for the State of Indiana" [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign site [14].

### What we couldn't verify

- No news coverage of his campaign was found; local outlets The Republic and Shelby County news sites could not be read.
- No fundraising totals were found.
- His campaign site does not give detailed stances on property taxes or the state budget.

## Jennifer Meltzer

Jennifer Meltzer is the two-term incumbent, a Shelbyville attorney who represents the city of Shelbyville [3]. She won the Republican primary with 66.6% [10].

Campaign website: https://www.meltzerforindiana.com/

### Background

- Attorney who represents the city of Shelbyville; previously a deputy attorney general and an Indiana Department of Health employee [3].
- Psychology degree from Xavier University and law degree from the IU McKinney School of Law [3].
- Lives in Shelbyville [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Edward Comstock, 66.6% to 33.4% [10].

### Positions & plans

**Public safety**

- Authored the 2023 and 2024 xylazine laws on overdose testing and criminal penalties [5, 6].

**Abortion**

- Her campaign site describes her as "Pro-Life" [12].

**Gun rights**

- Her campaign site describes her as "Pro 2nd Amendment" [12].

**Education**

- Campaign site lists "Empowering parents to choose the best education for their children" [12].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted no on the December 2025 congressional redistricting bill, HB 1032 [9].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements about her challenger were found [12].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Campaign site lists "Fighting unnecessary government regulation" [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by Gov. Mike Braun, the NFIB Indiana PAC and the NRA Political Victory Fund, per Wikipedia's race listing [10].

### What we couldn't verify

- No public statement explaining her no vote on HB 1032 was found.
- Search snippets (unverified) say she authored HB 1312 (2025, public notices), which passed the House 57-36, and filed HB 1055 (December 2025) to let cities and towns move municipal elections to even-numbered years; final status not confirmed.
- No 2025–2026 bills she authored were confirmed as enacted.
- No fundraising totals were found.
