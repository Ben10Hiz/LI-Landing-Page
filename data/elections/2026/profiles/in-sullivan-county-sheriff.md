# Indiana County Sheriff — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-11 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

The sheriff is an elected county officer created by the Indiana Constitution who arrests offenders, keeps the peace, serves court papers, keeps order in county courts and runs the county jail [1, 2]. The Indiana Sheriffs' Association describes sheriffs as constitutional officers elected by the people, whose work includes operating jails, serving civil process, providing court security and coordinating emergency response [3]. Every county elects its sheriff in 2026 for a four-year term, and no one may serve as sheriff more than eight years in any twelve-year period [1].

- **Term:** 4 years; the sheriff serves until a successor is elected and qualified [4, 1]
- **Elected:** Countywide by the county's voters on the partisan general election ballot; all county sheriffs are elected in 2026 [1]
- **Term limit:** No more than eight years in any period of twelve years (Indiana Constitution, Article 6, Section 2) [1]
- **Qualifications:** Must have lived in the county for at least one year before the election, be registered to vote in the county by the filing deadline, and file a statement of economic interests; a sheriff who moves out of the county forfeits the office [1, 4]
- **Pay:** A county may pay the sheriff a salary from the county general fund of at least 50% to 100% of the state minimum salary of a full-time prosecuting attorney, depending on county population [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 92 races; 26 contested [6]

### What the office does

- Arrests without a warrant people who commit an offense in the sheriff's view, brings them before a county court, and holds them while the arrest is investigated [2].
- Suppresses breaches of the peace and pursues and jails felons [2].
- Takes care of the county jail and the prisoners held there [2, 3].
- Serves and carries out legal papers (process) directed to the sheriff by courts and the county commissioners [2].
- Attends and keeps order in all courts of the county, which the Sheriffs' Association describes as providing court security [2, 3].
- Takes photographs, fingerprints and other identification data from people held for crimes, and DNA samples for felonies [2].
- Reports twice a year to the Indiana Department of Correction the average daily cost of holding a jail prisoner [2].
- Holds state prisoners, including people convicted of Level 6 felonies, in the county jail, for which the state reimburses counties at a daily rate set in the state budget [7].
- Coordinates emergency response and works with local police, prosecutors and community partners [3].
- In a county whose council creates a sheriff's merit board by ordinance, appoints three of its five members, while the county police officers elect the other two; no more than two sheriff appointees and one elected member may belong to the same party [8].
- Must report information to federal authorities when a person arrested for a felony or misdemeanor is believed to lack legal immigration status, and, since July 2026, notify the judge setting bail when holding a person subject to an immigration detainer [9, 10].
- May supervise and inspect businesses such as pawnbrokers and secondhand dealers [2].
- What the sheriff does not control: the county commissioners must establish and maintain the county jail building [11]; the county makes the appropriation for feeding prisoners and sets the sheriff's salary [5]; the prosecuting attorney decides which crimes to prosecute [12]; and judges decide bail [13].

### Recent changes to the office

- February 2025: The Indiana Department of Correction said funding to reimburse county jails for holding Level 6 felons and state prisoners, then $40 per day, was exhausted for state fiscal year 2025; Tippecanoe County Sheriff Robert Goldsmith, then president of the Indiana Sheriffs' Association, called it very disappointing, and Rep. Greg Steuerwald (R-Avon) said everything owed would be paid [14].
- 2025: The new state budget raised the daily rate for Level 6 offenders to $42, raised the jail reimbursement line to $45 million and then $41 million a year, and moved to direct reimbursement with costs submitted within 60 days; Sheriffs' Association executive director Stephen Luce called the rate better than nothing, citing an association study that put the average cost at about $74 per inmate per day [7].
- July 2025: The Department of Correction planned to begin paying about $12.7 million owed to county jails for housing state offenders [7].
- July 2025: House Enrolled Act 1393 took effect, requiring jails to alert federal immigration authorities, with the county sheriff passing the information to federal officials, after felony or misdemeanor arrests when there is probable cause the person lacks legal status [9].
- July 2026: Senate Bill 76 took effect, requiring sheriff's offices and police departments to notify the bail judge when they hold a person subject to an immigration detainer and barring local policies that restrict cooperation with federal officials; St. Joseph County Sheriff Bill Redman said his county already cooperates with ICE, while South Bend Mayor James Mueller said the city will protect the constitutional rights of everyone living there [10].
- January 2026: The Sheriffs' Association's 2026 president, Porter County Sheriff Jeffrey Balon, listed jail overcrowding, staffing shortages, the drug epidemic and mental illness among the issues sheriffs raise with legislators [3].
- November 2026: Public Question 1 asks voters whether courts may deny bail, for offenses other than murder or treason, when the state proves by clear and convincing evidence that no release conditions will reasonably protect others or the community [15]; Sen. Eric Koch (R-Bedford) said releasing some defendants before trial is too dangerous, while Zach Stock of the Indiana Public Defender Council raised concerns about jail overcrowding [13].

## Current officeholder: Jason Bobbitt (Democratic)

Jason Bobbitt, a Democrat and former chief deputy, won the open Sullivan County sheriff's seat in November 2022 with 53% of the vote over Republican Billy Snead and was sworn in January 1, 2023, succeeding term-limited Sheriff Clark Cottom [16]. He is seeking a second term [17, 6].

- November 2022: Elected sheriff, 53% to 47%, with 6,499 votes cast in the race [16].
- October 2023: During construction of the new county jail, Bobbitt said move-in was looking more likely in March than April, with a transition period for staff to learn the control tower, new equipment and new policies [18].
- March 2024: The county held a public open house for the new 55,000-square-foot, 197-bed jail, built to address overpopulation at the old jail [19].
- July 2025: The sheriff's office announced a recovery treatment program for jail inmates with the nonprofit Quality 360 Healthcare, funded by a state grant backed by the Indiana Sheriffs' Association; Bobbitt said it is 'of no cost to the taxpayers of Sullivan County' and that the county would consider next steps such as community housing for ongoing support [20].
- March 2026: At a Farm Bureau candidate forum, Bobbitt and all three Republican candidates identified recruiting and retaining deputies, jail officers and dispatchers as the department's top challenge, citing competition from higher-paying agencies and the demands of covering a large rural county [21].

## This race

- Sheriff Jason Bobbitt is completing his first term and is eligible to run again; he faces Republican Justin Copeland, a sergeant in Bobbitt's own office, on November 3, 2026 [22, 6].
- In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Copeland received 1,785 votes (55.3%) to Matt Price's 1,089 (33.7%) and Mike Luttrell's 356 (11%) in results reported by the Sullivan Times [23]. No contested Democratic sheriff primary appears in that report.
- On March 20, 2026, Sullivan County Farm Bureau and the Sullivan Civic Center held a moderated candidate forum that included Bobbitt, Copeland, Luttrell and Price; discussion covered recruitment and retention, expanding reserve deputies, proactive patrols and leadership qualifications [21].
- Key differences in available statements: Bobbitt points to his administrative experience and record, while Copeland says the county has fallen behind on investigative resources and that the sheriff should be more visible in schools and at public events [17].
- No general-election forum or debate between Bobbitt and Copeland was found in available coverage.

## Jason Bobbitt

Jason Bobbitt is the incumbent Sullivan County sheriff, elected in 2022 after serving as the office's chief deputy; he says he has been a police officer for 30 years [17, 16].

### Background

- Sullivan County sheriff since January 2023 [16].
- Previously chief deputy of the Sullivan County Sheriff's Office: described as seven years in 2022 coverage [24], and as eight years by Bobbitt in an April 2026 interview, in which he also said he has been a police officer for 30 years [17].
- Said in 2022 that as chief deputy he sat in county council budget hearings for seven years and was familiar with the budget line items, though the sheriff managed the budget [24].

### Path to the ballot

- Democratic nominee for sheriff in 2026 [6]; no contested Democratic sheriff primary was reported [23].

### Positions & plans

**Jail & corrections**

- In 2022 said he would emphasize rehabilitation in the new jail, including Bible studies for inmates who want them, and that 'the majority of the people in that jail are really decent people. They just made a bad decision' [24].
- In 2025 said of the inmate recovery program, 'If we can help one person, it's worth the whole effort,' and pointed to a lack of state hospital beds for people with mental illness, many of whom end up incarcerated [20].

**Staffing & pay**

- At the March 2026 forum, identified recruitment and retention of deputies, jail officers and dispatchers as a top challenge, as did the other candidates [21].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. In April 2026 he said, 'I feel like I've earned the community's trust,' and that he had proven his leadership over 12 years in administration, including four as sheriff [17]. No statements about Copeland specifically were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- At the March 2026 forum he said, 'I feel like I've accomplished quite a bit, but I'm not done' [21].
- Said in July 2025 that after inmates complete program assessments, the county would consider further steps such as community housing for ongoing support [20].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign-finance totals were found in available coverage.

### What we couldn't verify

- No 2026 campaign website or questionnaire was found; detailed forum answers beyond the summary in the Sullivan Times were not available.
- Reported length of his chief-deputy service differs (seven years in 2022 coverage; eight years in his own 2026 statement).
- The exact date the new jail opened to inmates, current jail population and staffing vacancy numbers were not found.
- No information found on school resource officers, immigration-detainer agreements or budget requests to the county council during his term.

## Justin Copeland

Justin Copeland is a sergeant at the Sullivan County Sheriff's Office who won a three-way Republican primary in May 2026 [22, 23].

### Background

- Sergeant at the Sullivan County Sheriff's Office [22, 17].
- Said at the March 2026 forum that he has served Sullivan County for the last 10 years [21].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 1,785 votes (55.3%) over Matt Price and Mike Luttrell [23, 22, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Patrol & response**

- Says Sullivan County has 'fallen behind some neighboring counties' in the resources it uses, citing the Internet Crimes Against Children (ICAC) task force and the High-Tech Crimes Unit, which he said also help with human trafficking cases [17].

**School safety**

- Wants deputies 'in the schools' and at public events, building rapport and trust with the community, 'especially the kids' [22].

**Staffing & pay**

- At the March 2026 forum, identified recruitment and retention of deputies, jail officers and dispatchers as a top challenge, as did the other candidates [21].

### On the current officeholder

- Has not been reported criticizing Sheriff Bobbitt by name. He says the county has fallen behind other counties on investigative resources, and that 'I believe the sheriff should be out in the county engaging in the schools at the events with the public,' adding that he does not see the job as one 'where you are in an office all the time' [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said his 'main objective as sheriff is to be more community-oriented' [17].
- Said at the March 2026 forum he looks forward to 'leading from the top and continuing to serve just how I've always served' [21].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign-finance totals were found in available coverage.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website or questionnaire was found; his law-enforcement career before becoming a sergeant (start date, assignments, training) was not found beyond his statement of 10 years serving the county.
- An undated Sullivan County staff directory listing a 'Justin Copeland' as a deputy appeared only in search results and could not be opened (unverified).
- A Sullivan County Ag Day listing reportedly named 'Justin Copeland for Sheriff' as a sponsor (search snippet only; unverified).
- No stated positions found on the jail, drugs, budget or immigration enforcement.
