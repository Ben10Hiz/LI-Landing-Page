# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Ryan Dvorak (Democratic)

Ryan Dvorak, a Democrat and attorney from South Bend, has represented House District 8 since 2002 and serves on the Rules and Legislative Procedures, Natural Resources and Judiciary committees, according to the House Democratic Caucus [3, 4].

- First elected in 2002, succeeding his father, Michael A. Dvorak; he chaired the House Courts and Criminal Code Committee beginning in 2003 and was named Assistant Democratic Leader after the 2012 elections [4].
- Before joining the House, he was a senior aide to U.S. Rep. Tim Roemer; the House Democratic Caucus describes his focus areas as renewable energy policy, public health and environmental protection, workers' wages and sick time, and college affordability [3, 4].
- February 2023: the full House adopted his amendment to House Bill 1639 (regional watershed development commissions) requiring river work to have a scientific basis and keeping St. Joseph River communities in the watershed effort; he said "The rivers of Indiana are an irreplaceable natural resource" [5, 6].
- Was re-elected without opposition in 2024 [7].
- December 5, 2025: voted no on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57–41 with all House Democrats and 12 Republicans voting no [8, 9].
- 2026 session (per BillTrack50's vote record): voted no on Senate Bill 12 (prohibiting ranked-choice voting), Senate Bill 76 (immigration matters) and House Bill 1343 (public safety matters); voted yes on Senate Bill 285 (housing matters) and Senate Bill 91 (syringe exchange program); recorded absent on House Bill 1177 (child care assistance) [8].

## This race

- House District 8 covers portions of South Bend and northern St. Joseph County, including the University of Notre Dame, Saint Mary's College and Holy Cross College campuses, according to the House Democratic Caucus [3].
- Dvorak, the incumbent, is seeking re-election; Wikipedia's listing of the May 5, 2026 primaries shows Dvorak as the only Democratic candidate and no Republican primary candidate [7]. Republican Eric Harris appears on the general-election ballot [2, 10].

## Ryan M Dvorak

Ryan Dvorak, a Democrat and South Bend attorney, has held the District 8 seat since 2002 and is seeking another term [3, 4].

Campaign website: https://ryandvorak.com

### Background

- Holds a bachelor's degree in philosophy from the University of Notre Dame and a law degree from the Indiana University McKinney School of Law [3].
- Practices law with May Oberfell Lorber and was a senior aide to U.S. Rep. Tim Roemer from 1996 to 2001 [4].
- Ran unsuccessfully in the 2011 Democratic primary for mayor of South Bend [4].
- Lives in South Bend [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only Democratic candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary, according to Wikipedia's listing [7].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted against the December 2025 mid-decade congressional redistricting bill (HB 1032) and, in 2026, against Senate Bill 12, which prohibits ranked-choice voting [8].

**Energy & utilities**

- The House Democratic Caucus describes renewable energy policy as one of his focus areas [3]; Wikipedia credits him with authoring legislation on net metering, renewable energy standards and utility accountability [4].

**Environment**

- Secured a 2023 amendment requiring a scientific basis for regional watershed commission projects and keeping St. Joseph River communities in watershed management [5].
