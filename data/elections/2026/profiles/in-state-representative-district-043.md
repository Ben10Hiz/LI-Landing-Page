# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Tonya Pfaff (Democratic)

Tonya Pfaff, a retired Terre Haute high school math teacher, has represented House District 43 since she was first elected in 2018 [3]. She serves on the Ways and Means and Education committees and is ranking minority member of the Agriculture and Rural Development Committee [3, 4].

- Committees: Ways and Means; Education; Agriculture and Rural Development (ranking minority member) [4].
- April 10, 2025: Voted no when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [5]; she said it "offers very little to individual homeowners while shifting the burden to increased local income taxes" and that Vigo County "stands to lose $20.5 million" [6].
- April 2025: Added an amendment requiring consent instruction in human sexuality curriculum to SB 442; after the language was removed in conference committee, it was restored in the final version passed April 23, 2025 [7].
- April 25, 2025: Voted no on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [8].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted no when HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, passed the House 57-41 [9].
- 2026 session: Authored HB 1325, directing the education, child services, health and family and social services agencies to share data on services for children with special needs; it passed the House 93-0 and the Senate 45-0 and was signed Feb. 24, 2026 [10, 11]. Jan. 28, 2026: Voted yes on HB 1002 (electric utility affordability), which passed 89-4 [12].

## This race

- House District 43 is in Vigo County [3]; Pfaff's campaign describes it as covering Terre Haute, West Terre Haute and Seelyville [13].
- Wikipedia's 2026 election summary notes Pfaff was re-elected without opposition in 2024 and that Donald Trump narrowly carried the district in 2024 [14].
- Pfaff and Lore were the only candidates listed in their parties' May 5, 2026 primaries [14]; both are on the November ballot [2].
- Key differences: Pfaff opposed the 2025 property-tax law, the 2025 budget and the 2025 redistricting bill and opposes universal private school vouchers [5, 8, 9, 15]; Lore, a Vigo County school board member, emphasizes reducing administrative paperwork for schools, spending accountability and private-sector job growth [16].
- No general-election debate or forum for this race was found.

## Amy Lore

Amy Lore is a Vigo County School Board member and former board president with a background in communications and public policy [17, 18]. She is running on school quality, spending accountability and economic growth [16].

Campaign website: https://www.amylore.com/

### Background

- Elected to the Vigo County School Corporation board in 2020 and re-elected in 2024; board president 2023–2024 [18].
- More than two decades in communications and public policy, including as an adviser and speechwriter to former Lt. Gov. Becky Skillman [17].
- Indiana State University graduate (bachelor's and master's in public administration); lives in Terre Haute's Farrington's Grove neighborhood [17, 18].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Republican in early February 2026 [17]; the only Republican listed in the May 5, 2026 primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Wants to improve student results and teaching quality while reducing administrative paperwork and keeping decisions local; says "Every child needs access to high-quality education and the opportunity to succeed" [16].

**Government spending**

- Supports cost-conscious government with fewer unnecessary rules; says "Hoosiers need practical solutions and responsible spending from their leaders" [16].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says "Government does not create jobs. It creates an environment where Hoosiers can create jobs" [16].
- Wants the state to support local partnerships among residents, institutions and businesses in Vigo County [16].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Lore about Pfaff or her record were found; her announcement said she is running to keep state government "focused on serving taxpayers" [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said she is running to strengthen schools and grow economic opportunity [17].

### Endorsements & fundraising

## Tonya Pfaff

Tonya Pfaff is the Democratic incumbent, first elected in 2018 and seeking a fifth term [3, 17]. Her 2026 special-needs services bill became law, and she voted against the 2025 property-tax law, budget and redistricting bill [11, 5, 8, 9].

Campaign website: https://www.tonyapfaff.com/

### Background

- Taught high school math for 32 years at Terre Haute South, West and North high schools, retiring in 2025 [3].
- Bachelor's degree from Saint Mary-of-the-Woods College and master's from the University of Oklahoma; lives in Terre Haute [20].

### Path to the ballot

- The only Democrat listed in the May 5, 2026 primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Says "education funding for our schools, Indiana State University and Ivy Tech are the first priority" and that the teacher shortage "must be addressed" [21].
- Opposes universal private school vouchers, saying they "take funding meant for the kids in our public schools"; she cites a figure that Vigo County schools have lost more than $36 million since the program expanded [15].

**Property taxes**

- Opposed SEA 1 (2025), saying homeowners' savings would be offset by higher local income taxes and asking, "Of course everyone wants to pay less in property taxes, but at what cost?" [6].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said the 2025 congressional maps "were not drawn to benefit Hoosiers, but to artificially boost the political performance of one party" [22].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says "Terre Haute and Vigo County must be aggressive in competing for jobs" and supports expanded vocational and apprenticeship programs [21].

**Infrastructure**

- Says local roads depend on adequate state funding and calls for affordable high-speed broadband for every home and business [21].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Pfaff about Lore were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists education and economic development for Terre Haute, West Terre Haute and Seelyville as her legislative goals [3, 13].
