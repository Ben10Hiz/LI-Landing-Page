# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Randolph Southern School Corporation (RSSC) is based in Lynn in southern Randolph County and was formed from Washington and Greensfork townships; it runs Randolph Southern Elementary, Randolph Southern Jr./Sr. High School and a preschool [24, 25]. State enrollment counts were 511 students in 2023-24, 478 in 2024-25 and 461 in 2025-26 [26]. The superintendent's January 2026 financial report said enrollment fell from 445 to 436 between October 2024 and October 2025 and is down about 20% since 2017 [27].
- Superintendent: Dr. Donnie Bowsman [25, 27].
- The board has five members, all elected at large; the State Board of Education approved the change from township-based seats (two from Washington Township, two from Greensfork Township, one at large) to five at-large seats in June 2021 [24]. Current members are Heather Good (president), Crystle Austin (vice president), Trent Hunt (secretary), Andrew (Andy) Tillson and Patricia Jeffries [28, 27].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: the district lists the terms of Good and Austin as running Jan. 1, 2023 to Dec. 31, 2026, and the other three members' terms to Dec. 31, 2028 [28]. In 2022 Austin, Good and Janet M. Caudle ran for 'the two open positions' [29]. The state list shows one At Large race, 'vote for 1', with Crystle N Austin and Heather M Good, both Republican [6]. Because both expiring terms are on the ballot, this guide believes the race elects two members; the News-Gazette reported on Oct. 6, 2026 that none of Randolph County's local school board races are contested [30]. If so, both incumbents will be returned.
- Budget and enrollment: the superintendent reported in January 2026 that RSSC took in $3,984,361.81 and spent $4,263,464.50 in 2025, and the board moved $162,172 from the Rainy Day Fund to the Education Fund and $44,000 from the Windmill Fund to the Cafeteria Fund to cover 2025 shortfalls [27]. His report said the board 'will need to consider other funding streams, or no pay increases, or a reduction in force, or programming cuts, or a referendum, or consolidation if the pattern of decreased enrollment continues' [27]. The board also approved a resolution allowing up to $400,000 to move from the Rainy Day Fund to the Education Fund in 2026 in case of lost tuition support [27].
- Property taxes: assessed value rose 26%, from about $252.9 million to $319.3 million, largely from solar and wind farms, and the tax rate fell from $0.7509 to $0.5974, the lowest of Randolph County's school corporations; the superintendent said a state cap held the levy increase to 4% and that the per-acre solar assessment will stay frozen under SEA 1 unless an appeal is granted [27]. No operating or safety referendum was found.
- Teacher pay: on Sept. 30, 2025 the board approved a 2025-26 contract raising starting teacher pay to $45,000 as required by state law, with a 1.5% base increase; the average teacher salary rose from $58,222.87 to $58,722.87 [31]. In January 2026 the board voted 5-0 to keep member pay at $2,000 a year and $60 per meeting rather than raise it to amounts newly allowed by state law [27].
- Facilities bond: in March 2026 architects presented a study of projects including a high school gym HVAC replacement, an elementary entrance, a bus canopy and soundproofing the Student Activity Center, to be paid by a bond of up to $2 million [32]. The board hired bond counsel in April and approved project, appropriation and final bond resolutions in July and August 2026; the August minutes say the bond would sell for about $2,125,000 [33, 34, 35].
- Special education: in August 2026 the superintendent told the board that Monroe Central and Liberty Perry plan to leave the Greater Randolph Interlocal Cooperative (GRIC), the special education cooperative RSSC shares with them, Randolph Central and Randolph Eastern, on July 1, 2029, which he said would affect RSSC's ability to provide services [35].
- Safety and other policies: in July 2026 the board approved a 2027 Secured School Safety Grant of $43,748.75 for a full-time school resource officer [34]. In March 2026 the board discussed LifeWise Academy, an off-site, Bible-based character education program during the school day for students with parent permission; the superintendent said he would recommend it for grades 3-6 if all members agreed [32].
- Academics: the superintendent's January 2026 report said the 2025 graduation rate was 97.2% and that RSSC ranked first in Randolph County in grade 3-8 ILEARN English scores and second in math [27].
- Forums: this guide found no 2026 candidate forum, questionnaire or campaign coverage for this race, which appears to be uncontested [30].

## Crystle N Austin — At Large

Incumbent and board vice president, first elected in 2022; her race appears to be uncontested [28, 29, 30].

### Background

- Parent of students in the district; at a May 2026 meeting she said she had a child graduating that month [36].
- Ran for the board in 2022 and has served since Jan. 1, 2023; re-elected vice president in January 2026 [29, 28, 27].
- Serves on the board's negotiations and athletic committees for 2026 [27].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed on the state's 2026 general-election list as a Republican [6].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Supported keeping board pay at the existing $2,000 a year, saying, 'It's not about the money. It's about the teachers and school having everything they need' [27].
- During the 2026 bond planning she questioned a proposed $40,000 wall relocation, saying, 'It is not worth $40,000 to move a wall' [34].

**School safety**

- Asked whether volunteers in the elementary 'lunch buddies' program complete background checks; the principal said all must [37].
- Made the motion to approve the 2027 school safety grant for a full-time school resource officer [34].

**Transparency & community input**

- Said in January 2026, 'I love all the transparency of the board packets' [27].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. Minutes reviewed by this guide show her voting with the 5-0 majority on the 2025-26 teacher contract, the 2026 Rainy Day Fund transfers and the 2026 bond resolutions; she made the motions on two of the July 2026 bond resolutions [31, 27, 34].

## Heather M Good — At Large

Incumbent and board president, first elected in 2022; her race appears to be uncontested [28, 29, 30].

### Background

- Ran for the board in 2022 and has served since Jan. 1, 2023; re-elected president in January 2026 [29, 28, 27].
- Serves on the board's discussion committee and, with Trent Hunt, on its collaboration/consolidation committee for 2026 [27].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed on the state's 2026 general-election list as a Republican [6].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Supported keeping board pay at the existing $2,000 a year, saying, 'We are already dipping into funds' [27].
- Questioned whether some 2026 bond projects were worth the cost, saying of one, 'I just don't know that it is worth all the money for something we don't use as much' [34].

**Facilities**

- Said in February 2026 that 'The HVAC definitely has to be prioritized,' along with light sensors and soundproofing the Student Activity Center [38].

**School safety**

- Said in November 2025 that she would like more screening on windows and doors so people could not see into the building [37].

**Academics**

- Credited teachers for ILEARN and IREAD results and said that fed students 'are going to be able to learn and pay attention more' [39].

**Special education**

- When told in August 2026 that two districts plan to leave the GRIC special education cooperative, asked whether RSSC could join another cooperative [35].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member and its president. Minutes reviewed by this guide show her voting with the 5-0 majority on the 2025-26 teacher contract, the 2026 Rainy Day Fund transfers and the 2026 bond resolutions [31, 27, 34]. On LifeWise Academy she said she would be 'more comfortable with grades 3-6' and that teachers would need to track which students go off site [32].
