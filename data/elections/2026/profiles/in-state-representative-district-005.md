# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Dale DeVon (Republican)

Dale DeVon, a Republican home builder from Granger, was elected to the House in 2012; he chairs the Family, Children and Human Affairs Committee and serves on the Utilities, Energy and Telecommunications Committee [3].

- March 2024: Sponsored Senate Enrolled Act 2, which the caucus says made full-time child care workers automatically eligible for child care assistance and created a pilot for small child care facilities; DeVon said "Increasing access and affordability to child care is a top priority" [4].
- 2025: Authored HEA 1099, which raised the age limit for a surrendered infant under the Safe Haven law from 30 to 60 days; he said "Safe Haven Baby Boxes are an incredible lifesaving measure that protect our most vulnerable" [5].
- On April 10, 2025, voted for SB 1, the property-tax and local government finance bill, which passed the House 65–29 [6]. On April 25, 2025, voted for the conference report on HEA 1001, the 2025–2027 state budget, adopted 66–27 [7].
- December 5, 2025: Voted for HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57–41 [8].
- January–February 2026: Voted for HB 1002, the electric utility affordability bill, on third reading (89–4) and on final concurrence (94–2) [9, 10].
- April 2026: The caucus credits him with HEA 1092, which addresses child support accountability, requires adoption petitions to list open CHINS or delinquency cases, and expands background checks to volunteers in licensed care settings [11].
- 2026: Co-sponsored the Northwest Indiana Stadium Authority law, according to the caucus, and was named chair of the Interim Study Committee on Child Services [3, 12].

## This race

- House District 5 covers portions of St. Joseph County, according to the House Republican caucus [3]. Wait's campaign names Osceola, Granger and Mishawaka as communities in the district [13].
- DeVon was re-elected in 2024 with 54.6% of the vote, according to Wikipedia [14].
- Both candidates were unopposed in their May 5, 2026 primaries, according to Wikipedia [14].
- A proposed Microsoft data center near Granger has drawn resident concerns about transparency, utility rates and flooding; the St. Joseph County Redevelopment Commission approved a memorandum of understanding for early planning on March 17, 2026 [15, 16]. Wait has criticized the project; no statement from DeVon on it was found [17].
- No debate or forum between the candidates was found as of October 10, 2026.

## Alex Wait

Alex Wait, the Democratic nominee from Granger, is a scriptwriter for a YouTube channel who says he is running to lower family costs and support neighborhood schools [13, 18].

Campaign website: https://www.voteforwait.com

### Background

- His family has lived in Granger for 25 years; he attended Penn-Harris-Madison schools, Xavier University and King's College London, and has worked as a scriptwriter for a YouTube channel, according to his campaign [13].
- Lives in Granger [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced his campaign in February 2026 and was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [18, 14].

### Positions & plans

**Data centers**

- Says "Microsoft has not explained the benefits of building a data center in the middle of our community" and that "they have hidden the costs of this project from the public"; suggests the land be used for a community center with a pool or child care center [17].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says "Housing, groceries, and utilities keep climbing, and paychecks are not keeping up," and attributes rising prices in part to past state decisions [17].
- Says new parents are "constantly squeezed by the rising costs of childcare" [13].

**Education**

- Says "Neighborhood schools are a community anchor" and opposes using schools as political battlegrounds [17].
- Says "Indiana's state government is depriving our kids of needed resources" [13].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Says "Let's make it so career politicians can't choose their own voters" [17].

### On the current officeholder

- His campaign pages reviewed do not name DeVon; he says "Indiana's out-of-touch state government is the biggest problem" [13].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists practical help for families, investing in children's futures, and opposing politicians who "sell out" voters as priorities [18].

## Dale DeVon

The Republican incumbent since 2012 and chair of the House Family, Children and Human Affairs Committee [3].

### Background

- Founded DeVon Custom Homes in 1995 and serves as a state and national director of the Home Builders Association [3].
- Served on the St. Joseph County Council beginning in 2002, where he chaired the Land Use Committee [3].
- Born in Clay Township and has lived in Granger since 1989; serves on the school board of The Crossing, a school for at-risk youth [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Child care & families**

- Said in 2024, "Across the state we're experiencing child care deserts, especially in our rural communities" [4].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by DeVon about his challenger were found [3].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Chairs the 2026 Interim Study Committee on Child Services and serves on the Commission on Improving the Status of Children in Indiana [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising
