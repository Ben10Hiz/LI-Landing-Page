# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Heath VanNatter (Republican)

Heath VanNatter, a Kokomo Republican and homebuilder, has represented House District 38 since 2010 [3]. He chairs the House Employment, Labor and Pensions Committee and also serves on the Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development and Public Policy committees [3].

- Committees: Employment, Labor and Pensions (chair); Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development; Public Policy. 2026 interim: chair of the Interim Study Committee on Employment and Labor and member of the Pension Management Oversight committee [3, 4].
- Jan. 2025: Authored HB 1630, which would have legalized marijuana for adults 21 and older and created a Cannabis Commission; it was referred to the Courts and Criminal Code Committee and received no further action [5, 6].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [7]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [8].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [9]; the Senate rejected it 31-19 on Dec. 11 [10].
- 2026 session: House sponsor of SEA 185, which targets foreign-made vaping products with harmful chemicals and tightens tobacco and e-cigarette permit and age-verification rules [11]. Co-authored HEA 1145, which provides a one-time extra payment to retired public employees by Oct. 1, 2026 [12].

## This race

- House District 38 includes all of Carroll and Tipton counties and parts of Cass, Clinton, Howard and Tippecanoe counties, per the House Republican caucus [3]; the Pharos-Tribune describes it as including the west side of Kokomo and southern Cass County [13].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: VanNatter won the Republican nomination with 4,676 votes (58.5%) to 3,316 (41.5%) for Mark Hufford; Nate Stout was unopposed for the Democratic nomination [14]. In Howard County, VanNatter received 1,432 votes to Hufford's 733 [15].
- Key differences: Stout emphasizes public school funding, rural health care and Medicaid, and opposes prioritizing private-school vouchers [16, 17]; VanNatter voted for the 2025 budget, property-tax law and redistricting bill [8, 7, 9] and has authored a bill to legalize adult-use marijuana [5].
- The Cass County Democrats hosted a candidate spotlight with Stout in Logansport in August 2026 [13]. No debate between the candidates was found.

## Heath R. VanNatter

Heath VanNatter is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2010, and chairs the House Employment, Labor and Pensions Committee [3]. He won a contested Republican primary in May 2026 [14].

### Background

- Owner of VanNatter Construction and a small business owner for more than 20 years; past president of the Howard County Home Builders Association; director of the Indiana Builders Association and the National Association of Home Builders; former Howard County Plan Commission member; Northwestern High School graduate; lives in Howard County [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Mark Hufford, 58.5% to 41.5% [14].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- The caucus says he emphasizes economic development, job retention, fiscal responsibility and affordable housing [3].

**Public safety**

- Said of SEA 185: "This new law is about drawing a clear line to protect Hoosiers and prevent dangerous products from reaching consumers" [11].

**Retirement & pensions**

- Said the extra retiree payment in HEA 1145 "ensures their fixed incomes can better keep pace with rising costs" [12].

**Property taxes**

- Voted for SEA 1 (2025) [7].

**Other**

- On HB 1630 (2025), said "It's time for Indiana to join our neighbors and most of the rest of the country in legalizing marijuana" [18].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by VanNatter about Stout were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said interim study committee work would support workers while being responsible with taxpayer dollars: "Thoughtful data-driven policymaking will ensure we continue supporting workers" [4].

### Endorsements & fundraising

### What we couldn't verify

## Nate Stout

Nate Stout, a Tipton native and business consultant, is the Democratic nominee [17, 16]. He emphasizes public schools, rural health care and local economies [17].

### Background

- Owner and founder of Stout Consulting Group since 2025; previously in sales management at Séura, Johnson Outdoors and Callaway Golf; bachelor's degree in marketing from Indiana University Bloomington [16].
- Tipton native [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced in February 2026 [17]; unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [14, 15].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Wants to "Raise teacher pay and restore respect for the teaching profession" and "Fully fund our public schools first," with private and voucher programs not taking priority [16].

**Health care**

- Wants to "Protect our Medicaid program," keep rural hospitals and clinics open and staffed, and expand maternal and neonatal care in rural counties [16].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports small-town grocery and local service businesses, main street renewal, and working with local leaders on grants and infrastructure such as broadband and roads [16].

**Government spending**

- Says he wants to hold government accountable for its spending and that the election will decide "whether resources continue to be siphoned away from rural communities" [17].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about VanNatter were found [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Presents his platform as practical rather than party-line, focused on education, rural health care and local economies [17].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising figures were found.
