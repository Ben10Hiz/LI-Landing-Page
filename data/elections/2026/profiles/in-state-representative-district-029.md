# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Alaina Shonkwiler (Republican)

Alaina Shonkwiler, a Republican from Noblesville, was first elected in 2024 and sworn in on November 21, 2024; the House Republican caucus lists her on the Government and Regulatory Reform, Public Policy, and Utilities, Energy and Telecommunications committees [3]. She previously worked as district director for U.S. Rep. Victoria Spartz and in government consulting and economic development [3, 4].

- December 5, 2025: Voted yes on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 before the Senate defeated it [5, 6].
- January-February 2026: Authored House Bill 1315, which would have required certain smaller townships to merge into municipalities or counties by 2028; it passed the House 55-44 on February 2, 2026, and PolicyRisk lists no action after its February 5 referral to a Senate committee [7, 8]. Carmel City Council President Matthew Snyder said, "Eliminating it does not shrink government, it centralizes power," and the Indiana Township Association opposed the bill; Shonkwiler said the goal was to "deliver better value for taxpayers" [7].
- April 6, 2026: Gov. Mike Braun ceremonially signed House Enrolled Act 1002, which the caucus says was authored by Shonkwiler; the caucus says it sets utility cost-reduction and reliability performance measures, auto-enrolls income-eligible energy-assistance households in smoothed billing, and bars disconnections on the hottest and coldest days [9].
- 2026 session: Voted yes on Senate Bill 1 (human services matters), HB 1177 (child care assistance) and SB 285 (housing matters), and no on SB 91 (syringe exchange program), according to BillTrack50's vote record [5]. The Reporter said the Indiana Association of Chiefs of Police named her Legislator of the Year for law enforcement legislation [4].

## This race

- House District 29 lies entirely within Hamilton County and covers Noblesville and a small portion of Fishers [10, 4, 3].
- Shonkwiler is seeking a second term; she filed for re-election in February 2026 [4].
- Devon Wellington won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [11].
- The Reporter published side-by-side profiles of both candidates in July 2026; no debate or candidate forum for this race was found in available coverage as of October 10, 2026 [10].
- Both candidates cite affordability: Shonkwiler points to utility regulation, township reform and housing work, while Wellington emphasizes public school funding, utility and data-center costs, and targeted property tax relief [10, 12].

## Alaina Shonkwiler

Shonkwiler is the first-term incumbent and a Noblesville native who worked in economic development and congressional district staff roles before joining the House [3, 10].

Campaign website: https://alainafornoblesville.com

### Background

- Works as a business development associate with Garmong Construction and previously managed government consulting at Katz, Sapper & Miller and served as district director for U.S. Rep. Victoria Spartz, according to the House Republican caucus [3].
- The Reporter said she worked for former District 29 Rep. Chuck Goodrich and as a workforce development coordinator for Noblesville Schools [10].
- Graduated from Noblesville High School and holds a public affairs management degree from Indiana University Bloomington; lives in Noblesville [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for re-election in February 2026 and is the Republican nominee on the general-election ballot [4, 2].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Told The Reporter she is focused on reforming regulation of investor-owned electric utilities given rising energy demand [10].
- Said of HEA 1002, "we want to ensure reliable energy at the lowest possible cost" [9].

**Public safety**

- Cited House Bill 1232 as giving law enforcement tools and resources while supporting transparency and accountability [10].

**Housing**

- Said she has pushed for housing affordability through the Government and Regulatory Reform Committee [10].

**Local government**

- Described her work on township modernization in Senate Bill 270 as the first meaningful township reform in over 20 years, and said she wants to stop taxpayers from paying twice for the same service [10].

**Education**

- Listed among her priorities an education system that gives parents more say and serves every child [4].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. Asked about the Make My Move relocation program Wellington used, she said she did not want to disparage people who came through it but does not necessarily agree with using tax dollars for it [10].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said she weighs whether legislation will make life more affordable, make government work better and keep communities safe [10].
- Said, "I'm excited to seek re-election and to continue earning the trust and support of the people I'm privileged to serve" [4].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign finance figures were found in reviewed sources.

### What we couldn't verify

- Search snippets said she had no 2026 primary opponent; not verified.
- As a 2025 freshman, her individual votes on the 2025 budget (HEA 1001) and property tax law (SEA 1) were not verified from roll calls.
- Her campaign website lists few specific policy positions.
- No fundraising reports were reviewed.

## Devon Wellington

Wellington is an education policy consultant and small business owner from Noblesville running for office for the first time [13, 11].

Campaign website: https://votedevon.com

### Background

- Has worked in education policy at the district, state and federal levels, according to The Reporter, and describes herself as an education researcher and small business owner with a master's degree in applied developmental psychology [10, 11].
- Moved to Noblesville from northern Virginia through the Make My Move program and has served on several Noblesville school district committees [10].
- Is involved with the civic group MadVoters, according to LarryInFishers.com [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced her candidacy in January 2026 and won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [13, 11].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Told The Reporter the state is "tragically underfunding our schools" and that voters should decide before tax dollars go to private or charter schools [10].
- Her platform calls for funding alternatives to public schools from a separate appropriation, reversing partisan school board elections and an independent audit of the special education funding formula [12].

**Energy & utilities**

- Proposes reforming utility rate-tracker laws, restoring the state energy efficiency program and capping utility costs relative to income [12].

**Data centers**

- Says the state should "stop billing families for data-center infrastructure" [12].

**Property taxes**

- Says she would "target property-tax relief to seniors and working families who need it" [12].

**Housing**

- Would limit how many single-family homes out-of-state investors can convert to rentals and help first-time buyers [12].

**Health care**

- Supports stronger hospital price caps, price transparency with penalties, and holding insurers "accountable for improper denials" [12].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports raising the minimum wage, a state earned income tax credit, child care tax credits and tying corporate incentives to verified job creation, and says her plan needs no new taxes [12].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements by Wellington about Shonkwiler were found in reviewed sources; she has said District 29 residents deserve "a representative who listens" [13, 10].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Told The Reporter she would rather be a "workhorse" than a "show horse" [10].
- After the primary she said the community is "ready for a representative who prioritizes results over rhetoric" [11].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements were named on her campaign home page, and no campaign finance figures were found in reviewed sources [14].

### What we couldn't verify

- Primary vote totals were not verified; a search snippet attributed to Current Publishing reported Wellington 1,921 votes to Coumba Kebe's 1,655, but the page returned an error. Kebe's subsequent status (reported as the Democratic Treasurer nominee) was not verified here.
- Sources differ on when she moved to Noblesville: LarryInFishers.com says she has lived there since 2023, while The Reporter's July 2026 article says 2022.
- Current Publishing's March 2026 Q&A with the Democratic primary candidates could not be loaded.
- No fundraising reports were reviewed.
