# Indiana County Sheriff — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-11 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

The sheriff is an elected county officer created by the Indiana Constitution who arrests offenders, keeps the peace, serves court papers, keeps order in county courts and runs the county jail [1, 2]. The Indiana Sheriffs' Association describes sheriffs as constitutional officers elected by the people, whose work includes operating jails, serving civil process, providing court security and coordinating emergency response [3]. Every county elects its sheriff in 2026 for a four-year term, and no one may serve as sheriff more than eight years in any twelve-year period [1].

- **Term:** 4 years; the sheriff serves until a successor is elected and qualified [4, 1]
- **Elected:** Countywide by the county's voters on the partisan general election ballot; all county sheriffs are elected in 2026 [1]
- **Term limit:** No more than eight years in any period of twelve years (Indiana Constitution, Article 6, Section 2) [1]
- **Qualifications:** Must have lived in the county for at least one year before the election, be registered to vote in the county by the filing deadline, and file a statement of economic interests; a sheriff who moves out of the county forfeits the office [1, 4]
- **Pay:** A county may pay the sheriff a salary from the county general fund of at least 50% to 100% of the state minimum salary of a full-time prosecuting attorney, depending on county population [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 92 races; 26 contested [6]

### What the office does

- Arrests without a warrant people who commit an offense in the sheriff's view, brings them before a county court, and holds them while the arrest is investigated [2].
- Suppresses breaches of the peace and pursues and jails felons [2].
- Takes care of the county jail and the prisoners held there [2, 3].
- Serves and carries out legal papers (process) directed to the sheriff by courts and the county commissioners [2].
- Attends and keeps order in all courts of the county, which the Sheriffs' Association describes as providing court security [2, 3].
- Takes photographs, fingerprints and other identification data from people held for crimes, and DNA samples for felonies [2].
- Reports twice a year to the Indiana Department of Correction the average daily cost of holding a jail prisoner [2].
- Holds state prisoners, including people convicted of Level 6 felonies, in the county jail, for which the state reimburses counties at a daily rate set in the state budget [7].
- Coordinates emergency response and works with local police, prosecutors and community partners [3].
- In a county whose council creates a sheriff's merit board by ordinance, appoints three of its five members, while the county police officers elect the other two; no more than two sheriff appointees and one elected member may belong to the same party [8].
- Must report information to federal authorities when a person arrested for a felony or misdemeanor is believed to lack legal immigration status, and, since July 2026, notify the judge setting bail when holding a person subject to an immigration detainer [9, 10].
- May supervise and inspect businesses such as pawnbrokers and secondhand dealers [2].
- What the sheriff does not control: the county commissioners must establish and maintain the county jail building [11]; the county makes the appropriation for feeding prisoners and sets the sheriff's salary [5]; the prosecuting attorney decides which crimes to prosecute [12]; and judges decide bail [13].

### Recent changes to the office

- February 2025: The Indiana Department of Correction said funding to reimburse county jails for holding Level 6 felons and state prisoners, then $40 per day, was exhausted for state fiscal year 2025; Tippecanoe County Sheriff Robert Goldsmith, then president of the Indiana Sheriffs' Association, called it very disappointing, and Rep. Greg Steuerwald (R-Avon) said everything owed would be paid [14].
- 2025: The new state budget raised the daily rate for Level 6 offenders to $42, raised the jail reimbursement line to $45 million and then $41 million a year, and moved to direct reimbursement with costs submitted within 60 days; Sheriffs' Association executive director Stephen Luce called the rate better than nothing, citing an association study that put the average cost at about $74 per inmate per day [7].
- July 2025: The Department of Correction planned to begin paying about $12.7 million owed to county jails for housing state offenders [7].
- July 2025: House Enrolled Act 1393 took effect, requiring jails to alert federal immigration authorities, with the county sheriff passing the information to federal officials, after felony or misdemeanor arrests when there is probable cause the person lacks legal status [9].
- July 2026: Senate Bill 76 took effect, requiring sheriff's offices and police departments to notify the bail judge when they hold a person subject to an immigration detainer and barring local policies that restrict cooperation with federal officials; St. Joseph County Sheriff Bill Redman said his county already cooperates with ICE, while South Bend Mayor James Mueller said the city will protect the constitutional rights of everyone living there [10].
- January 2026: The Sheriffs' Association's 2026 president, Porter County Sheriff Jeffrey Balon, listed jail overcrowding, staffing shortages, the drug epidemic and mental illness among the issues sheriffs raise with legislators [3].
- November 2026: Public Question 1 asks voters whether courts may deny bail, for offenses other than murder or treason, when the state proves by clear and convincing evidence that no release conditions will reasonably protect others or the community [15]; Sen. Eric Koch (R-Bedford) said releasing some defendants before trial is too dangerous, while Zach Stock of the Indiana Public Defender Council raised concerns about jail overcrowding [13].

## Current officeholder: Scottie Maples (Republican)

Scottie Maples was first elected Clark County sheriff in 2022 and sought a second term in 2026 [16]. He lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to Kenny Kavanaugh and will serve through the end of 2026, so the seat is open in November [17, 18]. He has worked in the sheriff's office for more than two decades, starting as a corrections officer [17].

- Career: Began as a corrections officer. He became a sheriff's office police officer in 2007, then worked as a detective, rose to colonel and served more than two years as chief deputy under a prior sheriff [19].
- November 2022: Elected sheriff, defeating Democrat Ed Byers [20, 19].
- Since 2023: Has given the County Council detailed statements and receipts for commissary spending, according to Maples [19].
- 2025: The sheriff's office entered an agreement with the federal government to house people in ICE custody. Maples said the county is paid $90 per person per day plus mileage and staff escort costs, and estimated $2–3 million a year. The money goes to county government rather than the sheriff's office, and he said it helps offset a 2025 property-tax overhaul. Some residents oppose the arrangement, and small, regular protests have been held outside the courthouse [19].
- Operations, by his own account: added jail programming, increased commissary reserves, began upgrading jail cameras for the first time in nearly 20 years, reduced unmarked cars, added patrols, and bought body-worn cameras with commissary funds. He says crime is down [19].
- Helped raise the service requirement for sheriff's pension eligibility from eight to 10 years [19].
- February 2026: Said in a social-media post that he had spent three years "restoring professionalism, rebuilding trust, and bringing our office into the modern era" [16].
- May 5, 2026: Lost the Republican primary to Kavanaugh, 35.4% to 64.6%. He congratulated Kavanaugh and pledged to support him as the nominee [17].

## This race

- Clark County (Jeffersonville, Clarksville, Sellersburg) is in the Louisville metro area. The sheriff runs the county jail, provides courthouse security and patrols the county [19].
- Open seat: Incumbent Republican Scottie Maples lost his primary, so a new sheriff will take office in 2027 [17, 21].
- Primary results (May 5, 2026): Kenny Kavanaugh defeated Maples, 64.6% to 35.4%. Tim Deeringer defeated retired federal agent Ed Byers for the Democratic nomination, 61.2% to 38.8% [17]. Election-night counts reported by the News and Tribune were Kavanaugh 4,934 and Maples 2,611, and Deeringer 3,755 and Byers 2,380 [18].
- Dwight T. Ingle filed in February 2026 for the Democratic primary [16, 20]. He is on the November ballot as an Independent [6, 21].
- Kavanaugh and Deeringer are both former Jeffersonville police chiefs who worked together at that department, and both have talked about improving community trust in the sheriff's office [18]. Ingle is a former Clarksville police chief [22].
- The 2025 agreement to hold ICE detainees in the county jail was a major issue at a pre-primary candidate forum. Kavanaugh said he supports keeping it. Deeringer said continuing the contract is not a simple yes-or-no question [19].
- No general-election forum or debate including all three candidates was found.

## Kenny Kavanaugh, Jr.

Republican nominee. He spent more than three decades at the Jeffersonville Police Department, including about a decade as chief, and is now a captain there [19, 17]. He defeated incumbent Sheriff Scottie Maples in the primary [17].

### Background

- Captain at the Jeffersonville Police Department, where he works as a second-shift supervisor [19, 18].
- Has 32 years of police service, according to a campaign press release, including 10 years as JPD chief that ended in August 2025 [20].
- Spent 13 years on the SWAT team, three as commander, and served on the Southern Indiana Drug Task Force [19].
- Board member of LifeSpring Health Systems [19].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Republican on the February 2026 deadline [16].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over incumbent Scottie Maples, 64.6% to 35.4% [17].
- Listed as the Republican candidate on the general-election ballot [6].

### Positions & plans

**Immigration enforcement**

- Supports keeping the county's agreement to house ICE detainees [19].

**Jail & corrections**

- Said he would maintain the jail to the standards the law requires [19].

**Staffing & pay**

- Wants an internal organizational plan and employee surveys to learn what staff and leadership need [19].

**Transparency**

- Said restoring trust requires identifying root causes rather than focusing on a single incident, and that "It's all about a culture" [19].
- Would add "To protect and serve" to official vehicles [19].

### On the current officeholder

- When he filed, said new leadership would bring a fresh perspective to the sheriff's office [16].
- After the primary, thanked Maples for his service and said he looked forward to working with Maples' team. He said the result showed people were ready to move forward [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Develop an internal organizational plan and survey employees [19].
- Keep the ICE housing agreement [19].

- WDRB coverage could not be retrieved because its pages redirect to the station's homepage. Search snippets say that at a pre-primary forum Kavanaugh pledged more transparent audits and said the sheriff's merit commission would meet monthly. These statements are unverified.

- A third-party site (The Ballot Brief) reported no campaign website as of September 2026.
- No general-election forum, budget proposals or school-safety positions were found.

## Tim Deeringer

Democratic nominee. He spent nearly 30 years at the Jeffersonville Police Department, including as chief from 2008 to 2012, and retired as a lieutenant in 2019 [19]. His stated priorities are mental-health response and regular public communication about the office's work [19].

### Background

- Spent nearly 30 years at the Jeffersonville Police Department: chief from 2008 to 2012, and retired as a lieutenant in 2019 [19].
- His 2026 campaign is his first run for public office [18].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary over retired federal agent Ed Byers, 61.2% to 38.8% [17].
- Listed as the Democratic candidate on the general-election ballot [6].

### Positions & plans

**Immigration enforcement**

- Supports deporting people who are in the country unlawfully and face violent charges. He said continuing the ICE housing contract is not a simple yes-or-no question, noted its financial impact, and said his first duty is protecting Clark County residents [19].

**Patrol & response**

- Would focus on how the office responds to people with mental-health issues [19].

**Staffing & pay**

- Would put trustworthy, qualified people in support positions and hold them accountable [19].

**Transparency**

- Wants the office to be as transparent as possible, including regular communication with the public about its work [19].
- Said the office should be run like a business [19].

### On the current officeholder

- Said rebuilding trust in the sheriff's office will take change and patience [19].
- After the primary, said that across the county he heard the same message: people want change [18].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Communicate regularly with the public about the office's work [19].
- Strengthen how deputies respond to people with mental-health issues [19].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign-finance figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Search snippets of WDRB coverage, which could not be retrieved, quote him as saying his "big promise is the safeguard of taxpayers," that the first thing he would do is look at the budget, and that he would focus on financial transparency. These are unverified.
- No campaign website was found; The Ballot Brief listed his priorities as trust, transparency, accountability and modern technology, but it is a third-party aggregator and is not cited.
- No general-election forum, budget detail or school-safety positions were found.

## Dwight T Ingle

Independent candidate and former Clarksville police chief who served in that post for 20 years [22]. He was the Democratic nominee for sheriff in 2018 [22]. Little current reporting on his 2026 campaign was found.

### Background

- Former Clarksville police chief for 20 years, according to 2018 News and Tribune coverage [22].
- Democratic nominee for Clark County sheriff in 2018. He received 14,205 votes (33%) and lost to Republican incumbent Jamey Noel, who received 62.9% [22].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed in February 2026 as a candidate in the Democratic primary [16, 20].
- Listed as an Independent on the general-election ballot [6, 21].
