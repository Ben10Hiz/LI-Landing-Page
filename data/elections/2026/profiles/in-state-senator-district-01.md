# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Dan Dernulc (Republican)

Dan Dernulc, a Republican from Highland and a former Lake County councilman, has held the District 1 seat since 2022; he lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to Trevor De Vries and is not on the November ballot [3, 4, 5].

- 2025–2026: the Senate Republican caucus lists his standing committees as Environmental Affairs, Local Government, Pensions and Labor, and Public Policy [3].
- December 11, 2025: was one of 21 Senate Republicans who joined the chamber's 10 Democrats in voting against the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which failed 19–31 [4, 6]. On November 19, 2025, before the vote, he issued a statement saying his family had been the target of a false "swatting" report at their home and that "Threats to public officials cannot and should not be the norm" [7].
- January 23, 2026: co-authored Senate Bill 146 with Sen. Rick Niemeyer to make affordability the top consideration in electric utility service changes; he said it was written "to address the NIPSCO energy increase," and the caucus reported the bill did not receive a committee hearing [8].
- April 29, 2026: Gov. Mike Braun ceremonially signed Senate Enrolled Act 119, authored by Dernulc, which creates a sexual grooming offense and expands the places convicted child predators are barred from [9].

## This race

- Senate District 1 covers part of Lake County; the Senate Republican caucus's news items for the district mention Highland, Schererville, St. John, Hammond and Dyer [3].
- This is an open seat: incumbent Dan Dernulc lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to Trevor De Vries, 75.1% to 23.3%, with Nader Liddawi at 1.6% [5]. Ahead of the primary, both President Donald Trump and Gov. Mike Braun endorsed De Vries over Dernulc, part of their endorsements against Senate Republicans who had opposed the December 2025 redistricting bill [10].
- Scott Houldieson was unopposed in the Democratic primary [11].
- No general-election debate or candidate forum for this race was found as of October 10, 2026.
- Both candidates list utility costs as a priority: De Vries's campaign site opposes NIPSCO's monopoly and supports energy choice, while Houldieson's site calls for oversight of regulatory agencies [12, 13]. They differ on labor policy; Houldieson supports repealing Indiana's right-to-work law and restoring prevailing wage, while De Vries emphasizes cutting income and property taxes and government spending [13, 12].

## Scott Houldieson

Scott Houldieson, a Democrat from Highland, is a Ford Chicago Assembly Plant electrician and an officer of UAW Local 551 who has not held elected public office [14].

Campaign website: https://www.scott4senate.com/

### Background

- Joined UAW Local 551 in 1989 at the Ford Chicago Assembly Plant, starting on the assembly line and beginning an electrician apprenticeship in 1994, according to his campaign site [14].
- His campaign site says he has served Local 551 as financial secretary, vice president, and chairperson of the trustees (elected in 2025), and edited the local's newspaper for over a decade [14].
- Co-founded and chaired Unite All Workers for Democracy in 2019, chairs the steering committee of UAW Member Action, and helped found the Calumet Region chapter of Progressive Democrats of America in 2017, per his campaign site [14].
- Is a precinct committeeperson in Highland, where he has lived for 25 years [14].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary unopposed [11].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- His campaign site calls for raising the minimum wage to "a family sustaining wage," repealing Indiana's right-to-work law, and bringing back "prevailing wage standards for projects funded by the state" [13].
- Supports restoring childcare relief vouchers [13].

**Education**

- Calls for "fair taxation to fund our public schools," keeping local control through elected school boards, and letting teachers bargain over working conditions [13].

**Economic development**

- Says "We need oversight of our regulatory agencies" and would give local communities the ability to decide land use "after meaningful public input" [13].
- Would require "community benefit assurances with claw backs for non compliance" and project labor agreements on subsidized projects [13].

### On the current officeholder

- No public statements by Houldieson about Dernulc or the seat's record were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he will "fight for Hoosiers in Northwest Indiana" at the Statehouse [15].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Wikipedia's 2026 Indiana Senate election page lists endorsements from Pete Buttigieg, Sen. Bernie Sanders and the Indiana AFL–CIO [11].

## Trevor G. De Vries

Trevor De Vries, a Republican from St. John, is a transactional-risk insurance broker and Republican precinct committeeman who defeated Sen. Dan Dernulc in the May 2026 primary [16, 5].

Campaign website: https://www.trevordv.com/

### Background

- Lists his occupation as principal at HUB International, working in transactional risk insurance [16].
- Lifelong St. John resident; Purdue University graduate (B.S., sales and marketing) [16, 12].
- Has served as Republican precinct committeeman for St. John 04 since 2024 and has not previously held public office [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 75.1% of the vote over incumbent Dan Dernulc (23.3%) and Nader Liddawi (1.6%) [5].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- His campaign site pledges to lower income and property taxes [12]. In his iVoterGuide questionnaire, he listed "family-focused tax reform," including property tax relief for young families, as a top legislative priority [16].

**Energy & utilities**

- His campaign site opposes NIPSCO's monopoly, supports energy choice, and pledges to "FIGHT these utility hikes" [12].

**Health care**

- Proposes changes to pharmacy benefit manager practices, passing rebates through to patients, protecting independent pharmacies, and letting pharmacists tell patients when a cheaper option exists [12].

**Education**

- Lists parental rights as his top legislative priority and says he supports policies giving parents a voice [16, 12].

**Government spending**

- Pledges to cut government waste and increase transparency [12].

**Other issues (questionnaire)**

- In his iVoterGuide questionnaire, he answered that abortion should not be allowed, opposed red flag laws and universal background checks, supported photo ID for voting, and opposed legalizing recreational marijuana [16].

### On the current officeholder

- His campaign site does not mention Dernulc or redistricting; it says he will work with the Senate majority to address NIPSCO, taxes and health care affordability [12].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Listed his legislative priorities, in order, as parental rights and family strengthening, family-focused tax reform, and government efficiency and spending cuts [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed in the primary by President Donald Trump and Gov. Mike Braun [10].
- His campaign site also lists Lt. Gov. Micah Beckwith, Attorney General Todd Rokita, U.S. Sens. Todd Young and Jim Banks, and U.S. Rep. Erin Houchin among endorsers [17].
- iVoterGuide lists endorsements from the NRA-PVF, Indiana Family Action PAC, Indiana Right to Life, Turning Point Action and Americans for Prosperity Action [16].

### What we couldn't verify

- No fundraising totals were found; the Indiana campaign finance portal could not be read.
- The Indiana Capital Chronicle voter guide (search snippet only) reportedly says his site lists goals without details on how they would be accomplished — unverified.
- Wikipedia lists post-primary endorsements from Indiana Farm Bureau's AgELECT PAC; not confirmed from the PAC's own list.
- No direct statement by De Vries about Dernulc or the redistricting vote was found.
