# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The 'Grant Eastbrook Community School Board' races on the state list are for Eastbrook Community Schools Corporation in eastern Grant County, whose offices are at 560 S. 900 E., Marion [6, 24]. Eastbrook High School serves students from Upland, Washington Township, Matthews and Van Buren, and the district's elementary schools are Eastbrook South in Upland and Eastbrook North in Van Buren [25]. Federal data reported through PlainSchools show 1,579 students in 2024-25, down from 1,668 in 2022-23 and 1,621 in 2023-24, in five schools [26].
- Superintendent: on April 27, 2026, the board voted 5-0 to hire Patrick McLaughlin, a former Eastbrook principal, on a three-year contract running July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2029 at a $135,000 base salary, with McLaughlin serving as interim superintendent from May 4 [27]. He replaced Brett Garrett, who led the district for more than 13 years and retired on Feb. 1, 2026; Stephan Darnell served as interim superintendent during the search [28, 27].
- The board has five members. The district lists Roger Richards (president), Eric Hinderliter (vice president), Patrick Pinkerton (secretary), Chris Duckwall and Daniel Spencer, whom it lists as 'Interim Board Member (12-31-26)' [24]. The district's board page does not say which district each member represents [24]. In the 2024 election, Duckwall won the District 5 at-large seat over incumbent Daniel E. Spencer, and Pinkerton was reelected; both took office in January 2025 [29, 30].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: one seat each in Districts 1, 2 and 3. The state list says 'vote for 1' in each [6], and the Chronicle-Tribune's Oct. 3, 2026 ballot list agrees [31]. District 1 and District 2 are contested; Eric C Hinderliter is the only candidate in District 3 [6, 31].
- Who holds the seats: District 2 is held by Daniel Spencer, whom the board appointed by roll-call vote on July 13, 2026 to fill a District 2 seat 'for the remainder of the term ending December 31, 2026' [32]. Spencer is not on the ballot, so District 2 is an open seat [6]. The minutes do not say who left the seat; Danny Combs was a board member in April 2026 [27] and is not on the board's July 2026 roll [32]. The district does not publish which of the other members hold District 1 and District 3. Pinkerton and Duckwall were elected in 2024, which leaves Richards and Hinderliter as the members whose terms likely end in 2026 [30, 24]. Hinderliter is the District 3 candidate, and Roger Richards is not on the ballot [6]. This guide therefore believes District 1 is an open seat, but it could not confirm that from a district record. Richards said in April 2026 that he had served 23 years on the board [27].
- 2027 budget (September-October 2026): the district advertised a 2027 budget of $20,696,761 across the Rainy Day, Debt Service, Education and Operations funds, with a public hearing on Sept. 28 and adoption set for Oct. 12, 2026 [33]. The notice estimates a $291,900 property-tax-cap credit [33]. On July 13, 2026, the board voted 4-0 to transfer amounts from the Debt Service Fund to the Operations Fund [32]. The Oct. 12 agenda includes adopting the budget and a transfer from the Education Fund to the Operations Fund [34]. In January 2025, then-superintendent Garrett said funds were down 'between $300,000 and $400,000' compared with the start of 2023 because of falling enrollment, but added that 'we are still in a very good spot' [30].
- Facilities and bonds (2024-2026): in December 2024, a resident told the board he opposed spending '$10 million to $18 million' on a proposed sports complex and urged spending on vocational programs instead [35]. In March 2025, Garrett said that ideas for main-campus improvements were 'not imminent' [36]. On Sept. 14, 2026, the board voted 5-0 to approve a $1,000,000 additional appropriation of bond proceeds for renovations, site improvements, technology, equipment and buses, and it adopted a final bond resolution [37, 38]. On Sept. 28, 2026, it voted 4-1 to hire Viridian Architectural Design for a districtwide facility assessment. Spencer voted no, citing the $22,000 cost [39, 40]. The 2027 capital projects plan lists boiler replacement ($400,000), security cameras ($250,000) and other building work [41].
- School safety (September 2026): the board accepted a $51,600 Secured School Safety Grant toward school resource officers. McLaughlin said the amount was not enough to expand from one and a half officer positions to two [42, 38].
- Referendum: Eastbrook has no referendum on the November 2026 ballot. The Chronicle-Tribune's ballot list includes referendum questions only for Mississinewa and Oak Hill [31].
- Forums: this guide found no 2026 candidate forum or questionnaire for the Eastbrook races. The Chronicle-Tribune printed the candidate list on Oct. 3, 2026 [31].

## Eric C Hinderliter — District 3

Board vice president Eric Hinderliter is the only candidate in District 3. This guide believes he holds the seat, but the district does not publish members' districts [24, 6].

### Background

- The district lists Eric Hinderliter as board vice president [24]. He was vice president in February 2026 [28]. In January 2025 he was nominated to be board president, and the nominations for 2025 officers were approved 3-1, with each nominee abstaining [30].
- During the 2026 superintendent search, the Chronicle-Tribune reported that he was the board's main spokesperson on the search [28].

### Path to the ballot

- The state lists him as a Republican [6]. The Indiana Citizen's copy of the state list gives May 29, 2026 as his filing date [43]. He has no opponent [6, 31].

### Positions & plans

**Facilities**

- On Sept. 28, 2026, he moved to hire Viridian Architectural Design to do a facility assessment of all district buildings. The motion passed 4-1 [39, 40].
- On Aug. 24, 2026, he moved to accept a bid to replace the stadium's perimeter fencing, which passed 4-1. On Sept. 14, 2026, he moved to adopt the additional appropriation resolution for bond proceeds, which passed 5-0 [44, 38].

**Transparency & community input**

- In February 2026 he said the board's online survey on the superintendent search had received more than 300 responses [28]. He said the district had received 16 applications and called it 'a strong pool of candidates' [28].

**Teachers & staff**

- He moved the first and second readings of the updated classified (non-teaching) staff handbook and the first reading of the certified (teaching) staff handbook in September 2026. Each passed 4-1 [38, 40].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. When McLaughlin was hired in April 2026, Hinderliter said, 'I think we're really blessed to have a leader like you, and I'm excited for the next step in Eastbrook's future.' [27]

- Occupation and when he first joined the board could not be found.
