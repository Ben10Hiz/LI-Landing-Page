# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The 'Brown County School Board' seats belong to the Brown County School Corporation (Brown County Schools), based in Nashville, Indiana, whose voters are all registered voters of Brown County [24, 25]. The district lists an early education center, three elementary schools (Helmsburg, Sprunica and Van Buren), Brown County Middle School, Brown County High School and a Career Resource Center [26]. Federal data list 1,549 students in five schools in 2024-25 [27]. Emily Callahan signs as superintendent in the district's 2025-26 and 2026 publications and board minutes [28, 29, 30].
- The Board of School Trustees has five members serving four-year terms: one must live in each of three residence districts (whose boundaries match the three county commissioner districts), and two are at-large members who may live anywhere, no more than two from any one district; every board candidate is voted on by all registered voters in the county [24, 25].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: the three residence-district seats, each electing one member, matching the state list's 'vote for 1' [31, 25, 32, 33, 6]. The two at-large seats, held by Jenise Bohbrink (board president) and Amy Oliver (vice president), run through 2028 [31].
- Who holds the seats now: Carol Bowden holds District 1, Vicki Harden (board secretary) holds District 2 and Doug Payne holds District 3, all with terms listed as 2022-2026 [31]. Bowden and Payne are running again; Harden is not on the ballot, so District 2 will have a new member, and Michael Fulton is the only candidate there [6, 32]. Payne was board president and Bowden vice president in 2025; at the January 5, 2026 reorganization the board elected Bohbrink president, Oliver vice president and Harden secretary [34, 35].
- Party labels: the League of Women Voters' VOTE411 guide lists Andrew Reed and Stephen Douglas Payne as Republicans, Michael Fulton as Independent, and Carolyn V. Bowden, Julia Smith and Jane Mitchell as nonpartisan, matching the state list [25, 32, 33, 6].
- Budget and property taxes: on September 22, 2025 the board heard a 'Policy Analytics Budget and SEA 1 Update' presentation and voted 5-0 to hire Policy Analytics LLC for school finance support services; the minutes do not summarize what the presentation said about the 2025 property-tax law [30]. The board adopted its 2026 budget ordinance 5-0 on October 20, 2025 [36].
- Referendum: in May 2024 voters approved a Brown County Schools referendum with 55 percent of the vote; Indiana Public Media reported the money would go to teacher salaries and to recruiting staff for apprenticeship and internship programs, at about $157 a year for a property assessed at $300,000 [37].
- Facilities: on October 20, 2025 the board approved, 4-1, an athletic facility project designed and built by Tarkett Sports; the minutes do not say who voted no [36]. The district says the project moves its athletic field out of the floodway and raises it more than four feet to end repeated flooding, with a new track carrying a 50-year warranty [38]. The superintendent has also highlighted high school restroom renovations [28].
- Safety and staffing: in 2025-26 the district created a Director of Safety and Operations position and added a school nurse and a career coach, according to Superintendent Callahan [28]. The board heard presentations on school threat assessment guidelines (December 18, 2025) and on 2026-27 school resource officer funding (September 8, 2026 agenda) [39, 40].
- Programs and planning: on February 16, 2026 the board voted 5-0 to start a high school agriculture program with FFA for 2026-27 [41]. The district ran a parent survey in spring 2026 to inform 2026-2029 strategic priorities, discussed its 2026-2029 Priority Plan at the August 3, 2026 meeting, and says it will launch a public dashboard to track the plan's goals [38, 42, 29]. The board discussed a cell phone policy on June 1, 2026 following new state law [43, 29].
- Most votes recorded in the 2025-26 minutes reviewed for this guide were unanimous; the October 2025 athletic facility vote was the only split vote found [36].
- Forums and questionnaires: the League of Women Voters' VOTE411 guide posted questionnaire answers from Carolyn V. Bowden, Julia Smith, Michael Fulton and Jane Mitchell; Andrew Reed and Stephen Douglas Payne had not responded when it was checked [25, 32, 33]. The Brown County Democrat has covered the race, but its site could not be accessed for this guide. Key differences in District 1: Smith points to 26 years working in Brown County Schools' business office and classrooms, while Bowden, the incumbent, is a physical therapist; in District 3, Mitchell's answers stress one-on-one intervention with students, and Payne posted no answers [25, 33].

## Carol Bowden — District 1

Incumbent; a physical therapist who has held the District 1 seat in the 2022-2026 term and was board vice president in 2025 [31, 34, 25].

### Background

- Physical therapist; education at Boston University's Sargent College of Allied Health Professions, per her VOTE411 listing (as Carolyn V. Bowden) [25].
- Board member for District 1, term listed as 2022-2026; vice president in 2025 [31, 34].
- She wrote that she has had a disability since birth and went through school before the Americans with Disabilities Act and Title IX, which motivates her to seek equal opportunity for all students [25].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for reelection to District 1 with no party label (listed as Nonpartisan) [6, 25].

### Positions & plans

**Academics**

- On chronic absenteeism, she wrote that the district needs to find out why it is happening for each student, check whether the student is in the right pathway, and support learning recovery both by computer and in person [25].
- She supports history education on the U.S. and the world so students understand how things happened and what could be done differently [25].
- On balancing college-prep, technology and trades, she wrote that the pathways share basic science and math requirements, and cited the C4 program in Columbus, the on-campus Career Resource Center, Eagle Manufacturing and pre-K childcare/education in the old Nashville elementary building [25].

**School safety**

- She wrote that bullying should always be stopped, with education and emotional support for the student being bullied and discipline plus an effort to learn why the other student is bullying; a student may need to be removed from the classroom for safety until behavior is corrected [25].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. She describes the board member's role as asking questions to understand the district's finances and academics, learning what the law allows board members to do, and passing community, staff and student questions to the right department and following up [25].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- How she first joined the board (election or appointment) was not confirmed.
- Her VOTE411 listing uses the name Carolyn V. Bowden; the state list and district site use Carol Bowden.
- The Brown County Democrat (bcdemocrat.com), which covers the race, could not be accessed for this guide; its candidate articles were not read.

## Julia A Smith — District 1

Candidate for District 1; a school business manager who worked 26 years in Brown County Schools, including as corporation treasurer, by her own account [25].

Campaign website: http://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=61593167607077

### Background

- School business manager (employer not stated); she says she has 30 years in public education, 26 of them in Brown County Schools in roles from paraprofessional and extracurricular (ECA) treasurer to payroll and corporation treasurer [25].
- MBA, Western Governors University; bachelor's degree in psychology, Indiana University [25].
- VOTE411 lists her campaign Facebook page and a Nineveh mailing address [25].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for District 1 with no party label (listed as Nonpartisan) [6, 25].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- She says she understands both the educational and financial sides of running a school corporation and would weigh the effect of decisions on students, staff and taxpayers [25].
- She does not think one pathway should be funded at the expense of another; funding should be based on student interest, participation, outcomes and program cost [25].

**Academics**

- On chronic absenteeism, she wrote the board should study attendance data and the reasons students miss school, and check student achievement and learning-gap data to see whether strategies are working [25].
- She would keep supporting programs such as the district's TEDx event, along with community service, student leadership and lessons on how government works [25].

**School safety**

- She wrote that bullying reports must be followed up, students need clear expectations and consequences, and schools should look at why a student is struggling; the board's role is to make sure good policies exist and are followed [25].

### On the current officeholder

- No statement on the current board's decisions was found; she described the board's role as governance and oversight: setting policy, financial oversight and evaluating the superintendent [25].

## Michael Fulton — District 2

Unopposed candidate for the open District 2 seat, listed as Independent; a retired corporate executive and 40-year county resident, by his own account [6, 32].

### Background

- Retired; he says he was a senior executive and officer of a Fortune 500 company who prepared multimillion-dollar budgets, ran IT and software projects and managed large teams [32].
- B.A. in liberal arts (1977) and MBA (1981), University of Kansas [32].
- Says he has lived in Brown County for 40 years [32].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for District 2 as an Independent; he is the only candidate, and incumbent Vicki Harden is not on the ballot [6, 32, 31].

### Positions & plans

**Academics**

- He would follow state law and district policy on absenteeism, and supports a formal learning recovery plan built with the student, family and school staff and reviewed periodically [32].
- He supports continued college-prep programs, expanding trades and health care programs (including adult education), entrepreneurial skills through EAGLE Manufacturing, the new agriculture programs, and stronger arts, music and theatre programs; funding could be roughly proportional to student interest and enrollment, subject to state law [32].

**School safety**

- On bullying, he would refer to and follow the district's existing policies and procedures [32].

### On the current officeholder

- No statement on the current board's decisions was found; he wrote that Brown County Schools currently offers 'numerous avenues' for students to reach their goals [32].

### How they say they'd run the office

- He would support staff-led projects connecting students with local organizations to study real-world issues [32].
