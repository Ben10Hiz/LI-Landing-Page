# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Linda Rogers (Republican)

Linda Rogers, a Granger Republican in the Senate since 2018, lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to Brian Schmutzler, so the seat will have a new senator after November. [3, 4] The Senate Republican caucus lists her as chair of the Pensions and Labor Committee and a member of the Education and Career Development, Elections, Family and Children Services, and Tax and Fiscal Policy committees. [3]

- 2025: Co-authored Senate Enrolled Act 1, the 2025 property and local income tax law; before the 2026 primary she said the roughly 350-page law needed adjustments but was not a bad bill, and pointed to House Bill 1210 as a follow-up she said helps homeowners and local governments. [5]
- 2025-12-11: Was one of 21 Republicans who joined all 10 Democrats in the 31–19 Senate vote that defeated House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill. [4] She said she followed constituents who overwhelmingly opposed it, and told ABC57 that thousands of constituents asked her to vote no, mainly because the proposed maps divided Elkhart County. [5, 6]
- 2026-01: Filed Senate Bill 248, which would have replaced the elected South Bend Community Schools board with a state-appointed board; after largely negative committee testimony she withdrew it, saying, "After hearing the testimony this week, I agree that this is not the right solution at this time." [7, 3]
- 2026-04-07: President Trump called her a "RINO" in a Truth Social post and endorsed Schmutzler; Rogers said she still supports the president, "Don't agree with everything he does," and called false a rumor that a person named Rudy had called her to urge a no vote on redistricting. [6]

## This race

- Senate District 11 covers parts of St. Joseph and Elkhart counties, including the Granger area. [5, 6]
- Open seat: incumbent Linda Rogers lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to Brian Schmutzler, 5,461 votes to 3,758 (about 59%) in unofficial results. [4] Gabrianna Gratzol was the only candidate in the Democratic primary. [1]
- The Republican primary centered on Rogers' December 2025 vote against congressional redistricting; Trump and Turning Point Action endorsed Schmutzler. [4, 6] An IU South Bend political scientist, Elizabeth Bennion, told ABC57 the endorsements were more about punishing those who opposed the push than about Rogers' overall voting record. [6]
- Key differences in stated positions: Schmutzler has called SEA 1 a bad bill and described eliminating property taxes as a long-term goal; Gratzol has warned that eliminating property taxes without replacing school, police and fire funding would cut local services. [5, 8] On schools, Schmutzler wants state funding to follow families to homeschooling, microschools and private schools, while Gratzol opposes the expansion of private school vouchers. [9, 4, 8]

## Brian S. Schmutzler

Brian Schmutzler is a physician and anesthesiology executive who won the 2026 Republican nomination by defeating incumbent Sen. Linda Rogers. [4, 10]

Campaign website: https://www.drbrianforindiana.com/

### Background

- Physician; his BallotReady profile lists him as executive vice president of medical affairs at CCI Anesthesia (2023–present), chief anesthesiologist and medical director of the operating room at Unity Physicians Hospital (2021–present), and owner of Anesthesia Consulting and Clinical Specialists (2018–present). [10]
- First run for office; he told WNDU that COVID-era shutdowns, elective-surgery restrictions, and mask and vaccine mandates first drew him into politics, and that his grandfather was a state senator in the 1960s and 1970s. [5]
- Announced his campaign in January 2026, citing school choice and property taxes as priorities [9].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over incumbent Linda Rogers, 5,461 votes to 3,758 in unofficial results. [4]
- Said the Senate's December 2025 redistricting vote was the final push for his candidacy. [5]

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Called the 2025 property tax law (SEA 1) a bad bill that did little to fix property taxes and raised local income taxes; said his long-term goal is eliminating property taxes while acknowledging side effects, and floated taxing homes on purchase or build value rather than current market value. [5]
- His campaign site says state and local government can do more to ease the tax burden; his BallotReady profile says he will fight "Endless government debt and unfair property taxes." [11, 10]

**Education**

- Wants to expand school choice beyond public and charter schools to homeschooling, microschools and private schools. [4]
- Supports broader eligibility for microschools, hybrid schooling and homeschooling, with state funding directed to families rather than institutions. [9]

**Health care**

- Describes himself as a supporter of the MAHA movement and says patients deserve care "without insurance companies standing between patients and doctors"; calls rural access a top priority. [10, 9]
- His campaign site says he would advocate for transparency in health care billing and more care options in underserved and rural communities. [11]

**Energy & utilities**

- His campaign site says he will focus on rising energy, grocery and housing costs and would "hold energy companies accountable." [11] He also named gas taxes and energy costs as priorities after the primary. [4]

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said he would have voted for redistricting, calling the vote "a huge missed opportunity for us as a republican party, and for us here in Indiana," and said most voters he met while door-knocking favored it. [6]

**Government spending**

- His campaign site pledges transparency on how government spends taxpayer money and opposes "more spending on special projects and corporate handouts." [11]

### On the current officeholder

- Said the Senate was not prepared to address what Hoosiers need, citing the redistricting vote and rising property and gas taxes, and disputed Rogers' defense of SEA 1. [5]
- At his campaign launch he said some lawmakers have lost touch with Hoosiers and pledged to restore accountability in the Senate. [9]

### How they say they'd run the office

- Campaign site lists three areas: affordable living, access to health care, and accountability. [11]
- After the primary he named property taxes, gas taxes, energy costs, health care and school choice as priorities. [4]

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed in the primary by President Trump and by Turning Point Action. [6, 4]

## Gabrianna Gratzol

Gabrianna Gratzol is a plastics-plant worker and first-time candidate who was unopposed for the Democratic nomination. [8, 1]

Campaign website: https://www.gabriannagratzolforsenatedistrict11.org/

### Background

- A profile by the Progressive Indiana Network, an advocacy group, reports she has worked in retail, residential behavioral health with teens, with children with autism, at a plastics manufacturing plant, and at the nonprofit health organization Beacon. [8]
- The St. Joseph County Democratic Party lists her among its 2026 candidates. [12]

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only candidate in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary, receiving 4,577 votes. [1]
- Announced as a challenger to Rogers by January 2026. [13]

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- The St. Joseph County Democratic Party says she supports a $15-per-hour living wage and strong labor unions. [12]
- Supports protecting unions and adjusting corporate tax policy so companies that underpay workers are held responsible, according to Progressive Indiana. [8]

**Property taxes**

- Has warned that eliminating property taxes without replacing school, police and fire funding would gut local services. [8]

**Education**

- Supports funding public schools and opposes the expansion of private school vouchers, particularly the removal of income caps. [8]
- Opposed Rogers' bill to replace the elected South Bend school board, saying action is needed to improve public schools but removing locally elected members is not the answer. [13]

**Health care**

- Supports expanding the Healthy Indiana Plan, attention to Medicaid provider pay, and a state-level public option. [8]

**Child care**

- Supports restoring child care vouchers and moving toward universal pre-K. [8]

**Data centers**

- Opposes relying on data center projects as an economic growth strategy. [8]

**Energy & utilities**

- Supports holding utility companies accountable for what she calls price gouging. [8]

### On the current officeholder

- Criticized Rogers' 2026 South Bend school board bill as "justification for government overreach" and linked it to a charter-school operator who she said donated to Rogers. [13]
- Progressive Indiana reports she credited Rogers for work on firefighter health benefits, for pulling the school board bill after public feedback, and for voting against redistricting maps she believed were wrong. [8]
- Says the Senate is made up of people "who employ other people" and argues it needs more working people and parents. [8, 14]

### How they say they'd run the office

- Campaign site lists public education, worker protections and affordability as priorities. [14]
