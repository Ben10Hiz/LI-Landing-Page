# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Ed DeLaney (Democratic)

Ed DeLaney, an Indianapolis Democrat and retired lawyer, has represented House District 86 since winning the seat in 2008 [3, 4]. He serves on the House Education and Ways and Means committees, is an alternate member of the State Budget Committee and is assistant Democratic caucus chair [4].

- Committees and roles: Education; Ways and Means; alternate member of the Budget Committee; Assistant Democratic Caucus Chair, according to the House Democratic caucus [4].
- April 10, 2025: Voted no on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (passed 65-29) [5]. April 25, 2025: Voted no on HEA 1001, the 2025-27 budget (adopted 66-27), citing "insufficient funding for K-12 public education, cuts to higher education as well as the expansion of the voucher program," in the caucus's description [6, 7].
- Aug.-Dec. 2025: Opposed mid-decade congressional redistricting, asking Republican colleagues in an August statement, "Would you allow the 40% of Hoosiers who vote Democratic to have any representation in Washington?" [8]. Voted no on HB 1032 on Dec. 5, 2025; it passed the House 57-41 [9].
- 2026 session: Offered amendments, all defeated, to create a House select committee to investigate the Indiana Economic Development Corporation (Jan. 29), require state contractors to disclose campaign contributions (Feb. 12), and exempt school resource officers and hospital and campus police from immigration-enforcement duties under SB 76 (Feb. 10) [10, 11, 12]. Voted yes on HB 1002, the utility-affordability bill, on Jan. 28, 2026 [13].
- 2026 interim: Called on the Department of Correction to speed the end of its contract with medical provider Centurion Health after a July 2026 IndyStar investigation (July 21), and urged Gov. Mike Braun to pause executions after a failed execution in Tennessee (Oct. 8) [14, 15].

## This race

- House District 86 is in northwest Indianapolis (Marion County); WFYI described it in 2022 as including part of Butler Tarkington and Traders Point [16, 4]. Glenn Bill's campaign describes it as northwest Indianapolis and Pike Township, with about 67,900 residents [17].
- DeLaney was reelected without opposition in 2024 and won 73% to 27% over Republican Mark Small in 2022 [3, 16].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Wikipedia's 2026 election page lists DeLaney as the only declared Democrat and Glenn Bill as the only declared Republican [3].
- No debate or forum between the candidates was found.
- Key differences: Bill supports working toward "the total elimination of property taxes through a responsible, phased approach" [18]; DeLaney voted against the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) and the 2025 budget [5, 7]. On spending, DeLaney has urged using the state surplus for preschool, health insurance and public schools [19], while Bill lists opposing "wasteful government spending" and reducing the tax burden [18].

## Edward O. DeLaney

Democratic incumbent since 2008 and member of the Education and Ways and Means committees [4, 3]. In 2025-26 he focused on budget and education funding, oversight of state agencies and contractors, and opposition to mid-decade redistricting [7, 10, 8].

Campaign website: https://indianahousedemocrats.org/members/ed-delaney

### Background

- Retired lawyer; practiced 30 years at the Indianapolis firm now named Barnes & Thornburg and later at DeLaney & DeLaney; Harvard Law School graduate (1973) and former Navy junior officer [4].
- Chaired the Indiana Development Finance Authority under Gov. Evan Bayh (1990-96) and served on the Indianapolis Police Merit Board; was the founding lawyer for Investigative Reporters and Editors [4].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed as the only declared Democrat for District 86 in the May 5, 2026 primary [3].

### Positions & plans

**State budget**

- Opposed the 2025 budget, saying "We have done very little for K-12 public education in this budget while being disproportionately generous with private school vouchers" and that it cut local public health support by more than two-thirds [7].
- After the July 2026 fiscal year-end report, said "We should be using our surplus to address the problems that Hoosiers elected us to solve" and asked, "Why not fully fund preschool, shore up health insurance, or return to our previous robust support of public education?" [19]

**Elections & redistricting**

- Responding to the announced redistricting special session in October 2025, said he would be "happy to have a special session to help the people we were elected to serve – Hoosier taxpayers – not to play a political game" [20].
- Asked Secretary of State Diego Morales in May 2026 to step aside from any recount involving Sen. Spencer Deery, citing Morales's public support for redistricting [21].

**Government oversight**

- Sought a House investigation of the IEDC in 2026, saying "Hoosiers deserve to know if their tax dollars were indeed misused" [10].
- Said of his contractor-disclosure amendment: "Contracts involving taxpayer dollars should be awarded to the best possible service provider, not to the highest campaign contributor" [11].

**Energy & utilities**

- Criticized the House majority in January 2026 for blocking a vote on an amendment to bar utilities from charging customers for lobbying costs [22]. In August 2026 criticized Gov. Braun's removal of Utility Regulatory Commission member Andy Zay [23].

**Education**

- Called the drop in Indiana's college-going rate to 51.7% a result of reduced financial aid and legislative actions toward colleges (July 2025) [24]. Offered a 2026 amendment, defeated, to reverse 2025 budget changes giving the governor sole power to appoint IU trustees [25].

**Public safety & immigration**

- Said school resource officers, campus police and hospital police should be exempt from immigration-detention duties, and offered an amendment requiring violent offenders to serve Indiana sentences before deportation (Feb. 2026) [12].
- Opposed letting the governor deploy National Guard military police to a city without local consent (Jan. 2026) [26]. Joined students at a September 2025 Statehouse rally calling for gun legislation [27].

## Glenn Bill

Republican nominee; Indianapolis real estate broker, author and former high school football coach who served two terms on the Washington Township Board of Trustees [17]. His listed priorities are roads, property-tax relief and accountable government [17].

Campaign website: https://voteglennbill.com/

### Background

- Real estate broker for more than 38 years; his campaign says he bought a franchise at 23, grew it, later sold it, and now leads the Glenn Bill Group; also a keynote speaker, author and podcast host [17].
- High school football position coach for 25 years at his alma mater, Bishop Chatard; the campaign says he played or coached on 13 state championship teams [17].
- Served two terms on the Washington Township Board of Trustees, was elected a State Republican Delegate, and is a precinct committee member, and served on the MIBOR REALTOR Association board [17].
- Describes himself as a lifelong resident of District 86 and Indianapolis [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed as the only declared Republican for District 86 in the May 5, 2026 primary [3].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Supports "working toward the total elimination of property taxes through a responsible, phased approach"; says any transition must be "carefully planned, fully modeled, and transparent" and suggests gradually broadening the tax base, "including selected services" [18].
- Says relief should also reach renters, noting the campaign's estimate that about 60% of occupied homes in the district are renter-occupied [18].

**Roads & infrastructure**

- Lists as his first priority fixing Indianapolis roads "by fighting for a fairer share of state funding for District 86" [17].

**Data centers**

- Opposes "blanket or open-ended incentives" for data centers; says incentives should require commitments on investment, jobs and local revenue, that data centers must pay "their fair share" of utility and infrastructure costs, and that local communities should keep a voice over zoning, water, noise and setbacks [18].

**Housing**

- Supports reducing regulatory barriers that raise construction costs, streamlining permitting, first-time homebuyer programs and mixed-income development [18].

**Education**

- Lists investing in public education, competitive teacher pay, expanded school choice, vocational education and parental rights [18].

**Public safety**

- Supports funding local law enforcement, stronger penalties for violent crime and mental health resources [18]. Told iVoterGuide firearms should be kept from violent felons, domestic abusers and people legally found dangerous, and that he supports background checks and due process [28].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Bill about DeLaney or his record were found. He told iVoterGuide he is running to represent all District 86 residents [28].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Top three priorities: Indianapolis roads, property-tax relief for homeowners, seniors and working families, and "accountable government" with greater transparency [17].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements were found. iVoterGuide's contribution listing shows past donations by Bill to candidates including Todd Young (2019) and Kyrsten Sinema (2018) [28]. No 2026 fundraising totals were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No news coverage of his candidacy was found; information comes from his campaign site and iVoterGuide.
- The dates of the Washington Township Board terms were not found.
- Campaign finance reports were not reviewed.
