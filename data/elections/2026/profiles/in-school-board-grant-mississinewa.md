# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The 'Grant Mississinewa Community School Board' races on the state list are for the Mississinewa Community School Corporation, based in Gas City in Grant County [6, 24]. The district says it serves Gas City and Jonesboro and runs four schools: Westview Elementary, Northview Elementary, R.J. Baskett Middle School and Mississinewa High School [25]. Its per-school figures (about 450, 700, 600 and 750 students) add up to roughly 2,500 students [25]. The district accepted 597 out-of-district transfer students in 2025-26, down from 700 the year before [26].
- The superintendent is Jeremy Fewell, who started on July 1, 2024 [27]. On March 16, 2026, the board voted 7-0 to extend his contract through June 30, 2029 [27].
- The board has seven members. One is elected from each of Districts 1, 2 and 3, and four are elected at large in District 4 [28, 29, 30]. Terms are four years [24]. Denny Smith is board president and Chris Cunningham is vice president [31, 30]. In 2026 the other members are Jarrett Richards, Kyle Speakman, Mike Crouch, Conrad Herrera and Lani Stearns [30, 32, 33].
- District 2 (one seat): Conrad Herrera won the seat in 2022 and is running unopposed [28, 34].
- District 3 (one seat): Michael D. Crouch was re-elected without opposition in 2022 [35, 28]. He is not on the 2026 ballot, so the seat is open. Robert Couse is the only candidate [34, 6].
- District 4 at large: two seats, not one. The state list says 'vote for 1' [6], but the Chronicle-Tribune lists the race as 'District 4 At Large (vote for 2)' [34, 36]. Denny Smith and Susan Miller won the two at-large seats on the 2022 ballot [28]. Miller resigned in 2026 after moving out of the district [37]. In April 2026 the board appointed Lani Stearns to serve through Dec. 31, 2026, and the seat was put on the November ballot for a term from 2027 through 2030 [24, 38]. Smith and Stearns are both running, and Zach Aument (American Solidarity) is challenging them [36, 6].
- Budget and the 2025 property-tax law (fall 2025): in October 2025 the district said its operations fund 'will be completely depleted by the end of 2026' because of circuit-breaker losses and Senate Enrolled Act 1 (SEA 1) [39]. Its forecast showed the fund going from a $1.39 million positive balance in 2025 to a $1.13 million deficit in 2027 [39]. In February 2026 the district said SEA 1 'is projected to reduce Mississinewa's Operations Fund revenue by an additional $1.2 million annually by 2028' [30].
- Building plans (October 2025): the district dropped plans for a capital (building) referendum and turned to an operating referendum instead [40, 41]. Fewell said this 'doesn't make the issues at R.J. Baskett go away' [40].
- Transportation (December 2025 - February 2026): the board heard public comment in December 2025 on a proposal to end bus service [42]. The district says transportation costs about $1.2 million a year [42]. The board put off its Jan. 12 vote until all members could attend [43]. On Feb. 16, 2026, it voted 5-0 to end the transportation program in 2027-28 if voters reject an operating referendum in November 2026 [30]. The district says it would still have to transport students with an IEP [30].
- Operating referendum (Nov. 3, 2026 ballot): on June 15, 2026, the board voted 7-0 to place an operating referendum on the ballot [44]. The question asks for up to $0.4950 per $100 of assessed value for no more than eight years, capped at $2 million a year, to pay for 'student transportation, school security upgrades, and the competitive compensation needed to recruit and retain teachers and staff' [45]. Under the question, the tax bill on a $150,000 median home would rise by $294 a year [45]. Fewell said ending transportation 'would be catastrophic for the district' and could lead to consolidation 'five to 10 years out' [44].
- Staffing (spring 2026): the district carried out a reduction in force and plans to cut central-office staffing from five positions to two between summer 2026 and summer 2027, the Chronicle-Tribune reported [46].
- 2027 budget (September 2026): the board held its public hearing on the 2027 budget, capital projects plan and bus replacement plan on Sept. 21, 2026. The same agenda included a bond lease hearing and resolutions on a lease and construction bids [47].
- Forums: this guide found no 2026 candidate forum or questionnaire for these races. In 2022 and 2024 the Chronicle-Tribune printed candidate answers or short profiles [48, 49]. The only contested race is the District 4 at-large race, with three candidates for two seats [36].

## Conrad Herrera — District 2

Incumbent, running unopposed for another term in District 2 [28, 34].

### Background

- In a 2022 Chronicle-Tribune questionnaire he said he grew up in the community, attended Mississinewa schools and is 'a proud Mississinewa alum' [48].
- Was elected board secretary in January 2025 [31].

### Path to the ballot

- Ran as the incumbent in 2022 and won the three-way District 2 race with 1,013 votes (45.65 percent) [35, 28].
- Filed for reelection; The Indiana Citizen's copy of the state list gives May 19, 2026 as his filing date [50]. The state lists him as Nonpartisan [6].

### Positions & plans

**School safety**

- In 2022 he said the community's vision is 'education, safety of the students and faculty' and that he would 'continue to support the education, safety of our students and faculty' [48].

**Transparency & community input**

- In 2022 he promised to 'continue to have open lines of communication between myself and the community I represent' [48].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member.
- In 2022 he described the board's role as overseeing and voting 'on policies, personnel and financials' and selecting and supporting the superintendent [48].
- On April 20, 2026, he seconded the motion to appoint Lani Stearns to the vacant at-large seat. The motion passed unanimously [38]. On July 27, 2026, he moved to approve the year's personnel changes, which passed 7-0 [32].

- No statement from him on SEA 1, the transportation resolution or the 2026 operating referendum was found; news reports did not record his individual vote on the June 15 referendum resolution beyond the 7-0 total.
