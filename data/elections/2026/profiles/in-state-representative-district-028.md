# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Jeff Thompson (Republican)

Jeff Thompson, a Republican from Lizton and a retired Danville Community High School science and math teacher, has represented House District 28 since he was first elected in 1998 [3]. The House Republican caucus lists him as chair of the House Ways and Means Committee, which writes the House version of the state budget, and he was described as the architect of the 2025 budget [3, 4].

- April 24, 2025: As the House's lead budget writer, presented the two-year, roughly $44 billion state budget (HEA 1001), which passed the House 66-27 and the Senate 39-11; he said it "lives within our means" [4]. The Post-Tribune reported that the budget cut most state agencies by 5% (excluding public safety, mental health, Medicaid and K-12), added about $640 million for K-12 over two years, expanded school choice to all families in the second year, raised the cigarette tax by $2 a pack, reduced public health funding, and let the governor appoint all Indiana University trustees; House Democrats including Reps. Mitch Gore, Greg Porter and Mike Andrade criticized the trustee change and funding levels for public health, pre-K and higher education [4].
- May 12, 2025: In a caucus release after the session, said the budget "once again focuses on government efficiency and maintaining sensible reserves" and that lawmakers "were also able to provide much-needed property tax relief and long-term reforms" [5]. In July 2025 he said the fiscal-year report showed "a better budget outlook than what we expected" in April [6].
- December 5, 2025: Voted yes on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 before the Senate defeated it [7, 8].
- 2026 session: BillTrack50 lists him as a House sponsor of Senate Bill 1 (human services matters), and the caucus says he supported a 2026 law expanding property tax relief for totally disabled veterans and, in April 2026, approval of an additional $200 million for child care assistance [7, 3].

## This race

- House District 28 includes portions of Boone, Hendricks and Montgomery counties in central Indiana, according to the House Republican caucus [3].
- Thompson won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary by about 12 points over registered nurse Sheila Zielinski, after the House Republican Campaign made a last-minute $60,000 contribution amid concerns the budget writer could be in trouble, the Indiana Capital Chronicle reported [9].
- The general election is a rematch of 2024: WFYI's 2024 voter guide listed Thompson and Democrat Karen Whitney as the District 28 candidates [10].
- No debate or candidate forum for this race was found in available coverage as of October 10, 2026.

## Jeffrey A. Thompson

Thompson is the incumbent, in office since 1998, and chairs the House Ways and Means Committee [3].

Campaign website: https://www.indianahouserepublicans.com/members/general/jeff-thompson

### Background

- Taught chemistry, physics and math at Danville Community High School for more than 30 years, serves as a director and vice chair of North Salem State Bank, and runs a hay and straw business, according to the House Republican caucus [3].
- Served on the Indiana State Fair Board from 1991 to 1998, including as president [11].
- Holds a physics degree from Purdue University and lives in Lizton [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Sheila Zielinski by about 12 points [9].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Called the 2025 budget "a budget of freedom" that "prioritizes and spends on our priorities" [4].
- In July 2025, noted rising Medicaid costs and said a lean budget was necessary, according to his caucus statement [6].

**Education**

- The caucus says he led drafting of the school funding formula that shifted state funding to follow students [3].

**Property taxes**

- Said in May 2025 that lawmakers provided "much-needed property tax relief and long-term reforms" [5].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Thompson about his challenger were found in reviewed sources.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said after the 2025 session, "I look forward to continuing work on these priorities," referring to the budget and tax measures [5].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Received a $60,000 contribution from the House Republican Campaign shortly before the 2026 primary, according to the Indiana Capital Chronicle [9].
- WFYI's 2024 guide listed his notable donors that year as including the Indiana Republican State Committee, Indiana Realtors PAC, Indiana BANKPAC and Duke Energy Indiana [10].

### What we couldn't verify

- His individual vote on the 2025 property tax law (SEA 1) was not verified from a roll call.
- He did not complete WFYI's 2024 candidate survey; no 2026 survey responses were found.
- Vote totals for the 2026 primary and 2024 general election were not verified.
- 2026 general-election fundraising reports were not reviewed.

## Karen Whitney

Whitney is a higher-education consultant and former president of Clarion University in Pennsylvania who was the Democratic nominee for this seat in 2024 [12, 10].

Campaign website: https://www.whitneyforindiana.com/

### Background

- Was president of Clarion University from 2010 to 2018 and earlier vice chancellor of student life at Indiana University Purdue University (1999-2010), and has worked as a consultant and leadership coach since 2017-2018, according to BallotReady [12].
- Holds a doctorate in higher education administration from the University of Texas and bachelor's and master's degrees from the University of Houston [12, 10].
- Her campaign site refers to the Brownsburg and Hendricks County community [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Is the Democratic nominee; BallotReady lists her as a candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary, and she was also the Democratic candidate in 2024 [12, 10, 2].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Supports term limits, saying "public office should be a responsibility, not a career" [14, 12].
- In 2024 she proposed term limits and citizen-led initiatives [10].

**Health care**

- In 2024 she called for a review of health care policies, including "trusting women to fully determine their own healthcare choices" [10].

**Economy & jobs**

- In 2024 she said she would use existing laws, and new tools if needed, to hold companies providing basic necessities accountable [10].

### On the current officeholder

- Says on her campaign site that "For almost 30 years, the same people have been running our government in Indiana," without naming Thompson [15].
- In 2024 she said many "career politicians get reelected over and over again because there are no other choices" [10].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she favors "sensible solutions that meet folks in the middle" [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- WFYI's 2024 guide listed endorsements that year from Moms Demand Action, Planned Parenthood, the Indiana Coalition for Public Education and others; 2026 endorsements were not found [10].

### What we couldn't verify

- Her campaign site lists few specific policy positions; detailed 2026 positions on property taxes, the budget or education were not found.
- Whether she faced a 2026 primary opponent was not verified.
- Her city of residence was not confirmed beyond campaign-site references to Brownsburg; a voter-guide listing seen in search results showed a Brownsburg address.
- No 2026 fundraising reports were reviewed.
