# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Jim Tomes (Republican)

Jim Tomes, a Republican from Wadesville, has served in the Senate since 2010 and is seeking a fifth four-year term [3, 4]. He chairs the Senate Committee on Veterans Affairs and The Military and is ranking member of Homeland Security and Transportation, according to the Senate Republican caucus [3].

- Sept. 16, 2025 and April 8, 2026: Met with CenterPoint Energy executives, including CEO Jason Wells, about constituent complaints over bill increases, month-to-month spikes and smart-meter accuracy [5, 6].
- Nov. 18, 2025: Said in a statement that if redistricting came to a vote, "that vote would then be whether to support our Commander in Chief Donald Trump, or the other party. I would have voted 'yes' for our president" [7]. WNIN reported that he voted for the redistricting bill in December 2025; it failed in the Senate [8, 9].
- 2026 session: Authored Senate Enrolled Act 176, which bars counties, cities and towns from adopting stricter local rules for shooting ranges than state law; it was ceremonially signed April 29, 2026 [10]. He also authored a bill to study urban microfarm zones; WNIN described him as second author of SB 164 [11, 12].
- 2026 session: The Senate Republican caucus reported that he did not miss a floor vote [3].
- June 25, 2026: Named vice-chair of the Interim Study Committee on Public Safety and Military Affairs and a member of the Indiana Veterans' Affairs Commission and the Agriculture and Natural Resources study committee [13].
- July 17, 2026: In a campaign statement, said he is concerned about losing farmland to data centers but considers siting "primarily a local issue—not a state issue" for county and city officials and property owners [14].

## This race

- Senate District 49 includes all of Posey County and part of Vanderburgh County, including western Evansville, Mount Vernon, New Harmony and Poseyville; the last Democrat to hold it was Bob Dieg, elected in 2006 [12, 15].
- Tomes had not planned to seek another term but changed his mind, and won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Brandi Durham Pugh with about 80% of the vote [4].
- Cindi Clayton filed as the Democratic candidate in early 2026 and is the party's nominee on the Nov. 3 ballot [16, 2].
- Both candidates name utility bills as a priority: Tomes points to his meetings with CenterPoint, while Clayton says state utility regulation has failed and pledges to take no energy-company money [12, 15]. They also differ on the 2025 redistricting bill, which Tomes voted for and Clayton opposed [12].
- The NAACP Evansville Branch and League of Women Voters of Southwestern Indiana invited all local and state candidates to a Sept. 12, 2026 forum in Evansville; coverage confirming which District 49 candidates took part was not found [17]. No head-to-head debate was found.

## Cindi Clayton

Clayton is a University of Southern Indiana business communication instructor and chairs the board of Holly's House, an Evansville victim advocacy center [12, 18]. She lists public-school funding, utility costs and responsiveness to constituents as her priorities [15].

Campaign website: https://www.cindiforsenate.com/

### Background

- Has taught business communication at USI since 2002 and runs a leadership coaching and HR consulting firm; earlier worked in human resources, according to her BallotReady profile [18].
- Holds a bachelor's degree in psychology and a master's in communication from USI and describes herself as a 30-year Evansville resident [18, 15].
- Board chair of Holly's House, a child and adult victim advocacy center [12].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Democratic candidate in January 2026; she wrote that she knew of no primary challenger at the time [16]. She is the Democratic nominee on the Nov. 3 ballot [2].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Says fully funding public schools would be her first priority and supports restoring income limits for school vouchers [12, 15].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission oversight of CenterPoint "is simply not working" and pledges to refuse campaign money from energy companies [15].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Opposed the 2025 congressional redistricting effort and said local residents who opposed it "weren't listened to" [12].

**Government accountability**

- Supports barring elected officials from signing non-disclosure agreements [12].
- Plans weekly office hours, answering her own email and attending neighborhood meetings outside session [12].

### On the current officeholder

- On her campaign site, Clayton writes that Tomes "has had 15+ years in office to act on energy gouging yet has chosen not to do so" [15].
- Told WNIN she would represent "only the constituents of district 49 — not federal administration, not a corporate sponsor" [12].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says restoring public-school funding would be her most important priority [15].
- Would hold weekly office hours if elected [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Her campaign site says she is endorsed by the United Mine Workers of America [15].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign-finance totals were verified.
- Her campaign site's About page returned a not-found error; background comes from WNIN, BallotReady and her home page.
- A WEHT story on her November 2025 campaign launch was cited by Wikipedia but not read.
- No statements found on property taxes, data centers or the 2025 state budget.

## Jim Tomes

Tomes, a Vietnam veteran and retired Teamster truck driver, has represented the district since 2010 [3]. He emphasizes personal responsiveness to constituents and lowering utility bills [12].

Campaign website: https://hoosiersfortomes.com/

### Background

- Served in the U.S. Army with the 101st Airborne Division in Vietnam; worked 33 years as a truck driver and was a union steward for 21 years, according to the Senate Republican caucus [3].
- Director of the 2nd Amendment Patriots and past commander of Wadesville VFW Post 6576; lives near Blairsville [3, 19].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Brandi Durham Pugh with about 80% of the vote [4].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Says getting something done on utility bills is a priority and cites his meetings with CenterPoint leadership [12, 6].

**Data centers**

- Says he is concerned about the loss of farmland, recognizes possible construction jobs and investment, and views siting decisions as mainly for local officials and property owners [14].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Supported the 2025 redistricting effort and told WNIN constituents were divided on it [7, 12].

**Public safety**

- Authored SEA 176 (2026) limiting local regulation of shooting ranges [10].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. He told WNIN, "I take all my calls, all my emails, all my handwritten letters, and respond to everyone personally" [12]. No statements by Tomes about Clayton were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Told WNIN he has "six or seven bills" in mind, including one setting aside two days of youth hunting season for children with disabilities, and further action on utility bills [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign lists endorsements from the Indiana State AFL-CIO, Indiana Farm Bureau, Indiana FOP, NRA Political Victory Fund, NFIB Indiana PAC, Indiana Right to Life PAC, several building-trades and steelworkers unions, and U.S. Sen. Jim Banks [20].
- Wikipedia's 2026 Indiana Senate election page lists President Donald Trump and Gov. Mike Braun as endorsing him [9].

### What we couldn't verify

- His votes on the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) and the 2025 state budget were not verified.
- Wikipedia lists primary results of 2,529 (81.8%) for Tomes to 562 (18.2%) for Pugh under a table mislabeled District 43; not confirmed against official results.
- No campaign-finance totals were verified.
