# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Brad Barrett (Republican)

Brad Barrett, a retired Richmond general surgeon, has represented House District 56 since 2018 and chairs the House Public Health Committee [3]. He announced in January 2026 that he would not seek reelection, giving no specific reason; his term ends in November [4].

- Committees: Public Health (chair), Agriculture and Rural Development, and Insurance [3]. First elected in 2018 after defeating incumbent Rep. Richard Hamm in the Republican primary [4].
- 2024: Authored HEA 1385, requiring insurers to reimburse out-of-network ambulance services and pay claims within 30 days [5]. 2025: Sponsored SB 2 on Medicaid oversight and fraud reporting, which passed both chambers [6]. 2026: Authored HB 1277 on Medicaid assisted-living services, which passed the House [7].
- Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [8]; the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [9]; and HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [10].
- On leaving, said: "Serving the people of Wayne and Henry counties has been one of the greatest honors of my life" [4].

## This race

- House District 56 covers all of Wayne County, including Richmond, and part of Henry County [3].
- Open seat: Rep. Brad Barrett announced on Jan. 7, 2026 that he would not seek a fifth term [4]. Barrett was reelected unopposed in 2024 [11].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Randy Retter 3,250 votes (53.0%), Pete Zaleski 2,603 (42.4%), Adam Blanton 281 (4.6%) [11]. Blanton, an assistant Richmond police chief, withdrew in March and endorsed Zaleski, but stayed on the ballot [12]. Rockwell was the only Democrat to file [13, 11].
- Key differences: Retter emphasizes public safety, school security and law-enforcement links to addiction treatment [14]; Rockwell emphasizes worker protections, public-school funding, universal health coverage and ending partisan gerrymandering [15].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Randy Retter

Randy Retter is the two-term Wayne County sheriff, who is term-limited in that office [13]. He won a three-way Republican primary with 53% of the vote [11].

Campaign website: https://www.randyretter.com/

### Background

- Began his law enforcement career in 1988 and was elected Wayne County sheriff in 2018; now in his second term [16]. Cannot run for sheriff again because of term limits [13].
- Bachelor's degree in criminal justice from Ball State University and graduate of the Indiana Law Enforcement Academy [13].
- Serves on the Boys & Girls Club board [17]; lives in Wayne County across from his family farm [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced Jan. 9, 2026 [16]; won the May 5 Republican primary over Pete Zaleski and Adam Blanton with 53.0% [11].

### Positions & plans

**Public safety**

- Says he would make school safety protocols a priority, support first responders and use data to reduce repeat offenses [14].

**Health care**

- Says he supports lowering health care costs and protecting rural access to care, building on Barrett's work [14].
- Says he has worked to expand partnerships for mental health and addiction treatment and wants to connect law enforcement and health care [14].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says he would reduce regulation on small businesses and family farms and bring jobs to eastern Indiana [14].
- Says he would apply the budget discipline of the sheriff's office to state government and seek a fair share of infrastructure funding for rural areas [14].

### On the current officeholder

- His campaign describes his health-care plans as building on "the work of Dr. Brad Barrett" [14]. At his announcement he said: "After many years in public service, I've seen firsthand the challenges and opportunities facing our rural communities" [16].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he wants "strong, steady leadership that puts Hoosiers first and reflects the values of the people who live and work here" [13].

## Thomas Scott Rockwell

Tom Rockwell is a Richmond Quaker minister and pastor running as the Democratic nominee [13, 15]. His platform focuses on wages and worker rights, public schools, health care, rural investment and election reform [15].

Campaign website: https://www.tom4indiana.com/

### Background

- Pastor of a small rural church and a denominational leader; has also run a small forestry business working with farmers and landowners [13].
- Earned a Master of Divinity from the Earlham School of Religion in 2024 and works at Western Yearly Meeting; he co-pastors a Quaker church [18].
- Age 34; has lived in Richmond for six years [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Feb. 3, 2026 [13]; the only Democrat in the May 5 primary [11].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports collective bargaining, a higher minimum wage, action on wage theft, apprenticeships, and tenant protections [15].
- Supports protecting workers from AI-related job loss and surveillance and requiring transparency when AI is used in major public systems [15].

**Education**

- Supports fully funding public schools, raising teacher pay and expanding vocational and community-college options [15].

**Health care**

- Supports universal coverage, preventive and maternal care, and mental health and addiction services [15]; has cited affordable, accessible health care as a rural concern [13].

**Public safety**

- Supports funding first responders, mental-health crisis response, addiction treatment and domestic-violence services [15].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Supports campaign-finance transparency, ending partisan gerrymandering, public town halls and local control over schools, libraries and public safety [15].

**Rural investment**

- Supports downtown revitalization, support for manufacturers and family farms, rural health care and infrastructure repair [15].

### On the current officeholder

- On the district's direction, said: "Too often, the proposed solutions for communities like ours focus on attracting outside companies" and "I believe the real expertise already lives here" [13]. No statements about Retter were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- His campaign says he would hold public town halls and Zoom sessions [15].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- iVoterGuide lists an endorsement from the Indiana Reproductive Freedom PAC [19].

### What we couldn't verify

- Little news coverage beyond his filing was found.
- He did not answer the iVoterGuide questionnaire.
- No fundraising data found.
