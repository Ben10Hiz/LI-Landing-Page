# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Randy Maxwell (Republican)

Randy Maxwell, a Republican from Guilford, has represented Senate District 43 since September 2023, when he was chosen to finish the term of Sen. Chip Perfect, who resigned [3]. He is CEO of Maxwell Construction and serves on the Senate committees on Homeland Security and Transportation, Pensions and Labor, Public Policy, and Veterans Affairs and the Military, according to the Senate Republican caucus [4].

- September 28, 2023: Sworn in to serve the rest of Chip Perfect's term, which runs through November 2026 [3].
- July 2025: Named to interim study committees on Commerce and Economic Development, Employment and Labor, and Environmental Affairs [5].
- October 24, 2025: Issued a statement saying that after hearing from constituents he believed the time was right to redraw Indiana's congressional districts, and expressing support for President Trump's agenda [6].
- December 11, 2025: Voted yes on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which the Senate defeated 31–19 [7].
- 2026 session: Co-authored Senate Enrolled Act 256, which extends a 2024 ban on foreign-adversary purchases of farmland to all Indiana land, requires agents of hostile nations to register with the attorney general, and bars certain government technology contracts with adversary-controlled businesses; it was ceremonially signed April 30, 2026 [8]. The ACLU of Indiana opposed the law, saying it discriminates against people based on national origin [9].
- 2026 session: The caucus reported he missed no floor votes and supported Senate Enrolled Act 1 (added SNAP and Medicaid eligibility checks), SEA 243 (state tax treatment of tips and overtime), and House Enrolled Act 1002 (performance-based utility ratemaking) [10]. BillTrack50 records him voting yes on the final 2026 votes for SB 1 and SB 76 (immigration) [11].
- April 8, 2026: Called on Gov. Mike Braun to temporarily suspend the state gas tax, joining Sen. Travis Holdman and other Republican senators [12].

## This race

- Senate District 43 covers Dearborn, Jefferson, Ohio, Scott and Switzerland counties and part of Jennings County in southeast Indiana [4].
- This is Maxwell's first general election for the seat; he was chosen by Republicans in 2023 to fill a vacancy [3, 13].
- May 5, 2026 primary: Maxwell defeated Joe Volk in the Republican primary, 7,874 votes (60.1%) to 5,234 (39.9%), as reported by the Madison Courier; Byron Holland was unopposed for the Democratic nomination [13].
- No debates or candidate forums for this race were found in published coverage as of October 10, 2026 [13].

## Byron E. Holland

Byron Holland is the Democratic nominee; his campaign says it is focused on health care, schools, and jobs and on building coalitions across party lines [14].

Campaign website: https://www.hollandforhoosiers.com/

### Background

- His campaign website and BallotReady profile do not describe his occupation, education, or community roles [14, 15].

### Path to the ballot

- Ran unopposed for the Democratic nomination in the May 5, 2026 primary [13].

### Positions & plans

**Data centers**

- His platform opposes new data centers on Indiana farmland and calls for ending state tax subsidies for them [16].

**Agriculture**

- Supports full funding for county Extension offices and agricultural research, and farmland property tax relief through what the platform calls a fair, stable, and predictable assessment system [16].

**Health care**

- Supports expanding rural emergency and critical care, addiction treatment and recovery services, and maternal health access, and raising Medicaid income-eligibility limits [16].

**Energy & utilities**

- Calls for transparent utility rate-setting with clear explanations of proposed increases [16].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports expanding broadband access and opposes what the platform calls unnecessary expansion of toll fees [16].

## Randall D. Maxwell

Randy Maxwell, the Republican incumbent, is a Guilford construction company CEO who joined the Senate in 2023 [4, 3].

### Background

- CEO of Maxwell Construction, a family-owned general contractor and development company in southeast Indiana; studied finance, real estate and entrepreneurship at Indiana University [4].
- Former president of the Dearborn County Home Builders Association, founding member and vice president of the 1Dearborn economic development organization, and appointed to the Indiana Unemployment Insurance Board in 2007, according to his caucus biography [4].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Joe Volk with 60.1% of the vote [13].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Supported mid-decade redrawing of Indiana's congressional map in an October 2025 statement and voted for HB 1032 in December 2025 [6, 7].

**Public safety**

- Co-authored SEA 256 on foreign-adversary land ownership and agent registration, saying the law "is a big step forward for our state" [8].

**Economy & jobs**

- Urged the governor in April 2026 to suspend the state gas tax, saying the state had the financial room to return money to taxpayers [12].

**Health care**

- Cited 2026 legislation to "crack down on health care fraud" as a session accomplishment [18].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Maxwell about his general-election opponent were found [4].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said at the end of the 2026 session that he would keep working with lawmakers and constituents during the interim [18].
