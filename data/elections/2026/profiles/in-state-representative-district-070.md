# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Karen Engleman (Republican)

Karen Engleman of Georgetown has represented House District 70 since 2016 and serves in House Republican leadership as assistant majority floor leader [3]. She decided not to seek re-election in 2026 [4]; she endorsed Republican nominee Scott Fluhr in the primary [5].

- Leadership and committees: assistant majority floor leader; chair of the Statutory Committee on Ethics; member of Government and Regulatory Reform and Local Government [3]. She was first named Ethics chair for the 2023 session [6].
- Before the legislature, served three terms as Harrison County auditor and seven years as Crandall town clerk-treasurer [3]. The caucus says Governing magazine named her a 2018 Public Official of the Year for her law requiring annual sexual-harassment training for legislators [3].
- Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [7]; the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [8]; and HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [9].
- Reelected in 2024 with 73.4% of the vote over Democrat Sarah Blessing [10, 11], after defeating John Colburn in the Republican primary [12].

## This race

- House District 70 includes all of Harrison County (including Corydon) and parts of Clark, Floyd and Washington counties [13, 3]. In Washington County, it covers Howard, Jackson, Pierce, Polk and Posey townships [14].
- Open seat: Rep. Karen Engleman decided not to seek re-election [4]. No public explanation from Engleman for her decision was found in the sources reviewed.
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Scott Fluhr 4,159 votes (68.5%), John Colburn 1,914 (31.5%) [10]. Democratic primary: Sarah Blessing 1,477 (49.9%), Jerry Finn 1,096 (37.0%), Tamyra Persinger-Andres 387 (13.1%) [10]. Libertarian Brandi Tibben is also on the November ballot [15, 2].
- A Floyd County Republican Party debate before the primary included the Republican House District 70 candidates [16]. No general-election debate or forum for this race was found.
- Key differences: Fluhr's campaign lists endorsements from Indiana Right to Life and statewide Republican officials, but posts no issue positions [5]. Blessing's platform emphasizes public-school funding, opposing tax breaks for data centers, rural hospitals and changing Indiana's abortion law [17, 18]. No statements by Tibben were found.

## Sarah Blessing

Sarah Blessing is a former teacher who was the 2024 Democratic nominee against Engleman [11]. She won a three-way Democratic primary in May 2026 [10].

Campaign website: https://www.sarahblessing.com/

### Background

- Raised in Floyds Knobs and a 1999 Floyd Central High School graduate; holds a bachelor's degree in elementary education from IU Southeast and a master's in education from Indiana Wesleyan University [19].
- Taught for 18 years at Our Lady of Perpetual Help Catholic School in New Albany, then volunteered with the Harrison County Democratic Party [19].
- Co-founded Project Next, a media company focused on community conversations, where she hosts interviews [19, 18].

### Path to the ballot

- Ran in 2024 as the Democratic nominee against Engleman [11], who won with 73.4% [10].
- Filed for 2026 by mid-January [13]; won the May 5 Democratic primary with 49.9% over Jerry Finn and Tamyra Persinger-Andres [10].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Says public tax dollars should fund public schools open to every child; says parents may choose private or religious schools but taxpayers should not pay the tuition, and notes voucher spending has grown to nearly $500 million a year [18].
- Says teachers, superintendents, parents and local school boards should have a stronger voice in school rules [18]; her platform calls for fair pay for educators and keeping curriculum decisions with local educators and parents [17].

**Data centers**

- Opposes the long-term sales-tax exemptions Indiana offers qualifying data centers without strong protections, and wants disclosure of water and electricity use and safeguards against higher utility bills [18, 17].
- Supports local control and zoning authority over data centers and large solar projects, and says host communities should receive real benefits [18].

**Health care**

- Supports protecting Medicaid coverage, paying rural hospitals fairly, and state investment to bring doctors, nurses and mental-health workers to small communities [18].
- Says Indiana's abortion law should change so medical decisions are made by a woman, her doctor and her family, citing doctors' warnings that the law can delay care [18].

**Property taxes**

- Platform calls for property-tax relief for working families, transparency in how taxes are calculated, and stopping utilities from shifting costs onto ratepayers [17].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports fast rural internet, help for small businesses and family farms, and job training as ways to grow rural jobs [18].

**Government & ethics**

- Says her top priority would be shorter, single-issue bills so the public can understand them, plus stronger state oversight when local officials fail to account for public money [18].

### On the current officeholder

- In 2024 she said she would "work together across the aisle to reverse these hurtful laws and restore freedom," citing energy bills, public-school funding and women's medical decisions [11]. In 2026 she said she is running because "regular Hoosiers have lost their voice in state government" [18].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she would work with anyone willing to help Hoosiers and would "sit at the table with anyone who is willing to put people over party" [18].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Her campaign site lists the Center for Freethought Equality among supporters [20]; iVoterGuide lists the Center for Freethought Equality and Indivisible NWI [21]. No fundraising totals were verified.

### What we couldn't verify

- A partisan blog post comparing the candidates (linked from her site) was not used.
- Her town of residence was not confirmed from a readable source (a search snippet from Blue Voter Guide listed a Palmyra mailing address).
- Her campaign site describes the district as parts of Harrison, Washington and Floyd counties and leaves out Clark County, which LPM and the House Republican caucus include.
- No campaign finance reports were reviewed.

## Scott L. Fluhr

Scott Fluhr is a Corydon human resources analyst who has chaired the Harrison County Republican Party for nearly 18 years [5]. He won the May 2026 Republican primary with 68.5% [10].

Campaign website: https://www.hoosiersforscott.com/

### Background

- Lifelong Harrison County resident from Corydon; graduate of Corydon Central High School and Indiana University Bloomington (business) [5, 22].
- Works as an HRIS (human resources information systems) analyst at Horseshoe Southern Indiana [22]; also writes the blog The Hoosierpundit [22].
- Volunteer chairman of the Harrison County Republican Party for almost 18 years; president of the Harrison County Parks & Recreation Board; member of the county tourism board; chairs the Popcorn Festival parade [5].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Jan. 7, 2026 [13]; won the May 5 Republican primary over John Colburn, 4,159 votes (68.5%) to 1,914 [10].

### Positions & plans

**Values**

- His campaign site cites an "AQ" rating from the National Rifle Association and an Indiana Right to Life endorsement [5].

### On the current officeholder

- Endorsed in the primary by Rep. Karen Engleman, the departing seat holder [5]. No statements by Fluhr on the seat's direction or on his opponents were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- No specific legislative plans were found on his campaign site or in news coverage.

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Primary endorsements listed by his campaign: Indiana Right to Life, U.S. Sen. Jim Banks, U.S. Rep. Erin Houchin, Attorney General Todd Rokita, State Treasurer Daniel Elliott, state Sen. Gary Byrne, Rep. Karen Engleman and the Indiana Manufacturers Association [5]. iVoterGuide lists contributions from Sen. Gary Byrne and state Republican Party organizations in 2026 [23]. No fundraising totals were verified.

### What we couldn't verify

- No issue positions on taxes, schools, health care or data centers were found. A partisan blog says he has backed private-school vouchers in the press, but this was not verified.
- What he said at the Floyd County Republican debate was not reported in the sources found.
- No campaign finance reports were reviewed.
