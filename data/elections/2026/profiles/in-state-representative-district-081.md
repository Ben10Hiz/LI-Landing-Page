# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Martin Carbaugh (Republican)

Martin Carbaugh, a Fort Wayne financial planner, has represented House District 81 since 2012 and chairs the House Insurance Committee [3, 4]. He authored the 2025 law (HEA 1004) setting price benchmarks for large nonprofit hospital systems [5].

- Committees: Insurance (chair), Education and Public Health; interim committees on Financial Institutions and Insurance and on Pension Management Oversight, according to the House Republican caucus [3].
- 2025: Authored HEA 1004, which requires the largest nonprofit hospital systems to bring average prices to or below a statewide average by June 30, 2029, or risk losing nonprofit status [5]. Carbaugh said at final passage: "Even with those changes, we are making meaningful, incremental changes to provide relief to our constituents" [6].
- HEA 1004 criticism: Rep. Robin Shackleford (D-Indianapolis) said "There is nothing in this bill that ensures that patients will pay less for quality health care"; Sen. Liz Brown (R-Fort Wayne) warned that loss of nonprofit status could lead to sales of facilities that leave out rural hospitals [6]. In April 2026 Carbaugh said large nonprofit hospitals were complying with the law's direct-to-employer provisions but that marketplace and other insured patients still need help [7].
- 2026: Sponsored SEA 88, which adds instruction on finishing high school, workforce training, full-time employment and waiting to become a parent until marriage to existing coursework, and recognizes the Classic Learning Test as a college entrance exam [8].
- Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [9]; the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [10]; and HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [11].

## This race

- House District 81 covers the northwestern part of Fort Wayne and part of central Allen County, according to the House Republican caucus [3]; the Journal Gazette describes it as including northwest Allen County [12]. Wight cites growth in Huntertown as a district issue [12].
- Carbaugh was reelected with 64.9% of the vote in 2024 [4].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Carbaugh 3,795 votes (75.1%), David Mervar 1,256 (24.9%). Democratic primary: Sharon Wight 1,976 (81.6%), Chad Clevidence 446 (18.4%) [4].
- Key differences: Carbaugh's legislative focus has been health care pricing and insurance [3, 7]; Wight emphasizes accessibility, public transit, public-school funding, union support and marijuana legalization [13].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Martin Carbaugh

Seven-term incumbent and chair of the House Insurance Committee [3]. Won the May 2026 Republican primary with 75.1% [4].

### Background

- Financial planner with Financial Focus; attended Indiana University-Purdue University Fort Wayne; lifelong Fort Wayne resident [3].
- Volunteers with Junior Achievement teaching financial literacy; member of the National Association of Insurance and Financial Advisors and the National Federation of Independent Business [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over David Mervar, 75.1% to 24.9% [4].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Says HEA 1004 is meant to "hold hospitals accountable for lower and more transparent pricing, prioritize patients and reduce health care costs" [7].
- In a June 2026 column on hospital pricing and the state's claims database he wrote: "This is meaningful progress, but the work is not finished" [14].

**Economy & jobs**

- The caucus lists his priorities as reducing regulations that hinder workforce growth, a competitive tax code and fiscally conservative state finances [3].

**Education**

- On SEA 88: "This new law helps ensure students are prepared not just for graduation, but for life after high school" [8].

**Energy & utilities**

- Was among northeast Indiana legislators announcing the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission's March 2026 energy affordability investigation, which included Indiana Michigan Power [15].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No public statements about his challenger were found. He did not respond to the Journal Gazette's primary questionnaire [12].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says more work remains on health care costs for people with marketplace and other non-direct-employer plans [7].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Wikipedia lists primary endorsements from Donald Trump, Gov. Mike Braun, Americans for Prosperity, the NFIB Indiana PAC and the NRA Political Victory Fund [4]. No fundraising figures were verified.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website was found.
- Campaign finance totals were not checked (state portal not readable).
- No statements on data centers, property taxes or the 2025 redistricting vote beyond his recorded votes were found.

## Sharon L Wight

Sharon Wight is a Fort Wayne project manager and former adjunct instructor making her first run for office [12]. She won the Democratic primary with 81.6% [4].

Campaign website: https://www.electsharonwight.com/

### Background

- Project manager at an educational technology company that builds websites, apps and communication systems for schools; previously an adjunct instructor at area universities, an academic advisor at Purdue Fort Wayne and briefly a dual-credit instructor at Homestead High School [16].
- Attended Fort Wayne Community Schools and holds two master's degrees [16]. Lives in Fort Wayne [12].
- Says she has worked as a disability advocate with the League for the Blind and Disabled [12] and has served as a church secretary since high school [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary over Chad Clevidence, 81.6% to 18.4% [4].

### Positions & plans

**Growth & infrastructure**

- Named "rapid development in parts of the district while others still need support and maintenance" as the top issue, citing strain on Huntertown's water source [12].
- Supports directing tax dollars to projects that serve residents rather than corporate developers, and accessible public transit, neighborhoods and buildings [13].

**Education**

- Supports public schools, tax funding for education, higher teacher pay and accountability for tax funding [13].

**Health care**

- Supports a health care system that keeps residents out of bankruptcy, citing Massachusetts as a model [12].

**Housing**

- Says rental housing should be held to a higher standard [12]. Opposed 2026's HB 1431 on street camping, saying it "effectively criminalizes homelessness" [13].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says pay has not kept pace with living costs; supports medical and family leave plans and describes herself as "Pro-Union" [13].

**Data centers**

- Lists support for HB 1043, "Data Center Water Regulation" [13].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Lists support for bills on straight-ticket voting and same-day voter registration [13].

**Social issues**

- Supports marijuana legalization, abortion rights, same-sex marriage and gender-affirming care [13].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements about Carbaugh or his record were found on her website or in the coverage reviewed [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says her first-year goal is to build relationships with constituents, businesses and colleagues [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising figures were verified.

### What we couldn't verify

- Limited news coverage beyond the Journal Gazette primary questionnaire.
- HD 83 candidate Wesley Haffenden's campaign site thanks Wight for opposing the proposed southwest Allen County quarry; no statement from Wight herself was found.
