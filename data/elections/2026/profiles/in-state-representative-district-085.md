# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Dave Heine (Republican)

Dave Heine, a Republican who lists Fort Wayne as his hometown, has represented House District 85 since 2016 and serves on the House Ways and Means, Agriculture and Rural Development, and Roads and Transportation committees [3]. He is a farmer and retired Do It Best Corp. executive [3] and is seeking reelection in 2026 [4].

- Committees: Ways and Means; Agriculture and Rural Development; Roads and Transportation (vice chair), according to his House Republican caucus page [3].
- 2025: Authored House Enrolled Act 1253, which the caucus says lets school-based child care programs serve children across a whole school corporation, extends child care voucher waivers from two years to three, and lets owners of multi-site child care centers operate under a single license; it passed both chambers and went to the governor in April 2025 [5]. Heine said the bill "provides a practical solution to increase availability and help providers operate more efficiently" [5].
- Feb. 14, 2025: The House Ways and Means Committee advanced Heine's bill to boost Indiana's teacher pipeline, according to the caucus [3].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (passed 65-29) [6]. Heine said in an April 2025 caucus release that it "delivers property tax relief to Hoosiers, especially in our rural communities where every dollar counts" [7].
- April 25, 2025: Voted yes on HEA 1001, the 2025-27 state budget, adopted 66-27 [8]. Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [9, 10]. He was recorded as excused on the Jan. 28, 2026 House vote on HB 1002, the utility-affordability bill [11].
- 2026: Highlighted House Enrolled Act 1177, which the caucus says expands the state's employer child care tax credit; Heine said it gives "employers and communities more flexibility to invest in childcare solutions" [12].

## This race

- House District 85 covers eastern portions of Allen County in northeast Indiana, according to Heine's caucus page [3].
- Heine is seeking a sixth full term; he won reelection in 2024 with 74.4% of the vote [4].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Wikipedia's 2026 election page lists Heine as the only declared Republican and Mark Wehrle as the only declared Democrat, so neither faced a primary opponent [4].
- No debate or forum between the two candidates was found, and no news coverage of the 2026 race was found in the Fort Wayne media searched.
- Differences on policy cannot be compared: Wehrle has published no positions that could be found, and iVoterGuide shows every question on its questionnaire as unanswered by him [13].

## Dave Heine

Republican incumbent since 2016; farmer and retired Do It Best Corp. vice president [3]. His legislative work has focused on child care licensing, teacher recruitment and tax measures [5, 3].

Campaign website: https://www.daveheine85.com/

### Background

- Owner of Heine Farms and retired vice president and corporate officer of Do It Best Corp.; B.S. in accounting from Indiana University (1979) [3].
- Community roles listed by the caucus include vice chair of the Lutheran Foundation board, the Big Brothers Big Sisters of Northeast Indiana board, the Central Lutheran School board in New Haven, and chairman of the New Haven Republican Committee [3].
- His campaign site describes him as a lifelong Allen County resident [14].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed as the only declared Republican for District 85 in the May 5, 2026 primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Supported SEA 1 (2025); said it "delivers property tax relief to Hoosiers, especially in our rural communities" and is "about supporting local homeowners and others who have been feeling the impact of rising property taxes" [7, 6].

**Child care**

- Said in 2025 that "Child care access is a major concern for Hoosier families" and that HEA 1253 is "an important step toward eliminating regulations that limit access to care" [5].
- Said in 2026 that "Finding quality childcare shouldn't be a barrier for Hoosier parents trying to work" [12].

**Taxes & government**

- His campaign site, which carries a 2022 copyright notice, lists co-authoring "the largest tax cut bill in Indiana history," "Safeguarding against government overreach," abortion restrictions, and "Empowering parents to choose the best education for their children" [14].
