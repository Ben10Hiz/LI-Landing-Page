# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Travis Holdman (Republican)

Sen. Travis Holdman (R-Markle), in the Senate since 2008, majority caucus chair and chair of the Tax and Fiscal Policy Committee, lost the May 2026 Republican primary to Trump-endorsed challenger Blake Fiechter after voting against the December 2025 congressional redistricting plan [3, 4, 5].

- 2025: Authored Senate Bill 1, the Senate Republicans' property-tax bill, which the caucus says provides about $1.3 billion in relief over three years, including a homestead credit of 10% up to $300, and lowers the local income tax cap from 3.75% to 2.9% [6, 3].
- Dec. 11, 2025: Was one of 21 Republicans who joined all 10 Democrats to defeat the mid-decade congressional redistricting plan 31–19; in November he had said his district did not support mid-cycle redistricting [5].
- Feb. 2026: Authored SEA 243, which, according to the Senate Republican caucus, aligns Indiana's tax code with federal tax provisions on tips, overtime, and interest on loans for American-made vehicles for tax year 2026 [7].
- Jan. 2026: President Trump called Holdman a 'RINO' in a social media post and endorsed Fiechter; Holdman did not respond to the Indiana Capital Chronicle's requests for comment [5].

## This race

- Senate District 19 covers all of Jay, Blackford, Wells, and Adams counties and the southwest corner of Allen County [4, 3].
- This is an open seat: incumbent Travis Holdman lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to Bluffton City Council member Blake Fiechter, 8,449 votes (61.4%) to 5,312 in unofficial results reported by The Commercial Review [4]. Wikipedia's results compilation lists 8,684 (61.5%) to 5,441 [8].
- President Trump targeted Holdman after his vote against the December 2025 redistricting bill; political action committees spent heavily on ads against Holdman, and Fiechter said the outside spending muddied the race but raised his name recognition [4]. WANE reported that super PACs tied to Sen. Jim Banks and Gov. Mike Braun and allied groups spent more than $8.3 million across seven such Senate primaries, according to AdImpact [9].
- Democrat Timothy C. Murphy was unopposed in the primary [4].
- No debate or forum between Fiechter and Murphy was found in available coverage.
- The candidates' published platforms differ on taxes (Fiechter calls for cutting government spending and reforming property-tax laws; Murphy proposes progressive income and property taxes), abortion, and labor law [10, 11].

## Blake Fiechter

Blake Fiechter is a real estate broker and first-term Bluffton City Council member who defeated Sen. Travis Holdman in the May 2026 Republican primary after President Trump endorsed him [5, 4].

Campaign website: https://www.blakefiechterin.com

### Background

- A real estate broker in his first term as an at-large Bluffton City Council member, elected in 2023 [5].
- His campaign says he is a small business owner, serves on a local redevelopment commission, and is a third-generation Bluffton resident [10].

### Path to the ballot

- Trump endorsed Fiechter on Jan. 22, 2026, before Fiechter had filed for the seat [5].
- WANE reported he announced in February that he was stepping away from the race, then reversed that decision; WANE also reported endorsements from Trump and Gov. Mike Braun [9].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 61.4% in unofficial results, including Jay County (1,633 to 1,136), and did best in Adams County (65.5%) [4].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- His campaign says he would address high costs for groceries, gas, and utilities by cutting government spending [10].
- On primary night he said he wants to bring relief from rising expenses and 'fresh perspectives' to the Senate [12].

**Property taxes**

- His campaign pledges to reform property-tax laws so families and seniors are not forced out of their homes [10].

**Agriculture**

- His campaign says rural communities and farmers should be heard and receive their fair share of state funding [10].

**Abortion & social issues**

- His campaign describes him as pro-life [10].

**Public safety**

- His campaign says he would oppose efforts to restrict firearms and protect Second Amendment rights [10].

**Elections & redistricting**

- His campaign says he supports term limits for elected officials [10].

### On the current officeholder

- On primary night he thanked Holdman for his years of service [12].

### How they say they'd run the office

- His campaign lists cutting government spending, property-tax reform, and term limits among his goals [10].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by President Trump and, per WANE, Gov. Mike Braun [5, 9].
- The Commercial Review reported Holdman's campaign outraised Fiechter's in the primary, while outside PACs ran ads against Holdman [4].

### What we couldn't verify

- No general-election fundraising totals found; the state campaign finance portal was not reviewed.

## Timothy C. Murphy

Timothy C. Murphy is a pastor running for office for the first time who was unopposed for the Democratic nomination [13, 4].

Campaign website: https://murphyfor19.com

### Background

- According to his campaign, he is the pastor of a local church, grew up in Kentucky, and has lived in Fort Wayne since 2018 [13].
- His campaign says this is his first run for office [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- His platform proposes a citizen-led ballot initiative process and multi-member districts with proportional representation [11].

**Property taxes**

- His platform proposes replacing Indiana's flat income tax with a progressive income tax and establishing a progressive property tax [11].

**Economy & jobs**

- His platform proposes raising the minimum wage with inflation indexing, repealing right-to-work, and tax incentives for businesses that put workers on their boards [11].
- It also calls for subsidized child care of no more than $10 per child per day, at least 13 weeks of paid parental leave, and at least five paid sick days a year [11].

**Education**

- His platform calls for spending transparency for charter and private schools that receive public funds [11].

**Health care**

- His platform calls for billing transparency, expanded access in every county, protecting Medicaid eligibility, and restoring abortion access [11].

**Energy & utilities**

- His platform calls for stronger air- and water-quality rules and expanded battery storage and transmission for clean energy [11].

**Housing**

- His platform calls for stronger tenant rights, community land trusts, and more affordable units in tax-advantaged public-private projects [11].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Murphy about Holdman, Fiechter, or the seat's record were found; his platform says the economic and political system 'does not work for most people' [11].

### How they say they'd run the office

- His platform also proposes more bus funding with free rides for riders under 18 and over 65 [11].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign site [14].

### What we couldn't verify

- No news coverage of Murphy beyond primary-result mentions was found.
- No fundraising totals found; the state campaign finance portal was not reviewed.
- Whether his Fort Wayne residence is within the district's Allen County portion was not verified.
