# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Shane Lindauer (Republican)

Shane Lindauer, a Jasper Republican, has represented House District 63 since he was appointed in November 2017 to finish Mike Braun's term [3]. In October 2025 he announced he would not seek reelection, saying it was "time to step away from public office and focus on what is best for my family and small business" [4].

- Chairs the House Natural Resources Committee and serves on the Roads and Transportation Committee; appointed to the White River State Park Development Commission [3].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (passed 65-29) [5]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 budget (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [6]. Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [7].
- 2026 session: Authored HB 1226, giving bidders a price preference on state purchases when they offer Indiana-made supplies; it passed the House Government and Regulatory Reform Committee unanimously [8]. Colleagues honored him on the House floor on Feb. 9, 2026, for nine years of service [3].

## This race

- House District 63 includes all of Martin County and parts of Daviess, Dubois and Pike counties [3, 9]. Arthur's campaign describes it as Daviess and Martin counties, most of Dubois County and part of Pike County [10].
- Open seat: Lindauer announced in October 2025 that he would not run again, citing his family and small business [4]. He won reelection in 2024 with 76.3% [11].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Amy Kippenbrock won the Republican nomination over Richard Moss, 77.4% to 22.6%; Tiffanie Arthur won the Democratic nomination with 63.8% over Tony Bolen (20.5%) and Adam Mann (15.7%) [11].
- Key differences: On the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1), Kippenbrock says the state is "moving in the right direction" while Arthur says it did not "get the balance right" [12, 13]. On data centers and solar, Kippenbrock favors local control and Arthur supports a temporary moratorium on large data centers [12, 13]. Arthur opposes the Mid-States Corridor highway as proposed; Kippenbrock supports added Budget Committee review and hopes the current pause brings "a better balance" [13, 12].
- The Mid-States Corridor is a local issue in the race; a September 2026 letter to the Dubois County Free Press asked candidates to state their positions for Martin, Daviess and Pike counties as well as Dubois [14].
- No debate or forum between the two nominees was found.

## Amy Kippenbrock

Amy Kippenbrock has been Dubois County Clerk since her 2018 election and chairs the Dubois County Republican Party [9]. Her stated priorities are election integrity, affordability and natural resources [12].

### Background

- Dubois County Clerk since her first election in 2018, reelected in 2022; led the county's move to vote centers and digital recordkeeping and was named Indiana's "Clerk of the Year" in 2020 and 2024 [9, 15].
- Elected Dubois County Republican Party chair in 2025 [9]. Completed Ball State University's Certificate in Election Administration, Technology, and Security program and has served on the Oversight Committee on Public Documents [15].
- Born in Martin County and raised in Jasper [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced in November 2025 after Lindauer's retirement announcement and filed in January 2026 [9, 15]. Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 6,838 votes (77.4%) to Richard Moss's 2,001 [11].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Lists strengthening election law as a top priority: "Hoosiers should have full confidence in the process" [12].

**Property taxes**

- Says SEA 1 is "a starting point that we must continue to refine"; wants "transparent reporting" on effects on schools and local services, possible formula changes, and review of whether the shift to local income taxes creates "unintended burdens" [12].

**Data centers**

- On siting solar, battery storage and data centers: "I believe local control is the best approach"; says communities should have "a meaningful voice" and the state should help protect farmland where appropriate [12].

**Transportation**

- Supports SB 27's requirement for State Budget Committee review of large projects like the Mid-States Corridor; says any path forward should show "meaningful, long-term benefits" for local communities [12].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says "Families across our district are feeling the pressure of rising everyday costs"; wants "quality jobs right here at home" and strong schools [12]. Lists support for small businesses and fiscal responsibility among her priorities [9].

**Natural resources**

- Calls the region's land and water "part of what makes this region strong" and supports both protection and tourism and recreation opportunities [12].

### On the current officeholder

- Open seat. Says the district "deserves steady, experienced leadership that understands how government works" [15]. No statements about Lindauer's record were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says a representative should be a strong advocate who listens and makes sure constituents' voices are heard in Indianapolis [12].

## Tiffanie Arthur

Tiffanie Arthur is a Daviess County small business owner from a farm family [16, 13]. Her campaign focuses on public schools, rural health care, energy costs and housing [17].

Campaign website: https://www.tiffaniearthur.com/

### Background

- Small business owner; has worked in local schools and coached children [13]. Lives in Daviess County [16].
- Says she has been involved in supporting local schools, youth programs and community organizations [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 1,071 votes (63.8%) over Tony Bolen (344) and Adam Mann (264) [11].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Agrees relief is needed but says "I don't think Senate Bill 1 got the balance right"; argues local income taxes move the burden "onto the same people in a different way" and wants more targeted relief [13].

**Education**

- Says "Strong public schools and responsible school choice can coexist, but only if we get the balance right" [13].
- Says a growing share of education funding goes to private school vouchers and that choices "should not come at the expense of the schools that educate the vast majority of Indiana's children"; supports more public school funding, teacher pay and career and technical education [17].

**Data centers**

- "I support a temporary moratorium on large-scale data center developments until we have clearer answers," with guardrails on water and energy use [13].
- Says businesses driving new energy demand, such as large data centers, should pay for the grid investments they require rather than existing ratepayers [17].

**Energy & utilities**

- Supports stronger transparency and oversight of utility rates, energy efficiency programs and a "balanced mix" of energy sources [17].

**Agriculture**

- Opposes letting solar, battery storage or data center projects bypass local zoning on farmland: "As part of a farm family, I understand how important that land is" [13].

**Transportation**

- "I oppose the Mid-States Corridor project as it is currently proposed," citing cost, farmland loss and local cost-sharing [13, 17].

**Health care**

- Supports fair Medicaid reimbursement for rural hospitals, rural health workforce incentives such as loan repayment, telehealth and expanded mental health services [17].

**Housing**

- Supports workforce housing, renovation of older homes, public-private partnerships and local control over housing decisions [17].

### On the current officeholder

- Open seat. Says "too many people in our communities feel like they're not being heard" and that rural communities "feel overlooked when decisions are made at the Statehouse" [13, 16]. No statements about Lindauer were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists the cost of living as a top priority: "The cost of living is at the top of that list" [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by the United Mine Workers of America [10, 18].

### What we couldn't verify

- Campaign finance totals not found.
- Search snippets describe her as a North Daviess native and county Democratic chair (local podcast) — unverified.
- Some local coverage spells her name 'Tiffany'; the ballot uses 'Tiffanie'.
- No position found on congressional redistricting.
