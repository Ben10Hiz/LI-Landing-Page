# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Greg Walker (Republican)

Sen. Greg Walker (R-Columbus) has represented District 41 since 2006 [3]. He initially planned to retire, then sought a fifth term after the congressional redistricting fight, and lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to state Rep. Michelle Davis, so he will not be on the November ballot [4, 5]. The Senate Republican caucus lists him as chair of the Family and Children Services Committee [3].

- Committee roles, per the Senate Republican caucus: chair of Family and Children Services; ranking member of Elections and Ethics; member of Pensions and Labor and Public Policy; chaired the 2023 Interim Study Committee on Child Services [3].
- December 2025: Was among the 21 Senate Republicans who opposed the mid-decade congressional redistricting plan, which the Senate rejected 31–19; he described the effort as federal overreach [4, 5].
- Voted against the 2022 law removing the handgun permit requirement, a 2025 bill allowing partisan school board elections and a 2022 bill barring transgender girls from girls' school sports; he points to his votes for the near-total abortion ban and for restricting gender-transition care for minors [4].
- After the primary, he urged Davis to "serve the people of the district. Serve them all, Republican, Democrat, Independent" [5].

## This race

- District 41 includes all of Bartholomew County and much of Johnson County [4, 3]. Bartholomew County cast about 62% of the votes in the 2022 primary and general elections for the seat [4].
- Open seat in November: incumbent Greg Walker lost renomination. In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Davis received 7,314 votes (58.8%) to Walker's 5,134 (41.2%), per Wikipedia's compilation; the AP called the race for Davis [6, 5].
- The primary centered on redistricting: Davis voted for the House plan and was endorsed by President Trump, while Walker opposed it; outside groups including a group aligned with U.S. Sen. Jim Banks, the Club for Growth and Americans for Prosperity backed Davis [4].
- Democrat Ross Thomas was unopposed for his party's nomination [4]. WFIU described the district as historically Republican [5].
- No general-election debate or forum between Davis and Thomas was found in reviewed coverage.

## Michelle Davis

Davis is a three-term state representative from Whiteland (House District 58, since 2020) and director of adult education at Central Nine Career Center who defeated Sen. Walker in the Republican primary [4, 5].

### Background

- Director of adult education at Central Nine Career Center in Greenwood and owner of a local outdoor storage lot, per the House Republican caucus [7].
- Played Division I basketball at Ball State University, where she earned an elementary education degree, and holds a master's in curriculum and instruction from Purdue [5].
- In the House, she is assistant majority floor leader and vice chair of the Education Committee and serves on Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development and Judiciary, per the caucus [7]. Lives in Whiteland [7].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Jan. 7, 2026 [8]; won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 58.8% over incumbent Greg Walker [6, 5].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted for the 2025 congressional redistricting bill in the House and said "the Senate should have stepped up and done the right thing for Hoosiers" [4]; argued the previous maps did not reflect how most Hoosiers vote [5].

**Education**

- Authored House Enrolled Act 1195 (2026), expanding eligibility for and extending the high school equivalency pilot program to mid-2028 [9].
- Authored House Enrolled Act 1041 (2025), barring transgender women from women's collegiate sports [10].

**Public safety**

- Co-sponsored Senate Bill 76 (2026), requiring local governments to comply with federal immigration detainer requests: "Public safety depends on cooperation at every level of government" [11].
- Authored a 2024 law requiring mental health resources after school armed-intruder drills and making school resource officers eligible for the 1977 Pension Fund [12].

**Economy & jobs**

- Authored 2023 laws doubling juror pay to $80 a day and allowing 25% of state adult education funding to go to integrated education and workforce training [13, 14].

### On the current officeholder

- Said her and Walker's voting records had "key differences" and that she would vote for what she believes Hoosier families support [4]. No statements about Thomas were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by President Trump in the primary [5, 4].
- Her campaign reported raising $115,000 for the Senate race as of late April 2026; pro-redistricting groups spent at least several hundred thousand dollars on ads supporting her [4].

### What we couldn't verify

- Her House votes on the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) and 2025 budget not verified.
- Campaign website not located; her campaign canceled a scheduled interview with the Capital Chronicle [4].

## Ross Thomas

Thomas is an attorney and chair of the Bartholomew County Democratic Party who was unopposed for the Democratic nomination; he previously ran for this seat in 2018 [6, 4].

### Background

- An attorney and chair of the Bartholomew County Democratic Party; was the Democratic candidate for this seat in 2018 and for Indiana House District 59 in 2022 and 2024, per Wikipedia [6].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Jan. 7, 2026 [8]; unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [4].
