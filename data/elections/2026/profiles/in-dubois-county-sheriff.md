## Current officeholder: Tom Kleinhelter (Republican)

Tom Kleinhelter, a Republican, has been Dubois County sheriff since January 1, 2019, and filed for a second term in January 2022; he was not eligible to run again in 2026 because of term limits [2, 3, 4].

- January 2019: Term as sheriff began [2].
- January 2022: In his re-election filing, Kleinhelter listed a new jail phone provider with video visitation, a renegotiated jail medical contract he said saves nearly $25,000 a year, an ongoing jail expansion, the Fresh Start counseling program for inmates with LifeSpring, dashboard cameras in road deputies' vehicles, a K-9 program, and an added school resource officer for the Southeast Dubois school system [3].
- July 2024: The State Board of Accounts filed a special compliance report on the jail commissary fund covering 2019–2023; it found internal-control deficiencies, including that expenditures outside the sheriff's discretionary authority were not approved in advance by the county council, and noted the council had formed a three-member committee in January 2024 to review commissary spending [2]. ## This race

- This is an open seat: Sheriff Kleinhelter is barred by term limits from seeking another term [4].
- Three Republicans sought the nomination in the May 5, 2026 primary: school resource officer Tim Lampert, Chief Deputy Jesus Monarrez and state trooper Brian LaRoche. In a Dubois County Free Press questionnaire, all three were asked about restoring trust and the use of public resources [4].
- Lampert won the Republican nomination with about 61% of the vote in uncertified results, ahead of Monarrez (about 25%) and LaRoche (about 14%) [5].
- Deputy Tim Rasche entered the race as an independent in July 2026 [6]. Both general-election candidates are longtime employees of the sheriff's department [6, 7].
- No general-election forum or debate between Lampert and Rasche was found in available coverage.

## Tim A. Rasche

Tim Rasche is a Dubois County deputy sheriff and field training officer who served as chief jailer from 2000 to 2004; he is running as an independent [6].

### Background

- Deputy sheriff with the Dubois County Sheriff's Department; his announcement says he has served the department for 32 years, including six years as a corrections officer and four years as chief jailer (2000–2004), and has been a field training officer for the past 10 years [6].
- U.S. Army veteran who also served in the Army Reserve and Indiana National Guard; studied law enforcement at Vincennes University [6].
- Member of the Fraternal Order of Police; has been drill instructor at the department's Kids Camp for 14 years [6].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced as an independent candidate in July 2026 [6]; listed on the general-election ballot as an independent [1].

### Positions & plans

**Jail & corrections**

- Says that as chief jailer from 2000 to 2004 he managed the jail during 'one of the most overcrowded periods' [6].

**Budget**

- Says he wants the office to operate 'as efficiently and effectively as possible while being a responsible steward of taxpayer dollars,' including pursuing every grant opportunity and evaluating how the department does business [6].

## Tim D. Lampert

Tim Lampert is a school resource officer with 35 years in the Dubois County Sheriff's Department, including eight years as chief deputy starting in 2011; he won the May 2026 Republican primary [7, 5].

### Background

- School resource officer with the Dubois County Sheriff's Department; started as a jail officer in 1990, became a road deputy in 1994, and served as chief deputy for eight years beginning in 2011 [7].
- Certified general instructor, firearms instructor and ALICE active-shooter instructor; licensed teacher through the Indiana Department of Education's Workplace Specialist program [8].
- Has served on the multi-agency emergency response team, the Substance Abuse Council and the 911 Advisory Board [8].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced in February 2025 and filed in January 2026 [8, 7].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with about 61% of the vote over Jesus Monarrez and Brian LaRoche (uncertified results) [5].

### Positions & plans

**Jail & corrections**

- Supports expanding substance-abuse treatment and programs such as RISE in the jail, and better coordination with community partners so people leaving jail are connected to services; said 'when individuals are released without a plan, they are more likely to reoffend' [4].

**Staffing & pay**

- Pledged ongoing training and cross-training and said 'I want every deputy and jail officer to know that I have their back' [4].

**Budget**

- Pledged to pursue state and federal grants to reduce local taxpayer costs [7].
- Said 'There will be no gray areas when it comes to misuse of public funds, equipment, or time,' with clear policies, oversight and accurate records [4].

**Drugs**

- Named drugs and mental health as top public-safety challenges and called for accountability paired with treatment and reentry support [4].

**School safety**

- Said 'I would like to add another School Resource Officer' and wants cross-training so deputies can cover schools [4].

**Transparency**

- Pledged that retaliation against people who report misconduct in good faith 'will not be tolerated,' with clear policies and multiple reporting channels [4].

### On the current officeholder

- In the primary questionnaire, said trust needs to be rebuilt internally and with the public after recent negative publicity; he did not name Sheriff Kleinhelter [4].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Expand Coffee with a Cop to all levels of staff and make deputies visible at community events [4].
- Work with groups such as the Justice Reinvestment Advisory Committee, the Dubois County Community Foundation and CARES on addiction and mental-health programs for people after incarceration [8].
