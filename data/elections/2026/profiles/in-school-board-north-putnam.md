# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The district: North Putnam Community School Corporation, with its central office in Bainbridge, serves northern Putnam County through four schools: Bainbridge Elementary, Roachdale Elementary, North Putnam Middle School and North Putnam High School [24, 25]. Federal data list 1,370 students in the 2024-25 school year [25]. Dr. Dustin LeMay is superintendent and Dr. Rodney Simpson is assistant superintendent [24].
- The board has seven members elected to four-year terms: six are elected from their township of residence (Clinton, Floyd, Franklin, Jackson, Monroe and Russell) and one at large [26]. Only the Clinton, Floyd and Jackson township seats are on the 2026 ballot, one seat each, matching the state list's 'vote for 1' [26, 27, 6].
- Current members, per the district: Joanie Knapp (president, Jackson), Nathan Scott (vice president, Franklin), Zac Williams (secretary, Russell), Randy Bee (Monroe), Maria Brewer (Clinton), Ron Spencer (Floyd) and Travis Lambermont (at large) [26].
- Clinton Township: Maria Brewer, appointed by the board on April 16, 2026 by a 4–1 vote (Nathan Scott voting no) to fill a vacancy created by a resignation, for a term ending Dec. 31, 2026, is the only candidate [28, 27]. David McCollough won the Clinton seat in 2022 and was still listed as a member in the March 2026 minutes [29, 30].
- Floyd Township: incumbent Ron Spencer, who won a third term in 2022 by 18 votes, did not file; Scott Miller is the only candidate, so the seat will change hands [29, 27, 26].
- Jackson Township: incumbent and board president Joanie Knapp faces Roger H. Busch II, a rematch of 2022, when Knapp won 1,379–1,155 [29, 27].
- Board turnover in 2026: the board accepted Heather Lawson's resignation in January 2026 and appointed Randy Bee to the Monroe Township seat on Feb. 12, 2026 by a 3–1 vote; Travis Lambermont joined as a new member in January 2026 [31, 32].
- Party labels: 2026 is the first year Indiana school board candidates could list a party on the ballot; Maria Brewer filed as a Republican and the other three candidates did not list a party [27, 6].
- Facilities (2025–2027): the district is carrying out a roughly $34 million renovation program, including HVAC replacement, pool ventilation, paving and parking, a new fieldhouse and athletic field upgrades, with full completion scheduled for summer 2027; LeMay said in March 2025 the project would not increase tax rates [33]. In 2023 the district's financial adviser told the board that falling debt payments would let the district take on the project while keeping its tax rate steady [34].
- Budget (2026): the board approved advertising its 2027 budget, capital projects plan and bus replacement plan in August 2026, with a public hearing set for Sept. 17 and adoption scheduled for Oct. 15, 2026 [35, 36]. In January 2026 the business manager reported $110,000 in interest earned on certificates of deposit [31].
- Academics (January 2026): the board voted 5–0 to adopt Indiana's new diploma early, with the new track optional for 2026-27 and 2027-28 and requiring parent consent; LeMay reported a 98.97% graduation rate for the Class of 2025 [31].
- Preschool (March 2026): the board approved a new full-day, tuition-based community preschool program, which LeMay said would not be state-funded; proposed rates were $135 per week for families and $125 for staff [30].
- Cell phones and safety (2026): in July 2026 the board adopted updated Chapter 6 policies for Indiana's new 'bell-to-bell' cell phone law, and the 2026-27 high school handbook requires phones to be powered off in lockers all day [37, 38]. The September 2026 agenda included a school resource officer agreement with the Town of Bainbridge [39].
- Board pay (January 2026): members discussed whether to raise the $2,000 annual board salary, and Lambermont raised meeting attendance; the board kept the salary at $2,000 [31].
- Superintendent turnover: Carrie Milner served as interim superintendent in fall 2023 after the administration of Nicole Allee, and LeMay has led the district since at least January 2024 [34, 40].
- Forums: no 2026 candidate forum or questionnaire for North Putnam was found. In 2022 the Banner Graphic published candidate-written biographies [41].

## Joanie Knapp — Jackson Township

Incumbent and board president, seeking a third term for Jackson Township; she is a retired North Putnam teacher [26, 29].

### Background

- Retired teacher who taught 34 years in the corporation, at Roachdale Elementary (first, fourth, fifth and sixth grades) and as an eighth-grade social studies teacher at North Putnam Middle School, according to her 2022 biography [41].
- North Putnam graduate (1978) with a bachelor's degree in education and a Master of Arts in teaching from DePauw University [41].
- Listed community service on the North Putnam Alumni Association board, as a 21st Century Scholars recruiter, on the Putnam County Dollars for Scholars board and on the Putnam County Community Foundation Lilly Scholarship selection committee [41].
- Board president in 2023 and again in 2026, and vice president in 2025; serves on the board's collective bargaining committee [40, 43, 31].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed by the June 18, 2026 deadline without a party label [27, 6].
- Won the Jackson seat for a second term in 2022, defeating Roger Busch 1,379–1,155 [29].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Wrote in 2022 that 'it is crucial for our corporation to be financially responsible and accountable, using our funds strategically' [41].
- After the 2022 election said, 'We do our best to be financially responsible and provide the best education possible for our students' [29].

**Academics**

- Wrote in 2022 that 'all students deserve a quality education with the most engaging curriculum possible' and that her primary focus is students' academic experience and well-being [41].
- When the board adopted the early new-diploma plan in January 2026, she said she likes how work-based learning is built into it [31].

**Curriculum & library policy**

- When the board approved discarding 769 old library books at Roachdale Elementary in May 2026, she asked whether they could be recycled [42].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. In 2026 board comments she praised new elementary makerspaces, facility work and staff, and said she was excited about the new preschool program [30, 28, 35].

### How they say they'd run the office

- In 2022 said she looked forward to 'helping our students and parents to make North Putnam the best that it can be' [29].

## Roger H Busch, II — Jackson Township

Challenger and former North Putnam High School athletic director (2017–2021) who lost to Knapp for this seat in 2022 [41, 29].

### Background

- North Putnam graduate (1992) who attended Wabash College from 1992 to 1996 and coached track at Wabash from 2005 to 2015, according to his 2022 biography [41].
- Was North Putnam High School's athletic director from 2017 to 2021; he wrote that in that job he secured increases in stipends for coaches and sponsors that had been flat for more than 15 years [41].
- In 2022 described himself as a parent of three daughters in the district [41].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed by the June 18, 2026 deadline without a party label [27, 6].
- Ran for the Jackson seat in 2022 and received 1,155 votes to Knapp's 1,379 [29].

### Positions & plans

**Transparency & community input**

- Wrote in 2022 that the district should listen 'to teachers, families and community members when determining policies' and be 'transparent and honest, about our school district’s successes and shortcomings' [41].

**Teachers & staff**

- Cited, in 2022, his work as athletic director to raise long-stagnant extracurricular stipends [41].

### On the current officeholder

- In 2022 wrote that 'many aspects of our school district are working well' but 'there are some problems that need to be addressed,' and called for 'holding leadership accountable'; he did not specify the problems in that biography [41].

### How they say they'd run the office
