# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Lanesville Community School Corporation serves Franklin Township in Harrison County. Its board candidates appear on the ballot only in Franklin Township precincts 08-12 [24, 25]. It has two schools, Lanesville Elementary and Lanesville Junior-Senior High. NCES data show 733 students in 2024-25 (400 elementary, 333 high school) [26]. The superintendent is Dr. Ryan Apple [27].
- The board has five members, elected at large in staggered years: two seats were on the 2022 ballot and three in 2024 [28, 29]. Current members: Ryan Lind (president), Sharon Rothrock (vice president), Tim Bridges (secretary), Donnie Hussung and Margaret Meyer [30].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: two at-large seats. The state list shows 'vote for 1', but the county candidate list and the Franklin Township sample ballot both say 'vote for not more than two' [6, 24, 25]. These are the seats won in 2022 by Ryan Lind (719 votes) and Tara S. Smith (612 votes) [28].
- Neither 2026 seat has an incumbent on the ballot. The Corydon Democrat's candidate list marked no Lanesville candidate as an incumbent [31]. Lind is not on the ballot [6]. Smith announced at the July 21, 2026 meeting that she had bought a home outside the county and would leave the board [32]. On Aug. 4 the board voted 3-1 to appoint Margaret Meyer to serve through Dec. 31, 2026, and made Tim Bridges secretary [33]. Meyer was a 2022 board candidate [28]. She is not on the 2026 ballot [6].
- All three candidates are listed as Republicans [24, 6]. John T. (Tom) Walter announced in September that he is no longer an active candidate, but his name stays on the ballot [34]. In practice, Keith Baird and Doug Burbrink are the two active candidates for two seats [34].
- Building capacity: in February 2026 the board looked at a referendum for a new elementary school. A 'bare bones' project would cost about $45 million and roughly double property taxes for district homeowners [35]. At a Feb. 11 public work session Apple presented tax-rate estimates for $45 million, $55 million and $65 million projects. He also described options to add space at the current campus, including a first mortgage bond of up to about $5.46 million (1% of assessed value) [36]. On Feb. 17 the board agreed to drop the referendum and move forward with improvements to the existing school. Hussung said the tax impact was too high and building should wait, and Bridges said plans would be drawn up [37, 38].
- Air quality: an Aug. 31, 2026 Indiana Department of Health inspection, done after a citizen complaint, found no mold problem. It found carbon dioxide above the state limit in every room measured, and the department recommended increasing outdoor air [39, 40]. At the Sept. 15 meeting Apple said every classroom will get a CO2 sensor, with readings posted for parents. Hussung suggested bidding out a complete HVAC replacement rather than repairing the 36-year-old unit, which could cost about $1.5 million. The Corydon Democrat reported that the corporation could bond up to about $4 million under its current obligations [40].
- 2027 budget: the proposed budget includes an education fund of $7,861,625, an operations fund of $3,206,940, a debt service fund of $763,750 and a referendum fund of $550,000. The board is expected to adopt it on Oct. 20, 2026 [40, 41]. The budget notice estimates $140,900 in property tax cap credits for 2027 [41].
- Other 2026 actions: the board hired Stephanie Tostaine as elementary principal after April Holder resigned [42, 43]. It approved 2026-27 teacher contracts and a memorandum of understanding with the Harrison County Sheriff's Department for a school reserve officer [32].
- No reporting was found on SEA 1's specific effect on Lanesville. No candidate forum was found; the Corydon Democrat published questionnaires from all three candidates on Sept. 9, 2026 [44].

## Doug Burbrink — At Large

Retired Indiana Farm Bureau agency manager running for an open at-large seat; he ran for this board once before, in 2000 [45].

### Background

- Retired after 43 years with Indiana Farm Bureau as agency manager for Harrison, Floyd, Crawford and Orange counties; part-time agent for Reedy Insurance [45].
- Graduate of Seymour High School (1976); attended IU Southeast [45].
- Previously served on the St. John's Lutheran School Board [45].
- Age 68 [45].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed as a Republican on the county and state candidate lists [24, 6].
- Ran for the Lanesville Community School Board in 2000 [45].

### Positions & plans

**Transparency & community input**

- Says it is important to have "accountability, responsibility and clear transparency" among parents, teachers, administration, the school board and taxpayers [45].

**School safety**

- Says his top priority would be supporting students "in a way that is conducive in learning and excelling in a safe environment" [45].

**Budget & taxes**

- Lists creating and managing operational budgets in his career as a qualification [45].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements found about the current board's decisions.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Top priority: support students in a safe learning environment and improve accountability, responsibility and transparency among parents, teachers, administrators and the community [45].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Walter, who stopped campaigning, said in a Facebook post that he supports both other candidates "100%" [34].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website or finance filing found.

## John T (Tom) Walter — At Large

Former Lanesville town council member; announced in September 2026 that he is no longer an active candidate, though his name stays on the ballot [46, 34].

### Background

- Retired after 25 years with Fiserv Inc.; semi-retired and works part time as a laborer for the Town of Lanesville [46].
- Lanesville High School graduate (1976); computer science studies at Computer Career Center [46].
- Has grandchildren attending Lanesville schools [46].
- Age 68 [46].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed as a Republican on the county and state candidate lists [24, 6].
- Served as an at-large Lanesville town council member from 2020 to 2026. He says he stepped aside to avoid a conflict of interest with his town laborer job and to run for this office [46].
- After his questionnaire ran on Sept. 9, he announced on Facebook that he would no longer be an active candidate, citing a non-life-threatening health issue. The July 15 withdrawal deadline had passed, so his name remains on the ballot [34].

### Positions & plans

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Names campus growth as a main concern, given Franklin Township's expected population growth. Wants steps to prevent overcrowding from out-of-district attendance [46].
- Says he believes "every child should have the opportunity to attend the school of their choice," but that the board and administration must manage it to stay within building and staffing limits [46].

**Facilities**

- Calls for analysis of needed facilities before growth decisions are made [46].

**Transparency & community input**

- Proposes a workshop-style planning session that would bring parents, staff, administration and local and state government into oversight and financial planning [46].

### On the current officeholder

- Says the board needs members willing to speak up and not be "just another 'yes' member," and that the board must "challenge and correct ineffective decisions" [46].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Workshop-style planning session with parents, staff, administrators and government for facility and financial planning [46].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Said of the other two candidates: "Both of the other gentlemen in this race are more than qualified as candidates and will do an excellent job and I support them 100%" [34].

### What we couldn't verify

- His questionnaire says he served 10 years as the town's chief law enforcement officer; no details were found.
- No finance filing found.
- Unclear whether he would serve if elected.

## Keith Baird — At Large

Parent of four Lanesville students and a first-time candidate, running for an open at-large seat [47].

### Background

- Retired from Norfolk Southern Railway in 2004 [47].
- Clarksville High School graduate (1997) [47].
- Parent of four daughters at Lanesville Community School. Volunteers with the PTSO and school events, and has volunteered in classrooms and on field trips [47].
- Says he has regularly attended Lanesville school board meetings for the past six years [47].
- Age 47 [47].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed as a Republican on the county and state candidate lists [24, 6].
- First-time candidate for public office [47].

### Positions & plans

**Transparency & community input**

- Wants to strengthen communication between the board and the public and to "bridge the gap" between the community, board members and administrators [47].
- Says the community "deserves to understand not only what decisions are being made, but why they are being made" [47].

**Budget & taxes**

- Says he will weigh the long-term impact of financial decisions and ask questions to make sure resources go to uses that best benefit students and teachers [47].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements found about specific board decisions. His stated top priority is "building greater trust" among the board, administration, teachers, parents and the community [47].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Better communication and transparency; parents, teachers and residents should "feel heard"; careful review of spending [47].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Walter, who stopped campaigning, said in a Facebook post that he supports both other candidates "100%" [34].

### What we couldn't verify

- No statements found on the facility-capacity decision, the air-quality issue, the 2027 budget or SEA 1.
- No campaign website or finance filing found.
