# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Chris May (Republican)

Chris May, a Republican from Bedford, was elected to the Indiana House in 2016 and chairs the House Local Government Committee; he also serves on the Roads and Transportation and Financial Institutions committees, according to the House Republican caucus [3]. He previously served three terms as a Lawrence County commissioner [3].

- December 2022: Appointed chair of the House Local Government Committee; reappointed for the 2025 session, according to the caucus [3, 4].
- 2023: Co-authored legislation phasing in a state income-tax exemption for active-duty military pay, and sponsored a bill easing licensing for out-of-state military families, according to the caucus [3].
- February 2024: Co-authored the 13th check for public retirees, which the House approved unanimously [3].
- On April 10, 2025, voted for SB 1, the property-tax and local government finance bill, which passed the House 65–29 [5]. On April 25, 2025, voted for the conference report on HEA 1001, the 2025–2027 state budget, adopted 66–27 [6].
- December 5, 2025: Voted for HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57–41 [7].
- February 19, 2026: Voted to concur in Senate changes to HB 1002, the electric utility affordability bill (94–2) [8].
- January 28, 2026: His HB 1354, on employee health plans for county elected officials, passed the House 95–2 and was referred to the Senate Local Government Committee; no further action is listed [9].
- February 2026: A concurrent resolution he sponsored urging that part of State Highway 558 be renamed "Commodore Crane Highway" passed the House [3].
- June 2026: Highlighted new laws on housing supply (HEA 1001), K-12 regulation (HEA 1004), employer child care tax credits (HEA 1177) and farm and local vendor sales (HEA 1424), saying "Housing affordability starts with availability" [10].

## This race

- House District 65 covers all of Lawrence County and parts of Jackson, Orange and Washington counties in south-central Indiana, according to the House Republican caucus [3]. (Older news descriptions that place the district in Brown, Monroe or Johnson counties reflect a previous map.)
- May has held the seat since 2016 and was re-elected without opposition in 2024, according to Wikipedia [11]. WBIW reported in January 2026 that May filed for re-election [12].
- There is no Democratic nominee; Sean S. Steele appears on the ballot as an independent [2, 13].
- No debate or forum between the two candidates was found as of October 10, 2026.
- Steele's campaign site emphasizes affordability, public school funding, rural hospitals, capping assessed-value increases and opposing AI data centers in rural areas; May has not posted a campaign website, according to The Ballot Brief [13, 14].

## Chris D. May

The Republican incumbent, in the House since 2016 and chair of its Local Government Committee [3].

### Background

- Has worked in manufacturing management, engineering and design at Ford, Cook, General Motors, Otis Elevator and Whitney Tool Company, and owns Hoosier Tech Properties, a residential housing business, according to the caucus [3].
- Served three terms as a Lawrence County commissioner and on boards including the Lawrence County Economic Growth Council and Dunn Memorial Hospital [3].
- Graduated from Bedford North Lawrence High School and Vincennes University; lives in Bedford [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for re-election in January 2026 [12]; Wikipedia lists him as the only declared candidate in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [11].

### Positions & plans

**Housing**

- Said of the 2026 housing law: "Housing affordability starts with availability" [10].

**Education**

- Said reducing school regulations "allows teachers and schools to focus on what matters most" [10].

## Sean S. Steele

Sean Steele, an independent, is a Lawrence County native, attorney and longtime teacher and coach, according to his campaign site [13].

Campaign website: https://www.steele4indiana.com

### Background

- Graduated from Bedford North Lawrence High School in 1986 and Indiana University School of Law in 1994, according to his campaign [13].
- Describes himself as a 30-year educator and coach who has taught at Orleans Jr./Sr. High, Oolitic Middle School and currently Bedford Middle School [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Appears on the Indiana Election Division's general-election candidate list as an independent [2]. His site says, "I'm an Independent candidate. I will be on the ballot in November." [13]

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Wants to promote local businesses and entrepreneurs and increase access to affordable child care, housing and utilities [13].

**Education**

- Supports fair and equitable public school funding and vocational training [13].

**Health care**

- Wants to keep local hospitals open, pursue affordable coverage, and expand OB and mental health resources [13].

**Property taxes**

- Would cap increases in assessed property values so families and farmers are not taxed out of their homes [13].

**Data centers**

- Opposes AI data centers and Flock surveillance in rural communities [13].

### On the current officeholder

- Does not name May; his site says "our elected leaders seem absent and unconcerned" and pledges to show up, listen and respond to residents [13].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he is running as an independent to be "a candidate for ALL of the citizens of our district" [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign site; his committee is Steele4Indiana [13].

### What we couldn't verify

- No news coverage of his campaign was found.
- How he qualified for the ballot (petition signatures) was not confirmed.
- No fundraising totals found.
