# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Elizabeth Rowray (Republican)

Elizabeth Rowray, a Yorktown nonprofit consultant and former Yorktown school board member, has represented House District 35 since 2020 [3]. She serves on the Ways and Means; Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development; and Family, Children and Human Affairs committees [3, 4].

- Committees: Ways and Means; Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development; Family, Children and Human Affairs [3]. 2025 interim appointments: Indiana Protection and Advocacy Services Commission, Doula Reimbursement Advisory Board and the Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. Holiday Commission [5].
- 2023 session: Authored HB 1568 to let pharmacists prescribe hormonal contraceptives to women 18 and older after a self-screening; the House Public Health Committee approved it in February 2023 [6].
- 2025 session: Authored HEA 1248, which expands Child Care and Development Fund eligibility for foster families and reserves 200 vouchers for children with licensed foster parents [7]. Sponsored SEA 491, letting Alexandria annex noncontiguous land for an industrial park [8], and SB 498 on qualifications for DCS casework supervisors [9].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [10]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [11].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [12].
- 2026 session: Authored HEA 1357, requiring staff at child caring institutions, group homes and child placing agencies to undergo federal background checks every four years; ceremonially signed in May 2026 [13].

## This race

- House District 35 includes parts of Delaware and Madison counties [3]; the Herald Bulletin describes it as including most of northern Madison County [14].
- The race is a rematch of 2024, when Rowray defeated Gift with 71% of the vote [14, 15].
- Rowray and Gift were the only candidates listed in their parties' May 5, 2026 primaries [15]; both are on the November ballot [2].
- Key differences: Gift opposes expanding private school vouchers, wants an end to partisan gerrymandering and supports legalizing adult-use marijuana [16, 17]; Rowray voted for the 2025 budget and the 2025 redistricting bill [11, 12]. Gift says he opposes data center development [17]; no statement from Rowray on data centers was found.
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Elizabeth Rowray

Elizabeth Rowray is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2020 [3]. Her recent bills have focused on child welfare, foster care and child care [7, 13].

### Background

- Nonprofit consultant; lives in Yorktown; graduated from Muncie Burris High School and Ball State University [3].
- Former legislative director for U.S. Rep. Mark Souder; worked at the Muncie-Delaware County Economic Development Alliance; served on the Yorktown school board [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for a fourth term in January 2026 [14]; listed as the only Republican candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [15].

### Positions & plans

**Child care & families**

- Said of HEA 1248: "Child care costs should never stand in the way of families who are willing to foster" [18].
- Said of HEA 1357: "Families deserve confidence that children placed in state-regulated care are surrounded by qualified and trustworthy adults" [13].

**Health care**

- Said pharmacist-prescribed contraceptives "will greatly expand access to thousands of Hoosier women" (2023) [6].

**Economy & jobs**

- Said SEA 491 "could be a game changer" for Alexandria's economic development [8]; the caucus says she scored above 90% on the Indiana Chamber's 2025 scorecard [19].

**Property taxes**

- Voted for SEA 1 (2025) [10]; the caucus listed it among the bills on the Chamber scorecard [19].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Rowray about Gift were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- After the 2026 session, said her focus was "delivering results for our district" through tax relief and more efficient government [20].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- The House Republican caucus says Rowray scored above 90% on the Indiana Chamber of Commerce's 2025 legislator scorecard [19].

### What we couldn't verify

- Final outcome of HB 1568 (2023) and SB 498 (2025) not confirmed.

## Phil G. Gift

Phil Gift is a retired Veterans Affairs nurse from Gaston, running as a Democrat for the second straight cycle [21, 22]. He focuses on public school funding, rural health care, opposition to data centers and legalizing marijuana [17].

Campaign website: https://www.phil4house35.com

### Background

- Registered nurse who spent 17 years with the Department of Veterans Affairs, including as a triage nurse at the Marion VA Medical Center and patient care manager at the Muncie VA clinic [22].
- Raised in Fairmount; degrees from Vincennes University, the University of Cincinnati and Ball State University [22]. Lives in Gaston [21].
- Was the Democratic nominee for this seat in 2024 [21, 23].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed in January 2026 [14]; listed as the only Democratic candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [15].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Says Indiana "should fully fund its public schools and stop expanding taxpayer-funded private school vouchers" [16].

**Data centers**

- Wants to protect farmland and water from "unchecked data center development" that he says offers few long-term jobs while receiving large tax incentives [17].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says residential customers "shouldn't subsidize massive new energy demands"; supports more transparency in utility rate cases and stronger IURC oversight [16].

**Health care**

- Wants to keep rural hospitals open and expand access to affordable insurance [17].

**Housing**

- Supports workforce-housing construction, fewer building barriers and rehabilitation of aging homes [16].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Wants to "end partisan gerrymandering" [17].

**Other**

- Supports legalizing, regulating and taxing adult-use marijuana with expungement review for low-level possession [16].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about Rowray or her record were found; Gift says he is "determined to bring fresh leadership and real change to our community" [21].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Top priorities: "securing strong, well-funded public education for every child, access to affordable health care and housing, and providing a living wage for all Hoosiers" [21].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- In 2024 the Indiana Democratic Party chair praised Gift's VA experience when promoting his candidacy [23]. No 2026 endorsements or fundraising found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Little news coverage of the 2026 race; WFYI's District 35 guide was not found.
- The Herald Bulletin article also says Gift ran against Rowray in 2022; other sources reviewed mention only 2024. Unresolved.
- Middle initial "G." from the ballot list only; age not verified.
