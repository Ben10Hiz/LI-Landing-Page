# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The 'Grant Marion Community School Board' races on the state list are for Marion Community Schools in Marion, Grant County. Federal data reported through PlainSchools show 3,668 students in 2024-25 and seven schools [24]. The district also runs the Little Giants Preschool, the Grant Regional Career Center and the Walton Performing Arts Center [25, 26].
- The superintendent is Dr. Stephanie Lockwood [27]. The board approved her contract in December 2024, and the Chronicle-Tribune reported that she would start Jan. 4, 2025, replacing retiring superintendent Keith Burke [28]. On July 28, 2026, the board voted unanimously to extend her contract from June 30, 2028 to June 30, 2030 [27].
- The board has seven members who serve four-year terms, with elections every two years [29]. The district lists the members as Alan Beck (president, District I), Chuck Griffin (first vice president, District IV at-large), Shanna Bento (second vice president, District II), Gabe Delgado (secretary, District IV at-large), Dana Gault (District I), Richard Hart (District I) and Serafina Salamo (District III) [30].
- District 1 race: three seats, not one. The state list says 'vote for 1' [6], but the Chronicle-Tribune's ballot list says 'District 1 (vote for 3)' [31]. The district lists three District I members whose terms end Dec. 31, 2026: Beck, Gault and Hart [30]. Beck and Hart are running again; Gault is not on the ballot [6, 31]. Four candidates are running for the three seats [6, 31]. In 2022 the Chronicle-Tribune also described District 1 as a three-seat race [32].
- At-large race: one seat. The state list and the Chronicle-Tribune call it 'At Large, District 2' [6, 31]. The district's roster labels its at-large seats 'District IV (at-large)', and the at-large seat with a term ending Dec. 31, 2026 belongs to Chuck Griffin [30]. Griffin was appointed to the at-large seat in 2022 and was a candidate for it that November [33, 32]. He is not on the 2026 ballot, so the seat is open [6, 31].
- Budget and the 2025 property-tax law (July 2026): on July 14, 2026, the board unanimously approved moving $97,460.15 from the Debt Service Fund to the Operations Fund to account for the supplemental homestead credit [26]. Superintendent Lockwood told the board that the credit is money 'we will lose beginning in 2027' and described it as about a $300 loss per household [26].
- 2027 budget (August-October 2026): on Aug. 11, 2026, the board moved its budget dates after the former chief financial officer left, and it approved a contract with Comprehensive Systems of Support for Schools to handle chief financial officer duties [34]. The new calendar set a public hearing for Sept. 22 and adoption for Oct. 27, 2026 [34]. At the Aug. 25 meeting, President Beck said the district was 'having to cut 3.7 million dollars' [35]. Marion Community Schools is not on the state's list of districts with a referendum in November 2026 [36]. Two other Grant County districts, Mississinewa and Oak Hill, have operating referendums on the ballot [31].
- Facilities (August 2026): on Aug. 11, 2026, the board unanimously approved lease amendments through its building corporation to fund improvements in 2026-27 and 2027-28 [34]. Bond counsel said the improvements would focus on heating and air conditioning, lighting, flooring and wall coverings, with combined annual lease payments of no more than $3.6 million [34]. On Aug. 25 the board heard updates on storm damage to the Bill Green Athletic Arena, a replacement stadium scoreboard, the tennis courts, the track and lighting at the Walton Performing Arts Center [35].
- Curriculum and policy (2026): in September 2026 the board approved new curriculum, including Amplify CKLA for K-6 English language arts [37]. Revisions to the computer acceptable-use policy were discussed on Sept. 8 and held for a later meeting [37]. Collective-bargaining hearings with the Marion Teachers Association began on the same date [37].
- Forums: this guide found no 2026 candidate forum or questionnaire for these races. The Chronicle-Tribune printed a candidate list on Oct. 3, 2026 [31].

## Alan M Beck — District 1

Incumbent and board president, seeking another term in District 1, where voters choose three members [30, 31].

### Background

- The district lists his occupation as 'Landscape designer, business owner of Comfort Landscapes' and says he joined the board in 2019 [30].
- In a 2022 Chronicle-Tribune questionnaire he described himself as a local business owner and lifelong Marion resident, and he said he had coached for 19 years [32].

### Path to the ballot

- Was elected in District 1 in 2018 and 2022, according to BallotReady [38].
- Filed for reelection; The Indiana Citizen's copy of the state list gives June 4, 2026 as his filing date [39]. The state lists him as Nonpartisan [6].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- At the July 14, 2026 meeting, discussing the homestead-credit transfer, he said: 'This SB1 is killing us in so many ways.' [26]
- At the same meeting, Dr. Richard Hart asked whether the district could earn money from its facilities. Beck said the district should not have to 'come up with creative ways of making revenue to fund our operations' and called it 'a pipe dream' that facility rentals could pay for teacher salaries and buses [26].
- On Aug. 25, 2026, he said the district was 'having to cut 3.7 million dollars.' He urged caution on the cost of a new stadium scoreboard and said the public may face 'possibly having a referendum years from now.' [35]
- On the same date he said that in 2025 the state allowed 'a half a billion dollars of tax-funded money to go to private and charter schools,' and he urged residents to talk to legislators about public schools [35].

**Academics**

- In 2022 he said he wanted the board to focus on 'finding ways to lift up those that may have unfortunately fallen through the cracks.' He also called for more career-oriented learning [32].
- In July 2026 he praised the Grant Regional Career Center's programs and said the district should 'keep investing in that' [26].

**Facilities**

- On Aug. 25, 2026, he said the stadium scoreboard replacement should cover the next 20 years without 'putting too much into it at a time when we're struggling.' [35]

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member and the board's president [30]. In 2022 he said the board's relationships with teachers, administrators and superintendents gave it 'even more opportunity to put in opinions and help guide the direction of MCS.' [32]
- In August 2026 he thanked the transportation staff for the start of the school year [34].

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports were found.
