# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Craig Haggard (Republican)

Craig Haggard, a Mooresville commercial broker and retired Marine and Air National Guard pilot, has represented House District 57 since 2022 [3]. He did not seek reelection and instead challenged U.S. Rep. Jim Baird in the 4th Congressional District Republican primary, losing 60.5% to 30.5% on May 5, 2026 [4, 5].

- Committees: Environmental Affairs; Roads and Transportation; Veterans Affairs and Public Safety [3].
- 2026: Authored HEA 1184 on towing practices and signage [6]; supported HEA 1210, which raised the property-tax deduction for totally disabled veterans to 100% of assessed value [7].
- Voted no on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [8], and on the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [9]; voted yes on HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [10].
- In a February 2026 column on his tenure, said he focused on "serving veterans, bolstering public safety, protecting homeowners and upholding the 2nd Amendment" [11].

## This race

- House District 57 lies just southwest of Indianapolis and includes parts of Hendricks, Johnson and Morgan counties, including Mooresville [3, 12].
- Open seat: Haggard ran for Congress instead of reelection [5]; he lost the 4th District Republican primary to Rep. Jim Baird [4]. Haggard was reelected unopposed in 2024 [13].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Wes Bennett won a four-way race with 1,451 votes (31.2%) to Greg Knott's 1,372 (29.5%); a recount Knott requested in Hendricks County found no change, and the Indiana Recount Commission certified Bennett 3-0 in July [12]. Rob Stiles and Tina Turner also ran [5]. Fortenberry was the only Democrat [5].
- The Indiana Capital Chronicle describes the district as heavily Republican [12].
- Key differences: Bennett, a former state utility regulator and property-tax administrator, lists property tax reform, abortion restrictions and gun rights among his priorities [14]; no published policy positions from Fortenberry were found.
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Wes Bennett

Wes Bennett is a former Plainfield clerk-treasurer who led the state Department of Local Government Finance and later served on the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission [12]. He won the Republican primary by 79 votes after a recount [12].

Campaign website: https://wesbennettfordistrict57.com/

### Background

- Commissioner of the Department of Local Government Finance under Gov. Eric Holcomb, then appointed to the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission in 2023; resigned from the IURC in October 2025 before launching his campaign [12].
- Served twelve years as Plainfield clerk-treasurer and has private-sector experience in banking, insurance and investments, according to his campaign [14].
- Member of the American Legion, Fraternal Order of Police, Plainfield Optimist and the Plainfield and Mooresville chambers of commerce [14].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Jan. 8, 2026 [15]; won the May 5 Republican primary with 31.2% over Greg Knott, Rob Stiles and Tina Turner, confirmed by recount [12, 5].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Lists "Continuing to Fight for Property Tax Reform" among his priorities and cites six years leading the agency that oversees Indiana's property tax and assessment systems [14].

**Abortion**

- Lists "Protecting the Unborn" among his priorities [14].

**Guns**

- Lists "Defending the Second Amendment" among his priorities [14].

**Government spending**

- Says he would protect taxpayers and advance "affordable and sustainable government spending" through data-driven decisions [14].

**Economy & jobs**

- Lists working for small businesses and jobs, supporting farmers and backing first responders among his priorities [14].

### On the current officeholder

### How they say they'd run the office

- His campaign describes a goal of "transparent governance, reliable and affordable services, and responsible fiscal management" [14].

## Suzanne Fortenberry

Suzanne Fortenberry is the Democratic nominee; she was the party's 2024 nominee for state Senate District 36 [5, 16]. Few details on her 2026 campaign were found.

### Background

- Described in 2024 as a courier and executive director of Greenwood Pride [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Jan. 7, 2026 [15]; the only Democrat in the May 5 primary [5].
- Lost the 2024 Senate District 36 race to Sen. Cyndi Carrasco, 62.5% to 37.5% [16].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Fortenberry about Haggard's record, the seat or Bennett were found.

### Endorsements & fundraising

- iVoterGuide lists endorsements from Better Indiana and the Indiana Reproductive Freedom PAC [17].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website or published policy positions were found; her Facebook page (Fortenberry4Indiana) could not be read.
