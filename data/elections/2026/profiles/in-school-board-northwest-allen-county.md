# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Northwest Allen County Schools (NACS) serves northwest Allen County north of Fort Wayne, with one high school; a third middle school, Willow Creek, was scheduled to open in August 2026 [24]. Wayne Barker is superintendent [25, 24]. A current districtwide enrollment figure could not be confirmed from a fetched source.
- The board has five members who serve four-year terms [26]. The district lists Liz Hathaway (president), Kristi Schlatter (vice president), Darren Vogt (secretary), Erica Jamison and Liz Chaffee [27]. Jamison and Chaffee were elected in 2024 [28].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: District 3, held by Schlatter, and the at-large seats held by Hathaway and Vogt, who were the top two finishers for two at-large seats in 2022 [29, 30]. The Allen County Election Board's list, as reported by The Journal Gazette, shows Schlatter (District 3), Vogt (at large, Republican) and Hathaway (at large, Republican) as the only NACS candidates, all incumbents [31].
- Seat count: the state list shows the at-large race (labeled District 4) as vote for 1 with two candidates [6]. Both at-large incumbents' terms end in 2026, and The Journal Gazette describes both as unchallenged incumbents [31, 29], which indicates two at-large seats are being filled. If so, all three NACS seats on the ballot are uncontested.
- Filing: newcomer Matthew Cohee also filed for an at-large seat in June 2026 [30], but he is not on the October candidate list [31] or the state list [6].
- Party labels: 2026 is the first year Allen County school board candidates could declare a party; Hathaway and Vogt filed as Republicans and Schlatter left the box blank [30, 31]. School board races are excluded from straight-party voting [31].
- Operating referendum (Nov. 4, 2025): voters rejected an eight-year operating referendum of up to $0.2787 per $100 of assessed value (about $12.2 million a year), 3,952 to 3,368, according to final unofficial results [25, 32]. The money would have gone mainly to staff pay, instructional support, the career and technical education (CTE) facility, and safety and well-being staffing [32]. Barker said NACS receives about $8 million a year less than the state average and that the district would move forward within its existing budget [25, 32].
- Budget and SEA 1 (2025–2026): for 2026, NACS prepared a budget of nearly $126 million and estimated a $2.7 million property tax cap loss, about $1.6 million of it in the operations fund; the CFO said the plan to absorb this was $11 million in general obligation bonds over two years for technology and transportation [33]. A third-party summary of the Aug. 17, 2026 board meeting reports a financial presentation on SEA 1 and says the board decided not to make a $38 million capital investment in a swim-trials pool project [34].
- Enrollment and school choice (2026): in April 2026, Barker put enrollment about 300 students below projections, which officials attributed to vouchers, housing trends and falling birth rates; the board approved open enrollment for non-residents and changes to the preschool [35]. The board agreed to accept up to 340 non-resident students in fall 2026 and had received 129 transfer requests by May 4 [24].
- Facilities: the board redrew middle school boundaries in January 2026 ahead of Willow Creek Middle School's scheduled August 2026 opening, and limited 2026 middle school transfers to Willow Creek [24].
- Forums: no 2026 NACS candidate forum or questionnaire was found. Because the seats appear uncontested, the most recent issue statements from these candidates come from 2022 campaign coverage [26].

## Kristi Schlatter — District 3

Incumbent and board vice president, unopposed for reelection in District 3; listed as Nonpartisan [6, 27, 30].

### Background

- In 2022 she was listed as a former teacher and homemaker [26].
- She is a NACS graduate whose children have graduated from Carroll High School or attend NACS [26].
- She was appointed to the board in 2017, ran unopposed in 2018, and won District 3 in 2022 with 53.2% of the vote [26, 29].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for reelection to District 3 and left the party box blank; she is unopposed [30, 31].
- Listed on the state ballot list as Nonpartisan [6].

### Positions & plans

- In 2022 she said the most pressing need was managing the district's population growth while maintaining educational excellence [26].

**Academics**

- In 2022 she said her focus would be meeting the needs of each student, adding that it mattered to her "to have no personal agenda" [26].

### On the current officeholder

## Darren Vogt — District 4

Incumbent at-large member and board secretary, elected in 2022; listed as Republican. He lost the May 2026 Republican primary for state Senate District 15 by 14 votes [6, 27, 29, 36].

### Background

- He is a staffer to U.S. Sen. Jim Banks [37, 36].
- In 2022 he was described as a local business owner and NACS parent with 12 years on the Allen County Council, six as president, and a seat on the Allen County Redevelopment Commission [26].
- He won the second of two at-large seats in 2022 with 21.7% of the vote, according to unofficial results [29].
- He ran in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary for state Senate District 15 against Sen. Liz Brown. Certified tallies gave Brown 5,241 votes to his 5,227 [36]. He asked for a recount on May 19 and withdrew the request in July 2026, saying the vote totals would not change enough to affect the outcome [37, 36, 38].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for reelection to an at-large seat as a Republican while the Senate primary recount was pending [30, 38].
- Listed on the state ballot list as Republican [6].

### Positions & plans

**Academics**

- In 2022 he said schools should focus on basics such as reading and writing [26, 29].

**Transparency & community input**

- In 2022 he supported redrawing NACS board voting districts to match population growth, and said the district might be better served by seven board members instead of five [26].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member.
- As a 2022 candidate, he said of the board he had watched in recent years that "They really needed some leadership" [26]. No 2026 statement on the board's record was found.

### Endorsements & fundraising

- In 2022 he was endorsed by then-Congressman Jim Banks; the Purple for Parents of Indiana group and a "Conservative NACS School Board Candidates 2022" Facebook page also backed him [26, 29].
- In his 2026 state Senate campaign, state filings show $90,000 from Attorney General Todd Rokita's campaign fund and $35,000 from Sen. Banks's campaign account [36]. No school board campaign finance report for 2026 was found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No 2026 school board website, questionnaire or forum statements were found; his school-board issue statements are from 2022.
- His votes on the 2025 referendum, SEA 1 budget actions and 2026 open enrollment were not found in fetched sources.

## Elizabeth Hathaway — District 4

Incumbent at-large member and board president (listed by the district as Liz Hathaway), first elected in 2014; listed as Republican [6, 27, 39, 26].

### Background

- BallotReady lists her as an engineer at Von Duprin with a 1992 electrical engineering degree from Purdue University [39]. In 2022 The Journal Gazette described her as a stay-at-home mother who had worked as an electrical engineer [26].

- She has served on the board since 2015, according to BallotReady; she won a third term in 2022 with 23.8% of the at-large vote [39, 29].

**Transparency & community input**

### On the current officeholder

### Endorsements & fundraising
