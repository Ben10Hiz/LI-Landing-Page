# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The School City of East Chicago serves the city of East Chicago in Lake County; the district's website lists enrollment of 3,200 students and a 90.91% graduation rate for the 2025 school year [24]. Dr. Stephen D. Bournés is superintendent [24, 25]; he led the district at least as far back as July 2024 [26].
- The board has five members: two elected at large and one each from Districts 1, 2 and 3 [25]. Voters choose only the district seat where they live: sample ballots for three East Chicago precincts each show a single school board race, District 1, 2 or 3, each marked 'Vote For One (1) Only' [27, 28, 29].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: all three district seats, one member each, which matches the state list [30, 6]. The terms of District 1 member Joel Rodriguez, District 2 member Pattie Jo Gibson-King and District 3 member Diane Smith expire Dec. 31, 2026, and all three filed to run again [25, 31]. District 1 is the only contested race: Rodriguez faces Jose Arteaga and Jesus Luis Ortiz, III, and all three are listed as Democrats [31, 27]. Gibson King (no party listed) in District 2 and Smith (Democrat) in District 3 are unopposed [31, 30].
- Seats not on this ballot: at-large members Maria Mercedez Mena-Taylor and Jesse Gomez serve terms through 2028 [25]. They won the two at-large seats in 2024 from a field of four [32].
- 2022 results for these seats: Rodriguez was unopposed in District 1; Gibson-King won District 2 with 34.08% of the vote in a three-way race decided by 16 votes; Smith won District 3 with 53.00% against three opponents [33].
- Budget (2025): a Sept. 23, 2025 district presentation, 'Securing Our Financial Future: A Reduced Spending Plan,' put the FY26 budget at $48,175,043, or about $53.6 million with grants [34]. It listed the 2025 property-tax law SEA 1, operations-fund sharing with charter schools and lower federal grants as pressures, and forecast a loss of about $850,000: $372,189 to charter schools, $350,000 in operations-fund revenue and $127,547 in federal grants [34]. The plan proposed cutting six certified and three non-certified positions, a hiring pause and attrition, a 10% cut in operational spending, and 'closing and/or consolidating a building,' using a sample building cost of about $1.99 million a year [34].
- Pre-K and buildings (2026): on April 28, 2026 the board approved a moving-company quote to help relocate Carrie Gosch staff, and it approved new Pre-Kindergarten classrooms and elementary playground renovations [35]. The district's Sept. 16, 2026 town hall listed 'changes including Pre-K' among the issues it had communicated to families [36].
- Bond and other 2026 actions: on April 28, 2026 the board passed resolutions advancing a $30 million bond for capital projects including HVAC at Central High School, voted to run its own food service, and approved transferring the administration building's deed to the City of East Chicago [35]. In April and May it approved an energy-savings project and contracts meant to improve indoor air quality and offset rising utility costs [35].
- Storm damage (2026): an Aug. 11, 2026 derecho, along with summer flash floods and power outages, closed the district from Aug. 12 to 21 and led to Lincoln Elementary's temporary relocation on Aug. 26 [36]. A 2025 facility assessment quoted by the district said many of Lincoln's systems, in a building from 1971, 'have reached or are approaching the end of their useful life' [36]. As of Oct. 9, 2026, the district reported transformer and sump-pump replacement finished and roof repairs nearly done, and said it was working toward reopening Lincoln [24, 37].
- Earlier funding votes: in May 2017, East Chicago voters rejected an operating referendum, 561 yes to 2,010 no (78% no) [38].
- Forums: this guide found no candidate forums, questionnaires or news coverage of the 2026 East Chicago races. The Times of Northwest Indiana (nwitimes.com) could not be read, and a January 2026 Lakeshore Public Media article on pre-K changes no longer loaded.

## Joel Rodriguez — District 1

Incumbent; has been on the board since 2013 and chairs the National School Boards Association's Council of Urban Boards of Education for 2025–27 [39, 25].

### Background

- Grew up in East Chicago [39]. Wrote in a September 2025 column that he joined the East Chicago school board in 2013 [39].
- Chairs the CUBE Steering Committee of the National School Boards Association for 2025–27. Before that he was a regional director of NSBA's National Hispanic Council and a CUBE steering committee member [39].
- Re-elected unopposed in District 1 in 2022 [33].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed June 18, 2026 and is listed as a Democrat [31, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- In a July 2025 NSBA interview he said, 'Urban districts have always been a target for lack of funding, not only from the state level,' and, 'We're tightening our belts and figuring out other ways that we can survive' [40].
- Said in the same interview that it is 'important for us to continue to advocate for that, to continue to fight for public education' [40].

**Academics**

- Said in July 2025, 'We've turned the tide a bit with our focus on student success' [40]. Wrote that 'equitable access to quality education is the foundation for thriving communities' [39].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Said the 2016 lead and arsenic contamination in East Chicago 'equated to close to 382 children that we've actually lost in our school system' [40].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member [25].
- In July 2025 he described the district as cutting costs to get by, and he spoke of arts programs as valuable but costly to keep [40].

### How they say they'd run the office

- As CUBE chair, wrote in September 2025 that his priorities include using data to find resource gaps at individual schools, expanding digital and hands-on learning, and building mentoring links between students and local professionals [39]. These were national goals for CUBE, not a plan for the East Chicago board.
