# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The Vigo County School Corporation (VCSC) serves all of Vigo County, including Terre Haute [24]. The district says it operates 15 elementary schools, 5 middle schools, 3 high schools, 2 alternative schools and 5 specialized education facilities [25]. Wikipedia lists enrollment of 13,787 in 2023 [24].
- Superintendent: Wikipedia lists Chris Himsel, hired in 2023 [24]; an October 2025 WTWO report referred to the corporation's 'new superintendent' without naming one, and this guide could not confirm who holds the post now [26]. Deputy Superintendent John Newport has presented the facilities plan [27].
- The board has seven members elected countywide; candidates must have lived in their district for at least a year, and members are paid $2,000 a year [28]. 2026 is the first year of party labels in school board races under a new state law; school board races are not included in straight-ticket voting [29].
- Four of the seven seats are on the 2026 ballot [28]. District 1 (listed on the ballot as District 1-1): incumbent and board president Carey LaBella faces Imer Holman and Chase Nattkemper [29]. District 2: Jenny Norris is the only candidate [28]; the district lists James D. Skelton as the current District 2 member, and he did not file [30, 28]. District 4: incumbent Ken Warner is unopposed [31]. District 5: incumbent Rick Burger faces former District 5 member Rosemarie Scott [29].
- District 1 has three board seats; the other two, held by Amy Lore and Christina Crist, were filled in 2024 and are not on this ballot [30, 32].
- Facilities consolidation (2025–2026): the district's 'Option 6.2' plan would cut 23 schools to 16, combining Terre Haute North and South in a new high school east of the Wabash River and making West Vigo a grades 7–12 school [33]. In February 2026 the new high school was estimated at $222 million, with a possible opening in August 2029 [27].
- County funding (2025–2026): in October 2025 the Vigo County Council voted 4–3 for an ordinance barring county funding of school projects or an oversight board without a referendum [26]. By 2026 a five-member county oversight board was reviewing the plan [27]; in August 2026 the county commissioners unanimously approved its recommendation that the county commit $15 million upfront and $6 million a year for the bond [34]. As of Sept. 2, the council had not voted, and commissioners hoped for a vote on Oct. 6 or 13 [35]. Voters rejected a 2022 school construction referendum, with 68.5% opposed [34].
- Carbon project and emergency planning (2026): forum candidates discussed emergency planning tied to the Wabash Valley Resources carbon sequestration project near Fayette Elementary [33].
- Censure (October 2026): on Oct. 2, 2026 the board voted 6–0 to censure District 4 member Ken Warner over his personal social media comments about possession of child sexual abuse material in a local criminal case; Warner did not attend and says his comments were taken out of context [31].
- Forum: the League of Women Voters of Vigo County held a candidate forum on Sept. 29, 2026 at the Vigo County Public Library [36, 33]. The five District 1 and District 5 candidates took part; Norris and Warner, who are unopposed, are not mentioned in the coverage [33].
- Key differences: LaBella, Nattkemper and Burger support Option 6.2, Holman says he needs more facts, and Scott opposes combining North and South [33]. Most candidates said community trust in the board is low, while Burger asked voters to judge the board's record [33].

## Carey Labella — District 1-1

Incumbent and board president seeking a second term [29].

### Background

- Oversees collection development at the Vigo County Public Library, where she has worked since 2013, per her district bio [30].
- Parent of students in the district [29].
- Took office in January 2023 after a judge ruled her 2022 opponent ineligible under the district residency requirement [37].
- Served on the Vigo County Oversight Board that reviewed the facilities plan [33].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed without declaring a party affiliation; listed as Nonpartisan [28, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Facilities**

- Supports Option 6.2 but wants the placement of West Vigo sixth graders reviewed, saying 'I don't think any door has closed on editing that plan' [33, 29].

**Transparency & community input**

- Said 'there has been a struggle with trust in the community' and that her approach is to 'always first listen' [33].

**School safety**

- On emergency planning near the carbon project, said 'As a parent, it's terrifying to think about' [33].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member [30].
- As a policy committee member, said 'I did lack follow up' and named 'telling our story' as her priority [33].
- On the Warner censure, said 'It's our only legally allowed step' to state publicly that the board does not condone the comments [38].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists student achievement, sustainable finances, facility planning, support for teachers and transparency as priorities [29].

### What we couldn't verify

- The ballot list spells her name 'Labella'; the district and Tribune-Star use 'LaBella'.
- No campaign website, endorsements or campaign finance reports found.

## Chase C. Nattkemper — District 1-1

Republican challenger and Terre Haute native who supports the Option 6.2 facilities plan [29].

### Background

- Indiana University political science graduate who runs a political consulting business and works at First Financial Bank; attended Vigo County schools [29].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Republican [29, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Facilities**

- Supports Option 6.2 'very strongly', saying it protects western schools and keeps a small high school option [33].

**Transparency & community input**

- Said trust and transparency are 'at an all-time low' and pledged 'I'll be an open book' [33].

**School safety**

- Called an emergency and evacuation plan 'absolutely' a priority and criticized the decision to locate the carbon project in the county [33].

**Academics**

- Lists students' academic and emotional well-being, including extracurriculars and mental health, as his top issue [33].

### On the current officeholder

- Says the board does not ask the administration enough questions; on his campaign Facebook page he wrote, 'We need to be able to ask the hard questions first and earlier' [33, 29].
- Called Warner's comments 'awful' and favored censure [39].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website found; his Facebook page was not reviewed directly.
- No endorsements or campaign finance reports found.

## Richard (Rick) Burger — District 5

Incumbent District 5 member seeking a second term; supports the Option 6.2 facilities plan he helped develop [29].

### Background

- District manager for Duke Energy for the Terre Haute area [30, 29].
- Served eight years on the Lafayette School Board and as a Vigo County Council at-large member [29]; his district bio gives his council service as 2012–2016 [30].
- Past chairman of the Terre Haute Chamber of Commerce and the Terre Haute Economic Development Corporation [30].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as an independent [28, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Facilities**

- Says he is '100% behind 6.2' after '2 1/2 years' of work and expects the County Council to approve funding: 'I believe we will get there' [33].

**School safety**

- Calls safety a 'top priority' [33].

**Academics**

- Wants a trade school and more business partnerships [33].

**Transparency & community input**

- Says attendance at community meetings has been disappointing and wants more parent involvement [33].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member [30].
- Cites improved academic scores and attendance and asks voters to 'judge us on what we've done in the last four years' [33].
- Asked at the forum whether he would support censuring Warner, said 'I probably would. Yes.' [39]

### What we couldn't verify

- Sources differ on his Duke Energy tenure (46th year per the district bio; 49 years per the Tribune-Star).
- No campaign website, endorsements or campaign finance reports found.

## Rosemarie Scott — District 5

Former District 5 board member (2019–2022) challenging the incumbent; opposes combining North and South high schools [29].

### Background

- Served on the board for District 5 from 2019 to 2022 and ran in District 1 in 2024 [29].
- Described by the Tribune-Star in 2026 as a retired educator [29]; in 2024 it reported she had worked as a childbirth instructor and lactation consultant and was a past PTO member [32].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as an independent [29, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Facilities**

- Opposes a consolidated North–South high school, which she believes would be too large; favors keeping and renovating both, with partial new construction [29, 33].

**Academics**

- Cites concerns about dropout rates and teen mental health [29].

**School safety**

- Called the carbon capture project 'a nightmare' for the community [33].

**Transparency & community input**

- Said 'I don't think there is any trust between the school board and the community' and that trust is built 'one-on-one'; wants better communication with parents [33].

### On the current officeholder

- Said she would vote to censure Warner and thinks he should resign [39].

### What we couldn't verify
