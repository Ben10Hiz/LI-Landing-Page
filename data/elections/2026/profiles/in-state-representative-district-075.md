# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Cindy Ledbetter (Republican)

Cindy Ledbetter of Newburgh, a psychiatric nurse practitioner and former Warrick County Council member, has represented House District 75 since November 2020 [3, 4]. She is an assistant majority floor leader [3].

- Leadership and committees: Assistant Majority Floor Leader; member of the Family, Children and Human Affairs, Insurance, and Public Health committees [3].
- 2025: Authored HB 1120 to extend a hyperbaric oxygen pilot program for veterans with PTSD to June 30, 2027 [5]; co-sponsored SEA 26, which criminalizes the sale, possession and use of signal-jamming devices and was signed by Gov. Mike Braun [6].
- 2024 guest column: backed lowering electricity rates, keeping existing power plants open amid supply warnings, and nuclear energy [7].
- Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [8]; yes on the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [9]; and yes on HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [10].

## This race

- House District 75 covers portions of Gibson, Pike and Warrick counties in southwestern Indiana, per the House Republican caucus [3].
- Ledbetter was reelected without opposition in 2024 and was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [11]. No Democratic primary is listed for this seat [11].
- Ballot: Ledbetter (R) and Mason Barclay (D); Jim Shields is a declared write-in candidate whose name will not be printed on the ballot [2].
- Key differences: Barclay focuses on utility rate reviews, data-center rules and changes to SEA 1, and criticizes Ledbetter's votes and utility PAC contributions [12, 13, 14]; Ledbetter's record centers on health care, veterans and public-safety bills and support for SEA 1 [3, 8].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Cindy Ledbetter

Cindy Ledbetter is the three-term incumbent and an assistant majority floor leader [3]. She was unopposed in the May primary [11].

### Background

- Nurse practitioner at an outpatient clinic providing counseling and medication management; nursing degrees from Ivy Tech and the University of Southern Indiana [3].
- Has owned a manufacturing company and a restaurant [3]. Served on the Warrick County Council [4].
- Lives in Newburgh [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [11].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Supported the goal of "lowering Indiana's high electricity rates" and argued existing power sources should not close amid supply warnings; called nuclear "clean, baseload power" [7].

**Property taxes**

- Voted yes on SEA 1 (2025) [8].

**Veterans**

- Said "Our veterans deserve the best mental health care" in authoring HB 1120 [5].

**Public safety**

- Co-sponsored SEA 26 on signal jammers, saying lawmakers must update laws for "any threat posed by new technologies" [6].

**Government operations**

- Supported HEA 1003 (2026), saying Hoosiers "will benefit from eliminating unnecessary bureaucracy" [15].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted yes on HB 1032, the December 2025 congressional redistricting bill [10].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No response to her challengers' criticisms was found.

## Mason Barclay

Mason Barclay is the Democratic nominee and a first-time candidate focused on utility costs, data centers and property taxes [16, 12].

Campaign website: https://www.barclayforin75.com/

### Background

- Describes himself as a lifelong Hoosier and a member of the District 75 community; his site does not give his occupation or hometown [17].

### Path to the ballot

- No Democratic primary for this seat is listed in the May 5, 2026 results [11]. How he was nominated was not found.

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Wants laws requiring "open, independent reviews of utility companies before any rate hikes are approved" and says he refuses utility PAC money [12].

**Data centers**

- Says large data centers "must build their own power grids," should use no city water for cooling, and should face a 40-decibel noise limit at the property line, a half-mile buffer from homes and upfront cleanup bonds [13].

**Property taxes**

- Says he would work to make tax laws fair for families buying or keeping a home and to fix SEA 1 so local services stay affordable [12].

**Health care**

- Says hospitals should have to show "the clear, upfront cash price for standard procedures" [12].

**Property rights**

- Says he is committed to protecting property rights and stopping "corporate overreach" [12].

### On the current officeholder

- His campaign site says Ledbetter voted yes on SEA 1, supported HB 1276, which it says reduced state oversight of certain electric generation, and voted yes on SB 373 on eminent domain for carbon storage; it also cites a 33% rating from the Citizens Action Coalition [14]. It says she took "thousands in campaign cash from utility PACs" [13]. Her SEA 1 vote is confirmed by the roll call [8].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he would focus spending on "job training, better roads, and helping working families" [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- iVoterGuide lists an endorsement from the United Mine Workers of America's COMPAC [18].

### What we couldn't verify

- Occupation, residence and community roles were not found.
- No news coverage was found in Evansville-area or Pike/Gibson outlets reviewed.
- How he reached the ballot (likely a Democratic ballot-vacancy caucus after the primary) is unconfirmed.
- His claims about Ledbetter's HB 1276 and SB 373 votes, the CAC rating and utility PAC donations were not independently checked.
- No fundraising totals were found.
