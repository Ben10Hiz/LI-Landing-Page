# U.S. Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the U.S. House of Representatives write and vote on federal laws, including the federal budget and taxes. Each represents one congressional district for a two-year term; Indiana has nine [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None
- **Voting members:** 435 [1]
- **Indiana seats:** 9 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces bills and resolutions, offers amendments, and votes on legislation; federal laws must pass both the House and the Senate [1].
- Serves on committees, which draft legislation in their subject areas and oversee federal agencies and programs [1].
- Must be at least 25, a U.S. citizen for at least seven years, and live in the state they represent [1].

## Current officeholder: André Carson (Democratic)

André Carson has represented the 7th District since 2008 and is seeking a 10th term [3]. In the current Congress he serves on the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee (Aviation; Railroads, Pipelines, and Hazardous Materials subcommittees), the Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, and the Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party [4].

- May 22, 2025: Voted no on House passage of H.R. 1, the budget reconciliation bill titled the One Big Beautiful Bill Act [5].
- July 3, 2025: Voted no on the motion to concur in the Senate amendment to H.R. 1, the final House vote on the reconciliation bill, which passed [6].
- Nov. 12, 2025: Voted no on H.R. 5371, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2026, the bill that passed the House to end the fall 2025 government shutdown [7].
- Nov. 18, 2025: Voted yes on the Epstein Files Transparency Act (H.R. 4405), which passed under suspension of the rules [8].
- Nov. 19, 2025: Announced his selection as ranking member (top Democrat) of the House Transportation and Infrastructure Subcommittee on Aviation, which oversees civil aviation and the Federal Aviation Administration [9].
- Jan. 22, 2026: Voted no on the fiscal year 2026 Department of Homeland Security appropriations bill, saying in a statement that it "continues to fund ICE without any substantive changes to stop unlawful actions and abuse of power" [10].
- Feb. 19, 2026: Announced $13.08 million in Community Project Funding for the district, including $1,092,000 for lead service line replacement in Martindale-Brightwood, $1.2 million for IndyGo vehicles and $2 million for an Indianapolis airport baggage-screening system [11].
- March 23, 2026: With committee ranking member Rick Larsen, criticized the administration for sending ICE officers to airports during a DHS funding lapse in which TSA agents were working without pay [12].
- April 27, 2026: Introduced, with two colleagues, the AI Data Center Site Selection Transparency Act of 2026, which would require communities to be informed of planned AI data centers before deals are finalized [13].
- May 15, 2026: His amendment raising VA Medical and Prosthetic Research Program funding to $994 million passed the House as part of the fiscal year 2027 Military Construction-VA appropriations bill, which his office said also includes more than $1.6 billion to replace and modernize the Roudebush VA Medical Center campus in Indianapolis; the bill was sent to the Senate [14].
- May 22, 2026: Voted in the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee to advance the Build America 250 Act surface transportation bill, which included his amendment on motorcycle profiling during traffic stops [15].
- June 11, 2026: Introduced the Summer Meals for Kids Act, which would make Summer EBT food benefits a standard part of the National School Lunch Program; his office noted Indiana opted out of Summer EBT [16].
- June 29, 2026: Joined Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez in introducing the AI Data Center Moratorium Act, which would pause new AI data center development until federal safeguards are in place [17].
- Sept. 2, 2026: Sent a congressional inquiry to the Department of Homeland Security seeking data on increased ICE arrests in Indianapolis; his office said he has repeatedly voted against ICE funding and called for eliminating the agency [18].
- Sept. 29, 2026: With Rep. Victoria Spartz (R-Ind.) and Sen. Elizabeth Warren, urged the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission to reject BlackRock/Global Infrastructure Partners' application to acquire AES Corporation, AES Indiana's parent, citing utility rates [19].
- In the 2026 primary, challengers George Hornedo and Destiny Wells criticized Carson; Hornedo cited a Center for Effective Lawmaking ranking of Carson as 197th of 220 House Democrats, and Wells criticized donations from AES Indiana and BlackRock's PAC [20]. Carson responded, "I have never, and I will never, allow anyone to impact my vote" [20].

## This race

- The 7th District includes the majority of Indianapolis and Marion County and has a population of more than 757,000 [3]. WFYI reported it is the most racially diverse district in Indiana, with nearly one-third of residents Black and 16% Hispanic [21].
- The Cook Political Report rates the district D+21, and Carson won in 2024 with 68% of the vote [3].
- In 2025, some Republicans sought to redraw the map to make the district more Republican, but state lawmakers voted the mid-cycle redistricting effort down; Carson opposed it [3]. Mirror Indy reported the effort failed after Indiana state senators joined Democrats in rejecting it [22].
- In the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary, Carson received 62.4% of the vote, ahead of Destiny Wells (23.4%) and George Hornedo (10.5%), per NBC News results [23]. Denise Paul Hatch was also on the Democratic ballot [22].
- In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Patrick McAuley received 84.4% of the vote to Felipe Rios's 15.6% [23].
- The Libertarian Party of Indiana nominated James Sceniak for the 7th District at its state convention in Fort Wayne in March 2026 [24].
- Voters responding to a WFYI survey most often raised affordability, data center development and whether elected officials listen to them [3]. On data centers, Carson supports a moratorium in Indianapolis until developers show a positive result for the community, McAuley opposes data centers in residential areas, and Sceniak favors clearer permitting and siting rules rather than a ban [3].
- No debates or candidate forums for the general election were found in news coverage reviewed for this profile [3].

## André Carson

Carson, 51, is the incumbent, first elected in 2008, and is seeking a 10th term [3]. He lists opposing Trump administration policies, preserving Medicare and Medicaid, and bringing federal funds to Indiana as priorities [3].

Campaign website: https://andrecarson.com

### Background

- Carson is 51 and lives in Indianapolis [3].
- He has represented the district since 2008 [3].
- In a 2026 congressional letter he described himself as a former law enforcement officer [18]. In a WFYI interview he said he is the only member of Congress who has worked in an intelligence fusion center [3].
- He serves on the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee, the Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence and the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party [4], and is ranking member of the Aviation Subcommittee [9].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 62.4% of the vote over Destiny Wells, George Hornedo and Denise Paul Hatch [23, 22].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Says he wants to preserve Medicare and Medicaid and argued that Medicaid cuts are affecting Hoosier families despite the state's budget surplus (Aug. 6, 2026 interview) [3].

**Data centers & utilities**

- Said he supports a data center moratorium in Indianapolis "until they can show us that their presence in our community will yield a positive result" (Aug. 6, 2026) [3].
- Cosponsored the AI Data Center Moratorium Act and introduced the AI Data Center Site Selection Transparency Act of 2026 [17, 13].
- Urged federal regulators to reject the BlackRock/GIP acquisition of AES, saying the deal could raise utility rates [19].

**Food & affordability**

- Introduced the Summer Meals for Kids Act to extend Summer EBT benefits to all states, including Indiana [16].
- Said he wants federal investment in economic development and has legislation to help nonprofits offer food at lower cost [3].

**Immigration**

- Said current immigration policy under the Trump administration is "riddled with xenophobia and anti-immigrant sentiment," while acknowledging national security, trafficking and cartel concerns, and called for a comprehensive system that protects asylum seekers and refugees (Aug. 6, 2026) [3].
- Voted against fiscal year 2026 DHS appropriations over ICE funding and sought information from DHS on ICE arrests in Indianapolis [10, 18].

**Transportation & infrastructure**

- Said he hopes to bring transportation resources to the district and supports a high-speed rail system linking Indianapolis with Louisville, Chicago or St. Louis [3].
- Supported the Build America 250 Act in committee, citing Bipartisan Infrastructure Law funding for IndyGo, highways and broadband [15].

**Economy & small business**

- Said he wants to protect federal contracting opportunities for small, veteran-owned, minority-owned and women-owned businesses [3].

**Foreign policy**

- Said the president has gone beyond the 60-day War Powers window in the Iran war and that the war has raised gas and food prices (Aug. 6, 2026) [3].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder [3].
- No statements by Carson about McAuley or Sceniak were found; during the primary he responded to challengers' criticism of his donors by saying, "In fact, I have voted directly against the interests of donors" [20].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said that if re-elected he expects to chair the Aviation Subcommittee, depending on the election outcome [3].
- Said he will continue to oppose ICE practices he considers excessive and to pursue federal funds for infrastructure, education and economic development [3].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Carson's committee, André Carson for Congress, raised $1,006,176 as of June 30, 2026, according to FEC data cited by WFYI, including $485,550 from PACs per OpenSecrets [3].
- He reported $646,000 in his campaign account at the end of 2025 [20].

### What we couldn't verify

- Carson told WFYI 27 of his bills have passed and Mirror Indy that 22 bills were signed into law; neither count was independently verified for this profile.
- Sources differ on his start date: WFYI says he has served since 2008, Mirror Indy says 2007.
- No endorsement list was reviewed.
- Third-quarter 2026 FEC figures were not reviewed (FEC API rate limit).

## Patrick McAuley

McAuley, 29, is an Indianapolis commercial real estate associate making his first run for office [3, 21]. He says his priorities are housing, jobs, homelessness and public safety [3].

Campaign website: https://mcauleyforthe7th.com

### Background

- McAuley is 29, lives in Indianapolis and is an associate at Broad Ripple Capital, Inc. [3].
- He is a North Central High School graduate and Wabash College All-American with a master's degree in international economics and finance, according to his campaign [25].
- His campaign says he has previous experience in the U.S. Senate [25]; Mirror Indy reported he interned for two U.S. senators [22].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 84.4% of the vote over Felipe Rios [23].

### Positions & plans

**Housing & homelessness**

- Proposes expanding housing supply, converting vacant properties to productive use, and supporting pathways off the street through partnerships and federal resources [26].
- Said he wants to help residents in Section 8 housing build credit to become homeowners and cited the federal Good Neighbor Next Door program and community land trusts to resell foreclosed homes to police, firefighters and teachers [3].

**Public safety**

- Calls for stronger community-police relationships and more officers living in the precincts they patrol [26].
- Cited a 900% increase in youth gun violence since 2008; WFYI said it could not find evidence for that figure and noted consecutive years of declining shootings and homicides in Indianapolis [3].

**Jobs & workforce**

- Proposes a 10-15% increase in federal resources for registered apprenticeships in the skilled trades [27].
- Says expanding trades, apprenticeships and skilled-job opportunities should be a priority because not every young person needs a four-year degree [26].

**Federal spending**

- Said federal appropriations fund duplicate accounts, cited humanitarian aid accounts as an example, and said spending should be redirected to cities [3].

**Infrastructure**

- Said Indiana lost more than $700 million in direct federal funding last year and that he would prioritize restoring road and infrastructure funding for urban districts [27].

**Data centers**

- Said data centers should not be placed in residential communities, citing what he has heard from constituents [3]. He told Mirror Indy he would support a congressional caucus focused on public communication and citizen feedback on data centers [27].

**Immigration**

- Said he is open to immigrants who come seeking opportunity and supports a standard for entry, but opposes violence by ICE agents and agents covering their faces (WFYI interview) [3].

**Elections**

- Said Indiana's elections are secure and that he supports voter ID [3].

### On the current officeholder

- Said in a WFYI interview that the district has lost 15,000 skilled-trades jobs "since my opponent's been in office"; WFYI said it found no record of that figure [3].
- Said that over the past 20 years of leadership in the district "no one has had oversight" of where communities are headed [3].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says his platform is based on a 2025 survey of more than 400 district residents [3, 25].
- Says his three main issues are public safety, housing and homelessness, and career pathways and jobs [26].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- WFYI found no FEC campaign committee or OpenSecrets donor data for McAuley; candidates raising or spending less than $5,000 are not required to file [3].

### What we couldn't verify

- Unverified (search snippets only): Axios Indianapolis (Oct. 8, 2026) described him as a political newcomer and a former intern for Sen. Todd Young; also listed Valparaiso University among his schools.
- Unverified (search snippet only): a search summary said McAuley did not return the Indiana Capital Chronicle voter-guide survey; the page could not be loaded.
- His campaign accepts donations through WinRed, but no FEC filings were confirmed; no endorsements were found.
- WFYI could not confirm several figures he cited (youth gun violence, lost jobs and wages, city budget).

## James M Sceniak

Sceniak is a behavior therapist and vice chair of the Libertarian Party of Indiana [3]. His stated priorities are privacy protections, lower taxes, workforce protections and veterans' health care choice [3].

### Background

- Sceniak is a lead registered behavior therapist at Centria Health Care and vice chair of Indiana's Libertarian Party [3].
- WFYI lists his residence as Indianapolis [3]; the March 2026 convention report listed him as from Greenwood [24].
- He described himself as a fourth-generation immigrant [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Nominated by the Libertarian Party of Indiana at its state convention in Fort Wayne in March 2026 [24].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & taxes**

- Said inflation and federal debt are the main affordability problems and that both parties "continually fund the spending" [3].
- Supports cutting spending through the "Penny Plan" associated with Sen. Rand Paul and said the Jones Act should be repealed to lower import costs [3].
- Said tax credits should go to individuals rather than businesses: "I don't think big corporations should get tax cuts" [3].

**Veterans**

- Proposes a plan he calls "Vet Care" to give veterans more choice in their health care outside set VA procedures [3].

**Data centers**

- Said data centers should pay their fair share, should not get tax cuts or be placed next to neighborhoods, and that water resources should be protected; he did not endorse a ban or moratorium, favoring clearer permitting and siting rules [3].

**Immigration**

- Said there must be borders, security and a process for entry, but called current ICE tactics "absolutely unconstitutional" and said the citizenship process should be less costly [3].

**Foreign policy**

- Said he would have voted for the House resolution to remove U.S. forces from Iran and generally opposes intervening in conflicts overseas while supporting a strong national defense (July 29, 2026 interview) [3].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements specifically about Carson were found [3]. Speaking generally, he said many officials who have served in Washington a long time "forget what it is to put food on the table" [3].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said he would work with local officials on infrastructure and economic issues in Marion County [3].
- Said he takes donations only from private voters [3].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- WFYI found no FEC campaign committee or OpenSecrets donor data for Sceniak [3].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website was found; WFYI lists facebook.com/SceniakForSenate.
- Unverified (search snippets only): prior runs for U.S. Senate (2022), Greenwood City Council and Indiana's 6th District (2024) were seen only in search results and older articles not reviewed for this profile.
- Residence discrepancy: Indianapolis (WFYI, Sept. 2026) vs. Greenwood (convention report, March 2026).
- No endorsements were found.
