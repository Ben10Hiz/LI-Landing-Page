# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Jake Teshka (Republican)

Jake Teshka, a Republican from North Liberty and former South Bend Common Council member, was elected to the House in 2020; he chairs the Financial Institutions Committee and serves on the Education and the Employment, Labor and Pensions committees [3].

- 2023: The caucus says a bill he authored to improve reading proficiency was ceremonially signed into law [3].
- On April 10, 2025, voted for SB 1, the property-tax and local government finance bill, which passed the House 65–29 [4]. On April 25, 2025, voted for the conference report on HEA 1001, the 2025–2027 state budget, adopted 66–27 [5].
- July 2025: Authored HEA 1634, which places middle schoolers who meet proficiency benchmarks in advanced math unless parents opt out and requires numeracy screening in kindergarten through second grade [6].
- December 5, 2025: Voted for HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57–41 [7].
- January–February 2026: Voted for HB 1002, the electric utility affordability bill, on third reading (89–4) and on final concurrence (94–2) [8, 9].
- 2026: Authored HEA 1035, which provides that children doing reasonable, age-appropriate independent activities such as walking or biking are not automatically treated as children in need of services; he said "That's not neglect, that's part of growing up" [10].
- 2026: Sponsored SEA 78, requiring schools to adopt policies restricting student use of personal wireless devices during the school day [11].
- September 2026: Named the Indiana Bankers Association's 2026 Legislator of the Year; the caucus says he co-authored HEA 1116, which it says made Indiana the first state to prohibit virtual currency kiosks [12].

## This race

- House District 7 includes portions of LaPorte, Marshall and St. Joseph counties, according to the House Republican caucus [3]. 95.3 MNC described it as including South Bend's west side and parts of western and southern St. Joseph County [13].
- Teshka was re-elected without opposition in 2024, according to Wikipedia [14].
- Both candidates were unopposed in their May 5, 2026 primaries, according to Wikipedia [14].
- No debate or forum between the candidates was found as of October 10, 2026.

## Jake Teshka

The Republican incumbent since 2020 and chair of the House Financial Institutions Committee [3].

Campaign website: https://jaketeshka.com/

### Background

- Works in business development; previously represented South Bend's 5th District on the Common Council and served on the Transpo board and Redevelopment Commission [3].
- Holds a B.A. from Saint Joseph's College and a Master of Public Affairs from IU South Bend; serves on the boards of the South Bend History Museum and the Boy Scouts' LaSalle Council [3].
- Lives in North Liberty [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- His campaign supports school choice and parental rights and credits his bills with reading and math requirements [15].
- Said of the school phone law, "This new law will support educators and students as we work to improve students' focus and academic engagement" [11].

**Taxes & spending**

- His campaign lists lower taxes, spending cuts and government accountability as priorities [15].

**Rural communities**

- His campaign says he supports agriculture, rural broadband and local businesses [15].

**Abortion & guns**

- His campaign says he opposes abortion and opposes legislation that would undermine gun rights [15].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. His campaign site does not mention his opponent [15].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Serves on the 2026 Interim Study Committees on Education and on Financial Institutions and Insurance [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign site lists no formal endorsements; it cites the Indiana Bankers Association award and resident testimonials [15, 12].

### What we couldn't verify

- No statements by Teshka on his redistricting, property-tax or budget votes were found.
- No fundraising totals found; the state campaign finance portal was not read.

## Oliver Davis

Oliver Davis, the Democratic nominee, is a longtime South Bend Common Council member now serving at large [17].

### Background

- Served 12 years as South Bend's 6th District council member and is in his third year as an at-large member, according to ABC57 [17]. WSBT reported in 2023 that he ran unsuccessfully for St. Joseph County Council in 2020 before filing for an at-large seat [18].
- ABC57 reported that he has served on a transportation board, a zoning board and other local boards [17]. 95.3 MNC reported that he previously ran for South Bend mayor [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced his campaign January 15, 2026, and was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [17, 14].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Lists public education as a priority, saying "We have to believe in our own children, our schools, our families" [17].

**Environment**

- Lists environmental protection and balancing rural and urban interests as priorities [17].

### On the current officeholder

- At his announcement, said "It is time for us to make a change in our district"; ABC57's report did not mention Teshka [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Emphasizes his local-government experience and local voices in decision-making, according to ABC57 [17].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising totals were found in the sources reviewed.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website was found; the St. Joseph County Democratic Party's page links to a Facebook page for him.
- Sources differ on his council tenure: 95.3 MNC said he is in his second term, ABC57 cited 12 years in the 6th District plus three years at large, and Wikipedia lists 2007–present.
- Little policy detail beyond his announcement was found; no statements on property taxes, the budget or redistricting.
- No fundraising totals found.
