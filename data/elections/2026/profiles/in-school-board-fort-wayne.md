# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Fort Wayne Community Schools is Indiana's largest school district, with about 28,200 students; its 2026 budget is $419 million across 62 buildings, 50 of them schools, and its superintendent is Mark Daniel [24].
- The seven-member Board of School Trustees has five members elected from residence districts and two elected at large [25].
- District 1 (the northwest side, including Northrop High and Shawnee Middle) pits four-term incumbent Julie Hollingsworth against Jeannette Jaquish, a rematch of 2022 [26].
- District 4 incumbent Antonette Payne, appointed in 2024, is unopposed [27].
- Two at-large seats are on the 2026 ballot, although the state candidate list shows the at-large race as vote for 1; at-large member Maria Norman did not file for reelection, and Noah Smith, appointed to the other at-large seat in May 2026, is running [27, 28, 6].
- In spring 2026 the board voted to stagger the at-large terms: of the two winners, the one with less board seniority (or, if neither has served, fewer votes) will serve two years, so one at-large seat is elected each cycle from 2028 [28].
- 2026 is the first year Indiana school board candidates could list a party; in this race only Crummey (American Solidarity) and Jaquish (Libertarian) are listed with a party, while others are listed as Independent or Nonpartisan [27, 6].
- Budget and SEA 1: property tax caps under the 2025 law were estimated to cut the district's $70.9 million operations fund levy by nearly $11 million, about 15%, leaving roughly $3.6 million less property tax revenue than in 2025 (reported January 2026) [24].
- Budget cuts: in March 2025 Superintendent Daniel said the district's six-year budget projection had fallen from about $55 million to about $18 million, and the board announced that more than 20 positions were eliminated, saving about $3 million [29].
- Building closure: in November 2025 FWCS announced South Wayne Elementary will close at the end of the 2025-26 school year, citing the cost to repair or replace the building and upcoming funding reductions; most students move to Abbett or Fairfield elementary [30].
- Board appointments: in May 2026 the board moved President Noah Smith from District 3 to a vacant at-large seat without an application process, drawing a complaint to the board and the Indiana Department of Education [31]. Willie Burton was later appointed to District 3 through 2028 [25].
- Personnel: in September 2026 the board approved the termination of at-large candidate Brandon Foster as Irwin STEM Magnet School principal, effective in May [32].
- No 2026 candidate forum for FWCS was found; the Journal Gazette published questionnaire responses from all contested candidates in October 2026 [28, 26].
- Key differences in the questionnaires: on state advocacy, Daly would press to redirect voucher money to public schools, Crummey to seek more local autonomy, Foster to have the state take over leadership of districts with multiple D or F schools, and Jaquish said she would not seek more per-student funding for FWCS; on enrollment, Crummey, Jaquish and Foster called for the district to compete harder or change leadership, while Smith, Follo, Jordan and Hollingsworth emphasized building on and publicizing existing programs [28, 26].

## Jeannette Rose Jaquish — District 1

Independent playwright and children's theater director who filed as a Libertarian; she has been the only challenger to District 1 incumbent Julie Hollingsworth since 2011 and ran for the seat in 2022 [26].

### Background

- Independent playwright and children's theater director; formerly ran The Firehouse Theater in Fort Wayne [26].
- Lives in Fort Wayne; age 68 [26].
- Says her children attended FWCS schools, citing Northwood Middle and Franke Park Elementary [26].
- Ran unsuccessfully for an at-large FWCS seat in 2014 and for the board in 2022 [26].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Libertarian for the District 1 seat [27, 6].
- 2026 is the first year Indiana school board candidates could declare a party; the state list shows her as Libertarian [26, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Said she would not advocate for FWCS to receive more state funding per student than other public schools, arguing the district's size lets it spend less per pupil through bulk purchasing and shared resources [26].
- Proposed that school funding be based on a blend of daily attendance and enrollment rather than the state's twice-yearly count days, and said funding should follow students who transfer [26].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Said FWCS should compete with charter and voucher schools rather than criticize them, and should use its size to offer families more choices and then compare outcomes [26].

**Curriculum & library policy**

- Proposed that, where possible, contested policies be applied by classroom, building or school so parents can opt their children in or out; her examples included sex education, restroom policies, cellphones in class, history curricula, dress codes, masks and armed teachers [26].

**Transparency & community input**

- Said board members should visit schools unannounced and listen to complaints, and that the board should survey parents before adopting new policies [26].
- Said she would listen and investigate concerns within the board's chain-of-command bylaw, adding that a board member has "no power except persuasion" [26].

### On the current officeholder

- Listed problems she says her children reported or she has heard about, including no air conditioning upstairs at Northwood Middle School, short lunch periods at Franke Park Elementary, bullying, and teachers leaving over student discipline, and asked whether they have been fixed [26].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said she wants to influence FWCS policy to teach job skills and work ethic, and said she supports tax-funded public schools where families have choices [26].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- The Journal Gazette's October 2026 race coverage does not report endorsements or fundraising for this race [26].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website or social media page found.
- No forum appearances found; positions come from a single Journal Gazette questionnaire.
- Her 2022 vote share was not reported in the article read; Hollingsworth won with 71% that year.
- No campaign finance filings were reviewed (Allen County filings page returned 403).

## Julia (Julie) Hollingsworth — District 1

Incumbent. Retired FWCS teacher and coach who has represented District 1 since 2011 and is seeking a fifth term [26].

### Background

- Retired educator; the FWCS board page says she spent 32 years in public education, 30 with FWCS, as a high school math teacher, coach and Elmhurst athletic director [25].
- Lives in Fort Wayne; age 73 [26].
- Joined the board in 2011 after defeating two other candidates and has served four terms; won reelection in 2022 with 71% of the vote [26].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for reelection to District 1 and left the party field blank; the state list shows her as Nonpartisan [27, 26, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Teachers & staff**

- Said keeping FWCS attractive to families requires emphasis on recruiting, hiring and developing teachers and supporting them with resources, which she said becomes harder with continued cuts to public education [26].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Supports continuing the district's clubs, arts programs and athletics and communicating with families on issues important to them [26].

**Facilities**

- Said the district must continue to provide clean, safe, educationally appropriate buildings [26].

**Transparency & community input**

- Said the board always considers the opinions of parents, students, staff, taxpayers and community members, that board contact information is public, and that FWCS may form focus groups for district planning [26].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member [25].
- Described board members' role at the state level as staying informed on education law and bills, informing parents and the community, communicating with legislators and testifying, and highlighting district programs and student and staff successes [26].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Did not describe specific new initiatives in the Journal Gazette questionnaire [26].

## Brandon J Foster — At Large

Educational consultant and former FWCS elementary principal whose termination the board approved in September 2026; he filed as an independent [28, 32, 27].

### Background

- Educational consultant; age 41; lives in Fort Wayne; first-time candidate [28].
- Was hired as principal of Irwin STEM Magnet School in summer 2024 [32].
- The FWCS board approved his termination as part of its consent agenda in September 2026, effective May 22; the district's May 14 termination letter said it had lost confidence in his leadership and cited "a pattern of concerns, including but not limited to employee complaints," the climate and culture of the school and "violation of written directives" [32].
- Foster told the Journal Gazette he attributed the firing to missed work during a family matter and to his questions about Black students' standardized test scores [32].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for the at-large race as an independent; the state list shows him as Independent [27, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Academics**

- Said a board member's job is to ensure taxpayers get a return on their investment, and that children are "stuck year after year" in schools rated D or F [28].
- Proposed that the state be able to take over the responsibilities of a superintendent and district leadership when a district has multiple schools rated D or F [28].

**Teachers & staff**

- Said improving enrollment starts with hiring the best superintendent and leadership team, who then hire the best principals and teachers, and said FWCS should hire and promote based on qualifications and results [28].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Said good schools drive enrollment, citing 180 nonresident transfer requests approved by Southwest Allen County Schools this year, and said enrollment will not improve without new district leadership [28].

**Transparency & community input**

- Said he would treat public input as evidence and require data and results before approving initiatives [28].

### On the current officeholder

- Said the district has school closures families cannot plan around and buildings some families do not feel are safe, and called for new leadership at the top of the district [28].
- Described an exchange in which he says a district leader dismissed setting higher proficiency targets, and said he intends to root out what he called a deficit mindset in district leadership [28].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Would push the state to adopt accountability measures for district leadership tied to school grades [28].

- The Journal Gazette's October 2026 race coverage does not report endorsements or fundraising for this race [28].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website or social media page found.
- No district response to Foster's account of his firing was reported beyond the termination letter; the Journal Gazette article does not mention an appeal.
