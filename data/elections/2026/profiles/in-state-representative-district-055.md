# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Lindsay Patterson (Republican)

Lindsay Patterson, a Brookville dental hygienist, was first elected in November 2022 and is seeking a third term [3]. She is the House Assistant Majority Caucus Chair and serves on the Environmental Affairs; Family, Children and Human Affairs; and Roads and Transportation committees [3].

- Committees: Environmental Affairs; Family, Children and Human Affairs; Roads and Transportation; leadership post of Assistant Majority Caucus Chair [3]. 2026 interim appointments: Interim Study Committees on Environmental Affairs and on Roads and Transportation [4].
- 2023 session: Supported the state budget, which the caucus says included a $7 million matching grant for the Whitewater Canal rehabilitation in Metamora [5].
- 2025 session: Authored HB 1507, letting county road and bridge boards undertake low-water-crossing projects and making them eligible for Community Crossings grants; the House passed it unanimously in February 2025 [6].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [7]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [8].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [9].
- 2026 session: Co-authored HEA 1131, updating licensing for estheticians and electrologists [10], and sponsored SEA 180, updating health-profession licensing rules and streamlining medical benefits for injured police officers and firefighters [11].
- September 2026: Directed Fayette and Franklin County residents affected by August flooding to state and federal one-stop recovery centers [12].

## This race

- House District 55 includes all of Fayette, Franklin and Union counties and parts of Decatur, Ripley and Rush counties in southeastern Indiana [3].
- The race is a rematch of 2024, when Patterson defeated Martz with 80.5% of the vote (24,820 to 6,016) [13].
- Patterson and Martz were each the only candidate listed in their party's May 5, 2026 primary [14]; both are on the November ballot [2].
- Key differences: Martz proposes phasing out the private-school voucher program, a full ban on hyperscale data centers, a $20 minimum wage phased in over five years, and legalizing cannabis [15]. Patterson voted for the 2025 property-tax law, the 2025 budget and the 2025 redistricting bill [7, 8, 9]; no 2026 campaign platform from Patterson was found.
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Lindsay Patterson

Lindsay Patterson is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2022 [3]. Her bills have dealt with rural road safety and health-profession licensing [6, 11].

### Background

- Dental hygienist in Greendale; born and raised in Brookville and lives on her family's farm in Franklin County [3].
- Associate degree in dental hygiene from the University of Cincinnati and a business degree from Indiana University [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed as the only Republican candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Said of SEA 180 and SEA 262 (2026): "Improving access to care and reducing barriers for both providers and first responders is critical to keeping our communities safe and healthy" [11].

**Roads & infrastructure**

- Said making sure communities have resources to improve low water crossings "will help protect drivers and enhance infrastructure safety" [6].

**Property taxes**

- Voted for SEA 1 (2025) [7]. After the 2026 session, highlighted HEA 1210's expanded property-tax relief for veterans among the session's new laws [16].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted for HB 1032, the 2025 congressional redistricting bill [9]; no public statement from her on it was found.

- Said of her 2026 interim committee work that she is "eager to work through these interim committees to evaluate priorities, strengthen long-term planning and ensure responsible stewardship of our resources" [4].

## Victoria C. Martz

Victoria Martz is a Batesville criminal defense attorney and chair of the Ripley County Democrats, running against Patterson for the second time [17, 15]. Her platform centers on opposing data centers, raising wages, public-school funding and health care [15].

Campaign website: https://votevictoriamartz.org/

### Background

- Criminal defense attorney; Batesville High School graduate with a 2015 bachelor's degree from Purdue and a 2018 law degree from Indiana University [17]. Her campaign says she passed the bar in 2019 and grew up between Milan and Batesville [15].
- Elected chair of the Ripley County Democrats in September 2025 [17].
- Lives in Batesville [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed in January 2026 for the May 5 Democratic primary [17]; listed as the only Democratic candidate [14].
- Was the 2024 Democratic nominee, receiving 19.5% of the vote [13].

### Positions & plans

**Data centers**

- Says she will support legislation for "an immediate full ban on hyperscale data center development," arguing tax exemptions for data centers have cost taxpayers $655 million [15].

**Education**

- Would introduce legislation to phase out the voucher program and redirect the money to public schools, with 75% going to at-risk schools and 25% to pilot alternative teaching methods [15].

**Economy & jobs**

- Proposes raising the minimum wage to $20 an hour over five years, with subsidies to help small businesses offset the cost [15].
- Proposes a paid family leave insurance program covering 8 weeks per qualifying event, funded by a payroll deduction of about $2 a week [15].

**Health care**

- Says she will vote against Medicaid eligibility cuts, support efforts to cap hospital prices, require disclosure of hospital ownership and acquisitions, and expand maternal care in rural areas [15].

**Property taxes**

- Would bar reassessment of homes of people on retirement or disability income unless sold or inherited (for homes worth more than $450,000), and would look at lower rates for elderly, disabled, low- and middle-income owners and farmers, without cutting funding for public services [15].

**Energy & utilities**

- Supports requiring Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission approval for the sale of a major utility, and says she would support eliminating the 7% sales tax on utility bills if rates remain unchanged [15].

**Child care**

- Says she supports universal child care as a long-term goal and would increase CCDF subsidies, raise the income threshold and fund grants for rural child care deserts [15].

**Cannabis**

- Supports legalizing cannabis and using the tax revenue for public services [15].

**Environment**

- Proposes a statewide task force on water contamination, PFAS standards, and protecting Natural Resources Commission funding [15].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Martz specifically about Patterson or her record were found. Her campaign says "Families are losing healthcare. Kids are going hungry. Public schools and libraries are facing cuts" [15].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she would "work across the aisle" on capping hospital prices [15].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Her campaign lists Run for Something, UFCW Local 700, and the Indiana Coalition for Public Education #VotePublicEd pledge; it also lists signing the U.S. Term Limits pledge and an 'A' grade from MAD Voters [15].

### What we couldn't verify

- No news coverage of her 2026 campaign beyond her January announcement was found.
- A search snippet described her as a former Marion County deputy prosecutor; this was not verified and conflicts with her campaign's description of criminal defense work.
- No fundraising data found.
