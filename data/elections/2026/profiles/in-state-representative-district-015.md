# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Hal Slager (Republican)

Hal Slager, a Republican from Schererville and a retired certified public accountant and small-business owner, served in the House from 2012 to 2018 and has held the seat again since 2020 [3, 4]. He is vice chair of the Rules and Legislative Procedures Committee and a member of Ways and Means, according to the House Republican caucus [3].

- Committees: vice chair of Rules and Legislative Procedures and member of Ways and Means and Financial Institutions, per the caucus [3].
- 2023 and 2025: authored laws extending Medicaid reimbursement for Hoosier children treated at children's hospitals in neighboring states, building on his 2021 law; the 2025 measure, House Enrolled Act 1024, extends the reimbursement to July 1, 2027, and Slager said it "ensures families can remain close to home while receiving quality care without the financial burden" [5, 3].
- Dec. 5, 2025: voted yea on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 before the Senate rejected it on Dec. 11, 2025 [6, 7].
- Feb. 2, 2026: visited La Rabida Children's Hospital in Chicago to discuss Medicaid reimbursement for Hoosier children, per the caucus [3].
- 2026: sponsored Senate Enrolled Act 270, which sets a performance-based review to identify townships that duplicate services or underperform and merge them with others, and requires townships to report fire-service operations and funding to the Indiana Department of Homeland Security; Slager said it "establishes a way for underutilized townships to consolidate" [8].

## This race

- House District 15 covers Dyer, St. John, and parts of Schererville and Hanover Township in Lake County, according to the House Republican caucus [3]. It is not an open seat; Slager is seeking re-election [2].
- Slager and Democrat Anthony Oberman were each unopposed in their May 5, 2026 primaries, according to Wikipedia's tabulation; Slager won 63.3% of the vote in 2024 [9].
- No 2026 debate or candidate forum for this race was found as of October 10, 2026; Lakeshore Public Media hosted a District 15 debate in 2020 between Slager and then-Rep. Chris Chyung [10].
- Slager's record centers on pediatric Medicaid access, township consolidation and Northwest Indiana projects [3, 8]; Oberman's campaign emphasizes utility-rate oversight, data-center costs, public safety and public-school funding [11].

## Anthony Oberman

Anthony Oberman, a Democrat, is a former Calumet City, Illinois, police patrolman who grew up in the Region and graduated from Crown Point High School [12].

Campaign website: https://voteoberman.com/

### Background

- Earned a criminology degree from Indiana State University and served about a decade as a patrolman with the Calumet City Police Department, according to his campaign [12].
- Was born in Hammond and graduated from Crown Point High School; his campaign says his father, a Navy veteran, worked as a welder and his mother as a millwright [12].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [9].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Calls for "Stronger oversight of NIPSCO" and a freeze on utility rate increases, which he says "have skyrocketed up to nearly 20% since 2023," plus billing transparency [11].

**Data centers**

- Opposes "giving data centers a blank check," wants scrutiny of their tax breaks and energy demands, and says "Make data centers pay their share and not pass costs on to communities" [11].

**Public safety**

- Says he would "introduce legislation to elevate crimes against children to felonies and limit plea bargains" and supports first-responder pay that allows them to live in their communities, ongoing mental health care, and modern training [11].

**Education**

- Opposes "voucher programs that drain funding from public education," supports a living wage for teachers, and says "Public schools mean we have elected school boards and local control" [11].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports "fair taxes, reasonable regulations, and access to resources" for small businesses and protecting collective bargaining rights [11].

### On the current officeholder

- Does not name Slager on his campaign site; says "Indianapolis politicians are using our hard-earned tax dollars for luxury vehicles," that Northwest Indiana revenue is sent downstate while local services are underfunded, and criticizes "politicians who rubber-stamp corporate giveaways" [11].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he wants to bring "common-sense, people-first leadership to the Indiana House of Representatives" [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- iVoterGuide lists endorsements from the AFL-CIO, Indiana AFL-CIO, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates, the Lake County Indiana Young Democrats and the Indiana Reproductive Freedom PAC [13].
- No fundraising totals were found; the campaign collects donations through ActBlue [11].

- Did not answer the iVoterGuide questionnaire.

## Hal Slager

Hal Slager, a Republican from Schererville, is a retired CPA and small-business owner who has represented District 15 since 2020 after an earlier stint from 2012 to 2018 [3].

Campaign website: http://www.voteslager.com/

### Background

- Graduated from Butler University in 1981; worked as a CPA and in corporate accounting at Melvin Simon & Associates and at Trico Graphics, and founded Triangle Equities LLC, a real-estate development and management firm, in 1997, per the caucus [3].
- Served about 10 years on the Schererville Town Council starting in 2002 [3].
- Lives in Schererville [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [9].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Has authored a series of laws, most recently in 2025, extending Medicaid reimbursement so Northwest Indiana children can be treated at children's hospitals in neighboring states [5].

**Local government**

- Sponsored the 2026 law creating a process to consolidate underperforming townships [8].

**Energy & utilities**

- His campaign website says "I believe that we need to establish a statewide energy policy to protect our rate payers" [14].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted yea on the December 2025 House passage of HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill [6].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No public statement by Slager about his challenger was found [3].

### How they say they'd run the office

- No 2026-specific platform was found; his campaign website's content appears to date from earlier cycles [14].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign website [14].
- No 2026 fundraising totals were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- His campaign website appears not to have been updated for 2026 (it references earlier-cycle accomplishments and a "November 5th" election date); a third-party guide reported no campaign website as of September 8, 2026.
