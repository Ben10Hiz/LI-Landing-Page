# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Mike Karickhoff (Republican)

Mike Karickhoff, a Kokomo Republican first elected in 2010, has served as Speaker Pro Tempore of the Indiana House since 2019; the House Republican caucus lists his committees as Natural Resources, Government and Regulatory Reform, Rules and Legislative Procedures, Joint Rules and the Statutory Committee on Ethics [3, 4]. He announced in November 2025 that he would not seek reelection, so House District 30 is an open seat in 2026 [5].

- November 2025: Announced he would not seek a new term, saying, "It has been the honor of a lifetime to serve the people of District 30 and the state of Indiana"; the Indiana Capital Chronicle (via WFYI) reported that his announcement highlighted work on the opioid crisis, economic development and natural resources, and that his earlier bills on driving-privilege cards and in-state tuition for some noncitizens stalled amid opposition from fellow Republicans [5].
- December 5, 2025: Voted no on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41, according to BillTrack50's record of the third-reading vote [6, 7].
- 2026 session: Authored House Enrolled Act 1145, which the House Republican caucus said provides retired public servants a "thirteenth check" by Oct. 1, 2026, without general fund appropriations or tax increases; the caucus said the bill passed the House unanimously [8].
- February 2026: Was recognized by the House for 16 years of service; the caucus listed his past work including 2017 state and local tax legislation, 2022 legislation on use of opioid settlement funds, and 2025 legislation on administrative reforms to state operations [4].

## This race

- House District 30 includes portions of Howard and Grant counties, including Kokomo, according to the House Republican caucus [3]. Democratic candidate Jack Chance's campaign site also names Tipton and Greentown among communities in the district; that description has not been confirmed against the official district map [9].
- This is an open seat: Rep. Mike Karickhoff, who has held it since 2010, announced in November 2025 that he would not run again [5, 10].
- In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Paula Davis defeated Kokomo Common Council President Ray Collins, 3,354 votes (66.3%) to 1,703 (33.7%), according to Wikipedia's results table; The Kokomo Lantern reported the result as 63-32 [10, 11]. Jack Chance was unopposed for the Democratic nomination [12].
- Forums: The Kokomo Lantern reported that Republican candidates for state office did not attend a Sept. 29, 2026 candidate forum held by the Kokomo Chamber of Commerce, the Kokomo Tribune and the League of Women Voters, which went forward with Democratic candidates including those for House District 30; the Howard County Republican Party said in a statement that its candidates had chosen to attend other events and would engage voters through door-to-door outreach and a party meet-and-greet [13]. No debate between Davis and Chance was found as of October 10, 2026.
- On stated priorities, Davis's campaign site lists parental rights, an academics-focused approach to education, the Second Amendment, immigration enforcement and opposing abortion, while Chance's lists rental-housing standards, ending school vouchers, replacing the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission and repealing Indiana's right-to-work law [14, 15].

## Jack Chance

Jack Chance, the Democratic nominee, is a Kokomo resident who works in affordable and senior housing management and is running on housing, utility costs, health care and public education [16, 17].

Campaign website: https://www.abetterindiana.org/

### Background

- Grew up in Tipton, graduated from Tipton High School and now lives in Kokomo, according to his campaign [16].
- Pursued pastoral studies and helped start an outreach for people experiencing homelessness; The Kokomo Lantern reported that he founded a homeless mission while attending seminary in Oklahoma City [16, 17].
- His campaign says he entered housing management in 2020, managed a HUD-regulated community for two years, served on the Indiana Apartment Association's recruitment committee in 2021, and now works for a nonprofit housing organization in Indianapolis serving senior and low-income residents [16].
- The Kokomo Lantern reported that, by his own account, he is "no politician"; he told the paper, "I'm just an everyday guy, and I'm here to serve everyday people" [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Ran unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [12, 10].

### Positions & plans

**Housing**

- Proposes a "Fair Hoosier Housing Act" that would set habitability standards for rentals (including working heat, air conditioning in extreme heat and safe electrical systems), require emergency repairs within 24 hours and non-emergency repairs within 10 days of written notice [18].
- Told the Lantern: "I see how incredibly broken housing is... the brokenness is supported by current laws" [17].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says he would introduce legislation to abolish the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission and replace it with a system in which rate increases are approved by voters [19].
- Supports solar on rooftops, warehouses, parking lots and brownfields rather than on farmland [19].

**Education**

- Calls for repealing the Indiana Choice Scholarship (voucher) program, citing Article 1, Section 6 of the Indiana Constitution, and for increasing public-school funding and teacher pay [20].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports repealing Indiana's right-to-work law, local-hiring standards for publicly funded projects, union apprenticeships, and tying state incentives to long-term job commitments [9].

**Health care**

- Proposes a "Better Indiana Health Plan" to lower out-of-pocket costs, protect rural hospitals and require emergency medical services in every community of more than 1,000 residents; the campaign says it is "not about government control of doctors or hospitals" [21].

**Agriculture**

- Supports barring all foreign ownership of Indiana farmland and protecting farmers' rights to own their seeds and crops [22].

**Government transparency**

- Would amend Indiana's Open Door Law to guarantee public comment at meetings where a governing body takes official action [23].

### On the current officeholder

- Has not directly addressed Rep. Karickhoff's record in available material; his campaign says Indiana's housing, utility and school systems need legislative changes [16, 17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists his priorities as housing and homelessness, health care affordability and public education [17, 15].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign lists endorsements from former Kokomo Mayor Steve Daily, P.U.R.G.E. (People United for Responsible Growth Everywhere), the Indiana Rural Summit, and a "Verified Politician" designation from MADVoters Indiana [24].
- The campaign says it refuses corporate and super PAC money [15].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign finance totals were found.
- No statements by Chance about his Republican opponent were found.
- His campaign's list of district communities (including Tipton) differs from the caucus description of the district (portions of Howard and Grant counties); not checked against the official map.

## Paula Davis

Paula Davis, the Republican nominee, is a Howard County community advocate and county chair of Moms for Liberty who won the May 2026 Republican primary over Kokomo Common Council President Ray Collins [10, 11].

Campaign website: https://www.votepauladavis.com/

### Background

- Wikipedia, citing State Affairs, describes her as a community advocate and chapter chair of the Howard County Moms for Liberty; The Kokomo Lantern also identified her as the county chair for Moms for Liberty [10, 11].
- Her campaign's slogan is "From the Schoolhouse to the Statehouse" [14].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Ray Collins, 3,354 votes (66.3%) to 1,703 (33.7%), according to Wikipedia; The Kokomo Lantern reported the result as 63-32 [10, 11].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Her campaign says she will "protect parental rights" and "strengthen education by focusing on academics" [14].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says she will "advocate for policies that support working families" [14].

**Public safety**

- Says she will "defend the 2nd Amendment" [14].

**Immigration**

- Says she will "support legal immigration while promoting enforcement of laws already on the books" [14].

**Abortion**

- Says she will "preserve life at all stages" [14].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Davis about Rep. Karickhoff's record or the seat's direction were found; her campaign site says voters would get "a principled, approachable, Hoosier-hearted leader who listens first and fights for families every day" [14].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Her campaign site lists the priorities above and does not provide detailed legislative proposals [14].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Americans for Prosperity-Indiana endorsed her in the primary on March 9, 2026 [25].

### What we couldn't verify

- Her occupation and professional background beyond Moms for Liberty were not found in fetched sources.
- No news interview or candidate questionnaire from Davis was found; she did not attend the Sept. 29 Kokomo forum, per the Lantern's report that Republican state candidates were absent.
- No campaign finance totals were found.
- Primary percentages differ between Wikipedia (66.3-33.7) and The Kokomo Lantern (63-32); the Lantern's figure may reflect Howard County only.
