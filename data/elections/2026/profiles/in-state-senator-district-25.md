# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Mike Gaskill (Republican)

Mike Gaskill, a Pendleton insurance and financial-services business owner, was first elected to the Senate in 2018 and has represented District 25 since 2022 redistricting [3, 4]. He chairs the Senate Elections Committee and was the Senate sponsor of the failed December 2025 congressional redistricting bill [5, 6].

- Committees: chair of Elections; member of Family and Children Services, Insurance and Financial Institutions, and Tax and Fiscal Policy; Assistant Majority Floor Leader for Parliamentary Affairs, according to the Senate Republican caucus [3].
- April 15, 2025: Voted yes when the Senate gave final approval (27-22) to SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law [7].
- April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the conference report for the 2025 state budget (HEA 1001), which passed the Senate 39-11 [8].
- Dec. 5, 2025: As Senate sponsor of HB 1032, said Indiana's representation had been "watered down by the redistricting actions of blue states" and pledged to "foster a civil debate" as Elections chair [6]. Dec. 11, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, which the Senate defeated 19-31 [9]; in closing debate he asked colleagues, "Do you have the will to stand in the gap and fight back?" [10].
- 2026 session: The caucus says Gaskill supported SEA 1 (2026), a welfare bill adding SNAP eligibility checks, barring unauthorized immigrants from benefits and adding Medicaid work requirements; SB 243, conforming state taxes to federal treatment of tips and overtime; and SEA 76 on cooperation with federal immigration enforcement [11]. He said the session ended with results he was "happy with" [12].

## This race

- Senate District 25 covers all of Madison County, including Anderson, plus part of Hamilton County [5]; Dixon-Tatum's campaign identifies the Hamilton County portion as White River Township and the northern part of Jackson Township [13].
- The 2026 race is a rematch: Gaskill beat Dixon-Tatum with 64% of the vote in 2022, after redistricting moved him into the district and longtime Democratic Sen. Tim Lanane did not seek re-election [14].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Gaskill won the Republican nomination over Katherine Callahan with 55% of the vote in Madison County and 58% in the district's two Hamilton County townships; Dixon-Tatum won the Democratic nomination over Todd Shelton with 65% [15].
- Gaskill's priorities include tax cuts, school choice and immigration enforcement [16]; Dixon-Tatum's include housing, cost of living, worker protections, health care and child care, and opposing partisan gerrymandering [13]. Neither candidate answered WFYI's 2026 survey [5]. No debate or forum was found.

## Mike Gaskill

Mike Gaskill is the incumbent and Senate Elections Committee chair, seeking a third term [15, 5]. He campaigns on lower taxes, school choice and immigration enforcement [16].

Campaign website: https://electmikegaskill.com

### Background

- Has owned an insurance and financial-services business in Anderson since 1993; previously served on the South Madison Community School Board and the Madison County Council, according to his campaign [17].
- Graduate of Pendleton Heights High School and Anderson University [3].
- Lives in Pendleton [5].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Katherine Callahan, who had argued that legislative decisions were made without enough transparency and said government "should be accountable to its constituents, not donors and lobbyists" [16, 15].

### Positions & plans

**Taxes**

- Says "I will vote for additional tax cuts so that Hoosiers keep more of their hard-earned income" [16]; his campaign says he has never voted for a tax increase [17].

**Immigration**

- Says he will strengthen Indiana's cooperation with federal officials on deportations and add safeguards to keep people in the country illegally from receiving welfare and Medicare [16].

**Education**

- Plans to expand school choice [16].

**Public safety**

- Plans to increase sentences for violent crimes [16].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Says he will work to safeguard election integrity [16]; sponsored the 2025 mid-decade redistricting bill [6].

**Social issues**

- Campaign lists opposition to abortion, support for Second Amendment rights and traditional family values [17, 5].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements about Dixon-Tatum were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says "Indiana has outpaced all other Midwestern states" economically and that he will keep pursuing tax cuts, school choice and immigration enforcement [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Top donors listed by WFYI from Transparency USA data: Insurance PAC, Indiana BANKPAC, Indiana Multi Family Housing PAC, Indiana Builders PAC and the American Property Casualty Insurance Association's political account; no totals given [5].
- His campaign website lists endorsements including Hoosiers for Quality Education, the Indiana Chamber, the NRA, Right to Life Indiana, Americans for Prosperity and the Indiana Manufacturers Association; the page describes him as a first-term senator, so these may date from an earlier campaign [17].

### What we couldn't verify

- Did not respond to WFYI's 2026 survey.
- Bills Gaskill authored that became law in 2023-2026 were not identified; the caucus page lists bills he supported rather than authored.
- No 2026-specific endorsement list or fundraising totals confirmed.

## Tamie Dixon-Tatum

Tamie Dixon-Tatum is an Anderson Democrat and former UAW member running for the seat a second time after losing to Gaskill in 2022 [13, 14]. Her priorities include housing, cost of living, worker protections, health care and voting rights [13].

Campaign website: https://ctetamiedixontatum.com

### Background

- Lifelong Anderson resident; graduate of Anderson High School, with master's degrees in legal studies (Trinity Law School) and government (Wesleyan University) and a bachelor's in telecommunications from IUPUI, according to her campaign [13].
- Former member of UAW Local 662/663; certificate in grievance handling and negotiations from Indiana University Labor Studies [13].
- Lists experience as a director of civil and human rights, overseeing compliance with fair housing, employment and other anti-discrimination laws [18].
- Was an Anderson city government employee when she ran in 2022 [14].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary over first-time candidate Todd Shelton with 65% of the vote; she said she was not surprised, citing her name recognition in the county [15].
- Was the Democratic nominee for this seat in 2022 [14].

### Positions & plans

**Housing**

- Supports housing stability and affordability and wants to address homelessness and food insecurity with faith and community partners "rather than criminalization" [13].

**Cost of living**

- Says she will address rising taxes and utility costs for families [13].

**Workers**

- Supports living wages, workplace safety and collective bargaining rights [13].

**Health care**

- Supports affordable, accessible health care and child care [13]; lists universal health care among her issues [18].

**Economy & jobs**

- Wants to bring sustainable jobs to the district and support small businesses and farmers [13].

**Education**

- Supports teachers and resources for students from preschool through college and career readiness [13].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Says she stands "against partisan gerrymandering and power grabs" [13].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about Gaskill or his record were found; her campaign says District 25 "deserves strong representation rooted in service and accountability" [13].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Describes herself as a "Listening Voice" for constituents at the Statehouse [18].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Top donors listed by WFYI from Transparency USA data are mostly individuals; no totals given [5].

### What we couldn't verify

- Did not respond to WFYI's 2026 survey; no news interviews about her general-election positions were found.
- Her current occupation is not confirmed (WFYI did not verify it).
- A Blue Voter Guide page (seen only in search results) said she was disqualified from a Democratic primary on May 7 of an unspecified year; this conflicts with her 2026 primary win and ballot listing and is unverified.
- No endorsements or fundraising totals found.
