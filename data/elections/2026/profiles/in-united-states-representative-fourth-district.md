# U.S. Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the U.S. House of Representatives write and vote on federal laws, including the federal budget and taxes. Each represents one congressional district for a two-year term; Indiana has nine [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None
- **Voting members:** 435 [1]
- **Indiana seats:** 9 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces bills and resolutions, offers amendments, and votes on legislation; federal laws must pass both the House and the Senate [1].
- Serves on committees, which draft legislation in their subject areas and oversee federal agencies and programs [1].
- Must be at least 25, a U.S. citizen for at least seven years, and live in the state they represent [1].

## Current officeholder: Jim Baird (Republican)

Jim Baird, a Greencastle farmer, has represented Indiana's 4th District since 2019 and is seeking a fifth term [3]. He serves on the House Agriculture, Science, Space, and Technology, and Foreign Affairs committees [4].

- Committees: Agriculture (vice chair of the Subcommittee on Conservation, Research, and Biotechnology, plus the Livestock, Dairy, and Poultry; Nutrition, Foreign Agriculture, and Horticulture; and Forestry subcommittees); Science, Space, and Technology (Energy; Research and Technology subcommittees); and Foreign Affairs (Africa; Oversight and Intelligence subcommittees) [4].
- May 22, 2025: Voted yes on House passage of H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (the budget reconciliation bill), which passed 215-214 [5].
- July 3, 2025: Voted yes to agree to the Senate's version of H.R. 1, which passed 218-214 and sent the bill to the President [6]. In a statement that day, Baird said the bill "delivers the largest tax cut in American history for workers, families, and seniors" and that it "reduces the deficit"; he also disputed the Congressional Budget Office's baseline assumptions [7].
- September 19, 2025: Voted yes on H.R. 5371, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2026, a stopgap funding bill that passed the House 217-212 [8]. November 12, 2025: Voted yes to agree to the Senate's amended version of H.R. 5371, which passed 222-209 [9].
- January 22, 2026: Voted yes on H.R. 7148, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (passed 341-88) [10]; on February 3, 2026, voted yes to agree to the Senate amendments to the bill, which passed 217-214 [11].
- January 8, 2026: Recorded as not voting on several House votes that day, including passage of a combined Commerce-Justice-Science, Energy-Water, and Interior appropriations bill [12]; the votes came days after Baird and his wife were hospitalized following a crash while he was traveling back to Washington, D.C. [13]. His office said he was expected to make a full recovery [13]. His wife, Danise Baird, died from complications of injuries from the crash, his office announced March 1, 2026 [14].
- February 11, 2026: Voted no on H.J.Res. 72, a resolution relating to a national emergency declared by the President on February 1, 2025, which passed 219-211 [15].
- March 5, 2026: Voted yes on H.R. 7744, the Department of Homeland Security Appropriations Act, 2026, which passed 221-209 [16].
- March 5, 2026: After the House Agriculture Committee approved the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026, Baird said it included "a critical fix for our pork producers by overturning California's Proposition 12" and several of his bills, including the Increased TSP Access Act and the NSF and USDA Interagency Research Act [17]. April 30, 2026: Voted yes on House passage of the bill (H.R. 7567), which passed 224-200 [18].
- March 26, 2026: H.R. 7194, the Nicholas Dockery Medal of Honor Act, which Baird sponsored to authorize the President to award the Medal of Honor to an Army soldier for actions in Afghanistan, became Private Law 119-2 [19].
- April 22, 2026: The House Foreign Affairs Committee voted 25-19 to report Baird's Interagency Coordination in Export Controls Act of 2026 (H.R. 8036) [20]. February 10, 2026: A House subcommittee forwarded his PART Act (H.R. 5221), a bill to prevent theft of catalytic converters and other precious-metal car parts, to the full committee by voice vote [21].
- Other bills Baird introduced in 2025-2026 include the Hemp Planting Predictability Act (H.R. 7024), a bill to delay changes to federal hemp production rules made in the fiscal 2026 agriculture appropriations law (H.R. 7010), the Small Modular Reactor Commercialization Act (H.R. 2813), and the BIO-SCALE Act (H.R. 8918); as of the latest congressional records reviewed, these had been referred to committee [22].
- June 4, 2026: Voted yes on H.R. 8646, the fiscal 2027 Agriculture-FDA appropriations bill, which passed 213-210 [23]. July 21, 2026: Voted yes on H.R. 9770, a continuing appropriations bill for fiscal year 2027, which passed 220-205 [24].
- March 2025: The Federal Election Commission fined Baird's campaign committee $7,475 for leaving a $160,000 loan repayment to Baird off a 2024 pre-primary report; the campaign self-reported the error, and a spokesperson said it "stemmed from a clerical mistake related to software use" and was promptly corrected [25].

## This race

- The 4th District covers west-central Indiana, including Plainfield, Crawfordsville, Lafayette, and West Lafayette, and counties including Boone, Clinton, Hendricks, Montgomery, and Tippecanoe [3]; it comprises 16 counties, including all of Tippecanoe County [26]. Republicans have held the seat for more than three decades [3].
- In a December 2025 Republican-proposed congressional map, Morgan County would have moved from the 4th District to the 9th District [27]. Baird and Indiana's other Republican U.S. House members endorsed the mid-decade redistricting effort in August 2025 [27].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Baird won with 60.5% of the vote; state Rep. Craig Haggard received 30.5%, according to the Indiana Secretary of State as reported by the Purdue Exponent [26]. Haggard had argued during the campaign that Baird's mailers featuring his son, state Rep. Beau Baird, signaled a planned succession; Baird called the comments "baseless" and "disgusting" [28].
- May 5, 2026 Democratic primary: Drew Cox won an eight-candidate field with 30.3% of the vote, about 7.5 points ahead of the next finisher [26].
- Three candidates are on the November 3, 2026 list: Baird (Republican), Cox (Democratic), and David E. Bokash (Independent, declared write-in) [2].
- Key differences in stated priorities: Baird's campaign has listed agriculture and family farms, government spending, border security, Second Amendment rights, rural health care, and veterans [3]; Cox lists single-payer health care, campaign-finance and anti-corruption measures, workers' rights and unions, affordable housing, and a federal moratorium on AI data centers [29].
- As of the reports covering through June 30, 2026, Baird's campaign had raised $358,293 for the 2025-2026 cycle and had $91,561 on hand; Cox had raised $20,377 and had $6,863 on hand [30, 31].

## Jim Baird

Jim Baird, 80, is a farmer from Greencastle who has represented the 4th District since 2019 and is seeking a fifth term [3]. He lists agriculture, border security, and government spending among his priorities [3].

Campaign website: https://baird.house.gov

### Background

- Lifelong district resident; previously served in the Indiana House of Representatives and as a Putnam County commissioner; a veteran, Purdue graduate, farmer, and small-business owner [29].
- Vietnam veteran and two-time Purple Heart recipient [28].
- Has represented the 4th District since 2019 [26].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for re-election in January 2026 [32].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 60.5% of the vote over state Rep. Craig Haggard (30.5%) [26].

### Positions & plans

**Agriculture**

- Lists agriculture and family farms among his priorities [3].
- Supported the 2026 House farm bill, citing its provision overturning California's Proposition 12 for pork producers, and said he wants to "deliver a full, five-year Farm Bill" (statement, March 5, 2026) [17].

**Taxes and federal spending**

- Lists the economy and government spending among his priorities [3].
- Supported the 2025 reconciliation law, saying it cuts taxes for workers, families, and seniors and "prevents a Death Tax increase that would devastate thousands of family farms" (statement, July 3, 2025) [7].

**Immigration and border**

- Lists border security as a priority [3]; his campaign cited border security and energy independence when he filed for re-election in January 2026 [32].

**Guns**

- Lists Second Amendment rights among his priorities [3].

**Health care and veterans**

- Lists rural health care and veterans among his priorities [3].

**Redistricting**

- Said in August 2025 that population migration made it important to redraw districts so "we have people represented in those districts" [33].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder.
- Responding to Haggard's primary challenge in August 2025, Baird said he would "not be distracted by political games" and that his mission "remains serving the people of the 4th District" [28]. In December 2025, after a proposed map moved Haggard's home county out of the district, Baird said "Hoosiers expect their representative to come from the community they want to serve" [27].
- No statements by Baird about Drew Cox or David E. Bokash were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said he looks forward to working with the House and Senate to pass a full five-year farm bill (March 5, 2026) [17].
- Campaign spokesperson said in January 2026 that Baird is focused on turning priorities such as border security and energy independence into results for Indiana [32].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Says he is endorsed by President Donald Trump, Indiana Right to Life, Indiana Farm Bureau, and the NRA Political Victory Fund [3].
- FEC: $358,293 raised and $481,342 spent in the 2025-2026 cycle, with $91,561 cash on hand as of June 30, 2026 [30].

## Drew Cox

Drew Cox, 42, of Lafayette, is a Marine Corps veteran and visiting instructor at Purdue University who won the Democratic primary [3, 34]. His platform centers on single-payer health care, campaign-finance and anti-corruption measures, workers' rights, and a federal moratorium on AI data centers [35].

Campaign website: https://www.drewcox.org/

### Background

- Visiting instructor/limited term lecturer at Purdue University since 2024; served in the U.S. Marine Corps as a sergeant from 2008 to 2012 [34].
- Doctorate in jazz performance from the University of Illinois (2023), master's in music performance from Indiana University (2014), bachelor's from Anderson University (2006), and an associate degree in energy technology from Ivy Tech (2025) [34]. Used the GI Bill for his education at Indiana University [29].
- Describes himself as a lifelong Hoosier raised by a farmer and a public schoolteacher [36, 37].

### Path to the ballot

- Held a campaign launch event in Lafayette on March 7, 2026 [37].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 30.3% of the vote in an eight-candidate field [26].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Supports a single-payer Medicare for All system that includes mental and reproductive health care, and strict limits on prices for essential medications [38].

**Data centers and energy**

- Calls AI data centers "the most present threat to resource security in the United States" and supports a federal moratorium on them, plus a requirement that data centers use green energy [35].

**Campaign finance and ethics**

- Supports overturning Citizens United, public matching of small-dollar donations, a ban on congressional stock trading (H.R. 5106), and the Ban Corporate PACs Act (H.R. 4799); says his campaign takes no corporate money [39].

**Workers and the economy**

- Supports raising the minimum wage, supporting unions, and reviving federal employment programs [35].
- Supports higher tax brackets for the highest earners and closing corporate tax loopholes [35].

**Housing**

- Says he would pursue affordable housing legislation and relief for residents facing high home prices [35].

**Immigration**

- Said "ICE needs to be held to the same standard as every other law enforcement agency" and listed oversight of ICE as a priority (April 2026) [3].

**Veterans and foreign policy**

- Says he will "vote against authorizing any more needless, illegal wars" and supports a well-funded VA [35].
- Says he is committed to pulling military aid from nations committing violent acts, citing Gaza [35].

**Abortion and personal autonomy**

- Says government should respect people's autonomy in decisions about their bodies and personal relationships [35], and called for reproductive freedom at his campaign launch [37].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements by Cox about Baird's votes or record were found in the sources reviewed. At his March 2026 launch, Cox framed the race as "billionaires and corporations versus everyday Hoosiers" and said "our current leaders in Washington aren't delivering" for working-class Americans [37, 36].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Would push to pass the Medicare for All Act [38].
- Says his campaign is focused on increasing young voter participation (May 2026) [26].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign website lists endorsements from the Progressive Voters Network, Track AIPAC, Take Bac Congress, the Indiana AFL-CIO, and DC Statehood PAC [40].
- FEC: $20,377 raised and $13,514 spent in the 2025-2026 cycle, with $6,863 cash on hand as of June 30, 2026 [31].
