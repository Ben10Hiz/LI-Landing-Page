# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Bob Heaton (Republican)

Bob Heaton, a Terre Haute financial services business owner and former Indiana State University basketball player, has represented House District 46 since 2010 [3]. He is the House majority whip and chairs the Higher Education Subcommittee of the Ways and Means Committee, which writes the House budget [3, 4].

- Leadership and committees: Majority whip; chair of the Ways and Means Higher Education Subcommittee; member of Financial Institutions [3].
- 2025 session: Co-authored HEA 1253, which lets school-based child care programs serve children from across a school corporation, extends child care voucher waivers from two to three years and lets multi-site centers operate under one license [5]. Co-authored HEA 1041, which bars biological males from competing in women's sports at the college level [4].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, which passed the House 65-29 [6]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [7]; he said he helped craft the budget as a Ways and Means member [4].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [8].
- 2026 session: Co-authored House Resolution 28, which calls the national debt a national security threat and urges Congress to adopt a structured budget process; it passed the Ways and Means Committee in February 2026 [9].
- October 2026: With Rep. Tim Yocum, welcomed Gov. Mike Braun's suspension of off-road diesel restrictions so farmers could use it for farm hauling during harvest, after lawmakers urged relief [10].

## This race

- House District 46 includes all of Owen County and portions of Clay, Monroe and Vigo counties [3].
- In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Heaton defeated former Brazil Mayor Tom Arthur, 4,479 votes (68.9%) to 2,022 (31.1%), according to Wikipedia's results table [11, 12]. James Pittsford III was the only Democrat listed [11].
- Heaton was re-elected with 67.9% of the vote in 2024 [11].
- No debate or forum for the general election was found.
- Key differences: Pittsford proposes eliminating the state sales tax on residential utility bills and ending state-subsidized vouchers for higher-income families, and says the House and Senate supermajorities have resulted in poor oversight of tax dollars [13]; Heaton voted for and defends the 2025 budget, which expanded school choice to all families, and SEA 1 [4, 7, 6].

## Bob Heaton

Bob Heaton is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2010, and serves as House majority whip [3]. His recent work includes the 2025 budget, child care licensing changes and a resolution on the national debt [4, 5, 9].

### Background

- Grew up on a family farm in Clay County; played basketball at Indiana State University, including the 1978-79 season, and earned a business administration degree [3].
- Has run Heaton Financial Services in Terre Haute since 2003 and has served on several local community boards [3].
- Lives in the Wabash Valley [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Tom Arthur with 68.9% of the vote [11]. Heaton did not fill out the Tribune-Star's pre-primary candidate questionnaire [12].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Supported SEA 1, saying "Two-thirds of homeowners will see a lower property tax bill next year than they did this year" [4, 6].

**Budget & spending**

- Described the 2025 budget as balanced while funding essential services, and said it "increases K-12 tuition support by $640 million over the next two years" [4].
- Said of the national debt: "If we're serious about protecting their future, we have to be serious about restoring fiscal responsibility." [9]

**Child care**

- Said HEA 1253 "cuts unnecessary bureaucracy and gives providers the flexibility they need to grow and serve more families" [5].

**Energy & utilities**

- Said after the 2026 session that "Making sure utilities and energy are more affordable was a key part of that effort," in a release highlighting HEA 1002 on utility performance-based accountability [14].

**Agriculture**

- Said "Lowering operating costs during harvest season means farmers can save money" in welcoming diesel-fuel relief for farmers [10].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Heaton about Pittsford were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- No 2026 campaign platform was found; his caucus releases emphasize keeping costs down and utility affordability [14].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by Indiana Farm Bureau's AgELECT PAC for the 2026 primary [15]. Wikipedia also lists primary endorsements from President Donald Trump, Gov. Mike Braun, the NFIB Indiana PAC and the NRA Political Victory Fund [11].

- No fundraising data found.

- No statements from Heaton on redistricting or data centers were found.

## James H. Pittsford (Jimmy), III

Jimmy Pittsford is a retired union plumber and steamfitter from the Ellettsville area running as a Democrat [16]. He proposes removing the state sales tax from residential utility bills, fully funding public schools and opposing tolls on Interstate 70 [13].

Campaign website: https://www.pittsford4indiana.com/

### Background

- Born in Bloomington, grew up in Ellettsville and graduated from Edgewood High School [16].
- Apprenticed with Plumbers and Steamfitters Local 136 and worked in the trade for 40 years before retiring; served two terms on the local's executive board and one on its finance committee [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only Democrat listed in the May 5, 2026 primary [11, 17].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Proposes eliminating the 7% state sales tax on residential utility bills, which he estimates would save most residential customers about $157 a year [13].

**Education**

- Supports fully funding public schools and "eliminating state subsidized vouchers for our most affluent citizens" [13].

**Transportation**

- Wants more road and infrastructure funding and will "oppose the establishment of making Interstate 70 a toll road" [13].

**Government accountability**

- Says he will work to "restore accountability and public integrity against public corruption in our state government" [13].

**Labor**

- Says the Statehouse has undervalued the working class and that he wants to be its voice in the House [16].

### On the current officeholder

- Does not mention Heaton by name on his site, but says "Super- majorities in the House and Senate have resulted in poor management and oversight of our tax dollars." [13]

### How they say they'd run the office

- His site features a "Pothole Report" series on local road conditions [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign lists endorsements from the Indiana State AFL-CIO, United Mine Workers of America, Plumbers and Steamfitters Locals 136 and 440, Indiana Stonewall Democrats and former state Rep. Vern Tincher [18].

### What we couldn't verify

- Very little independent news coverage of Pittsford was found.
- No fundraising data found.
- His current town of residence is not stated on his site (campaign mailing address is in Ellettsville).
- No statements on property taxes, redistricting or data centers were found.
