# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The district: North Gibson School Corporation covers Patoka, Washington and White River townships in northern Gibson County and runs Princeton Community High, Middle, Intermediate and Primary schools, all in Princeton [24, 25]. It is a consolidation of the former Princeton, Patoka, Hazleton and Mount Olympus schools [26]. Federal data list 1,864 students [27]. Dr. Eric Goggins is superintendent and Noah Velthouse assistant superintendent [25].
- The board has five members: Joe Williams (president), Brock Sievers (vice president), Beth Davis (secretary), Michael Ice and Amy Michelle Bingham [25, 28]. Seats are tied to townships: two represent Patoka Township (Williams and Sievers), one Washington Township (Ice), one White River Township (Bingham), and one is at-large (Davis, a Patoka Township resident) [26]. Members are paid $4,500 a year plus a per-meeting stipend, up from $2,000 in 2025 [28].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: two seats, one member each, matching the state list — District 1 (Washington Township) and District 2 (White River Township) [29, 6]. Both incumbents are running: Ice is unopposed in District 1, and Bingham faces Trevor Cotterman in District 2 [30]. In 2022 Ice and Bingham were each unopposed, according to the county's precinct results [31].
- Party labels: a 2025 state law lets school board candidates declare a party without running in a primary; in December 2025 the board updated its bylaws to drop the nonpartisan requirement and lower the minimum age for candidates from 21 to 18 [32]. Ice and Bingham are listed as Republicans and Cotterman as nonpartisan [6, 30].
- Board districts (December 2025): President Joe Williams proposed replacing the township-based seats with five at-large seats, saying 84% of the district's population lives in Patoka Township; he withdrew the proposal before the December 15 meeting after public feedback, saying it 'could unintentionally create division' [26]. At that meeting Ice moved that the board not change voting districts unless state law required it, then withdrew the motion after the board's attorney said such a motion would not bind future boards [32].
- Budget and SEA 1: in May 2025 Goggins projected a $296,334 drop in state funding over two years, tied to falling enrollment, and a loss of nearly $600,000 in property-tax revenue over three years under the state's property-tax law, while saying the district was better placed than many because a large tax-increment financing (TIF) district was ending [33]. With that TIF district expired in December 2025, the district's tax base rose by $195 million; the board approved a $32.89 million 2027 budget in September 2026, and Goggins said the combined tax rate was likely to stay under $1 per $100 of assessed value [34, 35].
- Facilities: in April 2025 the board unanimously adopted a plan to issue up to $40 million in bonds for campus projects, which consultants said would not raise the debt-service rate [36]. Projects include a new central office ($3.13 million bid), a fieldhouse, football stadium locker and concession buildings, Gil Hodges Field upgrades, a band tower and middle/intermediate school renovations; bonds sold at just under 4.25% interest, and the district expects about $900,000 in interest earnings on unspent proceeds [37, 38, 39].
- Staff pay: in October 2025 the board ratified a one-year teacher contract with a 1.25% raise to the salary base, a 2% stipend and a $45,000 starting salary [40]. In September 2026 Goggins reported a tentative new agreement with the teachers' association, with a public hearing set for October 14 [35].
- Marketing contract: the board voted 4–1 in May 2025 and again in May 2026, with Bingham dissenting both times, to renew a $7,000-a-month marketing contract; Goggins and Williams said it promotes the schools and supports enrollment, while Bingham questioned the cost [33, 41].
- Cell phones: Goggins told the board in April 2026 that the state's bell-to-bell student phone ban would take effect the next school year; board members disagreed over whether to restrict phones on school buses [42].
- Academics: Velthouse reported in April 2026 that iLearn results showed positive results in math, with growth still to come in English/language arts [37].
- Forums: this guide found no candidate forum or questionnaire for the North Gibson race as of October 10, 2026.

## Michael J. Ice — District 1

Incumbent who represents Washington Township on the board; unopposed for re-election [26, 30].

### Background

- A former school bus driver, according to the Daily Clarion [42].
- A Mount Olympus resident who volunteers at community events, including with the Lions Club [43].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for District 1 and is listed as a Republican [30, 6].
- Was elected unopposed in District 1 in 2022 [31].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Supported the up-to-$40 million bond plan in April 2025, saying 'Things have been stable in large part due to the excellent bond rating that this school has,' that the district was 'well-protected' given the expiring TIF district, and that 'This will be the only time we can do that' [36].
- On the marketing contract in May 2025, said he needed to see a report of the work to judge whether the expense was worth supporting [33].

**School safety**

- In the April 2026 discussion of the state's phone ban, said 'I'm definitely in favor of some sort of restriction on a bus,' warning that phone use can make for 'a very disruptive school bus' [42].

**Transparency & community input**

- Opposed converting the board to at-large seats and in December 2025 moved that the board not change voting districts unless state law required it, withdrawing the motion after the attorney said it would not be binding [32].
- In January 2026 moved to develop a policy rotating board officer posts; the motion failed 3–2 [44].

**Facilities**

- Asked in September 2026 to see the middle/intermediate school renovation work while it is in progress [39].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member.
- Called the board's April 2025 bond plan a sound use of the district's financial position: 'I think we've done a very, very good job of putting ourselves in the position where we can do this, and not put ourselves in a bind' [36].
- Was nominated for board vice president in January 2026 but lost 3–2 to Brock Sievers [28].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website, questionnaire or forum answers were found; he is unopposed.
- Current occupation could not be confirmed.
- Search-result snippets (unverified) show a Michael Ice as North Gibson board president in an earlier state audit period; his total years on the board could not be confirmed.
- A 'Michael Ice' was among substitute bus drivers approved by the board in July 2026 (Daily Clarion); this guide could not confirm whether that is the same person.

## Amy Michele Bingham — District 2

Incumbent who represents White River Township on the board; faces Trevor Cotterman [26, 30].

### Background

- Represents White River Township on the board [32].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for District 2 and is listed as a Republican [29, 6].
- Was elected unopposed in District 2 in 2022 [31].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Voted against renewing the $7,000-a-month marketing contract in May 2025 and May 2026, saying 'I just feel like we don't have the extra money for that' and suggesting student classes could make some promotional material [33, 41].
- Asked during the April 2025 bond hearing whether projects could be paid with cash on hand or grants, and said 'Our property taxes are kind of high, and they'll never go down if we don't get this paid off' [36].
- Asked in September 2026 whether bus routes could be merged to save on rising fuel costs [35].

**Teachers & staff**

- Said teachers spend nearly $800 of their own money on supplies, arguing in May 2026 that 'Every kid could have what they need' if the marketing money went to students and teachers; Goggins replied that the contract is paid from operations funds that could not be used for those purposes [41].
- Raised cafeteria workers' concerns about their jobs if food service were contracted out (October 2025) [40].

**Transparency & community input**

- Proposed in January 2026 that board officers serve on a rotating basis; Ice's motion to develop the policy, which she seconded, failed 3–2 [28, 44].
- Questioned Williams in December 2025 about why the at-large proposal came up a second time [32].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member.
- Has dissented on the marketing contract, saying in May 2026 'This money, I kind of feel like this is being thrown away'; Williams called her estimate of total spending 'dramatic,' putting it at about $336,000 over four years, and Sievers said she was 'painting a false picture' about where the money could go [41].
- Was nominated for board secretary in January 2026 but lost 3–2 to Beth Davis [28].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website, questionnaire or forum answers were found.
- Occupation and ties to the schools beyond board service could not be confirmed.
- The state list spells her middle name 'Michele'; the Daily Clarion spells it 'Michelle' and the district website lists 'Amy Michelle Bingham'.

### Path to the ballot

### Positions & plans

**School safety**

- Spoke during public comment in April 2025, asking the district to review its school resource officer policy and amend it to let the officer intervene with students [46].
