# U.S. Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the U.S. House of Representatives write and vote on federal laws, including the federal budget and taxes. Each represents one congressional district for a two-year term; Indiana has nine [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None
- **Voting members:** 435 [1]
- **Indiana seats:** 9 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces bills and resolutions, offers amendments, and votes on legislation; federal laws must pass both the House and the Senate [1].
- Serves on committees, which draft legislation in their subject areas and oversee federal agencies and programs [1].
- Must be at least 25, a U.S. citizen for at least seven years, and live in the state they represent [1].

## Current officeholder: Jefferson Shreve (Republican)

Jefferson Shreve, a Republican, was first elected to the 6th District seat in 2024 after Rep. Greg Pence did not seek reelection, and is seeking a second term [3]. He serves on the House Appropriations Committee, which he joined in April 2026 after serving on the Transportation and Infrastructure and Foreign Affairs committees [4].

- January 14, 2025: Shreve announced his appointments to the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee and the House Foreign Affairs Committee [5].
- May 22, 2025: Voted yes on House passage of H.R. 1, the budget reconciliation bill later titled the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which passed 215–214 [6]. In a statement that day, Shreve said the bill "extends the 2017 tax cuts that deliver relief for working families and local businesses" and "provides enhanced tools for our border patrol agents" [7].
- July 3, 2025: Voted yes on the motion to agree to the Senate's version of H.R. 1, which passed 218–214 and sent the bill to the president [8].
- September 19, 2025: The House Clerk records Shreve as not voting on H.R. 5371, a stopgap funding bill (continuing resolution) that passed 217–212; the same day his office announced that his Dismantling Foreign Scam Syndicates Act had passed the Foreign Affairs Committee as part of a State Department reauthorization bill [9, 10].
- October 1, 2025: Ahead of a possible government shutdown, Shreve said "I will not accept pay during any shutdown period" and that he would donate that pay to the Johnson County Boys and Girls Club in Franklin [11].
- November 12, 2025: Voted yes on the bill to end the government shutdown (agreeing to the Senate amendment to H.R. 5371), which passed 222–209 [12]. Afterward he said, "This shutdown didn't solve a thing — it just created real headaches for the people who count on federal services to work" [13].
- November 18, 2025: Voted yes on the Epstein Files Transparency Act (H.R. 4405), which passed 427–1 [14].
- December 17, 2025: Voted yes on the Lower Health Care Premiums for All Americans Act (H.R. 6703), which passed 216–211 [15].
- February 3, 2026: Voted yes on agreeing to Senate changes to the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (H.R. 7148), which passed 217–214 [16]. On February 12, 2026, his office said $11.7 million for seven district projects, including road, bridge, water and Indianapolis police projects, had been signed into law [17].
- March 5, 2026: Voted yes on the Department of Homeland Security Appropriations Act, 2026 (H.R. 7744), which passed 221–209 [18].
- April 20, 2026: Selected for the House Appropriations Committee, where he serves on the Transportation-HUD, Homeland Security, and Commerce-Justice-Science subcommittees; Shreve described himself as "the first Indiana Republican to serve on this committee in thirty years" [4].
- April 30, 2026: Voted yes on the Farm, Food, and National Security Act (H.R. 7567, the House farm bill), which passed 224–200 [19]. His office said the bill included provisions of his Rural Animal Shelter Investment Act, which makes animal shelters eligible for a USDA community facilities loan and grant program [20].
- May 22, 2026: His office said the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee approved the BUILD America 250 Act, a five-year surface transportation (highway) bill, with provisions he backed on faster environmental reviews, commercial driver training schools, rail crossing safety and freight corridors [21].
- June 24, 2026: His office said all 17 of his Community Project Funding requests, totaling more than $45 million, were approved by the Appropriations Committee, while noting they still require passage by the full House and Senate [22].
- July 21, 2026: Voted yes on a continuing appropriations bill for fiscal year 2027 (H.R. 9770), which passed 220–205 [23].
- September 2, 2026: The House passed his Protecting Domestic Mining Act (H.R. 1501), 218–201, with Shreve voting yes [24]. According to his office, the bill makes mining and mineral processing projects eligible for the FAST-41 federal permitting coordination program [25].
- October 1, 2026: Introduced the Stop SIM Box Scams Act with Rep. Rob Menendez (D-N.J.), which would bar the devices that let callers disguise calls as local numbers [26].

## This race

- The 6th District covers 11 counties in central and eastern Indiana, including the cities of Greenwood, Columbus and Richmond [27], and has a population of nearly 800,000 [3].
- In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Shreve narrowly defeated challenger Sarah Janisse Brown [27]; Hoodline reported he received 53% of the vote [28]. In Wayne County, Shreve received 3,285 votes (57.73%) to Brown's 2,405 (42.27%) [29].
- In the four-candidate Democratic primary, Cinde Wirth defeated William Kory Amyx, Nick Baker and David Lawrence Boyd [3, 27]; Hoodline reported she received 59% of the vote [28].
- The race is a rematch: Shreve defeated Wirth in the 2024 general election by more than 30 percentage points [3], 63.9% to 31.7%, according to Hoodline [28].
- In August 2026, a coalition of 13 nonpartisan groups canceled a planned candidate forum, saying Shreve's campaign never responded to invitations while Wirth's campaign did, according to Hoodline, which cited reporting by The Republic [28]. Shreve campaign spokesman Kory Wood described the event as "a far-left rally masquerading as a nonpartisan forum"; the coalition disputed that characterization [28].
- Both candidates have described affordability and health care as priorities: Shreve's campaign lists making life affordable, border security, cutting government spending and supporting farmers among its issues [30], while Wirth lists Medicare for All, affordability, and reducing corporate influence [31].

## Jefferson Shreve

Shreve is the Republican incumbent, a businessman who founded Storage Express and served two terms on the Indianapolis City-County Council [32]. He is seeking a second term after winning a narrow renomination in May 2026 [27].

Campaign website: https://shreveforcongress.com

### Background

- Founded Storage Express, which later merged into a publicly traded national company, and served on the board of Extra Space [32].
- Attended Indiana University, earned a master's degree in international studies and diplomacy from the University of London, and an MBA in agribusiness from Purdue University [32].
- Served two terms on the Indianapolis City-County Council and lost the 2023 Indianapolis mayoral race to Joe Hogsett [3, 32].
- Lives on the south side of Indianapolis with his wife, Mary [32].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Sarah Janisse Brown [27], with 53% of the vote according to Hoodline [28].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & taxes**

- Supported the 2025 reconciliation bill, saying it "extends the 2017 tax cuts that deliver relief for working families and local businesses" (May 22, 2025) [7].
- His campaign website lists "Make Life Affordable," "Cut Government Spending" and "Champion Small Business" among its issues [30].

**Immigration & border**

- Said the 2025 reconciliation bill "provides enhanced tools for our border patrol agents, who are working to secure our southern border" [7].
- His campaign website lists "Secure the border" as an issue [30].

**Energy & environment**

- Said "The bill bolsters American energy production" in support of the 2025 reconciliation bill [7].
- Sponsored the Protecting Domestic Mining Act, saying "My bill gives qualified mining projects a clear and accountable permitting process" (September 2, 2026) [25].

**Infrastructure & transportation**

- Supported the BUILD America 250 Act highway bill in committee, saying "Hoosiers sent me to Washington to solve practical problems" (May 22, 2026) [21].
- Requested federal project funding for district water, sewer, road, public safety and National Guard projects [22, 17].

**Consumer protection**

- Introduced the Stop SIM Box Scams Act, saying "I wrote this bill to stop scammers from using these machines to pose as our neighbors" (October 1, 2026) [26].

**Other issues**

- His campaign website also lists law enforcement ("back the blue"), supporting the military, "protect the unborn," the Second Amendment, supporting farmers, China, Israel and digital innovation as issues [30].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder.
- His campaign spokesman, Kory Wood, described a canceled August 2026 candidate forum to which Wirth had agreed as "a far-left rally masquerading as a nonpartisan forum" [28].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said of his 17 approved Community Project Funding requests, "My focus now is getting them across the finish line and delivering these investments back home" (June 24, 2026) [22].
- Has listed the economy, energy, health care and veterans affairs as priorities [3].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Shreve for Congress reported $2,426,804.72 in receipts, $950,284.62 in disbursements and $1,608,234.89 cash on hand for January 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026, and reported $3.9 million in debts owed by the committee [33].

## Cynthia (Cinde) Wirth

Wirth is a former high school biology and environmental science teacher and scientist from Columbus who served as a congressional fellow in 2018–2019 [34]. She was the 2024 Democratic nominee and won the 2026 Democratic primary [3, 27].

Campaign website: https://wirth4congress.com

### Background

- Taught biology and environmental science at Columbus Signature Academy for more than 10 years and has owned a community business for more than 20 years, according to her campaign [34].
- Holds a B.A. in biology and Spanish from the University of Evansville (1992), an M.A. in anthropology from Indiana University (2009), and a Ph.D. in educational policy and STEM education from Ball State University (2025) [34].
- Received an Albert Einstein Distinguished Educator Fellowship and worked as a science and education expert in the U.S. House in 2018–2019 [34].
- Describes herself as a seventh-generation Bartholomew County resident; she lives in Columbus [34].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary over three opponents [27, 3], with 59% of the vote according to Hoodline [28]. In Wayne County she received 883 votes (48.17%) [29].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Supports a single-payer system (Medicare for All), a cap on prescription drug costs, and continued protections for preexisting conditions [35].
- Listed Medicare for All as a top priority, saying "healthcare is a right" (October 2026) [31].
- Supports legalizing medical cannabis and full funding for VA health care [35].

**Social Security & Medicare**

- Said "We need to safeguard Social Security by eliminating the income cap" (October 2026) [31]; her campaign website proposes raising the taxable earnings cap to $400,000 [35].

**Economy & taxes**

- Proposes asking wealthy individuals and corporations to pay more, closing tax loopholes, and funding single-payer health care through a progressive payroll tax [31].
- Supports raising the minimum wage, expanding paid family leave, and federal investment in child care [36].
- Supports incentives for first-time homebuyers and criticizes private investors buying homes and raising rents [36].
- Argued that tariffs harm farmers and that the power to levy them belongs to Congress, according to a summary of a September 2026 virtual town hall [37].

**Education**

- Supports more federal funding for public schools, expanded access to higher education, and support for teachers [38].
- Supports expanded firearm background checks and gun-safety programs as school-safety measures [38].

**Energy & environment**

- Supports investment in solar and wind power and restoring federal environmental protections and agency funding [39].
- Opposes data centers on rural land [39], and supports a federal moratorium on new data centers pending more transparency and environmental safeguards, according to a September 2026 town hall summary [37].

**Immigration**

- Reaffirmed support for abolishing ICE at a September 2026 virtual town hall, according to the organizer's summary [37].

**Government & ethics**

- Said "Congress needs to stop fighting for partisan political advantage and actually solve problems for the people we serve" and opposes unrelated "poison-pill" amendments (October 2026) [31].
- Said "I will respect the results" of the election [31].

### On the current officeholder

- At a September 2026 virtual town hall, Wirth said Shreve has refused to hold town halls or debates and will not use congressional oversight tools to check the executive branch, according to the organizer's summary [37].
- Her campaign agreed to the August 2026 candidate forum that was canceled after Shreve's campaign did not respond, according to Hoodline [28].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Plans listening sessions, surveys, a year-round website and hotline, and an annual State of the District survey, saying "My accountability is to constituents, not lobbyists" [31].
- Said she would keep district constituent services that help with veterans' benefits, Social Security and Medicare [31].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- C Wirth for Congress reported $37,046.11 in receipts (including $4,919.40 in candidate loans), $30,400.38 in disbursements and $7,151.09 cash on hand for January 1 through June 30, 2026 [40].
- No endorsements were listed on her campaign homepage [41].

### What we couldn't verify

- Coverage of Wirth's 2026 campaign is thin; most statements come from her campaign website, a Western Wayne News Q&A, and an advocacy group's summary of a town hall rather than independent news reporting.
- The Western Wayne News Q&A labels the office as 'Indiana House District 6' even though her answers concern Congress.
- Certified district-wide Democratic primary totals were not found; the 59% figure comes from Hoodline.
- Statements attributed to Wirth from the Progressive Indiana Network town hall are the organizer's paraphrase, not a transcript.
- No endorsements were found.
- No debate or joint forum between the two candidates was found in coverage reviewed through October 10, 2026.
