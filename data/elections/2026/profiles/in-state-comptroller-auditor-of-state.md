# Indiana State Comptroller (Auditor of State) — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review. Coverage of this race is limited; no debate has been held.

## About the office

Indiana's chief financial officer: keeps the state's accounts, pays the state's bills and its employees, and distributes tax revenue to counties, cities, towns, and school corporations [1]. The constitution still calls the office Auditor of State; the ballot lists it as "State Comptroller (Auditor of State)" [2, 3].

- **Term:** 4 years [4]
- **Term limit:** 8 years in any 12 [4]
- **State employees paid:** 33,000+ [5]
- **Current officeholder:** Appointed 2023 [6]

### What the office does

- Accounting & Reporting: keeps the state's general ledger and publishes the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report, the official report of the state's financial position [5].
- Accounts Payable: pays grants, public aid, school tax revenues, and vendors [5].
- Payroll: pays more than 33,000 state employees [5].
- Distributes tax revenue to local governments [1, 5].
- Runs the Indiana Transparency Portal, which shows state spending [7].
- Chairs the committee overseeing Hoosier START, the state's deferred-compensation retirement plan [8].
- Serves on the three-member State Board of Finance with the state budget director and the treasurer; the board supervises the state's fiscal affairs, can transfer money between state funds and approve loans, and approves banks that hold state funds [9].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025 (SEA 221): set the office's legal name as "State Comptroller (Auditor of State)" and required that wording on the 2026 ballot [2].
- 2025 (SJR 17): a proposed constitutional amendment to rename the office State Comptroller; it must pass a second legislative session before going to voters [2].
- May 2026: a Federal Funding Expenditures Dashboard was added to the Transparency Portal [7].
- 2026 (SEA 14): raises the state's Hoosier START match for employees; the office describes it as budget-neutral, subject to State Budget Committee approval [8].

## Current officeholder: Elise Nieshalla (Republican)

Appointed by Gov. Eric Holcomb in November 2023 to finish the term of Tera Klutz, who resigned; this is her first election for the office [6, 10]. She is on the ballot seeking a full term [11].

- As chair of the Hoosier START committee, moved the plan's investments away from Chinese-entity holdings, following a state law that required the public retirement system to divest; she describes this as her proudest work [12]. In August 2026 the committee added a China-free international fund at the same fee and cut recordkeeping fees [8].
- Leads the State Financial Officers Foundation's national debt task force; in February 2026 supported a legislative resolution describing the national debt as a national security threat [13, 12].
- May 2026: launched the Federal Funding Expenditures Dashboard, reporting that federal grants were 42% of general revenue in fiscal 2025 [7].
- July 2026: announced fiscal-year results of $3.99 billion in reserves and a $1.86 billion surplus [14]. Some legislators argued reserves of that size should go to services or faster relief [15].
- July–September 2026: her office distributed State Board of Finance reimbursements to local governments for revenue lost during the gas-tax suspension [16, 17].
- No audits, ethics complaints, or lawsuits involving the office were found in this review.

## This race

- The office has no primary; each party nominates at its state convention [18].
- Republicans renominated Nieshalla without opposition in June 2026 [19]; Democrats nominated Porter County Clerk Jessica Bailey unopposed on June 6 [20]; Libertarians nominated John Schick in March [21].
- No debate or forum among the comptroller candidates was found as of this review [11].

## Elise Nieshalla

Comptroller since her appointment in late 2023, former Boone County Council president, and real-estate professional. Running on the state's fiscal position and plans to use technology to modernize the office [22, 12].

### Background

- Former president of the Boone County Council and of the Indiana County Councils Association [22].
- More than 20 years in real estate; bachelor's degree from Oral Roberts University and master's from Indiana University, where she was adjunct faculty at the O'Neill School [22].
- Lives in Zionsville [22].
- Sought the Republican nomination for state treasurer in 2022, losing at the convention to Daniel Elliott [6].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced June 26, 2025, the first candidate of any party in the race [10].
- Renominated without opposition at the Republican state convention in June 2026 [19].

### Positions & plans

**Fiscal management**

- Points to Indiana's balanced budget, AAA credit rating, and low debt per capita [10].
- Would simplify the fund numbers used by the State Board of Accounts, the Department of Local Government Finance, and local governments [12].

**Technology**

- Would use artificial intelligence to review accounts payable and flag anomalies, and says data protection is a priority [12, 23].
- Says she has already streamlined timekeeping and the state's financial management system, and wants to share what works with local governments [23].

**Transparency**

- Plans to keep expanding the Transparency Portal [12, 7].

**Retirement investments**

- Plans to continue moving state retirement investments away from Chinese-entity holdings [12, 8].

### On the current officeholder

- She is the current officeholder. No statements by her about her opponents were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she would serve the full four-year term [23].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising totals were found in news coverage.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website was found [24].
- Campaign finance totals were not available in news coverage; state filings were not reviewed.

## Jessica Bailey

Porter County Clerk (elected 2018, re-elected 2022) and former Portage Township school board member. Running on making state spending easier to follow and more scrutiny of State Board of Finance decisions [25, 26].

Campaign website: https://baileyforindiana.com/

### Background

- Grew up and lives in Northwest Indiana [25].
- Elected Porter County Clerk in 2018 and re-elected in 2022; served six years on the Portage Township School Board [25, 27].
- Bachelor's degree from Purdue University Northwest; master's in public administration and nonprofit management from Walden University [27].
- Named Clerk of the Year by the Association of Indiana Counties (2023) [25].
- As clerk, moved Porter County to vote centers and added a mobile polling location [27].
- Points to managing one of the county's largest budgets and paying poll workers as relevant experience [28].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced by the Indiana Democratic Party May 27, 2026 [25]; nominated without opposition at the state convention June 6, 2026 [20].

### Positions & plans

**Transparency**

- Would make the Transparency Portal easier to search (including partial or misspelled names), written in plain language, and kept current: "Hoosiers deserve to see how their tax dollars are spent" [26, 28].
- In a podcast interview, called for publishing contract information, including losing bids [29].

**State Board of Finance**

- "No money should move without an independent set of eyes on it"; says she would ask questions and request documentation before transfers [26].
- Says board meetings are brief with little public discussion, and wants residents to be able to watch them online [28].

**Payments and local governments**

- Lists on-time payments to local governments, state employees, and vendors as a top priority [26, 28].
- Would draw on county experience to help local governments operate more efficiently [18].

**Retirement investments**

- In a podcast interview, said retirement investments should be judged on both returns and ethics, citing the state's China divestment as a precedent [29].

### On the current officeholder

- Argues the office should be held by someone outside the party that controls state government: "I am accountable to no one but Hoosiers" [25, 18].
- Has criticized current State Board of Finance practice as allowing too little public discussion [28].
- No statements naming Nieshalla directly were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Rebuild Transparency Portal search; push for State Board of Finance meetings streamed online; prioritize on-time payments [26, 28].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on her campaign site [26]. Fundraising totals were not found in news coverage.

### What we couldn't verify

- No debate, candidate forum, or WFYI questionnaire answers were found [11].
- Some positions come from a podcast interview; the audio was not reviewed.

## John Schick

Information-technology consultant from Chesterton and three-time Libertarian nominee for this office. No 2026 platform has been published; his earlier campaigns focused on spending transparency [21, 30].

### Background

- Lives in Chesterton; described as a business consultant and former Libertarian Party of Indiana treasurer [21].
- Works as an information-technology consultant; earlier supervised a data center at Inland Steel [31, 32].
- Libertarian nominee for this office in 2018 and 2022 as well [33, 30].

### Path to the ballot

- Nominated at the Libertarian state convention in Fort Wayne, March 2026 [21, 34].

### Positions & plans

**Statements from earlier campaigns**

- 2018: called for more transparency in county-level spending, noting that much state revenue is passed to counties, and said he would work on the state's online transparency portal [33].
- 2022: said the office could provide more independent analysis of the effects of laws and mandates passed by lawmakers and the governor [30].

### On the current officeholder

- No 2026 statements about the incumbent were found.

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising figures were found [31].

### What we couldn't verify

- No 2026 campaign website, questionnaire answers, or forum appearances were found [35]; he did not respond to a University of Indianapolis student newspaper's interview request [36].
- Sources differ on his education and employer history.
