# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Wendy McNamara (Republican)

Wendy McNamara, an Evansville Republican, has represented House District 76 since 2010 and chairs the House Courts and Criminal Code Committee [3]. She directs EVSC Early College for the Evansville Vanderburgh School Corporation [3, 4].

- Chairs the Courts and Criminal Code Committee and serves on Roads and Transportation, per the House Republican caucus [3]. In August 2026 she chaired the interim study committees on Public Health, Behavioral Health and Human Services and on Corrections and Criminal Code [5].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (passed 65-29) [6]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 budget (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [7].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [8]. She told WNIN she would vote the same way again, citing concern about federal funding for Posey and Vanderburgh counties: "I wasn't willing to make that risk of not supporting that" [9].
- Jan. 28, 2026: Voted yes on HB 1002, the 2026 electric utility affordability bill (passed 89-4) [10]; she called it "a big step forward" [9].
- 2026 session: Authored HEA 1116, which prohibits cryptocurrency kiosks statewide; she said "These kiosks are a tool bad actors use to target seniors" [11]. Authored HEA 1303, which creates a separate code section for child sexual abuse material offenses, a new distribution crime and higher felony levels [12].
- Earlier laws the caucus lists as hers include juvenile justice reform (2022), a competency-to-stand-trial bill (HB 1238, 2024) and trucker training on spotting human trafficking (HEA 1196, 2023) [3]. She told WNIN that juvenile justice reform is the work she is proudest of [9].

## This race

- House District 76 covers all of Posey County and part of Vanderburgh County [3], including Evansville's west side, Mount Vernon, New Harmony and Poseyville, with about 65,000 constituents [9].
- McNamara is seeking a ninth term; WNIN reports no Democrat has held the seat since 2008 [9]. She was reelected without opposition in 2024 [13].
- Both McNamara and Logan Patberg were unopposed for their parties' nominations in the May 5, 2026 primary [13].
- Forums: Both candidates spoke at an Aug. 30, 2026 meet-the-candidates event hosted by the Posey County Farm Bureau [9]. The Evansville NAACP and League of Women Voters of Southwestern Indiana invited all local and state candidates on the ballot to a Sept. 12 forum in Evansville [14]; attendance was not reported.
- Key differences: On energy costs, Patberg proposes removing the sales tax on residential utility bills and capping how fast investor-owned utilities can raise rates, while McNamara points to the state utility regulator's role and the 2026 utility law (HB 1002) [9]. McNamara supported the 2025 congressional redistricting bill; Patberg's position on it was not reported [9].

## Logan A. Patberg

Logan Patberg is a public school science teacher and Evansville Teachers Association board member making his first run for office [4]. He calls energy affordability his top issue [9].

Campaign website: https://www.patbergforindiana.com

### Background

- Teaches science in the Evansville-area school district he attended [4, 15]. Serves on the Evansville Teachers Association board and chairs its Advocacy Committee [4].
- Describes himself as a lifelong resident of Southwestern Indiana and a union member [15, 9]. Has not held elected office [4].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed in early February 2026 [9, 16]; unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [13].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Proposes eliminating the 7% sales tax on residential utility bills, capping how quickly investor-owned utilities can raise rates (with safety and reliability exceptions) and considering retail energy competition with consumer protections [9].
- Lists keeping energy costs affordable for ratepayers as a priority [17].

**Data centers**

- "Data center sales tax extensions should be repealed, because 50 years of a corporate handout is way too long" [9].

**Education**

- Wants public schools funded at their pre-2008 level and expanded career and technical education; opposes cuts to public higher education [17].

**Health care**

- Says he will "Fight to protect Medicaid/HIP access," expand CHIP (Hoosier Healthwise) for children and lower prescription drug costs [17].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports expanding collective bargaining and workforce development partnerships; says Indiana "needs to have paid leave laws" and more child care capacity [17].

### On the current officeholder

- Says lawmakers have not answered concerns about jobs, benefits, child care and energy costs well in recent sessions; WNIN reported he did not name McNamara directly on most issues [9].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he wants to give educators, union members and "regular folks" a voice in Indianapolis [9].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements were listed on his campaign site, and no campaign finance totals were found [18].

### What we couldn't verify

- No position found on the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) or the 2025 congressional redistricting vote.
- Campaign finance totals not found.
- A WNIN photo caption from the Feb. 3 filing listed Patberg for District 78 and Busby for District 76 in one caption and the reverse in another; the ballot list places Patberg in District 76.

## Wendy (Mac) McNamara

Wendy McNamara has represented District 76 since 2010, chairs the House Courts and Criminal Code Committee and directs EVSC Early College [3]. She cites juvenile justice, anti-trafficking and child-protection laws as her main legislative work [9].

Campaign website: https://www.wendymcnamara.com

### Background

- Spent 31 years with the Evansville Vanderburgh School Corporation as a teacher and administrator and created its Early College High School [9]; the caucus lists her as a former teacher, social studies supervisor and assistant principal and adjunct professor [3].
- Member of the Justice Reinvestment Advisory Council and a non-voting member of the Indiana Criminal Justice Institute Board of Trustees [4]. Lives in Evansville [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [13].

### Positions & plans

**Public safety**

- Lists mental health, corrections and support for law enforcement among her priorities and says she is drafting bills on alert systems and competency to stand trial [9].
- On HEA 1303: "These predators can do lifelong damage to children" [12].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says rate-setting rests with the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission and calls HB 1002 "a big step forward," citing relief for lower-income customers and limits on payments [9].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Says she would again vote for the December 2025 congressional map, citing an estimated $25–30 million a year in federal funding for Posey County and $250–300 million for Vanderburgh County; says constituent responses were mixed [9].

**Education**

- Plans to expand career and technical education and improve students' transitions to college or work [9].

**Consumer protection**

- Authored the statewide ban on cryptocurrency kiosks, saying it gives the attorney general a path to penalize scammers [11].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. In response to Patberg's critique on energy costs, she pointed to HB 1002 and the complexity of rate-setting; WNIN reported no direct statements about him [9].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says lawmaking requires working across the aisle and that legislators must balance party ideals with constituent needs [9].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No 2026 endorsements or campaign finance totals were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Campaign website appears outdated (news items from 2019); no 2026 issue pages found.
- No statement found on the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) or data centers.
- Campaign finance totals not checked/found.
