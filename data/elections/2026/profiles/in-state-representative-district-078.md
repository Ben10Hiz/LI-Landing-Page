# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Tim O'Brien (Republican)

Tim O'Brien, an Evansville Republican and real estate broker, has represented House District 78 since 2021, when a Republican caucus chose him to fill the seat Holli Sullivan left on becoming Secretary of State [3, 4]. He chairs the House Ways and Means K-12 subcommittee [3].

- Serves on Government and Regulatory Reform, Ways and Means (chair of its K-12 subcommittee) and Veterans Affairs and Public Safety, per the House Republican caucus [3].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (passed 65-29) [5]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 budget (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [6]. Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [7].
- Jan. 28, 2026: Voted yes on HB 1002, the 2026 electric utility affordability bill (passed 89-4) [8].
- 2026 session: Authored HEA 1250, requiring the Department of Correction to give victims and local police seven days' electronic notice before a serious violent felon's release; co-authored HEA 1001, the House Republican housing bill that limits local regulations the caucus says raise home prices [9].
- Laws the caucus lists as his include a 2022 requirement that school boards allow public comment, 2023 protection of firefighters' personal information online, and 2025 laws creating an Indiana-Ireland trade commission and pediatric and breast cancer research grants [3].
- Critique: Challenger Sally Busby's campaign says O'Brien voted against eleven consumer-protection amendments to HB 1002 and links his campaign contributions from real estate and utility PACs to his legislation [10, 11]. No response from O'Brien was found.

## This race

- House District 78 covers parts of Vanderburgh and Warrick counties in southwestern Indiana [3]; Busby describes campaigning from McCutchanville to Newburgh [10].
- O'Brien was reelected without opposition in 2024 [12]. Both O'Brien and Sally Busby were unopposed for their parties' nominations in the May 5, 2026 primary [12].
- Forums: The Evansville NAACP and League of Women Voters of Southwestern Indiana invited all local and state candidates on the ballot to a Sept. 12, 2026 forum in Evansville [13]; which candidates attended was not reported. No head-to-head debate was found.
- Key differences: Busby centers her campaign on utility rate caps and public school funding instead of vouchers [14]; O'Brien cites state investments in education, housing supply and child care [15, 9].

## Sally Busby

Sally Busby is an assistant professor in the University of Evansville School of Education and a former middle school teacher, making her first run for office [16, 17]. She focuses on utility bills and public school funding [10].

Campaign website: https://www.sallyforindiana.com

### Background

- Assistant professor at the University of Evansville School of Education; previously taught middle school for fifteen years; holds a doctorate in literacy research [16, 17].
- Active in Rotary, Bridge, the NAACP and the League of Women Voters; has not held elected office [16]. Lives in District 78 [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Feb. 3, 2026 at the Vanderburgh County elections office [18]; unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [12].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Supports "real rate caps and accountability" for utilities such as CenterPoint, statutory caps on single-year rate hikes, outage compensation, repeal of the SB 271 carve-out and legalizing community solar and wind micro-grids [14].
- Says she opposes rate increases beyond what the state Office of Utility Consumer Counselor found justified [10].

**Education**

- "Fully fund public schools instead of draining them to pay for vouchers"; supports smaller classes and support for students who learn differently [14]. Says voucher funding "should follow a kid into a classroom that has to take them" [10].

**Health care**

- Seeks to "Protect and expand access to affordable healthcare for working families" and support families caring for relatives with disabilities [14].

**Housing**

- Supports protections against "unfair rent hikes and surprise fees" and accountability for landlords who do not maintain safe homes [14].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports apprenticeship pathways, collective bargaining rights and investment in the trades and health care workforce [14].

### On the current officeholder

- Says her opponent "voted no on every single one" of eleven consumer-protection amendments to HB 1002 and that she "will vote yes" [10].
- Her campaign's accountability page lists real estate, builder and utility PAC contributions to O'Brien and criticizes housing, utility and education bills he co-authored or supported, including HB 1001 (2026) and HB 1136 [11].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she holds regular town halls and wants a representative who is present year-round [17]. Says she is not taking corporate PAC money [10].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements listed on her campaign site; she says she is not accepting corporate PAC money [10]. No campaign finance totals found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No position found on the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1), data centers or redistricting.
- Campaign finance totals not found.
- A podcast episode (seen in search results only) discusses her reasons for running; not reviewed.
- Her campaign's figures for projected utility bill savings are campaign estimates and were not verified.

## Tim O'Brien

Tim O'Brien is a realtor who has represented District 78 since 2021 and chairs the Ways and Means K-12 subcommittee [3, 16]. He cites investments in education, housing and child care in seeking reelection [15, 9].

Campaign website: https://timobrienforstaterep.com/

### Background

- Real estate broker; economics graduate of the University of Southern Indiana (2015); in 2019 became the youngest president of the Southwest Indiana Association of Realtors [3].
- Chairs the Heritage Federal Credit Union board and has served on the Evansville Land Bank board; named 2025 Easterseals Evansville Legislator of the Year and a 2025 Boys & Girls Clubs of Indiana Legislative Champion [3, 16]. Lives in Evansville [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [12].

### Positions & plans

**Housing**

- On HEA 1001: "These new laws will help expand housing supply" [9]. Co-authored a 2026 bill limiting HOA fee increases and protecting home-based child care [3].

**Public safety**

- Authored the 2026 law on advance notice to victims and police before a serious violent felon's release [9].

**Education**

- Points to "historic investments in education" as an accomplishment [15].

**Child care**

- Co-sponsored SEA 4, allowing state reserve money to fund the Child Care and Development Fund voucher program [9].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. Said serving the district is "an honor I never take for granted" and "there's more to do" [15]. No statements about Busby were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says "Hoosiers deserve leaders who stay focused on real-world results" [15].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No 2026 endorsements or campaign finance totals were found; Busby's campaign lists contributions it says he received from real estate, utility and other PACs in 2022–2026 [11].

### What we couldn't verify

- Campaign website has no issue pages.
- No statement found from O'Brien on utility rates, data centers, the 2025 property-tax law or his redistricting vote.
- Campaign finance totals not independently checked.
- Busby's specific claims (eleven HB 1002 amendment votes, PAC totals, HB 1136 school-district provisions, SB 271/HB 1209) were not verified against roll calls or filings.
- The WEHT reelection story's exact publication date was not shown.
