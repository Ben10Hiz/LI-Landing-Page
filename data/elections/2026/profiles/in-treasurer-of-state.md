# Indiana Treasurer of State — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review. No debate has been held in this race; investment figures are the office's own.

## About the office

The state's banker and chief investment officer. The Treasurer invests state money not needed for immediate bills, chairs boards that run Indiana's college-savings, disability-savings, local-bond, and 911 programs, and sits on the State Board of Finance [1, 2, 3].

- **Term:** 4 years [4]
- **Term limit:** 8 years in any 12 [4]
- **Managed daily:** $16 billion+ (office figure) [1]
- **Held by:** Republicans since 1979 [5]

### What the office does

- Invests state funds; investment earnings go to the state's General Fund, and the legislature decides how they are spent [6].
- Chairs the Indiana Bond Bank, which helps local governments get lower-cost financing for public projects [1, 2].
- Chairs the Indiana Education Savings Authority (the Indiana529 college and career savings plan) and the ABLE Authority (INvestABLE Indiana accounts for people with disabilities) [1, 2].
- Chairs the Statewide 9-1-1 Board [1, 2].
- Runs TrustINdiana, the investment pool for local governments, and is sole trustee of the Indiana State Police Pension Trust [1].
- Member of the three-member State Board of Finance (with the budget director and comptroller) and of the Indiana Finance Authority [3, 7].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2023 (HEA 1008): requires the Treasurer to review financial firms serving the state's public retirement system that it has reason to believe made environmental, social, or governance (ESG) commitments [8].
- 2025: the state budget changed oversight of TrustINdiana, creating a board starting July 1, 2026 [9]. Elliott opposed the original budget language and later thanked state leaders for changes he said protected the program [10]. Published accounts differ on how much authority the new board has.

## Current officeholder: Daniel Elliott (Republican)

Elected in 2022 over Democrat Jessica McClellan and took office in January 2023 [11, 12]. He is on the ballot seeking a second term [13].

- Investments: the office reports more than $1.2 billion in General Fund investment earnings since he took office [1]. Elliott describes the goal as predictable income for budget writers: "These are dollars that have got to be there when the time comes to pay the state's bills" [6]. These are the office's figures, not an independent audit.
- June 2024: under the 2023 law, placed BlackRock on the state's ESG watch list. BlackRock responded that it is focused on performance and takes direction from its clients [8].
- December 2024: the Indiana Public Retirement System board voted to stop using BlackRock as an asset manager after Elliott argued the firm was ineligible under the 2023 law [14].
- 2025: the office reported growth in INvestABLE disability-savings accounts and expanded the 529 plan, renamed Indiana529, to cover trade schools and apprenticeships [15, 16].
- 911: his campaign cites training for more than 400 dispatchers and the launch of text-to-911 and translation services [17].
- March 2026: raised concerns about the proposed sale of AES Corp., parent of utility AES Indiana, to a group of investors including a BlackRock affiliate, while acknowledging the office has no direct authority over the sale. AES said the deal would deliver value to stockholders [18].

## This race

- The office has no primary; each party nominates at its state convention [19].
- Elliott was renominated without opposition at the Republican convention in June 2026 [13, 2]; Coumba Kebe was the only Democrat to file and was nominated June 6 [19, 5].
- No debate or forum between the treasurer candidates was found as of this review [13].

## Daniel Elliott

Incumbent treasurer since 2023 and former Morgan County Council member and software company founder. Says his top priority is continuing strong investment returns [12, 16].

Campaign website: https://danielelliott.org

### Background

- Grew up in Jennings County and lives in Morgan County; founded a software company in Martinsville [12].
- Bachelor's degree in business and computer science from Utah Valley University [12].
- Served on the Morgan County Council (elected 2016), led the county redevelopment commission, and chaired the Morgan County Republican Party [12, 2].

### Path to the ballot

- Launched his re-election campaign Sept. 17, 2025 [17]; renominated without opposition at the Republican state convention June 20, 2026 [2].

### Positions & plans

**Investments**

- "My top priority as State Treasurer is to continue to make good returns" [16].
- Opposes considering ESG commitments in public investment decisions and has applied the 2023 law to the state's retirement-system managers [8, 14].

**Savings programs**

- Wants more families using the 529 plan, which now also covers trade schools and apprenticeships [16].

**Utilities**

- Says ratepayers should not carry all of the financial risk in utility ownership changes, while noting the office has no direct authority over utilities [16, 18].

**911**

- Points to dispatcher training and text-to-911 as results he would build on [17].

### On the current officeholder

- He is the current officeholder. No statements by him about his opponent were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Continue investment returns and expand use of the 529 plan [16]. No detailed second-term agenda was found.

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign site [20]. Fundraising totals were not found in news coverage.

### What we couldn't verify

- He cited $1.8 billion in returns in an October 2026 interview [16]; the period that figure covers was not stated.
- Reported purchases of Israel bonds (2023–2024) could not be confirmed from a full article for this draft.
- Accounts differ on the authority of the new TrustINdiana oversight board.

## Coumba Kebe

Health care operations executive and consultant from Noblesville in her first general-election campaign. Says she would review the state's investments and explain them in plain language [21, 22].

Campaign website: https://www.kebeforindiana.com/

### Background

- Age 31, of Noblesville; describes herself as a first-generation American [21, 23].
- Bachelor's degree and Master of Public Health from Indiana University [22, 24].
- Founded Scope Consulting, which works with home care agencies; previously a director of operations managing multimillion-dollar budgets [25, 22].
- Lost the May 2026 Democratic primary for Indiana House District 29, 53.7% to 46.3% [24]. First drew attention through social-media videos on Medicaid and home health policy [23].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced by the Indiana Democratic Party May 27, 2026, as the only Democrat to file [21, 19]; nominated without opposition June 6, 2026 [5].

### Positions & plans

**Investments**

- Says her "top priority would be to understand our current investment profile" and report it to the public in plain English [22].
- Describes the job as protecting taxpayer dollars through low-risk investments that maximize returns [22].
- Has questioned how the state built roughly $4 billion in reserves, citing the Medicaid waiver waiting list and rural hospital closures [22].

**Savings programs**

- Would expand the state's 529 college-savings program [26, 27].

**Transparency**

- Wants state financial data presented so residents can understand it: "We've got to make it easy for folks to understand" [19].
- Notes the treasurer serves on boards that make statewide decisions but does not set policy, and can report findings to the public [22].

**Long-term planning**

- Asks whether Indiana is prepared for the next 20 years, citing workforce shortages, an aging population, and health care access [19].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements naming Elliott were found. In her nomination speech she said the office "can be about much more than just spreadsheets and investment reports" [5].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Review the state's investment profile and publish it in plain language; expand the 529 program [22, 26].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Campaign co-chairs: state Reps. Earl Harris Jr. and Cherrish Pryor, and Hamilton County Democratic Chair Josh Lowry [28, 26, 27].
- Fundraising totals could not be confirmed for this review.

### What we couldn't verify

- No published positions found on ESG-related investment rules, bank deposits of state funds, ABLE accounts, or the Bond Bank.
- Her campaign's issues page was unavailable; positions come from interviews and announcements [29].
