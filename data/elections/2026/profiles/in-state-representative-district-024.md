# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Hunter Smith (Republican)

Rep. Hunter Smith (R-Zionsville), a farmer and former NFL punter, was first elected in 2024 and serves on the Agriculture and Rural Development, Education, and Environmental Affairs committees, according to the House Republican caucus [3]. He won 56.2% of the vote in 2024, per Wikipedia's compilation [4].

- November 2024: Took the oath of office on Organization Day, saying his goal was to represent "the voices and values of House District 24" [5].
- December 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [6, 7].
- 2026 session: Authored House Enrolled Act 1424, which the caucus says expands the ability of small farms and homestead vendors to sell products from their own properties and sets guidelines for labeling, handling, refrigeration, transportation and storage; it took effect July 1, 2026 [8].
- August 2026: Hosted Gov. Mike Braun at his farm to mark HEA 1424; Smith said, "Government should make it easier not harder for Hoosier farmers and local producers to grow, make and sell food" [8].
- 2026 session votes recorded by BillTrack50 include yes votes on HB 1177 (child care assistance), SB 1 (human services matters), SB 76 (immigration matters) and SB 12 (prohibition of ranked choice voting), and no votes on SB 91 (syringe exchange program) and SB 277 (Indiana Department of Environmental Management) [6].

## This race

- House District 24 covers parts of Carmel, Sheridan and Westfield in Hamilton County and Zionsville in Boone County, according to the House Republican caucus [3]; The Reporter describes it as Westfield, Sheridan, portions of western Carmel and portions of eastern Boone County [9].
- Smith is seeking a second term; he won the open seat in 2024 after Rep. Donna Schaibley (R-Carmel) did not seek reelection [10, 4].
- Wikipedia's compilation lists Smith and Bleicher as the only declared candidates in their respective May 5, 2026 primaries [4].
- The Indiana Capital Chronicle listed the district among Democratic flip targets as Democrats seek four seats to end the Republican supermajority, noting Smith prevailed over an "aggressive" Democratic challenger in 2024 [11].
- No 2026 debate or forum between Smith and Bleicher was found in reviewed coverage.
- On HB 1032 redistricting, Smith voted yes [6]; Bleicher's campaign site lists fully funded public schools, community investment and balancing corporate growth with agricultural heritage as priorities [12].

## Hunter Smith

Smith is the first-term incumbent, a Zionsville farmer and former Indianapolis Colts punter [3].

### Background

- Owns and operates WonderTree Farm, which the caucus says focuses on regenerative farming practices [3].
- Punted for the Indianapolis Colts from 1999 to 2008 and then two seasons for Washington; graduated from the University of Notre Dame in 1999 [10].
- Lives in Zionsville [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed by Wikipedia's compilation as the only declared Republican candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Agriculture**

- Authored HEA 1424 on local food producers; said the law "supports local Indiana-made products, gives Hoosiers more choices at the local level" [8].

**Energy & utilities**

- At his 2023 campaign launch, named the environment, including conservation, farming and food, as a top priority and said neither party owns environmental issues [10].

**Education**

- Said in 2023 that he supports school choice; his children have attended public, private and home schools [10].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted yes on HB 1032, the December 2025 congressional redistricting bill [6], and yes on SB 12, prohibiting ranked choice voting, in 2026 [6].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Smith about Bleicher were found.
- In 2023 the Indiana Democratic Party criticized Smith over his affiliation with the Indiana Family Institute; Smith rejected the label the party used and said people often judge others on a single group affiliation without knowing them [10].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said in 2023 he would prioritize bipartisanship, balance and dialogue and start by listening and learning [10].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or 2026 fundraising totals were found in reviewed sources.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website or 2026 candidate questionnaire was found.
- Individual votes on SEA 1 (2025 property tax) and HEA 1001 (2025 budget) were not verified.
- Search-result snippets mention 2026 bills he authored (HB 1425 social service provider tax credit, HB 1426-1428, HR 26 on disabled veterans' property tax credits) via a third-party tracker; not verified.
- No campaign finance data was reviewed.

## Racheal Bleicher

Bleicher is a Westfield resident and marketing professional making her first run for the House [9, 13].

Campaign website: https://www.rachealforindiana.com

### Background

- Moved to the district in 2020 and lives in Westfield [9].
- Vice president of marketing for the Junior League of Indianapolis; volunteers with Changing Footprints, Gleaners Food Bank and the Indiana Diaper Bank, according to The Reporter [9].
- Described by the DLCC as a business leader from Westfield [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced her candidacy Sept. 30, 2025 [9]; listed by Wikipedia's compilation as the only declared Democratic candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Her campaign lists "Fully-Funded Public Schools" as a priority: "Giving every child the opportunity to get a world-class education" [12].

**Economy & jobs**

- Lists "Effective Community Investment": "Ensuring our tax dollars are used wisely to fix our roads, support our main streets, and keep our neighborhoods safe" [12].
- Says she will promote economic growth while keeping government finances responsible and transparent [14].

**Agriculture**

- Lists "A Balanced Future": "Fostering smart corporate growth while protecting the agricultural heritage that makes Indiana special" [12].

**Housing**

- Her site supports development that prioritizes sustainable growth while preserving the community's character, and lists environmental protection as a priority [14].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about Smith's record were found. At her launch she said she is running so that "everyone in House District 24 can truly be represented" [9].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Cites the district's rapid growth and changing demographics as reasons for her run [9].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Her campaign site lists support from groups including Moms Demand Action, Run for Something, the Indiana AFL-CIO, AFT Indiana, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates and Indiana Reproductive Freedom PAC [14].
- The Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee lists her as a target race [13, 11].
- Hamilton County Democratic Party Chair Josh Lowry called her "the perfect match for House District 24" [9].

### What we couldn't verify

- Current Publishing coverage of her launch (youarecurrent.com) could not be loaded.
- The Indiana Capital Chronicle voter guide (403) reportedly includes her answers, including support for capped rather than eliminated property taxes and community input on data centers; seen only in search snippets, not verified.
- No campaign finance data was reviewed.
