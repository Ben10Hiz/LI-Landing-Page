# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Mooresville Consolidated School Corporation serves the Town of Mooresville and Brown, Madison and Harrison townships in northern Morgan County, southwest of Indianapolis [24].
- The district runs Mooresville High School, Paul Hadley Middle School and five elementary schools (Neil Armstrong, Newby, North Madison, Northwood and Waverly) [25]; Mooresville High School enrolled about 1,380 students in 2023-24 [24]. A district-wide enrollment figure was not found.
- The superintendent is Dr. Jake Allen, who describes himself as a 20-year employee of the district [26].
- The Board of School Trustees has five members; the district website lists seats by area: Tim Cummins (president, Harrison Township), Jen Reed (vice president, Madison Township), Matt Saner (secretary, Brown Township), Dave Oberle (Town of Mooresville) and Deon Brimmage (at-large) [25].
- In February 2025 Indiana State Board of Education staff recommended approval of a change to the corporation's governing body plan that the school board had initiated by resolution [27]. The memo reviewed does not describe the new seat structure.
- Three seats are on the 2026 ballot, each electing one member: District 1, where board member Dave Oberle is unopposed; District 2, where board member Matt Saner is unopposed; and District 5 (listed by the state as At Large, District 5), where appointed board member Deon Brimmage faces Sharon (France) Atwood and Lea Sharp [28, 6].
- All five 2026 candidates are listed as Republicans; 2026 is the first Indiana election in which school board candidates may run with a party label, and no primary is held, so several candidates of one party can run for the same seat [28, 29, 6].
- Brimmage was appointed to the board at the end of 2024 to fill the vacancy left by the death of board member Dr. Bill Roberson [28, 30].
- Budget (2027): the district advertised a 2027 budget of $61.5 million, including $34.1 million for the education fund, $16.2 million for operations and $9.1 million for debt service, with a public hearing on September 8, 2026 and adoption set for October 6, 2026; the notice estimates $845,400 in property tax cap credits [31].
- Borrowing (2026): in June 2026 the board preliminarily determined to issue up to $6 million in bonds for renovations and improvements across the district, including site and athletic work, technology, buses and equipment [32]; in September 2026 it preliminarily determined to issue up to $5.5 million in bonds to refinance Series 2019 building corporation bonds [33].
- Career programs (December 2025): the board approved an agreement with the Mooresville Redevelopment Commission to establish a Paxton-Patterson career center at Paul Hadley Middle School starting in 2026-27 [34].
- Board meetings: regular meetings are generally held the second Tuesday of each month at the Education Center, with recordings posted on demand [35].
- No candidate forum was found. The Morgan County Correspondent's October 2026 voter guide published questionnaire answers from Brimmage and Sharp for District 5; Atwood did not respond, and the uncontested District 1 and 2 races were not included [36].

## Deon Brimmage — At Large, District 5

Incumbent appointed to the board at the end of 2024, running in his first school board election as a Republican; works as a strategic deployment specialist at Franciscan Health [28, 36, 6].

### Background

- Strategic deployment specialist at Franciscan Health; says he leads lean and rapid-improvement projects and manages projects to keep leaders on deadline [36].
- Bachelor of science in business administration, University of Indianapolis, 1998 [36].
- Volunteer coach for Mooresville wrestling; has a child in the district's schools [36].

### Path to the ballot

- Appointed by the board in late 2024 to fill a vacancy; this is his first board election [28, 30].
- Listed on the ballot as a Republican [6]. Said he does not believe partisan school board races are necessary because board members must represent all families, and that he chose his affiliation because it reflects his personal values [36].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Says referendums are "required at times but only as a last resort," and that schools should show they have been financially responsible and tried cost-saving measures before asking taxpayers for more [36].
- Lists effective allocation of resources with fiscal accountability as a core board duty [36].

**Academics**

- Says SAT scores are not the only measure of success but that periodically offering test-preparation resources or classes would help raise scores [36].
- Supports AI in schools "only as a tool to assist in the learning process," with students taught to use it responsibly and ethically [36].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- To attract families, would continue strong academic and extracurricular programs, safe schools and career-focused pathways, and highlight student success [36].

**Facilities**

- His top priority for a large donor-funded project would be a career and innovation center offering hands-on work in technology, skilled trades, engineering, health care and advanced manufacturing [36].

**Transparency & community input**

- Says the board "does an excellent job of transparency," noting meetings are published in advance, open to the public and open to public comment [36].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member [25]. Describes the board's transparency positively and says he wants to build stronger relationships with staff and faculty [36].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he would advocate for policies that keep schools safe and supportive for students and staff [36].

### Endorsements & fundraising

### What we couldn't verify

## Lea Sharp — At Large, District 5

Payroll specialist in the Mooresville school district's business office and first-time board candidate, running as a Republican [36, 28, 6].

### Background

- Payroll specialist; lists 16 years as a Mooresville schools employee (payroll) and says she works in the district's business office [36].
- Retired from UPS, where she was a part-time safety supervisor; also describes herself as a business owner/operator [36].
- Associate degree from ITT Technical Institute, with additional business courses at the University of Indianapolis; has lived in the Mooresville area for more than 40 years [36].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed by the June 2026 deadline to challenge Brimmage for the District 5 seat [28].
- Listed on the ballot as a Republican [6]. Said she has voted Republican because the party best represents her beliefs, but that politics should be kept "in the Civic class" [36].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- On referendums, says voter decisions could help or hinder the district and that transparency about where tax money goes, and what would happen if state funds are lost, would help voters decide [36].
- Cites her work on district finances as a qualification for overseeing budgets, contracts and expenditures [36].

**Academics**

- To raise SAT scores, would identify learning gaps, provide academic support and give students access to effective test preparation [36].
- Says AI can be positive but warns against students becoming dependent on computers and not thinking for themselves [36].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Says parents should have a voice in choosing their child's school, and suggests a community questionnaire to learn why families do not choose Mooresville [36].

**Facilities**

- For a large donor-funded project, would create a dedicated technology classroom with trained teachers [36].

**Transparency & community input**

- Says she is running because she has "witnessed and experienced voices ignored in a past school board meeting" [36].
- Says board meetings offer chances to be heard, but the board should encourage more involvement and build trust by being more transparent about decisions and spending [36].

### On the current officeholder

- Says voices were ignored at a past board meeting and that the board needs more transparency about how decisions are made and how taxpayer dollars are spent; she did not name specific decisions [36].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Would base decisions on facts and work with administrators and fellow board members even when opinions differ [36].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports for this race were found in the sources reviewed.

### What we couldn't verify

- The sources reviewed did not say whether she would keep her district payroll job if elected or how any conflict of interest would be handled.
- Her business was not named.
- No campaign website or social media page was found.
- A GoodParty.org profile under her name was seen only in search results and was not reviewed.
- No campaign finance filings were found.
