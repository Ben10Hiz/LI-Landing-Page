# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The Metropolitan School District of Perry Township serves about 16,000 students on the south side of Indianapolis in Marion County [24]. English learners make up nearly one-third of students [24]. The superintendent is Dr. Patrick Spray, who started July 1, 2023, after Patrick Mapes retired [25, 26].
- The board has seven members, all elected at large to four-year terms; every voter in the district may vote for any candidate [24, 27].
- Three of the seven seats are on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot, so voters choose up to three candidates; the state list shows "vote for 1," which does not match the three open seats [24, 6].
- The three seats now belong to Lee Shively, Chris Lewis and Hre Mang [27]. Shively and Lewis are running for reelection, Mang is not running, and newcomer Brianna Bible is running [24].
- Only three candidates filed for the three seats, so the race is effectively uncontested [24, 28]. In 2024, two candidates filed for four seats; a write-in candidate won one seat and one incumbent held over under state law [27].
- Budget: voters approved a $154 million operating referendum in 2022 [24]. Both incumbents named the 2025 state tax law (SEA 1) as a growing strain on school funding in their 2026 questionnaire answers [24].
- Federal grant: in February 2025, the U.S. Department of Education ended Perry's Teacher and School Leader grant, putting about $3.5 million in limbo for the second and third years. The grant paid for performance bonuses for highly effective teachers and two literacy coach positions. The district appealed, and a spokesperson said in May 2025 that no positions had been cut [29].
- Career program: with a $40 million Lilly Endowment grant, Perry is building a $30 million innovation and entrepreneurship center on the University of Indianapolis campus for Southport and Perry Meridian high school students. The program began in temporary space in July 2026, and the building is expected to open for the 2028-29 school year [26, 30]. The next board will oversee the construction [24].
- Academics: Perry students' scores on IREAD, the state third-grade reading test, improved over the past two years, and the district has been recognized for its English-learner instruction, Chalkbeat reported [24].
- Forums and differences: no candidate forum was found. All three candidates answered the Chalkbeat Indiana/Mirror Indy/WFYI questionnaire [24]. Shively emphasized teacher pay and a K-12 entrepreneurship focus. Lewis emphasized spending on needs, teacher salaries and facilities. Bible emphasized student support services, literacy and career readiness, and community engagement [24].

## Brianna Bible — At Large

First-time candidate, a housing navigator and founder of a behavioral and mental health services organization, listed on the ballot as a Democrat; she is running for the seat now held by Hre Mang, who is not seeking reelection [24, 6].

Campaign website: https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=61592467656733

### Background

- Works as a step-up housing navigator, finding housing for people leaving incarceration [24].
- Founded Impact Her Behavioral and Mental Health Services, which supports girls and women with mental and physical health needs [24].
- Perry parent and community member; age 42 [24].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed on the state candidate list as a Democrat [6].
- Running for an at-large seat; three candidates filed for three seats [24].

### Positions & plans

**Academics**

- Lists as a top priority strengthening student achievement through "high-quality academics and individualized support" [24].
- Said the district should keep strengthening literacy and career readiness, and expand career exploration and workforce partnerships [24].

**Budget & taxes**

- Said spending should put classroom instruction, student services and teacher support first, with careful review of spending decisions [24].
- Said the district should actively pursue grants and partnerships to add resources "without placing unnecessary financial burdens on taxpayers" [24].

**Teachers & staff**

- Supports continued investment in competitive pay, staff diversity, professional development, mentorship and wellness resources, and said educators should have a meaningful voice in district decisions [24].

**Transparency & community input**

- Plans to hold listening sessions, attend school events and communicate regularly through social media and community meetings [24].

### On the current officeholder

- In her questionnaire, did not comment on specific decisions of the current board; she said Perry "should continue" strengthening literacy and career readiness [24].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Wants to expand access to mental health resources, counseling and community partnerships, and to build partnerships among schools, families, community groups and local government to address issues such as food and housing insecurity [24].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign finance figures were found in reviewed sources [24].

## Chris Lewis — At Large

Incumbent board member and analyst at the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission, listed on the ballot as a Republican [24, 6].

Campaign website: https://www.facebook.com/100086389843421/

### Background

- Analyst at the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission [24].
- Perry parent; age 49 [24].
- Was one of seven candidates for three at-large seats in 2022; Chalkbeat could not reach him for that year's guide [31]. He was listed as a board member in November 2024 [27].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed on the state candidate list as a Republican [6].
- Running for reelection to an at-large seat [24].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Called school funding the district's most pressing near-term issue and said he would make sure available money is spent "for the needs versus the wants" the district requests [24].
- Said that because of SEA 1 (2025), schools "are going to have to do more with less," and money should go to items that help teachers and students succeed [24].

**Academics**

- Named student achievement as a top priority and said he trusts district leaders, principals and teachers to use data to identify student needs [24].

**Teachers & staff**

- Wants to work with administrators to give teachers the tools to educate the district's diverse students and to keep teacher salaries competitive [24].

**Facilities**

- Named updating and maintaining facilities as a top priority [24].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. In his questionnaire he described future funding pressure as the board's main challenge and expressed trust in district staff's use of data [24].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Plans to engage the community by attending community events and listening to residents' concerns and suggestions [24].

### Endorsements & fundraising

## Lee Shively — At Large

Incumbent board member since 2019 and a Beech Grove schools teacher, listed on the ballot as an Independent [24, 31, 6].

### Background

- Teacher at Beech Grove schools [24].
- Graduated from Perry Meridian High School in 2002; his children attend Perry schools [31, 24].
- Has served on the board since 2019 and was board president in 2022 [31]. Age 42 [24].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed on the state candidate list as an Independent [6].
- Running for reelection to an at-large seat [24]. He was reelected in 2022 in a seven-candidate race for three seats [31, 27].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Said Perry has stayed fiscally responsible since he joined the board in fall 2019, but that SEA 1 has made school funding "at best, complicated" [24].
- Said he has confidence in the staff who build and monitor the budget, and will keep attending Finance Committee meetings [24].
- Lists fiscal responsibility that also respects "the budgets of Perry Township taxpayers" as a top priority [24].

**Teachers & staff**

- Wants to keep pay competitive to attract and keep educators, and named this his top priority [24].

**Academics**

- Wants to build out the new UIndy innovation and entrepreneurship program and extend innovation and entrepreneurship into a K-12 focus [24].

**Transparency & community input**

- Encourages students, staff and residents to make public comment at board meetings: "we can't help make change on issues or concerns we don't know about!" [24].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. He said the district has kept a fiscally responsible outlook during his tenure and expressed confidence in its budget staff [24].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Plans to attend Finance Committee meetings regularly to check whether competitive pay can be sustained [24].
- Wants to see how innovation and entrepreneurship can be built into a K-12 focus [24].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign finance figures were found in reviewed sources [24].

### What we couldn't verify

- No current campaign website was found; Chalkbeat linked a campaign website in 2022 that was not reviewed.
- No record of his board votes was reviewed.
- Marion County Election Board campaign finance filings were not reviewed; fundraising is unknown.
- Ballotpedia's page (seen only as a search snippet, not verified) says his current term ends Dec. 31, 2026.
