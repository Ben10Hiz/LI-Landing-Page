# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Kyle Walker (Republican)

Sen. Kyle Walker (R-Lawrence), who has held the seat since 2020 and won reelection in 2022 with 55% of the vote, announced in December 2025 that he would not seek reelection and would finish his term through the November 2026 election [3, 4]. He described the decision as a new chapter in his professional career [3].

- November 2025: Announced opposition to the proposed mid-decade congressional redistricting, citing constituent feedback: "With 93 percent opposed, and as their voice in the Indiana Senate, I'll be voting no" [3]. The Senate rejected the plan 31–19 on Dec. 11, 2025 [5].
- December 3, 2025: Said he would not seek reelection; his statement did not mention redistricting, according to the Indiana Capital Chronicle, which reported he had resigned as executive vice president of MO Strategies, a consulting firm led by the chair of a pro-redistricting nonprofit [3].
- In his announcement, Walker cited changing the local income tax distribution formula so Fishers receives a more equitable share, and creating a Sports and Tourism Bid Fund to help communities host major events [3].

## This race

- Senate District 31 includes parts of Hamilton, Hancock and Marion counties, more than 135,000 residents, according to WRTV [6]; WFYI describes it as Fishers, parts of northern Lawrence Township and the Geist Reservoir area [4].
- It is an open seat: Republican Sen. Kyle Walker is not running again [3]. The Indiana Capital Chronicle called it one of the state's few politically competitive Senate districts; Walker won 55% in 2022 [3, 4].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Juanita Albright 3,516 votes (37.7%), Tiffanie Ditlevson 2,332 (25.0%), Travis Hankins 1,781 (19.1%), Jan Kepley Keefer 1,699 (18.2%), according to vote totals posted by Larry in Fishers and Wikipedia [7, 8].
- May 5, 2026 Democratic primary: Kerry Forestal 4,153 votes (37.7%), Catherine Torzewski 3,909 (35.5%), Lasima Packett 1,805 (16.4%), Andrew Dezelan 1,141 (10.4%) [7, 8].
- No general-election debate or forum between Albright and Forestal was found in reviewed coverage.
- Both nominees list cost of living, health care costs and public safety among their priorities; Albright's materials emphasize tax cuts and property tax relief, while Forestal's emphasize utility, health care and grocery costs [9, 4, 10].

## Juanita Albright

Albright is a Fishers physician and president of the Hamilton Southeastern (HSE) School Board who won a four-way Republican primary [11, 8].

Campaign website: https://albrightforindiana.com

### Background

- Has practiced medicine and lived in Fishers for more than 20 years [11].
- Elected to the Hamilton Southeastern School Board and serves as its president; her campaign says the district reached its highest graduation rate during her term and that she has overseen a budget of more than $340 million [9, 11].
- Appointed by Fishers Mayor Scott Fadness to the Fishers Senior Center Committee [11].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed Jan. 7, 2026 [11]; won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 37.7% over Tiffanie Ditlevson, Travis Hankins and Jan Kepley Keefer, per Wikipedia's compilation [8].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Her campaign site says she will work to lower property taxes in the district and help seniors stay in their homes [9].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says Hoosiers need "relief, not more government-driven costs" and supports tax cuts, balanced budgets and making housing, groceries and health care more affordable [9].

**Health care**

- Supports "practical, market-based reforms" to lower premiums and out-of-pocket costs, including prescription drugs, with emphasis on care for older Hoosiers [9, 11].

**Education**

- Supports adequate funding for local schools, teacher support, parental involvement, high academic standards and local decision-making [9].

**Public safety**

- Supports keeping violent offenders incarcerated and mental health treatment for low-risk, nonviolent offenders to reduce reoffending [9].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Albright about Sen. Walker's record or the seat's direction were found beyond her own platform. She told WFYI, "I bring vast experience in healthcare and education, which are two of the largest issues facing Hoosiers" [4].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Running on lowering inflation and the cost of living, fiscal responsibility, quality education and public safety [11].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by U.S. Sen. Jim Banks during the primary [12].
- Her campaign lists Americans for Prosperity–Indiana, Hoosiers for Quality Education, the Indiana Chamber of Commerce and the Indiana State Police Alliance [9]; she also told WFYI she had Indiana Right to Life's endorsement [4].
- No fundraising totals were found in reviewed coverage.

## Kerry J Forestal

Forestal is the Democratic Marion County sheriff, in his second and final term, and a former U.S. Marshal for the Southern District of Indiana [10]. He has not served in the General Assembly.

### Background

- Has worked in Marion County law enforcement since 1977, beginning as a patrol deputy [10, 6].
- Appointed U.S. Marshal for the Southern District of Indiana by President Barack Obama in 2010 [10].
- Elected Marion County sheriff in 2018 and reelected in 2022; he is term-limited and oversees a $150 million budget [6, 10].
- Received the NAACP's State Rep. Bill Crawford Legacy Award in 2025 [10]. Lives in the district [6].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced in December 2025 [6]; won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 37.7% over Catherine Torzewski, Lasima Packett and Andrew Dezelan, per Wikipedia's compilation [8].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Lists lowering household costs — housing, utilities and health care — plus infrastructure and economic development as priorities [4].
- Promised "pragmatic, values-driven leadership" on rising costs including utilities and health care [10].

**Public safety**

- Lists public safety, including mental health and addiction treatment, as a priority [4]; says he implemented evidence-based violence prevention programs as sheriff [6].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Forestal about Sen. Walker's record were found. On the seat, he said constituents' "voice in the State Senate will fight every single day to make their lives better" [10].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he will work to bring down utility and health care costs while continuing a public-safety focus [6].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising totals were found in reviewed coverage.

### What we couldn't verify

- The Indiana Capital Chronicle reported criticism of Forestal for working with federal immigration enforcement and holding detainees in the county jail [10]; no response from Forestal was found.
- He reportedly returned the Indiana Capital Chronicle voter-guide survey, but the guide was inaccessible (403).
- No campaign website, endorsements or finance data located.
- Not a current or former state legislator (the research note suggesting this was not borne out).
