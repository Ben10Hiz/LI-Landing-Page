# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Matt Lehman (Republican)

Matt Lehman, a Berne Republican and insurance agency president, has represented House District 79 since 2008 and is House Majority Floor Leader, a post he was re-elected to in December 2024 [3, 4].

- House Majority Floor Leader; serves on the Insurance, Public Policy, and Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development committees [3, 4]. Past president of the National Council of Insurance Legislators (2020–2021) [3].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (passed 65-29) [5]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 budget (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [6].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted no on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41; he was one of 12 House Republicans voting no [7, 8]. On PBS Fort Wayne he said constituents overwhelmingly told him "this is just not the right time to do this" [9].
- Jan. 28, 2026: Voted yes on HB 1002, the 2026 electric utility affordability bill (passed 89-4) [10].
- 2026 session: Authored HEA 1360, which sets new procedures for high volumes of automated and out-of-state public records requests, and sponsored SEA 258, removing state nuclear-energy requirements that duplicate federal oversight [11]. Supported HEA 1200 on commercial driver's license English proficiency and documentation, citing a Jay County crash [12].

## This race

- House District 79 covers all of Adams County and portions of Jay and Wells counties in northeast Indiana [3].
- Lehman was reelected without opposition in 2024 [13]. Both Lehman and Ian G. Richardson were unopposed for their parties' nominations in the May 5, 2026 primary [13].
- No debates, forums or local news coverage of the race were found.
- Key differences: Richardson's campaign emphasizes raising the minimum wage, medical and medication costs, legalizing and taxing cannabis, and local energy microgrids [14, 15]; Lehman's 2026 legislative work focused on public records, energy regulation and commercial driver licensing [11, 12].

## Ian G. Richardson

Ian G. Richardson is a Bluffton resident who has worked in food service and as a CVS pharmacy technician [16]. His campaign focuses on wages, medical costs and local energy projects [14].

Campaign website: https://www.iangforindiana.com/

### Background

- Says he has more than 25 years in service work, 20 of them in food service, and has worked as a pharmacy technician for CVS [16].
- Lists himself as owner/operator of Ian Richardson Co. and a former Allen County Democratic precinct committeeman (Precinct 298, 2021–2025) in his iVoterGuide profile [17]. Lives in Bluffton [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [13].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Says "Indiana hasn't given its workers a raise in sixteen years" and that he will raise the minimum wage; supports child care tax credits [14].

**Health care**

- Wants to "help to reign in the cost of medical care and medications" [14]; supports a state-backed supplemental health insurance system [17].

**Energy & utilities**

- Proposes community-scale microgrids pairing vertical-axis wind turbines with basalt thermal storage, which his campaign says "lowers home energy costs" [15].

**Environment**

- Supports "holding polluters accountable for our water quality" [14].

**Education**

- Supports expanded education spending and education grants [14].

**Cannabis**

- Supports legalizing cannabis and taxing it "like tobacco" [14, 17].

**Elections & redistricting**

- In his iVoterGuide answers, supports abolishing the Electoral College and ranked choice voting; listed himself as neutral on photo ID for voting [17].

### On the current officeholder

- Does not name Lehman on his campaign site; says "I've watched Indiana get more expensive while our legislature offers little more than silence" [16].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Cites medication costs and dealing with insurers as reasons for running [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His iVoterGuide profile reports an endorsement from the Indiana Reproductive Freedom PAC [17]. No campaign finance totals found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No news coverage of his candidacy found.
- Campaign finance totals not found.
- No position found on the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) or data centers.
- His iVoterGuide profile also lists a 2026 food-service job and memberships in several advocacy groups; current employer not confirmed.
- His precinct committeeman role was in Allen County, outside District 79; he says he now lives in Bluffton (Wells County). Whether his address is within the district's portion of Wells County was not verified.

## Matthew S. Lehman

Matt Lehman has represented District 79 since 2008 and is House Majority Floor Leader [3]. He broke with most House Republicans to vote against the 2025 congressional redistricting bill [8].

### Background

- Insurance agent and president of Bixler Insurance Inc., a Berne-based independent agency; Certified Insurance Counselor since 1995; associate degree in aviation technology from Vincennes University [3].
- Served on the Adams County Council from 1994 to 2008; named Berne Citizen of the Year in 2013 [3]. Lives in Berne [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [13].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said he voted no on the 2025 redistricting bill after constituents told him it was not the right time; said he had supported the existing maps three years earlier [9].

**Energy & utilities**

- "Reliable, affordable energy is critical to Indiana's economic growth"; says SEA 258 reduces "unnecessary red tape" for energy development [11].

**Government transparency**

- Says agencies "should not be burdened by bad-faith or automated requests" and that HEA 1360 "puts reasonable guardrails into place to protect access" [11].

**Public safety**

- On HEA 1200: "Hoosiers deserve safe highways" [12].

**Health care**

- Named health care access and cost, along with economic development, as areas to examine in 2025 [4].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements about Richardson were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says committee work is "the starting point in tackling issues that constituents are facing" [4].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No 2026 endorsements or campaign finance totals were found.

- No campaign website found.

- Campaign finance totals not found.
