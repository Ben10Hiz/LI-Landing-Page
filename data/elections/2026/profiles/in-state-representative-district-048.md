# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Doug Miller (Republican)

Doug Miller, an Elkhart homebuilder and property manager, has represented House District 48 since 2014 [3, 4]. He chairs the House Government and Regulatory Reform Committee and authored the House Republicans' 2026 housing law, HEA 1001 [3, 5].

- Committees: Government and Regulatory Reform (chair), Environmental Affairs, and Local Government; co-chaired a 2022 housing task force [3]. Named chair of the 2026 Interim Study Committee on Government [6].
- 2023 session: Authored HB 1005, which created the Residential Housing Infrastructure Assistance Program, a revolving loan fund for water, sewer and utility lines; according to the caucus, Goshen received $11 million from it for the 1,400-unit Cherry Creek development [7].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [8]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [9].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [10].
- 2026 session: Authored HB 1001, a House Republican priority bill that limits some local zoning and design rules and streamlines approvals for residential development; it passed the House 76-15 on Jan. 27, 2026 [11]. Rep. Chris Campbell (D-West Lafayette) voted no, saying, "Taking away that local decision is not good for my community"; Miller said he would keep working with city and county officials on local-control concerns [11]. The governor ceremonially signed it April 13, 2026 [5].

## This race

- House District 48 covers portions of Elkhart County, according to the House Republican caucus [3]; the Elkhart Truth describes it as based in the Elkhart area [12].
- Miller has held the seat since 2014 and was re-elected unopposed in 2024 [4]. He was the only Republican in the May 5, 2026 primary [4].
- In the Democratic primary, Emily Yaw defeated Carl Stutsman, 1,007 votes (54%) to 858 (46%) [4]. Green Party candidate Marshall Travis qualified by petition [12]. All three are on the November ballot [2].
- Key differences: Miller authored the 2026 housing law that limits some local development rules [11]; Yaw and Travis both propose more affordable and low-income housing and a Housing First approach to homelessness [13, 14]. Yaw and Travis also propose state-level health coverage plans [13, 14]. Travis opposes AI data centers in Indiana [14]; no data-center statements from Miller or Yaw were found.
- No debate or forum including all three candidates was found.

## Doug Miller

Doug Miller is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2014, and chairs the House Government and Regulatory Reform Committee [3]. His legislative work has centered on housing supply and development rules [7, 5].

### Background

- Homebuilder since the mid-1970s who later started his own business; owns Creekside Realty, LLC and White Pines Properties, LLC; lives in Elkhart; attended Concord High School and Goshen College [3].
- Served on the Elkhart County Area Plan Commission and Plat Committee and chaired its Board of Zoning Appeals; board member of the Builders Association of Elkhart County and Indiana's representative on the National Association of Home Builders board [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Housing**

- Said of HEA 1001 (2026): "As Indiana and our communities continue to grow, we must have the housing to meet demand" and that it "will help more Hoosiers achieve the American dream of owning a home at a price they can afford" [5].
- Told the House communities must be open to "safe and affordable housing at every single price point," and said some construction standards are necessary for safety but others add unnecessary design costs [11].

**Government & regulation**

- On his 2026 interim committee chairmanship: "We are always looking to cut red-tape" [6].

**Property taxes**

- Voted for SEA 1 (2025) [8].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted for HB 1032 (2025 congressional redistricting) [10].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Miller about Yaw or Travis were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- As chair of the 2026 Interim Study Committee on Government, said the focus is on cutting red tape [6].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No 2026 campaign website, candidate survey responses or campaign statements were found.

## Emily Yaw

Emily Yaw is an accountant and former foster parent from Elkhart County making her first run for office [15, 16]. She says foster care reform is her top focus, followed by housing, health care and wages [15, 13].

Campaign website: https://emilyyaw.com

### Background

- Accountant at ATC Trailers in Nappanee; grew up in Goshen and attended Fairfield High School and Bethel College in Mishawaka [15].
- Says she has lived in Elkhart County her entire life [16]. Was a foster parent for about six years [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary over Carl Stutsman, 1,007 votes (54%) to 858 (46%) [4].

### Positions & plans

**Foster care & child welfare**

- Says the DCS ombudsman "does not have the power to actually make changes" and that DCS should "be forced to work as equal partners with foster parents, therapists, service providers"; proposes letting judges act on placements without waiting for DCS paperwork [13].

**Housing**

- Says "We need to build more housing that the average person can afford," including more low-income housing in downtown Elkhart; supports restoring abandoned or condemned homes to code [13].
- Proposes Housing First communities with on-site caseworkers, saying, "Putting people in jail for not having a home, doesn't solve anything" [13].

**Health care**

- Says "Healthcare is a right, not a privilege" and proposes a state system covering all residents that negotiates prices with hospitals and drug companies [13].

**Energy & utilities**

- Proposes solar "communities" with shared battery hubs to lower household utility costs and solar-installation training at school career centers [13].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports raising the minimum wage from $7.25 [13].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Yaw about Miller's record were found; her site says her outreach to U.S. and Indiana representatives on foster care did not seem to make a difference [15].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says "My first priority is going to be our kids," including foster care, education and child care [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Search snippets describe an Elkhart Truth report on a town hall at Elkhart's Tolson Center where Yaw and Travis spoke; the article could not be opened, so its content is unverified.
- No positions found on property taxes, data centers, education funding or redistricting.
- No fundraising data found.

## Marshall Travis

Marshall Travis is the Indiana Green Party nominee, a construction framer at a mobile home manufacturing plant who lives in Osceola [14]. He ran for the state Senate as a Democrat in 2024 before leaving the party [12].

Campaign website: https://greenpartyin.com/travisforindiana48/

### Background

- Works as a construction framer at a mobile home manufacturing plant; lives in Osceola [14].
- Originally from California; has worked in the RV industry since moving to Indiana; ran for the state Senate as a Democrat in 2024 [12].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced in January 2026 after leaving the Democratic Party and said he had submitted enough petition signatures to run as a third-party candidate [12]; listed on the ballot as the Green Party nominee [2].

### Positions & plans

**Housing**

- Proposes state funds for affordable homes, rehabilitating vacant or deteriorating neighborhoods, removing first-time buyer barriers, a Housing First model for homelessness and full funding for local housing authorities [14].

**Data centers**

- Writes, "I support an end to AI Data Centers in Indiana," and calls for restoring the land and water to agricultural and conservation use [14].

**Health care**

- Proposes a "Healthcare for All" plan and more funding for mental health and addiction treatment [14].

**Education**

- Calls for funding public schools fully and immediately and for funding equity across income areas [14].

**Child care**

- Supports expanding affordable, accessible child care for working families [14].

**Public safety**

- Supports ending criminal penalties for marijuana possession and allowing responsible adult use; supports tougher penalties for animal neglect and abuse [14].

### On the current officeholder

- His campaign page does not name Miller but criticizes "entrenched Republican representation that has failed to serve the voters of Elkhart County" and "Unkept promises by Democrats and Republicans" [14].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Also lists expanded housing and mental health support for veterans and more social services for seniors and working families [14].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Search snippets say the Indiana Green Party filed a complaint with the Elkhart County Clerk/election board over the certification of Travis's petition signatures (307 needed); the party's page could not be opened and no official response was found, so this is unverified.
- The Elkhart Truth town hall report (see Yaw gaps) could not be opened.
- Little independent news coverage beyond his January 2026 announcement.
- No fundraising data found.
