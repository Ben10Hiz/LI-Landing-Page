# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Tony Isa (Republican)

Tony Isa, a Republican from Angola, took office in November 2024 after winning the open seat left by Rep. Denny Zent, and is seeking a second term [3, 4, 5]. He sits on the House Agriculture and Rural Development; Employment, Labor and Pensions; and Public Health committees, according to the House Republican caucus [3].

- April 2025: Authored HEA 1196, which the caucus says removes the requirement that aviation boards and airport authorities in communities of 38,000 or fewer people split seats equally between political parties; Isa called it "a common-sense approach to ensure effective governance at a local level" [6].
- 2025: Co-authored HEA 1003, which the caucus says tightens facility-fee billing rules at the largest nonprofit hospitals, requires labs and imaging centers to post prices, and expands the Medicaid Fraud Control Unit's powers; it was ceremonially signed in June 2025 [7].
- On April 10, 2025, voted for SB 1, the property-tax and local government finance bill, which passed the House 65–29 [8]. On April 25, 2025, voted for the conference report on HEA 1001, the 2025–2027 state budget, adopted 66–27 [9].
- December 5, 2025: Voted for HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57–41 [10]; the Senate defeated it 19–31 on December 11 [11].
- January 2026: Introduced HB 1370, which would bar insurers from requiring prior authorization for ambulance services and for emergency services provided in good faith within 24 hours; the caucus said it was referred to the House Insurance Committee [12]. January 28, 2026: Voted for HB 1002, the electric utility affordability bill, which passed the House 89–4 [13].
- February 2026: Co-authored HB 1038, which would set up a competitive process for relocating an existing casino license to Allen, DeKalb, Steuben or Wayne County; Isa said "Steuben County would make an ideal place to expand economic opportunity for the region and the state" [14].
- 2026: Co-authored the 2026 housing law, HEA 1001, which the caucus says limits local impact and development fees and makes it easier to convert commercial buildings into housing [15]. In June 2026 he was appointed to the Interim Study Committee on Employment and Labor [16].

## This race

- House District 51 includes all of LaGrange County and part of Steuben County, according to the House Republican caucus [3]; KPC News describes it as all of LaGrange County and most of Steuben County [17].
- This is a rematch of 2024, when Isa won the then-open seat over Rowe with 74.4% of the vote to her 25.6% [18].
- In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Isa defeated Theresa Steele, 3,995 votes (68.2%) to 1,862 (31.8%), according to Wikipedia; Rowe was the only Democratic candidate listed [19].
- No debate or forum between the two candidates was found as of October 10, 2026; Rowe's campaign events page listed no forums [20].
- Both candidates point to household costs: Isa highlights the 2025 property-tax law and the 2026 housing law he co-authored, while Rowe's campaign focuses on what she calls "The Hoosier Cost" of child care, utilities, housing and property taxes [21, 15, 22].

## Tony Isa

The Republican incumbent, first elected in 2024, a real estate agent and former Steuben County Council member [3].

Campaign website: https://friendsoftonyisa.com/

### Background

- Small-business owner and local real estate agent; served four years on the Steuben County Council, according to the caucus [3]. His campaign site says he owns Scoops Ice Cream in Angola [23].
- Lives in Angola [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Theresa Steele with 68.2% of the vote, according to Wikipedia [19].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- In a June 2026 column, said the 2025 property-tax law provides a homestead credit of up to $300, phases in a larger deduction for homeowners, adds local spending guardrails and moves property-tax referendums to general-election ballots [21].

**Health care**

- Said of his 2025 health-cost law, "For too long, patients have been left in the dark when it comes to the cost of care" [7].
- Said of his ambulance prior-authorization bill, "Medical emergencies do not wait for insurance approval" [12].

**Housing**

- Said "Too many Hoosiers are being priced out of the housing market by unnecessary regulations and rising construction costs" [15].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports relocating a casino license to Steuben County as an economic-development step [14].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No public statement by Isa about Rowe's 2026 campaign was found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- His campaign site lists priorities including fiscal responsibility, limited government, education, public safety, support for businesses, opposition to abortion and gun rights, without detailed proposals [23].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Wikipedia lists endorsements from President Donald Trump, Gov. Mike Braun, Americans for Prosperity, Indiana Farm Bureau's AgELECT PAC, NFIB Indiana PAC and the NRA Political Victory Fund [19].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign finance totals were checked for 2026.
- Final status of HB 1370 (ambulance prior authorization) and HB 1038 (casino relocation) in the 2026 session was not confirmed.

## Judy Rowe

The Democratic nominee, a Steuben County real estate broker who was also the party's nominee for the seat in 2024 [17, 18].

Campaign website: https://rowe4rep.com/

### Background

- A former small-business owner and RE/MAX real estate broker [17].
- Serves or has served on the boards of the Community Humane Shelter of Steuben County, the Steuben County Property Tax Board of Appeals and the Steuben Lakes Regional Sewer District, and is a member of the LaGrange County and Angola area chambers of commerce, according to KPC News [17].
- KPC News reported in 2024 that she is a former Steuben County Democratic Party chair and a former Angola Plan Commission member [5].
- Lives in the Lake Pleasant area [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed her candidacy on January 16, 2026, and was the only Democratic candidate listed for the May 5 primary [17, 19].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Her campaign says "Indiana families are paying more for child care, utilities, housing, and property taxes while too many decisions are made without the people having a real say" [22].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says households and small businesses face pressure from rising utility and energy bills, according to KPC News [17].

**Child care**

- Criticizes cuts to child care support, saying "Child care isn’t a luxury. It’s a workforce necessity" [17].

**Property taxes**

- Argues the state has shifted financial burdens to counties, towns and schools, increasing pressure on property taxpayers [17].

**Housing**

- Cites limited housing inventory, price pressure and barriers for first-time buyers [17].

**Elections & ballot access**

- Supports giving citizens the power to put issues on the ballot [22].

**Other issues**

- Her issues page also lists protecting public schools, legalizing, regulating and taxing cannabis, and protecting lakes and drinking water, without further detail [22].

### On the current officeholder

- No statement by Rowe about Isa's record was found; her campaign site and KPC's 2026 filing story do not mention him by name [22, 17]. She said, "I’m running because I’m done watching working families and rural communities get shortchanged" [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Her campaign says she is running "to lower the Hoosier Cost and give Hoosiers the power to put issues on the ballot" [22].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on her campaign site [24].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign finance totals were checked for 2026.
- Her campaign's issues page gives headings without detailed proposals.
- No 2026 news coverage beyond her January filing announcement was found.
