# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Mark Genda (Republican)

Mark Genda, a Frankfort Republican and licensed funeral director first elected in 2022, is vice chair of the House Natural Resources Committee and serves on the Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development and the Employment, Labor and Pensions committees, according to the House Republican caucus [3].

- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on Senate Bill 1, the 2025 property-tax law, which passed the House 65-29 [4].
- April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the final version of the 2025-27 state budget (HEA 1001), adopted by the House 66-27 [5].
- December 5, 2025: Was one of the Republicans who voted no on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [6].
- February 2025: Sponsored House Bill 1042 to exempt feminine hygiene and adult incontinence products from sales tax; the caucus said the provision was added to the House version of the state budget (HB 1001) [7].
- February 2025: Sponsored House Bill 1044 to allow alkaline hydrolysis, a water-based alternative to cremation, saying "Hoosiers deserve options when it comes to how they are laid to rest"; it passed the House [8].
- April 2025: Said of SEA 1, "Hoosiers are feeling the strain of continually increasing property taxes and need help" [9].

## This race

- House District 41 covers parts of Boone, Clinton, Montgomery and Tippecanoe counties in west central Indiana, according to the House Republican caucus [3].
- Genda has held the seat since 2022; Wikipedia reports he won 73.1% of the vote in 2024 [10].
- Both Genda and Hayes were the only candidates listed in their parties' May 5, 2026 primaries, according to Wikipedia [10].
- No debates, candidate forums or local news coverage of this race were found.
- Where they differ: Hayes's campaign calls for expanding Medicaid, closing private-school voucher funding, more regulation of data centers and reversing the state's abortion law; Genda voted for the 2025 property-tax law and budget, and no 2026 campaign platform from him was found [11, 4, 5].

## Jackson T. Hayes

Jackson T. Hayes, the Democratic nominee, is a Frankfort resident and first-time candidate whose campaign website lays out positions on utilities, health care, education and abortion [11].

Campaign website: https://sites.google.com/view/indianadistrict41jacksonhayes/home

### Background

- Grew up in rural Illinois near Champaign, studied history and American government at BYU-Idaho, and has worked in factories, theaters, gas stations, moving companies and call centers, according to his campaign [11].
- Moved to Indiana in 2016 and lives in Frankfort [11].

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only Democrat listed in the May 5, 2026 primary and is the party's nominee, according to Wikipedia [10, 12].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Says "Utilities companies are not people," wants utility monopolies reined in, and supports hardening infrastructure against storms [11].

**Data centers**

- Wants data center companies to "pay their fair share in taxes and utilities rates" and says "This industrial expansion must be stopped until it submits to environmental regulation" [11].

**Health care**

- Calls for expanding Medicaid by raising income caps and reducing paperwork, preserving funding for rural hospitals, and focusing fraud investigations on providers and insurers [11].

**Education**

- Says funds were "siphoned from Indiana public schools into for profit private organizations" and wants that money returned to public schools [11].

**Public safety**

- Wants fewer firearms in circulation and says police "need retrained to deescalate situations" [11].

**Immigration**

- Opposes laws requiring education and health care professionals to work with immigration authorities, calling that "an awful mistake" [11].

**Abortion**

- Says he will "always fight to reverse our state's harmful abortion ban" [11].

**Environment**

- Supports wind, solar and other clean energy and says "People deserve clean air, land, and water" [11].

**Civil rights**

- Opposes requiring identification documents to match birth certificates, which he says was designed to hurt transgender people [11].
- His website's "Thoughts" page contains an essay criticizing what it calls "MAGA Republicanism" [13].

### On the current officeholder

- Says, "My opponent, Mark Genda, does NOT believe in reproductive freedom," and that Genda will continue to support restrictions on abortion access; no response from Genda was found [11].

### How they say they'd run the office

- No legislative agenda beyond the positions on his website was found [11].

### What we couldn't verify

- No news coverage of Hayes's campaign was found.
- No endorsements or campaign finance totals were found.
- His current occupation is not stated on his campaign website.
- His website's claim that nearly $2 billion was diverted from public schools to private organizations gives no source and was not verified.

## Mark Genda

Rep. Mark Genda, the Republican incumbent seeking a third term, is a Clinton County native and longtime funeral director [3].

### Background

- Licensed funeral director for 37 years and former owner of Genda Funeral Homes in Flora, Frankfort, Rossville and Mulberry; owns Genda Rentals in Frankfort, according to the caucus [3].
- Served two terms on the Community Schools of Frankfort Board of Trustees, including as president [3].
- Graduated from Worsham College of Mortuary Science [3].
- Lives in Frankfort [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only Republican listed in the May 5, 2026 primary, according to Wikipedia [10].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Voted for SEA 1 in 2025 and said the law "will help homeowners, small business owners, veterans and farmers" [4, 9].

**Economy & jobs**

- Sponsored a 2025 bill to remove sales tax from feminine hygiene and adult incontinence products, saying, "I'm hoping we can ease some financial stress for Hoosiers" [7].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted no on the December 2025 mid-decade congressional redistricting bill (HB 1032) [6].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder.
- No statements by Genda about Hayes were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website or 2026 platform was found; BallotReady lists only his official House page and a Facebook page.
- No endorsements or campaign finance totals were found.
- No public statement explaining his no vote on HB 1032 was found.
- Whether the hygiene-product sales tax exemption remained in the final 2025 budget, and whether the alkaline hydrolysis bill became law, were not confirmed; a search snippet suggests a similar bill (HB 1240) was refiled in 2026 and referred to committee (unverified).
- No 2026 bills he authored that became law were found.
