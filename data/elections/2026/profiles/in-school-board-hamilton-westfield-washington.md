# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Westfield Washington Schools (WWS) serves Westfield and Washington Township in Hamilton County. The district says it serves more than 10,400 students and 8,400 families across 16 facilities with more than 1,600 employees [24]. Dr. John Atha is superintendent [24, 25].
- The Board of School Trustees has five members. Four are elected from geographic districts and the fifth seat is at-large, covering the whole township [26]. 2026 officers are Rebecca Ogle (president), Bill Anderson (vice president) and Carrie Larrison (secretary); Amber N. Willis and Mike Steele are members [27, 28].
- Three of the five seats are on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot, and each race elects one member. District 2 is held by Bill Anderson (first elected 2018), District 4 by Rebecca Ogle (first elected 2018) and the at-large seat by Amber N. Willis (on the board since January 2015) [29, 30, 31]. All three incumbents are on the ballot: Anderson is unopposed in District 2, Ogle faces Steven Castle in District 4, and Willis faces Gabriel Crawford-Paree at large [6]. Mike Steele (District 3) and Carrie Larrison, sworn in January 2025, are not up this year [28, 32].
- Party labels: 2026 is the first election cycle under Senate Bill 287 (2025), which lets school board candidates list a party on the ballot [33]. On the state list, Anderson, Ogle and Willis are listed as Republicans, Crawford-Paree as a Democrat and Castle as nonpartisan [6].
- Referendum: on June 9, 2026 the board voted unanimously to put an eight-year operating referendum on the ballot (Public Question 3), with a maximum rate of $0.3941 per $100 of assessed value and a maximum annual levy of $38 million. The district says the money would go to safety (including school resource officers), staffing, transportation, academic programs and student support [24]. It would replace the current $0.17 referendum, which voters approved in 2022 and which expires at the end of 2026 [34].
- Referendum cost: the state-required ballot language shows an $873 annual increase for a $450,000 home. Chalkbeat reported that, because the new rate replaces the current one, the district puts the increase closer to $494 for that home [35]. The district estimates a net increase of about $147.68 a year for the actual median home ($426,700) after the SEA 1 homeowner credit, and its tax calculator uses a rate of $0.3381 rather than the $0.3941 maximum [34]. In an Oct. 8 column, Atha called the state-required ballot wording "misleading" because it uses a rounded-up home value and the maximum rate, and does not subtract the existing referendum [25].
- Budget and SEA 1: in June, district projections cited a funding gap of about $14.8 million in 2026, linked to below-average state funding, property-tax caps and the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) [24]. The district's referendum page gives other figures: a $15.1 million gap and a projected gap of about $17.5 million "in the coming years" [34]. The OneZone Chamber board endorsed the referendum [36].
- Growth and facilities: the district calls itself the fastest-growing school district in Indiana. For 2026-27, fifth grade moved to the elementary schools and two new elementary schools opened, and a second middle school opens in 2027-28. A rebalancing committee finished new boundaries on Oct. 8, 2025, moving about 34% of elementary students [37]. In January 2026 the board hired an architect for a Westfield High School renovation and expansion estimated at $88 million in construction, built in phases through summer 2028 [27]. In January the board and the City Council also held a joint work session on growth and enrollment projections [38].
- Academics and schedule: on March 10, 2026 the board approved moving Westfield schools from trimesters to semesters starting in 2027-28 [39]. The district reported 2026 ILEARN proficiency of 65.2% in math and 61.2% in English language arts, compared with 42.7% and 42% statewide [40].
- Forums and coverage: no 2026 candidate forum or questionnaire for these races turned up in the sources reviewed. The superintendent scheduled community presentations on the referendum in September and October [41]. Anderson and Ogle share a joint campaign website [42]. Crawford-Paree has a campaign website [43]. No campaign website or public statements were found for Willis or Castle.

## William Wayne Anderson — District 2

Incumbent, unopposed in District 2. Board member since 2018 and current vice president, listed as a Republican [29, 27, 6].

Campaign website: https://www.westfieldschoolboardcandidates.com/elect-bill-anderson

### Background

- Goes by Bill Anderson on district and campaign materials. Elected to the District 2 seat in November 2018 [29].
- IT project manager for a contractor that serves as fiscal agent for the state Family and Social Services Administration's Office of Medicaid Policy and Planning [29].
- Graduate of Indiana University Bloomington (public affairs management, minor in economics); grew up in Fort Wayne [29].
- Longtime Westfield resident whose son graduated from Westfield High School in 2022. Has volunteered with schools, Westfield youth sports and Kiwanis [29, 44].

### Path to the ballot

- Appears on the ballot as a Republican, with no opponent in District 2 [6].

### Positions & plans

**School safety**

- His campaign site lists "Serving all of the children in the community and keeping them safe" as a focus [45].

**Teachers & staff**

- His campaign site lists a long-term plan to "retain and attract the best and the brightest" to the district [45].

**Transparency & community input**

- Says a school board "must be responsive and receptive to parents, staff, students and the community at large" [45].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. A joint campaign site with Ogle says the two ran on a list of commitments in 2018 and 2022 and asks voters to re-elect them [42]. In June 2026 he was part of the unanimous vote to place the operating referendum on the ballot [24].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he wants to create a long-term vision for the educational opportunities students need [45].

### What we couldn't verify

- The ballot lists him as William Wayne Anderson. District and campaign materials use Bill Anderson.
- The campaign-site text appears to have been written for earlier campaigns (it refers to 'running' and lists 15 years of IT work, while the district bio says 20). No 2026-specific statements on the referendum, budget or SEA 1 were found.
- Search snippets of a June 18, 2026 article in Current in Westfield (which could not be fetched) said he has served as board secretary, vice president and president. Unverified.
- No endorsements, fundraising reports or forum appearances were found.

## Rebecca L. Ogle — District 4

Incumbent, current board president and a certified public accountant. Listed as a Republican, she faces Steven Castle [30, 27, 6].

Campaign website: https://www.westfieldschoolboardcandidates.com/elect-rebecca-ogle

### Background

- Elected to the District 4 seat in November 2018; board president in 2026 [30, 27].
- CPA and certified fraud examiner. Spent 22 years in public accounting, where she became a partner, focusing on construction and forensic accounting. Has been chief financial officer of Kort Builders since August 2023 [30].
- Serves on the City of Westfield's Road Impact Fee Review Board and Audit Committee; former treasurer of Westfield Youth Sports, Inc. [30]
- Moved to Westfield in 2002, she says because of the schools. Her children are Westfield High School graduates [30, 46].

### Path to the ballot

- Appears on the ballot as a Republican [6].

**Teachers & staff**

- Lists protecting "qualified teachers and staff" as a priority [47].

**School safety**

- Lists "Protect our kids" as a priority [47].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member and the board's 2026 president [27]. The joint campaign site with Anderson asks voters to re-elect them based on commitments made in 2018 and 2022 [42]. In June 2026 she was part of the unanimous vote to place the operating referendum on the ballot [24].

### How they say they'd run the office

### What we couldn't verify

## Gabriel Crawford-Paree — At Large

Engineer, Westfield resident and first-time candidate running as a Democrat for the at-large seat, against incumbent Amber Huff Willis [43, 6].

Campaign website: https://crawfordpareeforwws.com

### Background

- Engineer who grew up in Hamilton County and graduated from Fishers High School (Hamilton Southeastern Schools) in 2016; now lives in Westfield [43].
- His campaign site says he wants to build on the schools' quality and plans to send future children to WWS [43].

### Path to the ballot

- Appears on the ballot as a Democrat [6]. His site says he filed with a party label, allowed under the 2025 law (SB 287). He says he is not running as a Democrat to bring partisanship to the board, but because he believes support for public schools has become a partisan issue, citing voucher programs [43].

### Positions & plans

**Teachers & staff**

- Lists supporting and retaining teachers as his top priority, including a review of "any administrative burden interfering with day-to-day operations" [43].

**Transparency & community input**

- Wants better transparency and community engagement through "more robust communications and documentation" [43].
- Proposes exploring a parental advisory committee to bring parents into board discussions more regularly [43].

**Special education**

- Wants a "robust system" so all students can succeed, including those with more specialized needs and those who have been bullied [43].

**Curriculum & library policy**

- Proposes adding guidance on artificial intelligence to board policy so teachers have guardrails for teaching about it [43].

### On the current officeholder

- His site says he would bring "a unique perspective not currently represented on the school board." It contains no specific criticism of the current board's decisions [43].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Describes his approach as an engineer's problem-solving: gathering information from stakeholders before deciding [43].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign takes online donations through ActBlue and is paid for by the Election Committee of Gabriel Crawford-Paree [43]. No contribution totals were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Search snippets of a June 18, 2026 article in Current in Westfield (which could not be fetched) said he moved to Westfield in 2022 and wants more informative board agendas and easier public comment at meetings. Unverified.
- No stated position on the 2026 operating referendum was found.
- No forum appearances, questionnaires or endorsements were found.
