# Indiana Secretary of State — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review. Quotes were taken from news reports; check key quotes against the Oct. 7 debate video before publishing.

## About the office

Indiana's chief election official and the head of four divisions: Business Services, Elections, Securities, and Auto Dealer Services. The office forms and registers businesses, regulates securities, oversees elections, commissions notaries, registers trademarks, and licenses vehicle dealers [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [2]
- **Term limit:** 8 years in any 12 [2]
- **Divisions:** 4 [1]
- **On the ballot:** No incumbent [3]

### What the office does

- Serves as Indiana's chief election official. For National Voter Registration Act purposes, that role belongs jointly to the two Indiana Election Division co-directors, who are appointed by the governor and may not be from the same party [4].
- Casts the deciding vote when the Election Division co-directors deadlock on the division's budget, spending, or contracts [4].
- Chairs the State Recount Commission, which handles recounts and contests for federal, statewide, and legislative general elections and for major-party primary nominations [2].
- Business Services: business formation and annual filings, commercial (UCC) lien filings, trademarks, and notary commissions. INBiz is the office's online business portal [1, 2].
- Securities Division: investigates and enforces Indiana securities law [2].
- Auto Dealer Services: licenses dealers of automobiles, watercraft, off-road vehicles, and salvage [1].
- Separate from the office: the bipartisan Indiana Election Commission handles election-law violations and penalties [2].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2024 (HEA 1264): state election officials compare Bureau of Motor Vehicles records of temporary-credential holders against the voter rolls; flagged registrants receive notice and 30 days to show citizenship [5].
- 2025 (HEA 1680, effective July 1, 2025): counties must request proof of citizenship from people who registered using a temporary-credential number, and remove those who do not respond within 30 days [6].
- 2025 (SB 10): bars public-college student IDs from being used as voter ID; sent to the governor on April 8, 2025 [7].
- 2025 (HB 1633): directs the Secretary of State to study statewide vote centers and moving municipal elections to even-numbered years [7].
- October 2025: the League of Women Voters of Indiana, Common Cause Indiana, and other groups sued the Secretary of State and the Election Division co-directors in federal court, arguing HEA 1264 and HEA 1680 violate federal voting law [8]. A request for a preliminary injunction was pending as of June 2026 [9].
- June 22, 2026: a federal judge in Washington, D.C., set aside the 2025 overhaul of the federal SAVE citizenship database nationwide; the ruling does not change Indiana's own statutes [10].

## Current officeholder: Diego Morales (Republican)

Took office in January 2023; his term ends in January 2027. He sought re-election but lost the Republican nomination at the June 2026 state convention, so he is not on the November ballot [3].

- 2023: the office paid spot bonuses totaling more than $308,000 to nearly all employees. Morales said they reflected salary adjustments promised before his tenure [11].
- 2023–2024: the office awarded information-technology contracts without competitive bidding, including more than $2.3 million to CleanSlate Technology Group and up to about $1.84 million to MTX Group. Reporters noted contributions to Morales's campaign from CleanSlate and from MTX's founder. The office said it chose vendors with strong track records under state procurement law; no-bid contracts are legal in Indiana [11].
- Reporting has also covered the office's purchase of a roughly $90,000 vehicle from a dealership whose owners donated to his campaign, the hiring of his brother-in-law, and a privately funded trip abroad [12].
- June 2025: at a State Budget Committee hearing, lawmakers questioned him about contracts and travel, then approved more than $10 million in additional funding for technology, the Securities Division, and election support. Morales: "I will not apologize to anyone because my work ethic is unmatched" [13].
- July 2025: signed an agreement with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services to use the federal SAVE database to check the citizenship of registered voters [5].
- November 2025: after Indiana sued the Department of Homeland Security for bulk SAVE access, the state settled; the office uploaded nearly 500,000 voter registrations to DHS and provided the voter list to the U.S. Justice Department [10].
- June 2026: an analysis filed by the plaintiffs in the federal lawsuit counted 3,234 people flagged under HEA 1680, of whom 981 had their registrations canceled and 113 were identified as false positives [9].
- September 2026: the office responded to campaign criticism, stating that all office vendors were contracted through public processes [14].

## This race

- May 2026: U.S. Sen. Jim Banks and Attorney General Todd Rokita withdrew their endorsements of Morales and backed Max Engling [15]. Morales called the criticism politically motivated and stayed in the race [16].
- June 6, 2026: Democratic convention delegates nominated Beau Bayh over Blythe Potter, 1,385 to 883 [17].
- June 20, 2026: Republican convention delegates nominated Max Engling on the second ballot with 867 votes, ahead of Knox County Clerk David Shelton (627) and Morales (134) [3].
- Lauri Shillings was nominated at the Libertarian state convention in March [18]; Greg Ballard qualified by petition under the new Lincoln Party name [19].
- Ballot status is at stake for minor parties: a party whose Secretary of State nominee gets at least 2% keeps its ballot line, and 10% would let it nominate through primaries [19, 18].
- Where all four ballot candidates agree: only eligible citizens should vote, and business filings should list owners, not only registered agents [20, 21].
- Where they differ most: Bayh, Ballard, and Shillings support an audit of the office; Engling prefers a review by the state inspector general. Engling supports the federal SAVE America Act; Ballard and Shillings oppose it [20, 22].
- Debates: Indy Chamber HobNob, Aug. 26, 2026 [20]; Indiana Debate Commission at WFYI, Oct. 7, 2026 [23].

## Beau Bayh

Attorney and former Marine Corps officer in his first run for office. Says his top priority is an independent audit of the Secretary of State's office [24, 25].

Campaign website: https://beaubayh.com

### Background

- Birch Evans "Beau" Bayh IV is the son of former Indiana governor and U.S. senator Evan Bayh [26].
- Bachelor's degree in government and law degree, both from Harvard [27].
- Served as a U.S. Marine Corps infantry officer, reaching captain; deployed to Europe as part of the U.S. response to Russia's invasion of Ukraine; honorably discharged in 2023 [28].
- Worked for Cummins and Barnes & Thornburg during law school, then clerked for Judge David F. Hamilton of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit [28].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced Oct. 6, 2025 [26]; nominated at the Democratic state convention June 6, 2026, with 61% of delegate votes [17].

### Positions & plans

**Election administration**

- Campaign position: "No illegal votes – period – but every legal Hoosier's vote should count"; wants higher turnout while protecting security [25].
- Supports Indiana's current voter ID law [22].
- Says county clerks from both major parties run Indiana's elections well and that the state's elections are secure; disputes Engling's statements about noncitizen voting [22].
- Would expand early voting and keep primaries open to all voters [24].
- Would end straight-ticket voting, which he says reduces competition in elections [29, 22].
- Supports citizen-led ballot initiatives and statewide referenda, including on redistricting; these would require action by the legislature [25, 24].
- On the 2026 Senate District 23 recount, said the disputed ballots should have been counted because voter intent was clear and no fraud was alleged [20].

**Business services**

- Wants to make it easier to start and grow a small business and to reduce paperwork [25].
- Supports requiring business filings to list owners, not only registered agents [20].

**Running the office**

- Would order an independent, forensic audit of the office on day one, publish the findings, and refer any wrongdoing to prosecutors [24, 22].
- Pledges not to award contracts to campaign contributors or hire relatives [22, 24].
- Would end the office's partnership with Turning Point USA and says any outside partnerships should be nonpartisan [24].

### On the current officeholder

- Has criticized the office's vehicle purchase, the hiring of relatives, and no-bid contracts awarded to campaign donors [17, 22].
- At the Oct. 7 debate, argued that the inspector general review Engling favors is not sufficient [22].
- Aug. 26, 2026, on Morales: "That's such an important responsibility and it's being entrusted to someone who I think is not up for the job" [30].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Sell the office's SUV and drive his own car [24].
- Work with the governor and legislative majority on changes that require new laws, such as ending straight-ticket voting [29].
- Says he would serve a full term; has not ruled out a future run for governor [24].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Reported $2,695,117 raised and $2,311,139 cash on hand through June 30, 2026 [31].
- Republican state Sen. Greg Walker of Columbus endorsed him in October 2026 [32].

### What we couldn't verify

- No published positions found on the Securities Division, voting equipment, voter-roll maintenance, mail voting, or the SAVE America Act.
- The audit plan he said was coming in June had not been published as of this review.
- Campaign finance reports covering July–September are due Oct. 16, 2026.

## Max Engling

U.S. Senate staff member and former U.S. House aide who won the Republican nomination over the incumbent. Says preventing noncitizen voting is his top priority [33, 34].

### Background

- Age 39, of the Cicero area in Hamilton County; returned to Indiana in 2023 [33].
- Graduated from IUPUI in 2010 [33]; master's degree from the U.S. Naval War College [35].
- About 12 years as a Republican staff member in the U.S. House, including roughly five years as an aide to then-Speaker Kevin McCarthy; cites experience with the House Administration Committee, which oversees federal election law [33, 3].
- Since January 2025, central Indiana regional director and adviser to U.S. Sen. Jim Banks; on leave to campaign [33].
- Finished third in the 2024 Republican primary for Indiana's 5th Congressional District [33].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced May 20, 2026, after Banks and Rokita withdrew their support for Morales [35, 15].
- Nominated at the Republican state convention on June 20, 2026, on the second ballot with about 53% of delegate votes [3].

### Positions & plans

**Election administration**

- Calls preventing noncitizen registration and voting his top priority, saying "even one instance of illegal voting is too many" [34].
- Cited the state's review that found 165 noncitizens registered and 21 who had voted [36].
- Supports checking legal status when driver's licenses are issued and a different license design for noncitizens [37, 38].
- Supports the federal SAVE America Act; he was the only candidate at the Aug. 26 forum to do so [20].
- Would work with legislators to close party primaries to voters not affiliated with the party [39].
- Supports early voting but suggests a period of about two weeks, shorter than Indiana's current 28 days [38, 12].
- Supports keeping the straight-ticket voting option [22].
- Supports post-election audits and paper audit trails [38, 37].
- On the 2026 Senate District 23 recount, said the recount commission followed the law [20].
- Opposes citizen ballot initiatives, saying issues should be worked through the legislature [22].
- Opposes a mailed paper voter guide because of its cost; prefers digital tools [20].

**Business services**

- Would screen business filings for shell companies, especially trucking firms that use multiple entities to avoid scrutiny, and refer suspected fraud to prosecutors or the attorney general [37, 38].
- Supports listing owners in business filings and wants agencies to reuse information businesses have already submitted [20].
- Proposes an online portal where Hoosiers can report fraud concerns [40].

**Running the office**

- Supports a review of the office by the state inspector general rather than an independent audit [20, 22].
- Says the office needs new processes and procedures to run well [38].

### On the current officeholder

- Points to his convention win over Morales: "I am the only candidate that has actually done something about the issues that we've seen in the office" [22].
- Said at the Aug. 26 debate that he would not hire relatives [20].

### How they say they'd run the office

- First priority: work with legislators to close primaries [39].
- Pursue distinct driver's licenses for noncitizens, a fraud-reporting portal, and screening of business filings for shell companies [38, 40, 37].
- Says he would serve a full four-year term [20].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by U.S. Sen. Jim Banks and Attorney General Todd Rokita [15].
- Reported $201,626 raised and $150,121 cash on hand through June 30, 2026 [31].

### What we couldn't verify

- No published positions found on the Securities Division, mail voting, or voting-equipment vendors.
- No campaign website or voter-guide questionnaire could be read for this review.
- News reports about specific campaign contributions could not be read in full and are not included.
- Campaign finance reports covering July–September are due Oct. 16, 2026.

## Greg Ballard

Former two-term mayor of Indianapolis and retired Marine officer, running on the new Lincoln Party line. Says the office should be run as a nonpartisan, professional office [12, 41].

### Background

- Mayor of Indianapolis from 2008 to 2016 [42].
- Retired from the U.S. Marine Corps as a lieutenant colonel after 23 years [43].
- Was a Republican until this campaign [42]; now a visiting fellow at the University of Indianapolis [43].
- Cites his management of the city budget during the recession and infrastructure rebuilding as mayor [44].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced an independent campaign March 4, 2026 [44]. Indiana law does not allow an "Independent Party" label, so he chose the name Lincoln Party [41].
- Qualified by petition; the Election Division lists his filing date as July 14, 2026 [45, 19].
- The Lincoln Party is chaired by Nathan Gotsch, Ballard's campaign manager, and has not published a platform [19].

### Positions & plans

**Election administration**

- Wants "a nonpartisan, low-key professional office"; says county clerks run elections well and that he sees no evidence of widespread voter fraud [12].
- Would keep Indiana's 28-day early voting period and end straight-ticket voting [12].
- Proposes mailing a paper voter guide to every registered voter, plus an app, at an estimated $2.5 million or more per cycle, and suggests the parties pay for their own primaries [20].
- Would register 17-year-olds through high schools and expand voter outreach [46].
- Considers Indiana's existing citizenship safeguards strong and calls the federal SAVE America Act an overreach [20].
- Would make the Indiana Election Commission and State Recount Commission nonpartisan and move some recount work to administrative law judges [46, 47].
- On the 2026 Senate District 23 recount, said voter intent should come first and the disputed ballots should have been counted [20].

**Business services**

- Says the office's business website is outdated and wants faster service for small businesses and farmers [43, 46].
- Supports requiring business filings to list owners [20].

**Running the office**

- Would post all office contracts online and pledges not to raise money for or endorse candidates while in office [43].
- Supports an audit of the office, with emphasis on travel expenses [20].

### On the current officeholder

- At his announcement, said the office's current leadership "has shown an eagerness to engage in partisan politics," while declining to rate Morales's performance [44, 12].
- At the Oct. 7 debate, said he would bring in a law-enforcement professional to investigate the office [48].
- Morales, in response to Ballard's entry, described him as a Democrat calling himself an independent [44].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Audit the office and bring in an outside investigator [20, 48].
- Mailed voter guide and app; nonpartisan election and recount commissions; contracts posted online; updated business website [20, 46, 43].
- Says he would serve a full four-year term [20].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- April–June 2026: raised about $672,000 and spent about $746,000, with nearly $450,000 from the PAC Hoosiers for Competitive Elections [49].
- Reported donors include former Eli Lilly CEO John Lechleiter ($100,000) and former Lt. Gov. John Mutz [19].

### What we couldn't verify

- No published positions found on the Securities Division, voting equipment, voter-roll maintenance, or mail voting.
- No campaign website was found.
- A reported endorsement by former Lt. Gov. Sue Ellspermann could not be confirmed from a full article.
- Campaign finance reports covering July–September are due Oct. 16, 2026.

## Lauri A. Shillings

University of Indianapolis creative director and small-business owner, nominated by the Libertarian Party. Focuses on modernizing the office's website and making election audit results public [50, 51].

### Background

- Age 55, of Carmel; grew up in Wingate [50, 52].
- Bachelor's degree in community and nonprofit leadership from the University of Indianapolis, where she is creative director in the marketing office [52, 18].
- Has run the small business Pirate Smile Media since 2000 [20].
- Chair of the Hamilton County Libertarian Party since 2021; the party's 2024 nominee for Indiana's 5th Congressional District [18].

### Path to the ballot

- Formed an exploratory committee in May 2025 [53]; nominated at the Libertarian state convention in Fort Wayne in March 2026 [18].
- Her party needs at least 2% of the vote in this race to keep its ballot line [18].

### Positions & plans

**Election administration**

- Calls Indiana's elections secure [50].
- Would publish the results of county post-election audits and the corrective actions taken, and expand audits to more counties [50, 51].
- Opposes additional proof-of-citizenship requirements such as the SAVE America Act, saying they burden people whose names changed through marriage, divorce, or adoption, and that existing checks work [20, 22].
- Supports hand-marked paper ballots counted by machine, while noting current equipment limits [50].
- Opposes restricting who may vote in a party's primary [50].
- Wants training and tools for county clerks without directing their methods, and more perspectives beyond the two major parties on election and recount boards [51].
- Would rebuild the online election portal so voters can see candidates and who funds them [51].

**Business services**

- Says her top priority is modernizing the office's website and business filing site, which she describes as outdated [54, 50].
- Would ask lawmakers to reduce business licensing paperwork and fees [52].

**Running the office**

- Supports an audit of the office's budget and spending, followed by regular audits [20, 51].

### On the current officeholder

- When she began exploring a run in 2025, said the current office had done very little for Hoosiers [53].
- Raised privacy concerns about the office sharing Indiana voter-roll data with the federal government [54].
- Says the office should be treated as a public trust rather than a political tool [18].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Overhaul the office website and business filing site [54, 50].
- Publish election audit results; audit the office's spending; support county clerks with training and tools [50, 51].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements found. Reported fundraising through June 30, 2026, differs by source: $21,073 (WFYI) and $28,678 (Wikipedia, citing state filings) [50, 31].

### What we couldn't verify

- No published positions found on the Securities Division, early or mail voting, or voter-roll maintenance.
- No campaign website was found.
- Campaign finance reports covering July–September are due Oct. 16, 2026.
