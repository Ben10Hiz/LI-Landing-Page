# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Matt Commons (Republican)

Matt Commons, a Republican from Williamsport, is a high school social studies teacher and Army veteran who took office in November 2024 and is seeking a second term [3]. He won the seat after defeating incumbent Rep. Sharon Negele in the 2024 Republican primary [4].

- Committees: Local Government; Natural Resources; and Veterans Affairs and Public Safety, as announced by the House Republican caucus in December 2024 [3].
- 2025: the caucus says Commons supported Senate Enrolled Act 1, the 2025 property-tax law, together with Rep. Mark Genda, and co-sponsored a parental-rights bill that Gov. Mike Braun ceremonially signed on May 29, 2025 [3].
- Dec. 5, 2025: voted yea on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 before the Senate rejected it on Dec. 11, 2025 [5, 6].
- 2026 session: BillTrack50 lists him as a sponsor of House Bill 1343 on public-safety matters, and records his yea vote on its conference committee report on Feb. 27, 2026 [5].
- 2025-2026: the caucus lists him as backing legislation on foreign adversaries' offices, technology and land holdings in Indiana, and supporting the 2026 expansion of property-tax relief for disabled veterans [3].

## This race

- House District 13 includes all of Benton and Warren counties and portions of Fountain, Jasper, Montgomery, Newton, Tippecanoe and White counties in west-central Indiana [3]. It is not an open seat; Commons is seeking re-election [2].
- Commons was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary; Brenna Geswein won the Democratic primary with 1,670 votes over Edward Moyer Jr.'s 457, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [7]. Ben Davis is the Socialist Party of Indiana nominee, chosen by party convention [7].
- No debate or candidate forum for this race was found as of October 10, 2026.
- Commons's campaign emphasizes local control, agriculture, gun rights and opposition to tax increases [8]; Geswein's emphasizes public-school funding, health care costs and scrutiny of data-center incentives [9]; Davis's emphasizes wages, public-school funding, local health care and opposition to AI data centers [10].

## Ben Davis

Ben Davis, a Lafayette native who describes himself as a career IT technologist, is the Socialist Party of Indiana nominee [11, 7].

Campaign website: https://www.bendavisforindiana.com/

### Background

- Describes himself as a "career IT technologist" and a "Lafayette native" [11].
- BallotReady lists him as a senior strategic consultant at GadellNet Consulting Services, a former Ivy Tech adjunct instructor and former Subaru of Indiana Automotive employee, with an MBA and bachelor's degree from Western Governors University [12].
- His campaign says he is an activist with groups including PFLAG and DSA, a longtime platelet donor, and a St. Baldrick's children's cancer fundraiser [11].

### Path to the ballot

- Nominated by the Socialist Party of Indiana at its convention, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [7]. His campaign site describes the minor-party route as a longer road to qualifying for the ballot than major-party candidates face [10].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Lists "Not living, but thriving wages" as a priority and says "Life for working Hoosiers continues to worsen under the two-party system" [10].
- BallotReady's profile says he believes workers should receive more of the value they create [12].

**Education**

- Lists "Fully funding public schools" as a priority, saying public education has been "hollowed out" [10].
- BallotReady's profile says he supports roughly doubling teacher pay and opposes public funds for private or charter schools [12].

**Health care**

- Lists "Investing in local health care" as a priority; BallotReady's profile says he supports guaranteed health care for everyone [10, 12].

**Data centers**

- Lists "No AI Data Centers" as a campaign position; BallotReady's profile cites noise, pollution, water and power use and rising electricity rates [10, 12].

**Energy & utilities**

- BallotReady's profile says he supports building out renewable energy and retiring coal and gas plants quickly, citing Benton County as a model [12].

**Immigration**

- BallotReady's profile says he calls to "Abolish ICE" and opposes state cooperation with federal immigration enforcement [12].

### On the current officeholder

- His campaign site does not mention Commons; it says "Indiana House District 13 needs a better option!" [11].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he will "fight against corporate interests" and "lower the cost of living" [11].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign site, which links to the Socialist Party of Indiana; donations are collected through Donorbox [10].
- No fundraising totals were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- His Substack profile describes him as a "Democratic socialist candidate"; the official ballot lists him as Socialist Party of Indiana.
- Several position details come from BallotReady's profile rather than his own campaign pages.
- No news coverage, interviews or forum appearances were found.
- No campaign-finance figures were retrieved from the Indiana campaign finance portal.

## Brenna Geswein

Brenna Geswein, a Democrat, is an engineer at Caterpillar in Lafayette and a financial coach with Habitat for Humanity of Lafayette [13].

Campaign website: https://electbrenna.com/

### Background

- Has worked as an engineer at Caterpillar in Lafayette for more than 15 years and holds a mechanical engineering degree and an MBA from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, per her campaign [13].
- Served six years on the board of Habitat for Humanity of Lafayette and now works there as a financial coach [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 1,670 votes to 457 for Edward Moyer Jr., a retired principal, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [7].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Supports fully funding public schools and opposes vouchers, saying "Public money should strengthen the schools that serve every child in every community" [9].
- Calls for smaller class sizes, competitive teacher pay and accountability for public dollars [9].

**Health care**

- Would "Demand fair pricing and greater transparency from hospitals, insurers, and drug companies," supports steady funding for local health departments and protecting rural clinics [9].
- Supports expanding access to contraception and maternity care "without political interference" [9].

**Data centers**

- Criticizes data centers' sales-tax exemption on utility use ("These facilities are exempt from sales tax on their massive utility usage") and calls for reviewing tax incentives, protecting families from shifted utility costs, and transparency on jobs and energy use [9].

**Housing**

- Lists attainable homeownership as a focus, calling housing, schools and health care "not partisan issues" [13].

**Government**

- Supports accessible voter information, civic education, and "Transparent decisions and clear reporting on public spending" [9].

### On the current officeholder

- Her campaign site does not name Commons; it says she believes the legislature has lost sight of basics and that she aims to "bring responsive representation back to the Statehouse" [13, 14].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she is running "to protect public schools, strengthening rural communities, expand access to healthcare" [14].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- iVoterGuide lists endorsements she reported from AFT Indiana, Indiana Reproductive Freedom, 314 Action and Planned Parenthood [15].
- No fundraising totals were found; the campaign collects donations through ActBlue [14].

### What we couldn't verify

- A search-result snippet referred to a Journal & Courier candidate Q&A in which she discussed property taxes, utility costs and data centers and gave West Lafayette as her residence; the article was not retrieved, so these details are unverified.
- No forum appearances were found.
- No campaign-finance figures were retrieved from the Indiana campaign finance portal.

## Matt Commons

Matt Commons, a Republican from Williamsport, is a high school social studies teacher and Army veteran who has represented District 13 since November 2024 [3].

Campaign website: https://www.commonsforstatehouse.com/

### Background

- Served five years in the Army's 82nd Airborne Division, including two tours in Afghanistan, and received a Purple Heart, per the caucus [3].
- Is a high school social studies teacher and youth football coach; holds a bachelor's degree from Purdue University, a master's in history from Liberty University and a master's in educational leadership from Indiana State University [3].
- Lives in Williamsport [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [7].
- First won the seat by defeating incumbent Rep. Sharon Negele in the 2024 Republican primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Local control**

- Says "State and national policies should not be pushed onto rural Hoosiers without the input of our local communities" [8].

**Agriculture**

- Says "I'll support our local farmers by advocating for pro-agriculture policies and cutting unnecessary regulations" [8].

**Education**

- Says "I'll be a voice for students, parents, and teachers to improve Hoosier student outcomes" [8].

**Taxes & spending**

- Says "I'll oppose tax increases and reduce spending by exposing government waste, fraud, and abuse" [8].

**Gun rights**

- Describes himself as an NRA Patriot life member and pledges to protect the right of self-defense [8].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted yea on the December 2025 House passage of HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill [5].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. His campaign site does not mention his opponents; it says "We've accomplished a lot, but there's more work to be done" [16].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists "stopping government overreach, defending our Constitutional freedoms, protecting parental rights" among his priorities [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign site has an endorsements page, but endorsers' names could not be read from it [17].
- No fundraising totals were found; the campaign collects donations through WinRed [16].

### What we couldn't verify

- 2024 primary vote totals against Sharon Negele were not retrieved.
- Individual votes on the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) and the 2025 state budget (HEA 1001) could not be confirmed from a readable roll call; the caucus describes him as supporting SEA 1.
- No news interviews or forum appearances for the 2026 race were found.
- No campaign-finance figures were retrieved from the Indiana campaign finance portal.
