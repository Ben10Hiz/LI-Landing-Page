# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Ryan Lauer (Republican)

Rep. Ryan Lauer (R-Columbus), a technical specialist at Cummins and former Bartholomew County Council president, has represented House District 59 since 2018 [3, 4]. He is vice chair of the Family, Children and Human Affairs Committee, and his recent bills have focused on child welfare, protective orders and cancer research [3, 5, 6].

- Committees: Family, Children and Human Affairs (vice chair); Financial Institutions; Veterans Affairs and Public Safety [3].
- 2024: Authored House Enrolled Act 1101, creating Safe Baby Courts, problem-solving courts for child-in-need-of-services cases involving children up to age 3; ceremonially signed in August 2024 [5].
- 2025: Co-authored House Enrolled Act 1453, creating the Pediatric Cancer Research and Treatment Grant Program and the Breast Cancer Research Fund; it passed the General Assembly unanimously [7].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [8]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [9].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [10].
- 2026: Authored House Enrolled Act 1286, letting courts consider military protective orders (and setting rules for Canadian protective orders) in civil protective-order cases; ceremonially signed in April 2026 [6].

## This race

- House District 59 covers part of Bartholomew County, including Columbus, per the House Republican caucus [3].
- Lauer was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary (4,186 votes); no Democratic candidate appeared on the District 59 primary ballot [11]. Josh Burbrink is the Democratic nominee on the November ballot [2].
- Lauer was re-elected with 63.8% of the vote in 2024, per Wikipedia's compilation [4].
- No debate or candidate forum for this race was found.
- Key differences: Burbrink criticizes Lauer's vote for the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1), saying it shifted costs to local services such as the library, and calls for limits on data centers' water and power use [12, 13]. Lauer voted for SEA 1 and lists lowering property taxes for lower- and middle-income families among his priorities [8, 14].

## Josh Burbrink

Josh Burbrink is a broadband business intelligence director, volunteer firefighter and library-support nonprofit president from Bartholomew County [15]. His campaign centers on the effects of the 2025 property-tax law on local services and on data center regulation [13].

Campaign website: https://www.voteburbrink.com

### Background

- Business intelligence director at Mainstream Fiber Networks, which brings high-speed internet to rural Indiana, per his campaign [15, 16].
- Volunteer firefighter with the Harrison Township Volunteer Fire Department since 2017; president of the Friends of the Bartholomew County Public Library; member of the Bartholomew County Planning and Zoning Board; executive committee member of the Sierra Club's Winding Waters Chapter [15].
- Ran unsuccessfully for Bartholomew County Council in 2022 and county commissioner in 2024 [15, 16]. Lives in Harrison Township, Bartholomew County [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Did not appear on the May 5, 2026 primary ballot, which had no Democratic candidate for District 59 [11]; he is listed as the Democratic nominee on the November ballot [2].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Says "SEA 1 handed businesses billions while homeowners got a $300 cap" and that the law's costs fall on schools, the library and fire departments [12, 13].

**Libraries & schools**

- Says the county library faces a $761,490 cut and pledges to "defend both the funding and the freedom to read" [12].

**Data centers**

- Calls for hard limits on water withdrawals with independent review before permits, and says data centers should pay for their own power and water infrastructure [16].
- Opposes undisclosed land deals and projects reclassified to skip public notice: "If it affects your town, your town gets a vote" [16].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says new electricity demand should be met with solar, wind and storage rather than extending existing coal and gas plants [16].

**Economy & jobs**

- Opposes tax breaks or subsidies that lack "a real, enforceable community benefit" [16].

**Other**

- His issues page also addresses reproductive rights, immigration and local control, public health, equality, and license plate cameras [13].

### On the current officeholder

- Says "Ryan Lauer voted for a property tax cut that doesn't cost the state a dime" and "He cut your services so the state could keep your money" [13].
- Says Lauer voted yes on HB 1406, a bill Burbrink connects to library funding [13].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he is running because "our community deserves someone who will show up and do the work" [15].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign site [12].

### What we couldn't verify

- How Burbrink was selected as nominee (likely a Democratic Party ballot-vacancy selection after the primary) was not confirmed; The Republic (Columbus) blocked access.
- A search snippet indicated Columbus attorney Ross Thomas had earlier filed for the District 59 Democratic nomination; Thomas is the Democratic nominee for Senate District 41. Not verified.
- Burbrink's claim that Lauer voted yes on HB 1406 was not checked against a roll call; the bill's content was not independently verified.
- He did not answer iVoterGuide's questionnaire. No fundraising data found.

## Ryan Lauer

Ryan Lauer is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2018 [3, 4]. He lists protecting abused and neglected children, property tax relief and a balanced budget as his priorities [14].

Campaign website: https://ryanlauer.com

### Background

- Technical specialist at Cummins for more than a decade; Indiana University biochemistry graduate [3].
- Elected to the Bartholomew County Council in 2010 and named its president in 2014; served on the Bartholomew County Sheriff's Merit Board and the Community Corrections Advisory Board [3].
- Lives in Columbus [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, receiving 4,186 votes [11].

### Positions & plans

**Child welfare**

- On Safe Baby Courts: "When dealing with a CHINS case, time is always of the essence to return them to their guardians or find a new home" [5].

**Public safety**

- On HEA 1286: "When a victim of domestic violence goes to court seeking a protective order, they should have every tool ready and available to them" [6].
- Says he co-authored Indiana's constitutional carry law, which he describes as allowing all adult citizens to possess firearms [14].

**Property taxes**

- Lists "Lowering or eliminating property taxes for lower and middle class families" as a legislative priority [14]; voted for SEA 1 in 2025 [8].

**Education**

- Wrote that "Taxpayer funds should follow the student" [14].

**Health care**

- On cancer research grants: "Through this new law, we can support their efforts to help those battling this terrible disease" [7].

**Abortion**

- Wrote: "I am pro-life and believe life is precious" [14].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted for the 2025 congressional redistricting bill (HB 1032) [10]; no public statement from Lauer on the vote was found.

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Lauer about Burbrink were found; his campaign site does not discuss the 2026 race [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Priority legislation he proposes, in order: "Protecting abused and neglected children"; "Lowering or eliminating property taxes for lower and middle class families"; "Improving road safety and maintaining a balanced budget" [14].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign site lists the Indiana Firefighters Union, Indiana Fraternal Order of Police, Indiana Right to Life, National Rifle Association, Indiana State Police Alliance, Indiana Chamber of Commerce, Indiana Manufacturers Association and NFIB [17]; the site does not say which election cycle these apply to.

### What we couldn't verify

- No 2026 news coverage of Lauer's campaign could be read (The Republic blocked access).
- No fundraising data found.
- No public explanation of his HB 1032 redistricting vote was found.
