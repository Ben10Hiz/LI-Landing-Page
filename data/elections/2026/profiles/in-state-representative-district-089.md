# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Mitch Gore (Democratic)

Mitch Gore, a Democrat from Indianapolis, was first elected in 2020 and is ranking minority member of the House Courts and Criminal Code Committee, an assistant minority whip, and a member of the Employment, Labor and Pensions and the Veterans Affairs and Public Safety committees [3]. The House Democratic caucus describes him as a major with the Marion County Sheriff's Office who lives in Warren Township [3].

- 2023–2024: Mirror Indy reported that a 2023 bill he authored clarified that devices converting semi-automatic guns to fire like automatic weapons are illegal, and that he called a bill creating a missing-persons alert for at-risk veterans his proudest achievement [4]. His campaign says he helped pass the law creating Indiana's Green Alert for missing veterans [5].
- March 2025: Authored HB 1518, which the House Democratic caucus said would require state entities to buy only non-luxury vehicles and was headed to the governor; Gore said "taxpayer dollars should not be used to buy luxury vehicles" [6].
- April 2025: Voted against SB 1, the property-tax and local government finance bill, which passed the House 65–29 [7], and against the conference report on HEA 1001, the 2025–2027 state budget, adopted 66–27 [8].
- December 5, 2025: Voted against HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57–41 [9]. He told Mirror Indy he offered an amendment calling on Congress to outlaw partisan gerrymandering nationwide, which was defeated along party lines [10].
- January 28, 2026: Recorded as not voting on HB 1002, the electric utility affordability bill, which passed the House 89–4 [11].
- February 2026: Said Republicans "decided to kill my bill in a closed door meeting" after HB 1066, which would have extended the luxury-vehicle ban to local governments and had passed committee unanimously, was pulled from the House calendar [12]. Offered an amendment to SB 76 (immigration enforcement) that the caucus said would have protected local officers from detaining people longer than the law allows; it failed 34–61 [13].

## This race

- Mirror Indy described House District 89 in 2024 as including parts of Center, Franklin, Perry and Warren townships in Indianapolis [4]; the House Democratic caucus describes it as portions of Marion County [3].
- Gore won the seat in 2020 by defeating Republican incumbent Cindy Kirchhofer and was re-elected in 2022 and 2024, the last time with 55.8% of the vote [4, 14].
- Gore was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary, and no Republican is on the November ballot; the Libertarian Party nominated Weingarten at its March 2026 state convention [14, 15, 2].
- No debate or forum between the candidates was found as of October 10, 2026. Mirror Indy's voter guide includes answers from Gore; Weingarten had not responded [10, 16].

## Mitch Gore

Mitch Gore is the Democratic incumbent, first elected in 2020, and a Marion County Sheriff's Office major who serves as ranking member of the House Courts and Criminal Code Committee [3].

Campaign website: https://www.mitchgore.com/

### Background

- The caucus says he has spent his life on Indianapolis' east and southeast sides, has helped run his family's Washington Square restaurant, and worked in community development for the City of Indianapolis before joining the sheriff's office, where he is a major [3].
- Told Mirror Indy he is the only currently serving law enforcement officer in the General Assembly [10].

### Path to the ballot

- Was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Public safety**

- His campaign says he has worked to keep nonviolent people with addiction out of the justice system and sponsored bipartisan legislation to expand mental health treatment [5]. He told Mirror Indy he wants improper use of license plate readers by law enforcement to be a felony and more law enforcement training [10]. Mirror Indy reported in 2024 that he planned to reintroduce a bill making failure to secure a firearm a low-level felony when a dependent uses it to cause injury or death [4].

**Health care**

- Supports limiting prescription drug costs, changing the prior authorization system, capping what hospitals can charge for procedures, and allowing women to make their own reproductive health care decisions, according to his campaign [5].

**Economy & jobs**

- Lists lowering costs, raising take-home pay, building affordable housing and making child care easier to find and afford among his priorities, and wants to shift Indiana's tax system so it "favors ordinary people, not the powerful or already-wealthy" [10]. His campaign says he supports repealing the right-to-work law [5].

**Ethics & government**

- Supports term limits, tougher ethics laws, more disclosure by officials and reducing money in politics [5, 10].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said he "opposed it fiercely" regarding mid-decade redistricting and called partisan gerrymandering wrong "no matter who does it" [10].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Gore about his challenger were found [10].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Told Mirror Indy he sees bipartisan opportunities in 2027 on regulating automated license plate readers, expanding officer training, and firearm safety measures [10].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign lists endorsements from the Indy Chamber, Indiana Chamber of Commerce, Professional Fire Fighters Union of Indiana, AFSCME, American Federation of Teachers, IBEW, Indiana State Teachers Association, United Association, Indiana State AFL-CIO, Fraternal Order of Police and Better Indiana [17].

### What we couldn't verify

- 2026 campaign finance totals were not found.
- Wikipedia reports that in 2025 Gore offered an amendment to HB 1041 (transgender athletes in college sports) concerning state-paid funerals; this was not confirmed in news coverage reviewed and is not used.
- A search summary said HB 1518 (2025) was signed by Gov. Braun; the caucus release read says only that it was headed to the governor.
