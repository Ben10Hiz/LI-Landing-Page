# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Hamilton Southeastern Schools (HSE) serves Fishers and surrounding areas of Hamilton County. The district counted 19,885 students on Sept. 1, 2026 (unofficial), its first count under 20,000 in a decade and the seventh straight year of decline [24]. Superintendent Matt Kegley, a longtime HSE administrator, was named in April 2026 after Patrick Mapes resigned [25].
- The seven-member Board of School Trustees has four seats elected from numbered districts (terms ending December 2026) and three township seats for Fall Creek, Wayne and Delaware townships (terms ending December 2028) [26]. In district races, voters vote only in the district where they live [27].
- Four seats are on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot, one per district. None of the incumbents is running: District 1 Tiffany Pascoe (president), District 2 Juanita Albright, District 3 Dawn Lang (vice president) and District 4 Ben Orr [26, 28]. Albright is the Republican nominee for state Senate District 31 [28]. Stephanie Braden is unopposed in District 4 [29].
- 2026 is the first HSE election in which candidates may list a party; there are no school board primaries [29]. Of nine candidates, two filed as Republicans, one as a Libertarian, one as an independent and five with no party [29].
- Referendum: the board voted 6-0 on June 10, 2026 (Pascoe abstaining) to place a replacement operating referendum on the ballot, up to 36 cents per $100 of assessed value for eight years, replacing the 2023 referendum approved with about 70% [30, 31]. District officials say the 2025 property tax law (SEA 1) shrinks the base the 2023 rate applies to and project losing about $45.3 million from 2026 through 2031 without a new levy; the first-year rate recommended is 22 cents, a projected $26 increase for the median homeowner, though state-required ballot language cites $700 a year on a $400,000 home [32, 31]. No organized local opposition had formed as of Sept. 23 [32].
- Budget and staffing: in spring 2026 the district issued reduction-in-force notices to 18 teachers and adjusted nearly 60 positions; all 18 were later rehired through attrition, and a budget task force identified about $7.8 million in cuts [33, 30, 34]. The proposed 2027 budget is $323.9 million, about $15 million below 2026; the board votes Oct. 28 [35]. Teachers received no raises the prior year and contract bargaining began in September [35].
- Leadership turnover: HSE has had several superintendent changes since 2023 (Yvonne Stokes resigned in 2023, Patrick Mapes was hired in 2024 and resigned effective April 2026) [25].
- Facilities: the board approved new attendance boundaries for 2026-27, driven by the expansion of Fishers Elementary from 450 to 700 seats and crowding at some east-side schools [36].
- Board divisions: at an Aug. 12, 2026 meeting, members voted 5-2 to send proposed wording for a homeless-student policy back to committee after disagreeing over terminology, and two trustees criticized President Pascoe's social media post inviting reports of political content in classrooms; Pascoe called the criticism "false accusations" [37].
- Academics: on the redesigned 2025-26 ILEARN, the district reported 68.1% math and 66.4% English proficiency in grades 3-8 versus about 42% statewide; the state calls 2026 a new baseline [38].
- Forums and coverage: LarryInFishers.com ran a podcast interview series with candidates; by Oct. 9 it had posted episodes with Wren, Maqsood, Wright, Keck, Perry, Turk and Fullhart [39, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45]. No joint candidate forum was found in reviewed coverage. All candidates who discussed the referendum except Wren said they support it; Wren said he is torn and wants an audit first [39, 42, 43, 40, 41, 44, 45].
- Endorsements: the HSE Education Association endorsed Maqsood, Keck, Fullhart and Braden; Fishers Mayor Scott Fadness endorsed Perry and Turk [46, 47, 48].

## Anthony Jason Wren — District HSE 1

Libertarian candidate and HSE parent whose top priority is school security [39].

### Background

- Parent of an HSE graduate [39].
- Served as vice president and then president of student government in college [39].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed June 17, 2026, as a Libertarian, the first third-party candidate in the HSE field [49, 29]. Listed on the state list as Libertarian [6].
- Said he left the Republican Party because he was frustrated with both major parties [39].
- Not an incumbent; the District 1 seat is open because board president Tiffany Pascoe is not running [28, 26].

### Positions & plans

**School safety**

- Said news of guns in schools, including incidents in Noblesville and at North Central High School in Indianapolis, pushed him to run [39].
- Main proposal: weapons-detection scanners at every entrance of every HSE school, like those at the Fishers Event Center; eventually a district police department instead of relying on Fishers Police school resource officers [39].

**Budget & taxes**

- Said he is torn on the operating referendum and wants an audit of district finances before more money is requested; questioned whether HSE applies for grants. Interviewer Larry Lannan noted the district regularly applies for grants and that most Hamilton County districts are pursuing referendums after state property tax changes [39].

**Academics**

- Wants more focus on reading, writing and arithmetic, including handwriting and cursive [39].
- Said teachers he has spoken with feel they have lost control of their classrooms, while acknowledging his examples came from outside HSE [39].

**Student health & conduct**

- Would require students to keep phones in lockers and have school Wi-Fi block social media, while supporting the medical and disability exemptions in state law [39].
- Raised concerns about folic acid in enriched flour in school meals, citing online personality Gary Brecka; LarryInFishers noted federal agencies have required folic acid in enriched grain since 1998 to help prevent neural tube birth defects [39].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said he has met with the HSE superintendent to discuss his safety concerns [39].

### What we couldn't verify

- No Hamilton County campaign finance reports were reviewed; fundraising is unknown.
- No candidate forum or written questionnaire (e.g., VOTE411, Current in Fishers) was reviewed; Current in Fishers pages returned 403.
- Occupation was not reported in reviewed coverage.
- LarryInFishers reported he had no findable campaign web presence as of June 18; none was found in this review.

## Greg Wright — District HSE 1

Dentist and small-business owner running as a Republican; supports the November referendum [29, 41].

Campaign website: https://wrightforhse.com

### Background

- Dentist practicing in Indianapolis and Lapel; owns his own practice [41, 51].
- Parent of three HSE graduates; has coached junior varsity lacrosse [41].
- Son of two retired Indiana public school teachers [41, 51].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Republican by May 26, 2026 [52]. Listed on the state list as Republican [6].
- Said he chose the party label on the advice of people he trusts and pledged: "You have my word that I will not make decisions based on politics" [41].
- Not an incumbent; the District 1 seat is open because board president Tiffany Pascoe is not running [28, 26].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Supports the referendum; estimates it will cost the average homeowner about $20 to $30 a year; his site says the new levy replaces, not adds to, the 2023 referendum [41, 53].
- Said the district should keep more than the state-minimum cash reserve because the legislature is "unpredictable," but cannot rely on reserves for recurring costs [41].
- Calls for a public-facing budget any resident can read and staffing decisions tied to a multi-year enrollment plan [54].

**Teachers & staff**

- If the referendum fails, cuts should protect classroom instruction and teachers first [41].
- Proposes a teacher-retention review using anonymous exit-interview data and says cuts in any future reduction in force should start as far from the classroom as possible [54].

**Academics**

- Would protect AP, IB, dual-credit and career-technical pathways and publish post-graduation outcomes by school each year [54].

**Special education**

- Calls for a staffed plan for special education services through the SEA 1 transition [54].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Wants HSE to "tell our story" better to attract students as enrollment shifts, and to be a destination for teachers [41].

**Transparency & community input**

- Proposes neighborhood listening sessions, plain-language summaries of each board meeting, and a published timeline for public comment before major decisions [54].
- Sees the board's role as oversight: "We only have one employee, and that's the superintendent" [41].

### On the current officeholder

- Has expressed confidence in Superintendent Matt Kegley, calling his appointment "the icing on the cake" [41, 53].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Said HSEA told him it did not endorse him only because he runs under a party label, and that there is "no bad blood" [41]. His campaign site's endorsements page said endorsements were "coming soon" [55].

### What we couldn't verify

- No Hamilton County campaign finance reports were reviewed; fundraising is unknown.
- No candidate forum or written questionnaire (e.g., VOTE411, Current in Fishers) was reviewed; Current in Fishers pages returned 403.

## Cyrus Keck — District HSE 2

HSE parent and regular board-meeting attendee, running with no party label; endorsed by the HSE teachers association [29, 46].

### Background

- Parent of two sons in HSE schools (Harrison Parkway Elementary) [29, 42].
- Has been a regular attendee of school board meetings [29].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed with no party; said he did so because "our school board should not be about politics" [29]. Listed on the state list as Nonpartisan [6].
- Not an incumbent; the District 2 seat is open because Juanita Albright is not running (she is the Republican nominee for state Senate District 31) [28].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Strongly supports the operating referendum, urging listeners to vote for it even if they choose another board candidate; acknowledged he would need deeper understanding of district expenses before specific spending decisions [42].
- Running on financial accountability and administrative oversight [29].

**Teachers & staff**

- Says compensation, benefits and a culture that values educators are key to retention; protecting teaching positions would be his first priority in any further cuts [42].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Not fundamentally opposed to charter schools, but before supporting one would want a clearly identified problem, evidence a charter is a better solution and an understanding of the financial impact on other students [42].
- LarryInFishers reported he cited concerns over the district's charter-school rollout [29].

**Transparency & community input**

- Would evaluate the superintendent on budget performance, student outcomes and staff satisfaction, with clear annual goals and regular progress reviews [42].
- Said: "I think we need to get back to the school board just being kind of boring" [42].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by HSEA; said he made no promises of specific pay or benefit increases and would use his own judgment if he disagreed with the association [46, 42].

## Josh Perry — District HSE 2

HSE graduate, HSE parent and insurance underwriting manager running with no party label; endorsed by Fishers Mayor Scott Fadness [56, 47].

Campaign website: https://www.perryforhse.com

### Background

- Commercial underwriting manager at Indiana Farm Bureau Insurance, leading a team of 15 [56].
- 2007 Hamilton Southeastern High School graduate; Purdue agricultural education degree (2011); formerly an instructional assistant in the emotional and behavioral disabilities program at Carmel Clay Schools [47].
- Parent of two Harrison Parkway Elementary students; lives in West Fishers [56].
- Member of the 2026-27 HSEngaged community cohort [47].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed June 1, 2026, as a nonpartisan candidate [56]. Listed on the state list as Nonpartisan [6].
- Not an incumbent; the District 2 seat is open because Juanita Albright is not running [28].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Says that because of SEA 1 the referendum is necessary for financial stability "with as little impact as possible to our property taxes" [57].
- If the referendum fails, would prioritize a competitive teacher compensation package and examine long-term enrollment projections before changing staffing, programs or building use [43].

**Teachers & staff**

- Says retention depends on pay, benefits and workplace culture, and that less board infighting would help [43].

**Academics**

- Supports the HSE Network Model and expanding trades and direct-to-workforce pathways; "A welding certification and an AP class deserve equal respect" [57].
- Favors teaching students to use AI as a tool and to recognize when its answers are wrong [43].

**School safety**

- Calls school safety "a fundamental obligation of this board" [57].

**Transparency & community input**

- Says members should be able to vote differently and explain themselves respectfully, then help carry out decisions [43].
- Says the board should set vision and policy and trust administrators and teachers to execute [57].

### On the current officeholder

- Calls political division within the schools "a serious problem that often distracts from the work that matters most" [57].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says voters should judge him after a year on following through on commitments, collaboration and progress toward a strategic plan [43].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by Fishers Mayor Scott Fadness [47]. His site also lists endorsements from Delaware Township Trustee Debbie Driskell and two former HSE educators [57]. Asked about the mayor's endorsement, he said his responsibility would be to do what is best for the schools [43].
