# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Beau Baird (Republican)

Beau Baird, a lifelong Putnam County resident, home health agency chief financial officer and horse breeder, has represented House District 44 since he was first elected in 2018 [3]. He chairs the House Environmental Affairs Committee and also serves on the Agriculture and Rural Development and Rules and Legislative Procedures committees [3].

- Committees: Environmental Affairs (chair since January 2025); Agriculture and Rural Development; Rules and Legislative Procedures; State Fair Advisory Committee [3]. 2026 interim: chairs the Interim Study Committee on Environmental Affairs [3]. Co-chairs the bipartisan Indiana Future Caucus, per the caucus [3].
- 2023 session: Authored HEA 1601, letting certified forestry operators who follow DNR best management practices skip certain DNR and IDEM permits in small watersheds; signed in May 2023 [4].
- 2025 session: Authored HEA 1555, creating a limited-scope medical license for qualified international medical graduates to practice in rural, underserved areas [5], and HB 1660, allowing excused school absences for FFA and 4-H events; both passed the General Assembly in April 2025, per the caucus [6].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [7]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [8].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes when HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, passed the House 57-41 [9].
- 2026 session: Co-authored HEA 1165, which raises pet theft to a felony and strengthens animal cruelty and neglect provisions; ceremonially signed April 29, 2026 [10].

## This race

- House District 44 includes all of Putnam County and part of Montgomery County [3, 11]. (Owen County is not part of the district.)
- Baird won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Clint Cooper, 3,471 votes (61.5%) to 2,171 (38.5%) [12]. Ahead of the primary, both President Trump and Gov. Mike Braun endorsed Baird [13].
- Kauffman was the only Democrat listed in the primary [14]; both are on the November ballot [2]. Baird was re-elected without opposition in 2024 [14].
- Key differences: Baird voted for the 2025 property-tax law, the 2025 budget and the 2025 redistricting bill [7, 8, 9]; Kauffman criticizes the property-tax changes as favoring corporations, opposes the mid-decade redistricting effort, and opposes requiring sheriffs to assist federal immigration enforcement without funding [15, 16].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Beau Baird

Beau Baird is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2018, and chairs the House Environmental Affairs Committee [3]. His recent bills have dealt with rural health care, agriculture education and forestry permitting [5, 6, 4].

### Background

- Chief financial officer of Indiana Home Care Plus, a home health agency; owns Baird Ranch, a quarter horse breeding operation near Greencastle [3].
- Greencastle High School and Purdue University graduate; serves on the American Quarter Horse Association international board and chairs the Putnam County Republican Party [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Clint Cooper with 61.5% [12].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Voted for SEA 1 (2025) [7]; said it "takes meaningful steps to provide relief to homeowners, farmers, veterans and fixed-income seniors" [17].

**Health care**

- On HEA 1555: "Access to quality health care remains a critical issue for Hoosiers, especially in rural communities" [5].

**Agriculture**

- On HB 1660: "4-H and FFA equip students with essential skills that prepare them for success beyond the classroom" [6].
- Said HEA 1601 "will cut red tape for Hoosier loggers" [4].

**Public safety**

- On HEA 1165: "This gives law enforcement the tools they need to hold animal abusers accountable" [10].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted for HB 1032, the 2025 congressional redistricting bill [9]. No public statement from Baird on the vote was found.

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Baird about Kauffman were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- After the 2026 session said lawmakers focused on "practical solutions that lower costs for Hoosier families" [18].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by President Trump and Gov. Braun in the 2026 primary [13]. No fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Unverified (Wikipedia list only): endorsements from Indiana Farm Bureau AgELECT PAC, NFIB Indiana PAC and the NRA Political Victory Fund.
- Whether HB 1660 was signed into law was not separately confirmed.
- No statements on data centers or energy policy found despite his environmental committee chair role.

## Kelsey Kauffman

Kelsey Kauffman is a Greencastle educator who founded a college program at the Indiana Women's Prison and has taught at DePauw University [16, 19]. Her campaign focuses on public schools, rural health care, property taxes, child care and immigration enforcement [15].

Campaign website: https://www.kauffmanindiana.com/

### Background

- Holds a Harvard doctorate in education; has taught at DePauw University and, through Indiana State University, in three Indiana prisons; founded and directed the Higher Education Program at the Indiana Women's Prison from 2012 to 2017 [19].
- Directed legislative education programs at the Statehouse for DePauw students and for formerly incarcerated people [19].
- Founded the Greencastle Summer Enrichment Program, directed Putnam County CASA in the 1990s and served as president of the Greencastle NAACP [19]. Has lived in Greencastle since 1987 [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced in January 2026 [16]; the only Democrat listed in the May 5, 2026 primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Says that when "families with modest homes pay more while corporations get $2 million breaks, that is not relief" [15].

**Education**

- Says she will protect local schools from "Statehouse budget games that put corporate tax breaks ahead of our children" [15].

**Health care**

- Wants to keep rural hospitals open and expand mental health services; says "No family should have to drive an hour to see a doctor" [15].
- Calls health care a basic right but says the system cannot move to Medicare for All overnight and proposes incremental steps on costs [20].

**Child care**

- Says Putnam County has fewer than 10 licensed child care options, three of which accept infants [15].

**Immigration**

- Says enforcement is a federal responsibility and opposes requiring county sheriffs to act as federal agents without funding or training [15].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Opposes the 2025 mid-decade redistricting effort, according to the Banner-Graphic [16].

### On the current officeholder

- The Banner-Graphic reported that she expressed respect for Baird's service and civility and said, "This campaign is not about demonizing individuals" [16].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said the General Assembly "may be the most important line of defense available to protect Hoosiers" from harmful federal policies; joined the Indiana Rural Summit Coalition of candidates supporting public education, affordable health care and living wages [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on her website [11]. No fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Little news coverage beyond her January 2026 announcement was found.
- No statements on energy, data centers or agriculture policy found.
