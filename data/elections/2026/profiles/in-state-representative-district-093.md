# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Julie McGuire (Republican)

Julie McGuire has represented House District 93 on Indianapolis' south side since November 2022 and is vice chair of the House Public Health Committee [3, 4]. Much of her authored legislation has dealt with health care costs and transparency [5, 6].

- Committees: Public Health (vice chair), Education, and Insurance; appointed in 2025 to the All Payer Claims Database Advisory Board, according to her caucus biography [3]. Took office in November 2022 after defeating incumbent Rep. John Jacob in the Republican primary [7, 8].
- 2025: Authored HEA 1604, which requires insurers to count cash payments for health services toward a patient's deductible and out-of-pocket maximum, and HEA 1666, which requires health care providers to disclose ownership details to state agencies; sponsored SEA 140 on pharmacy benefit manager networks [6]. Co-authored HEA 1004, which sets a statewide hospital price benchmark that large nonprofit hospital systems must meet by 2029 or risk losing nonprofit status [9].
- 2026: Authored HEA 1271, which requires hospitals to tell patients about financial assistance before sending bills to collections and limits insurers' use of automated systems to downcode claims without human review [5]. Authored HEA 1257, which requires the Department of Child Services to publicly disclose key information in child fatality and near-fatality cases; it passed both chambers unanimously [10]. Her HB 1137, removing certain chemical additives from school meals and requiring online ingredient lists, passed the House in January 2026 [11].
- Voted yes on the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [12], and on HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [13]. Was recorded as excused on the House's final vote on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [14].
- 2024: Mike Braun, then the Republican nominee for governor, named her as his preferred running mate; delegates at the state Republican convention instead nominated Micah Beckwith for lieutenant governor [7, 4].

## This race

- House District 93 covers part of Marion County on Indianapolis' south side, including Beech Grove, according to the House Republican caucus [3, 15]. Both candidates describe ties to Perry Township [3, 16].
- McGuire was reelected in 2024 with 62.2% of the vote [17]. In that race she defeated Democrat Ryan Hughey [4].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: McGuire and Rosberg were each the only declared candidate in their party's primary [17].
- Key differences: McGuire's campaign site lists opposition to abortion, support for gun rights and police, and school choice as priorities [18]; Rosberg lists affordability, infrastructure funding, wage and job growth, health care, family medical leave and public school funding [16]. Rosberg says she opposes redistricting outside census years [16]; McGuire voted for the December 2025 House redistricting bill [13].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Julie McGuire

Julie McGuire, the incumbent, is seeking a third term [3, 17]. Her legislative work has centered on health care costs, medical debt and child-welfare oversight [5, 10].

Campaign website: https://mcguireforstaterep.com/

### Background

- Born and raised in Indianapolis; bachelor's degree in political science from Indiana University [3].
- Community roles listed by her caucus include secretary of the Perry Township GOP Club, Roncalli High School board member, and former president of the Northwest Perry Township Neighborhood Association [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [17].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Said of HEA 1271: "High healthcare costs and medical debt continue to burden Hoosiers" and that the law is meant to "improve transparency, combat unfair practices and put patients first" [5].
- Said of HEA 1004 that nonprofit hospitals that are not reinvesting and pricing accordingly "will no longer be exempt from paying taxes" [9].

**Child welfare**

- Said DCS confidentiality rules leave "the public and lawmakers in the dark" after child deaths and that "We need major reforms" [10].

**Education**

- Her campaign site says she supports parents' right to choose their children's school [18].
- Said improving school meal quality is "one of the most effective and cost-efficient ways we can protect their long-term health" [11].

**Public safety**

- Her campaign site says she "Stands with police and their families" and lists support for the Second Amendment [18].

**Abortion**

- Lists "Pro-Life" on her campaign site [18]; in May 2025 she joined a letter from state legislators urging Congress to defund abortion providers, according to her caucus [3].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by McGuire about her opponent were found; her campaign site does not mention Rosberg [18].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Her caucus biography lists her priorities as parental rights, protecting the unborn, educational choice, the Second Amendment and law enforcement [3].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No 2026 endorsements or fundraising figures were verified; her campaign site lists none [18].

### What we couldn't verify

- Did not answer Mirror Indy's 2026 voter-guide questions by its Sept. 11 deadline (per Mirror Indy's profile page).
- No statement found explaining her votes on HB 1032 (redistricting) or the 2025 budget.
- Campaign finance reports were not reviewed.

## Eva M. Rosberg

Eva Rosberg, a Democrat, works as an executive assistant in the Marion County Assessor's office [19]. She says she is running "to restore balance and to bring policy that supports working people" [16].

### Background

- Executive assistant in the Marion County Assessor's office since 2020; earlier owned a hair salon (2017-2020) and worked as a hair stylist (2008-2017), according to her BallotReady profile [19].
- Describes herself as a fourth-generation Hoosier and lifelong south-side resident; lives in Perry Township [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [17].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Lists her top priorities as "Finding ways to make life more affordable, funding infrastructure, and wage and job growth" [16].

**Health care**

- Names health care and family medical leave among the policy areas she is most interested in [16].

**Education**

- Names public school funding as a priority area [16].

**Housing**

- Names affordable housing as a priority area [16].

**Child care & foster youth**

- Sees bipartisan opportunities in "Affordable quality childcare funding" and funding services for 18- to 25-year-olds who have aged out of foster care [16].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said: "I am against redistricting outside of census years" [16].

### On the current officeholder

- Has not been found criticizing McGuire by name. Says "many folks feel their voices are not being heard" and that the General Assembly "should represent all Hoosiers" [16].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she would be "a strong voice for our community" with a focus on affordability, infrastructure and wages [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Very little coverage: no news articles, campaign website or detailed issue statements were found beyond Mirror Indy's Q&A and a BallotReady work history.
- Work history comes from BallotReady, apparently drawn from her LinkedIn; not independently confirmed.
- Campaign finance reports were not reviewed.
