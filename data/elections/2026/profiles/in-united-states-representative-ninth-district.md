# U.S. Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the U.S. House of Representatives write and vote on federal laws, including the federal budget and taxes. Each represents one congressional district for a two-year term; Indiana has nine [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None
- **Voting members:** 435 [1]
- **Indiana seats:** 9 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces bills and resolutions, offers amendments, and votes on legislation; federal laws must pass both the House and the Senate [1].
- Serves on committees, which draft legislation in their subject areas and oversee federal agencies and programs [1].
- Must be at least 25, a U.S. citizen for at least seven years, and live in the state they represent [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Erin Houchin (Republican)

Erin Houchin, a Republican from Salem, represents Indiana's 9th District and is seeking a third term [3]. She serves on the House Rules, Budget, and Energy and Commerce committees and previously served eight years in the Indiana Senate [4].

- May 22, 2025: Voted yes on House passage of H.R. 1, the reconciliation bill later titled the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which passed 215–214 [5].
- July 3, 2025: Voted yes on the motion to concur in the Senate's version of H.R. 1, which passed 218–214 [6]. In a statement, she said the bill makes the 2017 tax cuts permanent, ends taxes on tips and overtime, and funds border security and energy production [7]. Democratic opponent Brad Meyer's campaign says the law cuts nearly $500 billion from Medicare over ten years and imposes Medicaid work requirements [8].
- September 19, 2025: Voted yes on H.R. 5371, a stopgap funding bill (continuing resolution), which passed the House [9]. After the Senate did not pass it and the government shut down on October 1, 2025, she blamed Democrats in a statement, saying they demanded $1.5 trillion in spending, including an extension of enhanced Affordable Care Act subsidies [10].
- November 12, 2025: Voted yes on the Senate-amended H.R. 5371, which ended the 42-day shutdown, funded the government through January 30, and included full-year Agriculture, Military Construction–VA, and Legislative Branch appropriations, according to her statement [11, 12].
- January 8, 2026: Voted no on H.R. 1834, which passed 230–196 with 17 Republicans in favor [13]. News reports described the measure, brought to the floor by a discharge petition, as a three-year extension of expired Affordable Care Act premium subsidies, which the Congressional Budget Office estimated would add about $80.6 billion to deficits over 10 years [14].
- February 3, 2026: Voted yes on final House agreement to the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (H.R. 7148), which passed 217–214 [15].
- March 2026: Two of her bills, the AWARE Act (AI chatbot education resources) and the SAFEBOTs Act (disclosure and safety requirements for AI chatbots used by minors), were included in the Kids Internet and Digital Safety (KIDS) Act, which the Energy and Commerce Committee approved on March 5, 2026 [16].
- May 11, 2026: The Rural Broadband Protection Act (S. 98), which requires the FCC to set up a vetting process for companies seeking high-cost Universal Service Fund broadband money, was signed into law [17]. Houchin sponsored the House companion bill, according to her office [18].
- May–June 2026: Announced House committee or floor approval of Community Project Funding requests she submitted, including $2 million for Ohio River shoreline protection in Clarksville and more than $3.1 million for water and wastewater projects in Switzerland County, Austin, Borden, and Greenville; the funding was not final as of those announcements [19, 20].
- September 2026: Introduced, with Rep. Jake Auchincloss (D-Mass.), the 16 & Up Social Media Act to set a national minimum age of 16 for social media platforms that use certain engagement features [21]. She co-chairs the House Kids Online Safety Caucus [16].
- September 16, 2026: The House passed her Science of Reading Act of 2026 (H.R. 7890), which would direct federal literacy grant funding toward instruction based on phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension research [22]. The House also passed her National Fossil Act (H.R. 9191), co-led with Rep. Debbie Dingell (D-Mich.), naming the American mastodon and T. rex national fossils [23].
- September 2026: The Energy and Commerce Committee advanced two of her bipartisan bills: the Strategic Task Force on Scam Prevention Act (H.R. 5967), 36–0, and the Data BRIDGE Act (H.R. 4950), 35–0, which would use USDA cropland data in broadband funding decisions [24].
- Public access controversy, 2025: In March 2025, her office paused regularly scheduled mobile office hours in Bedford, and about 30 protesters held an event there with an empty chair; her office did not respond to WFIU/WTIU's questions [25]. In April 2025, Indiana Democrats held a 'people's town hall' in Bloomington and criticized her for not making herself available [26]. Asked about this in October 2026, she said she made 36 stops around the district in August and regularly holds press conferences open to all media [27].

## This race

- The 9th District covers 18 counties in southeastern and south-central Indiana, including Bloomington, Franklin, Paoli, Salem, part of Greenwood, and Ohio River communities across from Louisville such as New Albany [3].
- The Cook Political Report rates the district R+15 [3]. In 2024, Houchin defeated Democrat Tim Peck with nearly 65 percent of the vote [28].
- Indiana lawmakers voted down a mid-cycle congressional redistricting push backed by President Trump less than a year before this election; Houchin supported mid-cycle redistricting [3]. The next regular redistricting is scheduled for 2031 [3].
- Brad Meyer won a four-candidate Democratic primary on May 5, 2026; Tim Peck finished second [28]. The other Democratic candidates were Jim Graham and Keil Roark, and an Indiana Daily Student primary guide listed no Republican primary contest for the seat [29].
- The Libertarian Party of Indiana nominated Tonya Hudson at its state convention in Fort Wayne in March 2026 [30]. Floyd Michael Taylor is running as an independent write-in candidate [3].
- Fundraising through June 30, 2026, per FEC data reported by WFYI: Houchin $1,856,572; Meyer $105,510; Hudson $1,719 [3].
- No debate among the candidates was found in news coverage reviewed. WFIU/WTIU published separate interviews with all four candidates on October 7, 2026 [27]. Meyer's campaign lists a 'Meet the Candidates' event at Bell Trace in Bloomington on October 12, 2026 [31].
- Key differences: on health care, Meyer supports Medicare for All, Hudson says health care is mainly a state issue and Medicare for All is off the table, Taylor says he has written a bill to repeal the ACA and cover everyone, and Houchin points to rural health funding and limits on pharmacy benefit managers [32, 33, 34, 27]. On tariffs, Houchin says manufacturers tell her the administration's tariff policy is working, while Meyer and Hudson say tariffs set without Congress are unconstitutional or illegal [27, 32, 33].

## Erin Houchin

Houchin is the Republican incumbent seeking a third term and lists her priorities as border security, lowering costs, and protecting children online [3].

Campaign website: https://erinhouchin.com

### Background

- Raised in southern Indiana and lives in Washington County; holds a B.A. from Indiana University and an M.A. in political management from George Washington University [4].
- Served eight years in the Indiana Senate (District 47), owned a communications firm, chaired the 9th District Republican Party, and worked as a regional director for U.S. Sen. Dan Coats [4].
- Age 49; lives in Salem [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Republican nominee and incumbent; an Indiana Daily Student primary guide listed no contested Republican primary for the seat in May 2026 [29].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & taxes**

- Says the Working Families Tax Cuts Act (the 2025 reconciliation law) made tax cuts permanent, including no tax on tips, overtime, or Social Security, and expanded small business expensing (September 2026) [3].
- Says tariffs are helping reshore manufacturing, citing reports from district manufacturers and a Jackson County bicycle maker (October 2026) [27].
- Says she has not disagreed with the White House on any particular issue over the past two years (October 2026) [27].

**Budget & deficit**

- Says she belongs to a bipartisan fiscal forum that proposed limiting the deficit to 3 percent of GDP, and names Medicare, Social Security, and the Highway Trust Fund as the biggest drivers of the deficit (October 2026) [27].

**Health care**

- Points to rural health funding in the 2025 reconciliation law, saying $207 million has come to Indiana, and supports removing pharmacy benefit managers as 'middlemen' in drug pricing (October 2026) [27].
- Says she wrote bipartisan legislation to lower drug prices (September 2026) [3].

**Housing**

- Says increased immigration strained housing supply, and cites House passage of a housing supply bill and the Affordable HOMES Act on manufactured-housing rules; says Congress should 'largely get out of the way of the market' (October 2026) [27].

**Energy & data centers**

- Says 'Data centers should be responsible for their own electric and utilities costs, not the local communities,' and supports coal, natural gas, and nuclear power (September 2026) [3].
- Says whether a data center comes to a community should be up to that community, and supports permitting reform (October 2026) [27].

**Children & technology**

- Wants to raise the minimum age for social media accounts from 13 to at least 16, require age verification, and require revocable parental consent (October 2026) [27].

**Agriculture & rural**

- Says the House passed a farm bill and a year-round E15 ethanol bill that she pushed for, both awaiting Senate action (October 2026) [27].
- Cites the Rural Broadband Protection Act as her work on rural internet access (September 2026) [3].

**Immigration & security**

- Cites border wall funding, support for law enforcement, and deporting criminal immigrants in the country illegally as accomplishments (September 2026) [3].

**Foreign policy**

- Says she does not consider the U.S. conflict with Iran a war, that the president needs flexibility, and that Iran cannot be allowed a nuclear weapon (October 2026) [27].

**Abortion**

- Says she is personally pro-life, is endorsed by Susan B. Anthony List, and believes abortion policy should be decided by the states (October 2026) [27].

**Guns**

- Supports the Second Amendment and has backed legislation to remove a tax on firearm suppressors (October 2026) [27].

**Government ethics**

- Says she voted for a ban on congressional stock trading (September 2026) [3].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder.
- No public statements by Houchin about her 2026 challengers were found. Responding to criticism about public access, she said in October 2026 that she made 36 stops in the district in August and that her district office is always available [27].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says her top goal for the next Congress is kids' online safety legislation, including a higher minimum age and age verification (October 2026) [27].
- Says she will keep seeking federal project funding, citing $14 million delivered and another $18 million in the pipeline (October 2026) [27].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Says she is endorsed by Susan B. Anthony List [27].
- Raised $1,856,572 as of June 30, 2026, per FEC data; OpenSecrets shows $864,000 from PACs, with NextEra Energy and Pivotal Ventures as the largest special-interest donors [3].

### What we couldn't verify

- Congress.gov and GovTrack could not be reached for a full sponsored-bill list; the record above relies on her office's press releases, House Clerk roll calls, and news reports.
- No coverage of her response to Meyer's or Hudson's criticisms was found.
- A 2024 report on her declining debate invitations was seen only in search results and is not used here.

## Brad A. Meyer

Meyer is a Bloomington engineer who formerly worked as a Navy civilian engineer at Crane, running on Medicare for All, a $20 minimum wage, and making the first $20,000 of income tax-free [3, 28].

Campaign website: https://bradmeyer.org

### Background

- Born in Brownsburg; earned bachelor's and master's degrees from Purdue University in Indianapolis; worked in local manufacturing and as a civilian engineer for Crane Naval Sea Systems Command [28].
- His campaign says he spent 25 years in manufacturing, including management roles, and nine years as a Navy civilian engineer in electromagnetic warfare [35].
- Age 59; lives in Bloomington; says he left his job at Crane to run [3]. He has never held elected office [32].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the four-candidate Democratic primary on May 5, 2026, finishing ahead of 2024 nominee Tim Peck [28].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Supports Medicare for All, saying 'Everybody should have health care. It should be a right' (October 2026) [32].
- Proposes adding dental, vision, and hearing coverage to Medicare and expanding Medicare drug price bargaining [36].

**Economy & taxes**

- Proposes a $20 federal minimum wage, making the first $20,000 of income tax-free, and raising the overtime salary threshold to $100,000 [3, 36].
- Says he would not raise taxes on low- and middle-income families and would raise rates only for households above $383,000 and large corporations [37].
- Supports the PRO Act, a ban on non-compete agreements, and taxing capital gains of top earners as regular income [36].
- Says tariffs are a tax paid by Americans, that Congress should set them, and that sudden changes harm the economy (October 2026) [32].

**Social Security**

- Supports raising the cap on income subject to Social Security payroll tax [36].

**Budget & deficit**

- Supports a constitutional amendment requiring a balanced budget, under which federal officeholders would be barred from their next election if a balanced budget is not passed by Oct. 1 (October 2026) [32].

**Energy & data centers**

- Says renewables now cost less than coal and the federal government should fund more generation and distribution capacity (October 2026) [32].
- Has called for a one-year data center moratorium so local governments can update rules, and says nondisclosure agreements between companies and local officials should be illegal (October 2026) [32].
- Says large users should pay the full cost of the infrastructure they require (September 2026) [3].

**Immigration**

- Says the number of work visas should match labor needs and enforcement should target illegal hiring by employers (October 2026) [32, 38].

**Agriculture & rural**

- Says high diesel and fertilizer costs and lost export markets from trade disputes are hurting farmers (October 2026) [32].
- Proposes shifting federal farm support from large corporate operations to independent family farms [37].

**Abortion**

- Supports restoring the Roe v. Wade framework (October 2026) [32].

**Guns**

- Supports universal background checks, red-flag laws, and barring gun purchases by people on terrorist watch lists [37].

**Education**

- Opposes eliminating the U.S. Department of Education [39].

**Foreign policy**

- Says Congress should decide when war is necessary and force should be a last resort [37, 39].

### On the current officeholder

- In October 2025, held a 'Ghosting Us' rally in Salem and said in a statement: 'Our Congresswoman has been ghosting the people of southern Indiana… too often voting for the special interests instead of the people's interests' [40].
- In October 2026, said 'This administration and Erin Houchin' have cut Medicaid, undercut the ACA so it costs 20 percent more, and done nothing about Social Security [32].
- His campaign criticizes the 2025 reconciliation law, which Houchin voted for, for Medicare cuts, Medicaid work requirements, and ending nursing home minimum staffing requirements [8].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says his first-year goal would be Medicare for All, along with raising the minimum wage, expanding overtime eligibility, and raising the standard deduction (October 2026) [32].
- Lists his top three legislative priorities as Medicare for All, wage and tax changes, and ending budget gridlock [37].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Raised $105,510 as of June 30, 2026, per FEC data; OpenSecrets shows only individual donors and $0 from PACs [3].

### What we couldn't verify

- No endorsements were found on pages reviewed.
- Primary vote shares (about 37% for Meyer, per a search snippet from the Bloomington Chamber of Commerce) could not be verified on a fetched page.
- WFYI lists his former occupation as 'Mechanical Engineer'; his campaign says he studied electronics engineering. Not resolved.
- A fundraising aggregator and Ballotpedia were seen only in search results and are not used here.

## Tonya L. Hudson

Hudson is a Mitchell real estate broker and the Libertarian nominee, running on abolishing the IRS, cutting regulations on small businesses and farmers, and voting against what she calls unconstitutional wars [3].

Campaign website: https://hudsonforhoosiers.com

### Background

- Age 64; real estate broker with Suddarth and Company; lives in Mitchell [3].
- Says she has been a real estate broker for more than 21 years and ran for lieutenant governor in 2024 on Donald Rainwater's Libertarian ticket [33].
- Lists past Libertarian runs for this House seat in 2020 and 2022, service as Libertarian Party of Indiana vice chair in 2023, and roles with the Mitchell Lions Club and Bedford Board of Realtors [41].

### Path to the ballot

- Nominated by the Libertarian Party of Indiana at its state convention in Fort Wayne in March 2026 [30].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & taxes**

- Has written a 'Hudson Resolution to Abolish the IRS' that would replace federal income taxes with a consumer sales tax (October 2026) [33].
- Says the federal government should not interfere with free-market principles and that lower fuel prices would help reduce grocery costs (September 2026) [3].
- Says Congress, not the president, should set tariffs, calls the president's tariffs illegal, and says Congress should vote on them (October 2026) [33].

**Budget & deficit**

- Calls for a balanced budget and specific cuts to wasteful spending, and calls the national debt unsustainable (October 2026) [33].
- Supports ending continuing resolutions and eliminating the U.S. Department of Education [41].

**Health care**

- Says health care should be mainly a state responsibility and that Medicare for All is off the table (October 2026) [33].
- Says Medicare and Medicaid should remain but no other taxpayer-funded health programs are needed [41].

**Energy & data centers**

- Says data centers are a state issue and state regulators 'should not allow for utility rate changes' (September 2026) [3].

**Immigration**

- Says border security is working but legal entry is 'bottlenecked,' and calls for more and better-coordinated ports of entry and improved screening (October 2026) [33].

**Agriculture & rural**

- Supports existing farm bills but says the federal government should not tell farmers what to grow (October 2026) [33].

**Foreign policy**

- Says she would vote to end the Iran conflict, which she calls unconstitutional because Congress did not authorize it, and favors minimal military involvement abroad (October 2026) [33].

**Abortion**

- Says the Supreme Court has settled the issue and she does not expect it to come before Congress (October 2026) [33].

**Guns**

- Opposes any federal gun laws: 'I don't think the federal government should say anything about it at all' (October 2026) [33].

### On the current officeholder

- Says the district's current representative and her predecessor 'never met with the public, never did town halls or public forums' and follow Republican leadership (October 2026) [33].
- Says 'Erin Houchin and Brad Meyer are two sides of the same coin' (October 2026) [33].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she would promote her resolution to abolish the IRS in her first year (October 2026) [33].
- Says she would hold periodic public town halls and answer constituent emails promptly (September 2026) [3].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Raised $1,719 as of June 30, 2026, per FEC data [3].

### What we couldn't verify

- No endorsements were found.
- Search results suggest she previously ran under the name Tonya Millis; her listed Facebook page uses that name, but no fetched source states it directly.
- Her campaign site is also reachable at tonyaforcongress.com, which iVoterGuide lists.

## Floyd Michael Taylor

Taylor is an independent write-in candidate who works in IT and says he would vote based on constituent input [34, 3].

### Background

- Works in information technology, has no prior political experience, and says he is a two-time cancer survivor [34].

### Path to the ballot

- Declared write-in candidate; says he filed with the Secretary of State after learning an independent ballot spot required about 4,500 signatures [34].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Says he has written bills to repeal the ACA while covering everyone and to fund mobile rural clinics (October 2026) [34].

**Budget & deficit**

- Says he has written a bill to prevent full government shutdowns (October 2026) [34].

**Energy**

- Supports letting communities and farms connect their own energy systems to the grid without utility pushback (October 2026) [34].

### On the current officeholder

- Says the district has 'representation of the party' but not 'of the people,' and that he received only form letters when contacting his representative (October 2026) [34].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says his first-year priority would be his health care bill (October 2026) [34].

### What we couldn't verify

- Write-in candidate: short entry only. No campaign website, FEC filings, or endorsements were found.
