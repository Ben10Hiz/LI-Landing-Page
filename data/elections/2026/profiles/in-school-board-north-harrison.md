# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- North Harrison Community School Corp. serves the northern part of Harrison County; its board meets in the North Harrison Middle School cafeteria in Ramsey [24, 25]. Superintendent Tim LaGrange, hired June 30, 2025 after six years leading Southwest Dubois County schools and two years at Kittitas School District in Washington state, replaced Nathan Freed, who left for South Harrison [26, 27]. LaGrange has described North Harrison as a '$25 million annual school organization' [24].
- Enrollment: LaGrange said in October 2025 that the corporation had 23 fewer students than the year before and 93 fewer than four years earlier, which he put at roughly $771,000 in lost state funding; the teachers' contract covers about 125 teachers [25]. A total enrollment figure was not found in fetched sources.
- The board has five members, elected by township seat or at large [27, 28, 29]: Josh Bowen (president), Angie Byrne (vice president, Morgan Township seat), Chris Bowyer (secretary), Kristina Gunter (at large) and Steve Nix (Blue River Township seat) [24, 29, 30]. Voters in every North Harrison precinct vote in each township race [31].
- Three seats are on the 2026 ballot, one member each: At Large, Jackson Township and Spencer Township [31, 32]. In 2022 Gunter won the at-large seat, Chris Lasley the Jackson Township seat and Joshua Bowen the Spencer Township seat [28]. Bowyer, appointed to the board in September 2024 to fill a vacancy left by a resignation, is running in the Jackson Township race [30, 31]. Bowen, who won the Spencer Township seat in 2022, is running in the at-large race and describes it as a run for re-election [28, 33]; Gunter is not on the 2026 ballot [31]. Josh Kendall is unopposed for the Spencer Township seat [31, 34].
- Ballot changes: Ray Von Allmen, listed as a Republican at-large candidate on the state list, was removed as a candidate on Sept. 1, 2026 after a challenge to his candidacy filed by Alfred Wood, according to the Corydon Democrat [35]. The county's general-election candidate list and sample ballot show only Bowen and Ashley Black in the at-large race [31, 32]. Ashley Black appears with no party; the state list shows her as nonpartisan [31, 6].
- Budget and state funding (Oct. 2025): the board introduced a 2026 budget with an $18 million education fund, a $7 million operations fund and a $3.02 million debt service fund; the Corydon Democrat reported that the 2025 state budget would cut an estimated $381,730 from North Harrison for 2026 and that the corporation would again have to pay for school resource officers, which Harrison County had covered in 2025 [25].
- Finances (Jan. 2026): on Jan. 8 the board unanimously hired Stifel to do a financial analysis and five- to 10-year projections, after the corporation paid about $780,000 in health insurance claims in late 2025; the board also voted, with only Steve Nix opposed, to allow moving up to 15% of the education fund to the operations fund [24].
- 2027 budget (Sept. 10, 2026 hearing): LaGrange presented a proposed 2027 education fund of $17,454,909 (down from $18 million), an operations fund of $7.6 million and debt service of $2,951,137; the advertised operations tax rate is 0.6944 and the debt service rate 0.4266 [36].
- Teacher pay and contract: more than 10 teachers, parents and students urged higher teacher pay at the Oct. 16, 2025 meeting, when the corporation's offer was a $120,000 total increase; board member Bowyer said the money was not there to pay teachers more [25]. Teachers' union leaders also objected in June 2025 to the new superintendent's contract terms [26]. The teachers' contract expired June 30, 2026, according to candidate Ashley Black, and a collective bargaining public hearing was held Sept. 10, 2026 [37, 36].
- Classroom display policy (July–Aug. 2026): the board unanimously approved a policy treating classroom displays as instructional materials and barring employees from posting materials that convey 'a political, religious, or personal message'; first reading passed July 24 and final reading Aug. 13 [37, 38]. Several teachers, residents and a student spoke against it, and the Corydon Democrat reported that no board member explained publicly why the policy was needed [37, 38].
- Student safety (Sept. 2026): at the Sept. 10 meeting, parents of middle school volleyball players criticized the district's response to an Aug. 26 crash involving the team bus on I-265; board members apologized, and LaGrange said, 'we can do better' [36]. The board also hired a special education consultant to audit services and staffing [36].
- Forums: no candidate forum was found. The Corydon Democrat (and its Clarion News edition) published questionnaire answers from the at-large and Jackson Township candidates in September 2026 [35, 39]. Key differences in the Jackson Township race: Bowyer is the appointed incumbent and says the current board is making progress; Schenck, a former superintendent, criticizes district management and proposes about $500,000 in savings; Inboden and Fanz, first-time candidates, emphasize finances and academics [30, 40, 41, 42].

## Ashley Black — At Large

Certified public accountant and 2004 North Harrison graduate making her second run for the board; says her priorities are a teachers' contract, finances and transparency [43]. Listed without a party [31].

### Background

- Licensed CPA with a bachelor's degree in accounting and a master's in strategic finance; says she has more than 20 years of experience in accounting and finance [43].
- 2004 North Harrison graduate [43].
- Says she began attending board meetings in late 2019 to advocate for more special education resources, particularly at the elementary schools [43].
- Ran for the Blue River Township seat in 2024, finishing fourth of five candidates [43, 29].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for the at-large seat; appears on the county list with no party and on the state list as nonpartisan [31, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Teachers & staff**

- Says her immediate priority is for teachers and the board to reach an agreed master contract, and that it would be her 'focal point' until resolved [43].
- In June 2025 public comment, urged the board to find ways to keep staff leaving for neighboring districts [27].

**Budget & taxes**

- Says SEA 1 reduces funding to public schools and that the board needs members who can review financial statements closely [43].
- Wants to find new revenue and cost savings that do not cut teacher and student resources, and to spend savings on things that add value to students' education [43].

**Transparency & community input**

- Says the board should explain every vote and major decision, make public documents easier to get, and post meeting notices widely with supporting documents attached [43].

**Curriculum & library policy**

- Opposed the 2026 classroom display policy, calling it too vague and saying more important issues faced the corporation, such as the expired teachers' contract and teacher retention [37, 38].

### On the current officeholder

- At the Aug. 13, 2026 meeting, said she disagreed with the classroom display policy and asked board members to explain their votes [38].
- Said in July 2026 that teacher retention has been a continuing issue she has raised for seven years [37].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she would bring 'strong financial oversight' to the board and keep asking where tax dollars go [43].

### What we couldn't verify

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports found.
- No candidate forum found; positions come from her newspaper questionnaire and public comments at board meetings.

## Joshua (Josh) Bowen — At Large

Incumbent board member and board president; elected to the Spencer Township seat in 2022 and now running at large [28, 33, 24]. Listed as a Republican [31].

### Background

- Worked at the Kroger distribution center in Louisville from 1992 to 2017, moving from order selector to a facility manager [33].
- Board president for two years after serving one year as secretary; named president again for 2026 [33, 24].
- Member of the Harrison County Parks Inclusive Playground Advisory Board since 2019 [33].
- Parent of a current North Harrison High School student [33].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for the at-large seat as a Republican [31, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Teachers & staff**

- Names staffing, pay, benefits, morale and retention as his top priority; says wages and benefits must stay competitive and the teachers' contract must be fair to educators and keep the corporation solvent [33].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Wants to maintain enrollment and grow the district's new virtual school program for families who prefer non-traditional options [33].

**Special education**

- Wants to expand special education services so all students can learn [33].

**Academics**

- Supports building career-ready pathways through North Harrison career and technical programs and the Prosser Career Education Center [33].

**Facilities**

- Wants to modernize the athletic department, facilities and processes, and says he would 'protect women's sports' [33].

**Budget & taxes**

- Says the schools 'must be run very efficiently' [33]. In January 2026 he said the late-2025 insurance costs came from keeping premiums low, which left the corporation effectively self-insured once coverage ran out [24].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member.
- Says he led the board through hiring the new superintendent; LaGrange was chosen from 12 applicants, seven of whom were interviewed [33, 26].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he will set clear expectations for the administration and 'Inspect what I expect' [33].

### What we couldn't verify

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports found.
- No news account found explaining why he moved from the Spencer Township seat to the at-large race.
- No candidate forum found.

## Chad Schenck — Jackson Township

Former West Clark superintendent who now works in sales for an education services company; parent of current students; running to change how the district is managed [40]. Listed as a Republican [31].

### Background

- Former teacher and administrator: junior high math and Orleans third-grade teacher, Crawford County middle school principal, assistant superintendent at Tell City-Troy Township and Southwest Dubois County, West Clark superintendent (2015–2018) and chief operations officer (2018–2021) [40].
- Lists his current job as senior account executive with Perfection Group since 2021 [40].
- Holds education degrees from Indiana University and an education specialist degree from Oakland City University [40].
- Parent of children who attend North Harrison schools [40].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Republican for the Jackson Township seat; first run for elected office [31, 40].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Says he would propose cost-saving measures within 30 days that would not cut any teaching position, and believes $500,000 can be saved in the education fund [40].
- Says the savings would pay for staff raises or stipends, offset enrollment declines and reduce transfers from the operations fund, freeing operations money for facility upgrades [40].

**Teachers & staff**

- Says the district is losing teachers and that staff should be valued and respected [40].

**Facilities**

- Wants freed-up operations money to go to facility upgrades [40].

### On the current officeholder

- Says board members and the superintendent have made serious errors in management and judgment and that he would hold leadership accountable [40].
- Says politics has no place in board governance and that he would not let it enter his decisions [40].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he has researched the tax rate, enrollment trends, staffing, facilities and inefficiencies and would bring proposals in his first 30 days [40].

### What we couldn't verify

- No endorsements or campaign finance reports found.
- He did not name specific cuts behind the $500,000 savings estimate in the questionnaire.
- No candidate forum found.

## Charles (Chris) Bowyer — Jackson Township

Incumbent board member; appointed in September 2024 to fill a vacancy and now board secretary [30, 24]. Listed as a Republican [31].

### Background

- Works for Consolidated Electrical Distributors; 1980 graduate of Jeffersonville High School and Prosser Vocational School [30].
- His three children are North Harrison graduates and three grandchildren attend the district [30].

### Path to the ballot

- Appointed in September 2024 from a group of volunteers to fill a vacancy left by a resignation; filed as a Republican for the Jackson Township seat in 2026 [30, 31].

### Positions & plans

**Academics**

- Wants instruction focused on math, science, social studies, English, special education and career/agriculture programs [30].

**Budget & taxes**

- Lists finance and budget among the issues needing attention [30]. In October 2025 he told teachers asking for raises that the money is not there to pay teachers more [25].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Lists declining enrollment and its effects as a priority [30].

**Special education**

- Acknowledges that not everyone who attends board meetings is convinced the district's special education programs are as good as they can be [30].

**Teachers & staff**

- Lists teacher and staff retention as a priority [30].

**Transparency & community input**

- Lists transparency with the public about school business as a priority [30]; said in January 2026 that 'the transparency that this board wants to parlay to you guys can't be overstated' [24].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member.
- Says the current board is productive and 'has made and will continue to make real progress' [30].
- Voted with the full board for the classroom display policy; when asked at the Aug. 13 meeting to explain the vote, he said, 'Appreciate everyone's opinion. We have our own.' [38]

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says priorities shift and need simultaneous attention; lists enrollment, special education, staff retention, budget, student safety and transparency, in no set order [30].

### What we couldn't verify

- Fetched sources do not state which seat he was appointed to; a 2024 district vacancy notice for a Jackson Township seat was seen only in a search snippet (unverified), and 2022 Jackson Township winner Chris Lasley is not on the 2026 ballot.
- No endorsements or campaign finance reports found.
- No candidate forum found.
