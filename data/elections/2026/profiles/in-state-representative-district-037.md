# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Todd Huston (Republican)

Todd Huston, a Fishers Republican first elected in 2012, has been Speaker of the Indiana House since March 2020, when he succeeded retiring Speaker Brian Bosma; House Republicans re-elected him as speaker in 2022 and 2024 [3, 4]. As speaker he leads a chamber with a 70-member Republican supermajority and serves on the Joint Rules Committee and the Legislative Council [3]. The House Republican caucus continued to identify him as speaker in September 2026 [5].

- Leadership: Speaker of the House since 2020; member of the Joint Rules Committee and Legislative Council [3, 4].
- 2025 session: The House Republican caucus lists the session's main laws under his speakership as the two-year budget (HEA 1001), K-12 deregulation (HEA 1002), health care pricing transparency (HEA 1003), nonprofit hospital rules (HEA 1004), housing infrastructure (HEA 1005), a board to review prosecutors who decline to enforce laws (HEA 1006) and energy policy (HEA 1007) [6]. Huston authored HEA 1008, allowing Illinois counties that voted to secede to join Indiana [6].
- April 10, 2025: The House passed an amended SEA 1, the property-tax law, 65-29, with Huston voting yes [7]; he said "Two-thirds of Hoosier homeowners will see a lower bill in 2026 than they did in 2025" [8]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes as the House adopted the budget conference report (HEA 1001), 66-27 [9].
- Nov. 18, 2025: Said House Republicans had the votes for mid-decade congressional redistricting and hoped the Senate would join in passing a map giving Republicans all nine seats [10]. Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes as the House passed HB 1032, 57-41 [11]; the Senate rejected it 31-19 on Dec. 11 [12].
- 2026 session: The caucus lists the House Republican agenda bills as HEA 1001 (housing regulation), HEA 1002 (utility performance targets), HEA 1003 (consolidating state boards) and HEA 1004 (K-12 deregulation); Huston said the session "was focused on affordability for Hoosier families and streamlining government" [13].
- April 2026: Issued a statement on HEA 1303 (child alert system) and HEA 1408 (social media safeguards for minors), laws inspired by the death of Fishers teen Hailey Buzbee [14].
- 2022: Left his job as a College Board senior vice president after the Indiana Democratic Party called him "a walking conflict of interest" as the House advanced education bills; Huston said he was "focused on a strong, successful finish to this legislative session" [15].

## This race

- House District 37 covers part of Hamilton County, including Fishers [3].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Huston was unopposed for the Republican nomination; Lauren Cole won the Democratic nomination with 2,167 votes (55.2%) to 1,757 (44.8%) for Joel Levi [16].
- Key differences: Huston has led the House Republican agenda on property-tax changes, the 2025 budget and the 2025 redistricting effort [6, 10]; Cole says she is focused on public school funding, working families and government transparency [17].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Todd Huston

Todd Huston is the Republican incumbent and Speaker of the Indiana House, first elected in 2012 [3]. As speaker he has led House action on the 2025 property-tax law and budget and the 2025 redistricting bill [6, 10].

### Background

- Bachelor's degree in political science from Indiana University; former Hamilton Southeastern school board member and Fishers Plan Commission member; appointed by Gov. Mitch Daniels to the State Board of Education and the Indiana Education Roundtable; lives in Fishers [3].
- Was a senior vice president at the College Board until 2022 [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [16].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Said SEA 1 delivered "robust property tax relief for Hoosiers" [8]; said that between property tax relief and income tax cuts, Hoosiers would see about $600 million in tax cuts in 2026 and $700 million in 2027 [6].

**Housing**

- Said the 2026 laws "expand Indiana's housing supply and drive down costs of homeownership" [13]; said "The best way to reduce cost burdens on Hoosiers is to roll back regulation and bureaucracy" [18].

**Energy & utilities**

- Listed HEA 1002 (2026), which sets performance targets for utilities on cost and reliability, as a caucus priority [18].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Supported mid-decade congressional redistricting in 2025, saying he hoped Congress would bar the practice but that Indiana should use every available legal tool in the meantime [10].

**Other**

- Fox59 reported that Huston had repeatedly expressed disinterest in a 2025 bill to legalize recreational marijuana [19].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Huston about Cole were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Announced a 2026 House Republican agenda aimed at keeping Indiana "a low cost of living state" through housing, utility, government-consolidation and education bills [18].

## Lauren S. Cole

Lauren Cole, a first-time candidate who works in health care, is the Democratic nominee [17]. She focuses on public education, working families and transparency in state government [17].

### Background

- The Reporter describes her as a senior coordinator in medical affairs at LabCorp Biopharma [17]; BallotReady lists her as a molecular technologist supervisor at Patients Choice Laboratories since 2022 [20].
- First-time candidate [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced in February 2026 [17]; won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 55.2% over Joel Levi [16].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Has criticized underfunding of public schools and says every child should have access to a high-quality education [17].

**Economy & jobs**

- Says she is running "to represent the people who work hard every day and just want a fair shot" [17].

**Government transparency**

- Says "government should be a window, not a black box" and that people deserve to know "where their tax dollars go" [17].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about Huston were found. She has said, "I'm not running to represent the powerful" [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says "This campaign is about bringing the power back to the people where it belongs" [17].
