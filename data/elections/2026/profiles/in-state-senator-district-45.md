# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Chris Garten (Republican)

Chris Garten, a Republican from Charlestown, has represented Senate District 45 since 2018 [3]. He was Senate Majority Floor Leader from August 2022 until he resigned the post in June 2026, and in August 2026 the Senate Republican caucus endorsed him to become the next Senate President Pro Tempore, a choice the full Senate is scheduled to vote on November 17 [4, 5].

- 2023: Authored Senate Enrolled Act 268, which requires Indiana pension funds to divest from entities controlled by the Chinese Communist Party; the caucus said the state pension plan had divested $1.2 billion within a year [6].
- April 2025: As majority floor leader, backed SEA 1 (2025), the property-tax law the caucus said provides about $1.3 billion in homeowner relief over three years and caps local income tax rates; Garten said "State government receives zero property tax dollars" and argued local governments should answer for tax increases [7].
- 2025: Authored SEA 2, which adds Medicaid eligibility checks and a 20-hour weekly work or volunteer requirement for able-bodied Healthy Indiana Plan adults, pending federal approval; it passed the Senate 37–10 [8, 9]. Democratic Sens. Shelli Yoder and Fady Qaddoura said the changes could add bureaucracy and cost eligible people coverage; Garten said Medicaid is meant for the poor and disabled [9].
- 2025: Senate sponsor of House Enrolled Act 1004, which requires nonprofit hospital systems with at least $2 billion in annual patient revenue to bring prices to the state average by June 30, 2029, or begin paying taxes [10].
- December 11, 2025: Spoke for and voted yes on HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill, which the Senate defeated 31–19; on the floor he said, "Some will say these maps are political. Let me be clear, you're damn right they are" [11, 12].
- 2026 session: Listed by the ACLU of Indiana as an author of SEA 256 (foreign-adversary land ownership and agent registration), which the ACLU opposed as discriminating based on national origin [13].
- June 4, 2026: Resigned as majority floor leader, writing that the role requires close alignment with Senate leadership's direction; IndyPolitics linked the move to fallout from the redistricting fight [4].
- August 18, 2026: Endorsed unanimously by Senate Republicans for President Pro Tempore; he has said he will appoint Sen. Eric Koch as majority floor leader [5, 14].

## This race

- Senate District 45 covers Clark County and part of Floyd County in southern Indiana [3].
- Garten was unopposed in the Republican primary; Nick Marshall won the Democratic nomination in the May 5, 2026 primary, and Larry Mahaney was nominated at the Libertarian Party convention [14, 15].
- Marshall also challenged Garten for this seat in the previous cycle, entering the race in October 2021 after redistricting placed Jeffersonville in District 45 [16].
- The race is drawing attention because Garten is his caucus's choice to lead the Senate starting in 2027 [14].
- No debates or forums for this race were found in published coverage as of October 10, 2026 [15].

## Chris Garten

Chris Garten, the Republican incumbent, is a Marine Corps veteran and countertop company founder who is in line to become Senate President Pro Tempore [3, 5].

Campaign website: https://www.gartenforsenate.com/

### Background

- Founder and president of Signature Countertops, Inc., in Jeffersonville [3].
- Gunnery sergeant in the U.S. Marine Corps who served two tours in Iraq (2003 and 2005–2006) [3].
- Serves on the One Southern Indiana Advocacy Council and previously served on the Scott County Public Defender Board, according to his caucus biography [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- His campaign site says he has pushed hospitals toward price transparency and an end to surprise billing, and cites his law requiring able-bodied adults to work, train, or volunteer to receive Medicaid [17].

**Property taxes**

- Supported the 2025 property-tax law and argued local governments that raise taxes should have to justify it to taxpayers [7].

**Housing**

- His campaign site says he wants to lower home costs by cutting regulations, increasing supply, and providing property tax relief [17].

**Economy & jobs**

- His campaign site says he opposes tolls on local bridges and voted against a statewide highway toll plan backed by his party [17].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Supported mid-decade congressional redistricting, writing in October 2025 that "This is not a fight Republicans picked, but it's at our doorstep" [18].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Garten about Marshall or Mahaney were found [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- As incoming caucus leader, he has said he wants to expand the Republican majority in the Senate [5, 19].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Lists honors from groups including the Indiana Association of Chiefs of Police (2025) and Parents for School Options Indiana (2025) in his caucus biography [3].
- No 2026 campaign finance totals were found in fetched sources [17].

## Nick Marshall

Nick Marshall, the Democratic nominee, is a foster-care advocate from Jeffersonville who also ran for the seat in the previous cycle [16, 21].

Campaign website: https://www.hoosiersfornickmarshall.com/

### Background

- Grew up in foster care and graduated from the University of Louisville, according to his campaign [21].
- Works with Kentuckiana organizations on foster care advocacy and child literacy, according to his campaign [21]. In 2021 he served on boards including the Coalition Supporting Young Adults and Home of the Innocents [16].
- Lives in Jeffersonville [16].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the Democratic nomination in the May 5, 2026 primary [15].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- His platform calls for fully funding K-12 schools, raising teacher pay, expanding funding for special-education services such as IEPs and speech pathologists, and investing in early childhood education [22].

**Property taxes**

- His platform says the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) drained local police and fire budgets and calls for returning that money to local governments without raising taxes [22].

**Health care**

- Opposes cuts to Medicare and Medicaid and would supplement those costs at the state level; supports funding addiction programs and access to emergency care for women [22].

**Energy & utilities**

- His platform calls for eliminating reconnection fees for disconnected utilities [22]. A podcast host's summary of an August 2026 interview says he has organized against a proposed data center in Charlestown [23].

**Public safety**

- Calls for more funding for local police, fire, and EMS and an updated D.A.R.E. program [22].

### On the current officeholder

- In an August 2026 podcast interview, as summarized by the host, Marshall described Garten as part of a faction seeking Senate leadership and tied Garten's support for redistricting to diluting the representation of communities of color [23].

### How they say they'd run the office

- His platform lists lowering taxes on working-class Hoosiers and small businesses and restarting child-care vouchers for low-income families [22].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements are listed on his campaign website; it links to an ActBlue donation page [21].

### What we couldn't verify

- Unverified (search snippet only): Ballotpedia reports Garten beat Marshall 62.1% to 37.9% in 2022.
- Podcast statements are the host's summaries, not transcripts.
- No news coverage of his 2026 campaign beyond candidate lists was found.
- No campaign finance totals were found.
