# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Sue Errington (Democratic)

Sue Errington, a Muncie Democrat, served in the state Senate from 2006 and has represented House District 34 since 2012; she announced in December 2025 that she would not seek reelection [3, 4]. She is the ranking minority member of the Environmental Affairs Committee [3].

- Committees: ranking minority member, Environmental Affairs; member, Elections and Apportionment; vice chair, Statutory Committee on Ethics, according to the House Democratic caucus [3].
- 2025 session: Authored HEA 1297, which created Indiana's Yellow Dot Program to give first responders quick access to medical information; the House honored her service with House Resolution 24 on Feb. 10, 2026 [5].
- 2025: Voted no on SEA 1, the property-tax law (passed House 65-29) [6], and on the 2025 budget conference report (adopted 66-27) [7].
- Dec. 2025: Offered an amendment to HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill, that failed 27-67 [8], then voted no on the bill, which passed the House 57-41 [9].

## This race

- House District 34 covers part of Delaware County, including most of Muncie [10, 11].
- Open seat: Errington is retiring after 14 years in the House [4]. IPR reports Democrats have held the seat nearly continuously for decades [12].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Richard Ivy won the Republican nomination with 53.22% over Randall McCallister [12, 13]; Sara Gullion was unopposed for the Democratic nomination [13]. Two other Republicans, Chris Walker and Tim Overton, withdrew in February [14].
- The Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee lists the seat among its target races [15].
- Key differences: Gullion lists raising the minimum wage, a moratorium on new data centers and restoring abortion access among her priorities [16]; Ivy lists infrastructure, public safety, economic development and fiscal prudence [17, 18]. No detailed statements from Ivy on data centers, property taxes or wages were found.
- No general-election debate or forum for this race was found. Muncie Resists invited state legislative primary candidates to a March 19, 2026 forum in Muncie [19]; which candidates attended was not reported.

## Sara Gullion

Sara Gullion is a Muncie City Council member and paralegal running as a Democrat with Errington's endorsement [4, 10]. She lists housing affordability, fair wages, accessible health care and public schools as priorities [20].

Campaign website: https://www.saragullion.com

### Background

- Paralegal; lives in Muncie [10].
- Member of the Muncie City Council, where she says she has "championed accountability, transparency and fairness" over the past two years [20, 4].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [13, 12].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Would raise the minimum wage, index it to inflation and raise the subminimum wage for tipped workers [16].

**Housing**

- Supports more state investment in affordable housing and stronger renter protections and eviction prevention [16].

**Education**

- Supports pre-K for all and ensuring "Hoosiers' taxes go toward public schools" [16]; calls child care infrastructure [16].

**Data centers**

- Supports a moratorium on more data centers, with public hearings, environmental impact assessments and limits tied to local capacity before permits are approved [16].

**Health care**

- Supports restoring abortion access, expanding mental health care access, and LGBTQ+ nondiscrimination protections [16].

### On the current officeholder

- Says Errington encouraged her to run and that she would remind state decision-makers "that their votes affect real people with real needs, who deserve real affordability" [20]. No statements about Ivy were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says she will continue to advocate for and respond to constituents and bring a local-government perspective to the Statehouse [20].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by Rep. Sue Errington, who IPR reported would run her campaign [4].
- Listed as a target race by the Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee [15].
- Top donors listed by WFYI via Transparency USA include Indiana Conservation Voters and Carey Hamilton [10].

### What we couldn't verify

- Did not answer WFYI's survey; positions come from her campaign website.
- Her campaign's endorsements page was empty when checked.
- Muncie City Council district and votes not reviewed; age not verified.
- No fundraising totals found.

## Richard M. (Rich) Ivy

Richard Ivy is human resources director for the Muncie Sanitary District and a former Muncie deputy mayor, running as a Republican [10, 18]. He lists infrastructure, public safety, economic development and opportunities for youth, seniors and working families as priorities [17].

Campaign website: https://richardivycampaign.com

### Background

- Human resources director, Muncie Sanitary District; lives in Muncie [10, 17].
- Former Muncie deputy mayor; the Star Press reported the city council eliminated funding for that position in 2024 [18]. As deputy mayor he oversaw departments including public works, parks and public safety, according to his campaign release [18].
- Previously worked at LifeStream Services, serving seniors and people with disabilities, per his campaign release [18].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 53.22% over Randall McCallister [12, 13].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Says his deputy mayor experience taught him how to get state money "funneled down to Muncie" and that constituents want "expansion, jobs, and lack of increasing taxes" [12].

**Public safety**

- Lists public safety and neighborhood revitalization among priorities [17, 18].

**Government**

- Lists transparency, restoring public trust, and "fiscal prudence and data-informed decision making" as priorities [18].

### On the current officeholder

- After winning the primary, Ivy said, "I think it's our time and our place to win this seat back and bring value back to Muncie through the state representation" [12].
- On Gullion: "Me and her have no difficulty getting along in the past. So, I think it'll be a clean race" [12].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he would support seniors, veterans and families and expand youth opportunities and workforce pathways [18].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Top donors listed by WFYI via Transparency USA include five individuals [10].

### What we couldn't verify

- Did not answer WFYI's survey; no detailed positions on property taxes, data centers, education or redistricting found.
- IPR said Ivy cited city council experience; his council service was not confirmed.
- No endorsements or fundraising totals found.
