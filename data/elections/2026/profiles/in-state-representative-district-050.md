# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Lorissa Sweet (Republican)

Lorissa Sweet, a Wabash small-business owner and former Wabash County councilwoman, has represented House District 50 since 2022 [3, 4]. She serves on the Agriculture and Rural Development; Family, Children and Human Affairs; and Local Government committees [3].

- Committees: Agriculture and Rural Development; Family, Children and Human Affairs; Local Government [3]. 2026 interim: Interim Study Committee on Government [5].
- Feb. 2023: Offered a state budget amendment barring state funding for Indiana University's Kinsey Institute, which the House approved 53-34 [6]. WFYI reported she repeated allegations about founder Alfred Kinsey and claimed, without proof, that the institute and IU shelter sexual predators; Rep. Matt Pierce (D-Bloomington) said it angered him "when people's good names are sullied and libeled from this floor" [6].
- 2024 session: Authored HB 1286, which would have replaced voting machines and electronic poll books with hand-marked, hand-counted paper ballots; it was assigned to the House Elections and Apportionment Committee [7].
- 2025 session: Co-authored HB 1413, creating a grant fund for the Indiana State Police to clear a backlog of about 600 untested rape kits, which passed the House unanimously [8]; co-sponsored a law making it a crime to use or sell radio-frequency jamming devices, ceremonially signed in August 2025 [9].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [10]. April 25, 2025: Was excused and did not vote on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [11].
- Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [12]. In January 2026 she filed HB 1166, an elections bill that was referred to committee with no further action recorded [13].

## This race

- House District 50 covers all of Huntington County and portions of Miami, Wabash and Wells counties, according to the House Republican caucus [3].
- Sweet was first elected in 2022, succeeding Dan Leonard, and won re-election in 2024 with 76.9% of the vote [4, 14].
- Sweet and Democrat Pepper Snyder were the only candidates listed in their parties' May 5, 2026 primaries [14]; both are on the November ballot [2].
- Key differences: Snyder opposes expanding private-school vouchers, would eliminate sales tax on residential utility bills and supports a regulated adult cannabis market [15]; Sweet's campaign emphasizes individual liberty, gun rights, limited government and opposition to abortion [16], and she voted for SEA 1 and HB 1032 [10, 12].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Lorissa C. Sweet

Lorissa Sweet is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2022 [3]. Her legislative work has included election administration, the 2023 Kinsey Institute funding amendment and public-safety bills [7, 6, 8].

Campaign website: https://www.sweet4indiana.com

### Background

- Owns Sweet Grooms, a pet grooming business opened in 2009, and Sweet Occasions LLC, a wedding decor rental business started in 2021; lives in Wabash; holds a Purdue University degree in animal agribusiness [3].
- Served on the Wabash County Council from 2016 until joining the House [3, 4].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Said of her 2024 paper-ballot bill: "Returning to hand-counted ballots will return us to the basics of voting and increase public confidence in our system" [7]. Voted for HB 1032 (2025 congressional redistricting) [12].

**Public safety**

- Said of HB 1413: "Due to limited resources, a backlog of untested rape kits has accumulated," and a dedicated fund would help ensure "violent criminals are held accountable" [8].
- Campaign site: "I will not compromise when it comes to defending your Constitutional right to bear arms" [16].

**Higher education**

- On her 2023 Kinsey amendment: "We, as the governing body, need to put limits on the access to tax dollars we allow Indiana University to garner" [6].

**Abortion**

- Campaign site: "Life begins at conception" [16].

**Government & regulation**

- Campaign site: "It is past time to reign in government overreach, unconstitutional edicts, and mandates" [16].

**Property taxes**

- Voted for SEA 1 (2025) [10].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Sweet about Snyder were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- On her 2026 interim committee: said study committees let lawmakers "take a deep dive into issues that impact constituents" ahead of the next session [5].

## Pepper Snyder

Pepper Snyder is a Huntington County resident who spent more than 13 years with the state's Family and Social Services Administration, including as a fraud investigator [17, 18]. Her campaign focuses on public school funding, utility costs, rural health care, child care and data-center tax policy [15].

Campaign website: https://www.peopleforpepper.com

### Background

- Worked more than 13 years for the Indiana Family and Social Services Administration as an eligibility specialist, fraud investigator and state eligibility manager [17]; her campaign says she supervised and investigated food stamp and Medicaid fraud [18].
- Lives in Huntington County; Seeger High School graduate [18].
- A Pepper Snyder of Huntington was the Libertarian Party's 2016 nominee for Indiana's 3rd Congressional District [19, 14].

### Path to the ballot

- The only Democrat listed in the May 5, 2026 primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Would "Protect public school funding instead of continuing to expand taxpayer-funded private-school vouchers" [15].

**Energy & utilities**

- Would eliminate the sales tax on residential utility bills and oppose rate hikes: "If billion-dollar data centers get the break, Hoosier families should too" [15].

**Data centers**

- Says she would "Make billion-dollar data centers pay their fair share, protect local control" and keep large developments from polluting land, water or air [15].

**Health care**

- Would "Fight to keep hospitals, maternity care, pediatric care and other essential health services available close to home" [15].

**Child care**

- Supports a child care tax credit for working families and full funding of CCDF vouchers [15].

**Cannabis & revenue**

- Supports a regulated, adult-only cannabis market, saying revenue could fund schools, roads and mental health services and "provide property tax relief" [15].

### On the current officeholder

- Her campaign does not name Sweet; it criticizes the Republican supermajority's record, including data-center tax breaks and child care voucher cuts, and says "It's time for representation that puts Hoosiers before corporations" [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says her goal is "making Indiana affordable again" for working families, seniors and the next generation [18].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Her campaign lists an endorsement from Veterans for Indiana [20].
