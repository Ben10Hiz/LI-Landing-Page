# Indiana County Sheriff — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-11 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

The sheriff is an elected county officer created by the Indiana Constitution who arrests offenders, keeps the peace, serves court papers, keeps order in county courts and runs the county jail [1, 2]. The Indiana Sheriffs' Association describes sheriffs as constitutional officers elected by the people, whose work includes operating jails, serving civil process, providing court security and coordinating emergency response [3]. Every county elects its sheriff in 2026 for a four-year term, and no one may serve as sheriff more than eight years in any twelve-year period [1].

- **Term:** 4 years; the sheriff serves until a successor is elected and qualified [4, 1]
- **Elected:** Countywide by the county's voters on the partisan general election ballot; all county sheriffs are elected in 2026 [1]
- **Term limit:** No more than eight years in any period of twelve years (Indiana Constitution, Article 6, Section 2) [1]
- **Qualifications:** Must have lived in the county for at least one year before the election, be registered to vote in the county by the filing deadline, and file a statement of economic interests; a sheriff who moves out of the county forfeits the office [1, 4]
- **Pay:** A county may pay the sheriff a salary from the county general fund of at least 50% to 100% of the state minimum salary of a full-time prosecuting attorney, depending on county population [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 92 races; 26 contested [6]

### What the office does

- Arrests without a warrant people who commit an offense in the sheriff's view, brings them before a county court, and holds them while the arrest is investigated [2].
- Suppresses breaches of the peace and pursues and jails felons [2].
- Takes care of the county jail and the prisoners held there [2, 3].
- Serves and carries out legal papers (process) directed to the sheriff by courts and the county commissioners [2].
- Attends and keeps order in all courts of the county, which the Sheriffs' Association describes as providing court security [2, 3].
- Takes photographs, fingerprints and other identification data from people held for crimes, and DNA samples for felonies [2].
- Reports twice a year to the Indiana Department of Correction the average daily cost of holding a jail prisoner [2].
- Holds state prisoners, including people convicted of Level 6 felonies, in the county jail, for which the state reimburses counties at a daily rate set in the state budget [7].
- Coordinates emergency response and works with local police, prosecutors and community partners [3].
- In a county whose council creates a sheriff's merit board by ordinance, appoints three of its five members, while the county police officers elect the other two; no more than two sheriff appointees and one elected member may belong to the same party [8].
- Must report information to federal authorities when a person arrested for a felony or misdemeanor is believed to lack legal immigration status, and, since July 2026, notify the judge setting bail when holding a person subject to an immigration detainer [9, 10].
- May supervise and inspect businesses such as pawnbrokers and secondhand dealers [2].
- What the sheriff does not control: the county commissioners must establish and maintain the county jail building [11]; the county makes the appropriation for feeding prisoners and sets the sheriff's salary [5]; the prosecuting attorney decides which crimes to prosecute [12]; and judges decide bail [13].

### Recent changes to the office

- February 2025: The Indiana Department of Correction said funding to reimburse county jails for holding Level 6 felons and state prisoners, then $40 per day, was exhausted for state fiscal year 2025; Tippecanoe County Sheriff Robert Goldsmith, then president of the Indiana Sheriffs' Association, called it very disappointing, and Rep. Greg Steuerwald (R-Avon) said everything owed would be paid [14].
- 2025: The new state budget raised the daily rate for Level 6 offenders to $42, raised the jail reimbursement line to $45 million and then $41 million a year, and moved to direct reimbursement with costs submitted within 60 days; Sheriffs' Association executive director Stephen Luce called the rate better than nothing, citing an association study that put the average cost at about $74 per inmate per day [7].
- July 2025: The Department of Correction planned to begin paying about $12.7 million owed to county jails for housing state offenders [7].
- July 2025: House Enrolled Act 1393 took effect, requiring jails to alert federal immigration authorities, with the county sheriff passing the information to federal officials, after felony or misdemeanor arrests when there is probable cause the person lacks legal status [9].
- July 2026: Senate Bill 76 took effect, requiring sheriff's offices and police departments to notify the bail judge when they hold a person subject to an immigration detainer and barring local policies that restrict cooperation with federal officials; St. Joseph County Sheriff Bill Redman said his county already cooperates with ICE, while South Bend Mayor James Mueller said the city will protect the constitutional rights of everyone living there [10].
- January 2026: The Sheriffs' Association's 2026 president, Porter County Sheriff Jeffrey Balon, listed jail overcrowding, staffing shortages, the drug epidemic and mental illness among the issues sheriffs raise with legislators [3].
- November 2026: Public Question 1 asks voters whether courts may deny bail, for offenses other than murder or treason, when the state proves by clear and convincing evidence that no release conditions will reasonably protect others or the community [15]; Sen. Eric Koch (R-Bedford) said releasing some defendants before trial is too dangerous, while Zach Stock of the Indiana Public Defender Council raised concerns about jail overcrowding [13].

## Current officeholder: Edward (Ed) C. Schroder (Republican)

Republican Ed Schroder is the current Cass County sheriff. He was first elected in 2018 and re-elected without opposition in 2022. He is not on the 2026 ballot, so the seat is open; the Pharos-Tribune reported in February 2026 that "Cass County will have a new sheriff next year" [16, 17, 18, 19, 6].

- November 2018: Elected sheriff over Democrat Dale Campbell. A sum of the county's official precinct-level results gives Schroder about 7,036 votes to Campbell's 4,487 (about 61%) [17].
- January 5, 2022: Announced a bid for a second term. In his release he said he had "improved administrative processes, modernized equipment, updated policies and added a fingerprinting service," that staff had "worked diligently to be fiscally conservative while enhancing public safety," and that he had been busy with "the jail addition construction that started before I began my first term" [20].
- Jail addition: The county's sheriff's office addition and renovation, owned by the Cass County Government Building Corporation and listing Schroder as sheriff, is described by its detention-equipment contractor as an $18 million, 47,000-square-foot project adding more than 150 beds for a total of more than 360, "to prevent future overcrowding." The contractor's page gives no dates [21].
- November 2022: Re-elected without opposition, with 7,242 votes in the unofficial county count [18].
- Before February 2024: After an inmate briefly escaped from the Cass County Detention Center and was recaptured the same day, Schroder said in a press release that the incident "will trigger scrutiny of all policies and practices as well as an after-action review" [22].
- July 2026: With Prosecutor Noah Schafer, announced deputies would have "an active presence throughout the fairgrounds" at the county fair to enforce a new state law barring serious sex offenders from events aimed at minors [23].

## This race

- Cass County (county seat: Logansport) will elect a new sheriff on Nov. 3, 2026. Sheriff Ed Schroder, in office since 2019 after winning in 2018 and 2022, is not on the ballot [17, 18, 19, 6].
- Four candidates filed: Republicans Dale Campbell (a lieutenant in the sheriff's office), Ryan Preston (a narcotics detective in the sheriff's office) and Brian Swartzell (a school resource officer), and Democrat A.J. Rozzi (a former Logansport police chief) [19, 16].
- May 5, 2026 primary (unofficial county results): Preston won the Republican nomination with 1,798 votes (49.4%), ahead of Swartzell with 1,166 (32.0%) and Campbell with 678 (18.6%). Rozzi was unopposed for the Democratic nomination with 1,025 votes [24].
- Both general-election candidates have local police backgrounds: Preston has worked in the sheriff's office since 2016 after starting with the Logansport Police Department in 2009, and Rozzi spent 35 years with the Logansport Police Department, including four years as chief [19, 25].
- Stated priorities differ in emphasis. Preston focuses on mental health and addiction, including services for inmates, and on recruiting and keeping staff. Rozzi focuses on community relations (published office policies and county listening sessions), a review of the office's role in the Cass County Drug Task Force, rehabilitation programs and officer mentoring [26, 19].
- No general-election forums or debates were found in sources reviewed.

## August (Aj) Rozzi, Jr

Democrat A.J. Rozzi is a part-time security officer at Ivy Tech Community College in Logansport. He spent 35 years with the Logansport Police Department, including four years as chief [19].

### Background

- Current job: part-time security officer at Ivy Tech Community College in Logansport [19].
- Served 35 years with the Logansport Police Department in a range of roles, including four years as chief [19].

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only Democrat to file for sheriff before the February 2026 filing deadline [19].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary unopposed, with 1,025 votes (unofficial results) [24].

### Positions & plans

**Transparency**

- Lists "improving law enforcement's relationship with the community" as a priority. As reported from his campaign site, this includes "developing and publishing the office's policies for consistency and establishing Cass County listening sessions" [19].

**Drugs**

- Plans to "evaluate the Sheriff's Office's roles within the Cass County Drug Task Force," with the goal of "disrupting trafficking networks and dealers" [19].

**Jail & corrections**

- Plans to "improve drug rehabilitation programs and support during incarceration" [19].

**Staffing & pay**

- Supports stronger mentoring and field training for officers, citing research that "mentorship programs for law enforcement is able to create better officers" [19].

### On the current officeholder

- Open seat. No statements by Rozzi about Sheriff Schroder's record or about his opponent were found in sources reviewed.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Publish the sheriff's office's policies and hold county listening sessions [19].
- Review the office's role in the Cass County Drug Task Force [19].
- Improve drug rehabilitation programs and support for people in jail [19].
- Strengthen mentoring and field training for officers [19].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or campaign-finance reports were found in sources reviewed.

### What we couldn't verify

- Campaign website not found. The Pharos-Tribune (via Yahoo) quotes "his site," but its address was not located.
- Dates of his Logansport Police Department service and of his time as chief were not found in sources read in full. A search-result snippet of an undated Pharos-Tribune article (paywalled, not read) says A.J. Rozzi succeeded Randy Rozzi as chief; unverified.

## Ryan Preston

Republican Ryan Preston is a narcotics detective in the Cass County Sheriff's Office with about 16 years in local law enforcement. He won a three-way Republican primary in May 2026 [19, 24].

Campaign website: https://ryanprestonforsheriff.com/

### Background

- Current job: narcotics detective with the Cass County Sheriff's Office [19].
- Served eight years in the U.S. Marine Corps after enlisting in 2000, including a combat tour in Kabul, Afghanistan, about three years as a recruiter, and service as a platoon sergeant, according to his campaign site [25].
- Hired by the Logansport Police Department in 2009 and attended the Indiana Law Enforcement Academy in 2010. Roles there included patrolman, school resource officer at Lincoln Middle School, field training officer and pension board secretary, plus work with the Meth Suppression Unit [25].
- Joined the Cass County Sheriff's Office in 2016. Roles there include deputy, school resource officer at Lewis Cass, K9 handler for eight years, and detective [25].
- Also lists himself as a youth leader at Cornerstone Community Bible Church and co-owner of The Gathering Grounds coffee and ice cream shop in Walton [26].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as one of three Republican candidates before the February 2026 deadline [19].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with 1,798 votes (49.4%) over Brian Swartzell (1,166) and Dale Campbell (678), per unofficial county results [24].

### Positions & plans

**Jail & corrections**

- Says he is "committed to working with mental health and drug addiction professionals in our community and surrounding areas" so that inmates receive services [26, 19].

**Drugs**

- Puts "a key emphasis on addressing both the mental health and drug crises," including "providing ongoing support to addicts, so that they are able to break free of addiction" [26].

**Staffing & pay**

- Says his Marine Corps recruiting experience will help him "not only identify, but also attract top talent" to work at the sheriff's office and jail, and stresses "seeking and maintaining quality candidates" [26, 25, 19].

### On the current officeholder

- Open seat. Preston's campaign site says he would "build on the strong foundation of our current administration" [26]. No statements about his opponent were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Work with regional mental-health and addiction professionals on services for inmates [26].
- Focus recruiting and retention on the sheriff's office and jail [26, 25].
- Lead with "a hands-on, proactive leadership approach" [26].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Campaign site lists endorsements from U.S. Rep. Jim Baird, Howard County Sheriff Jerry Asher, and "Travis Yike & Jacob Pomasl," identified as retired Logansport police chief and Logansport mayor [27].
- No campaign-finance reports were found in sources reviewed.

### What we couldn't verify

- No statements found on jail capacity, budget requests, school resource officers, immigration-detainer agreements or transparency.
- Campaign site lists only 2025 meet-and-greet events; no forums or debates found.
- Endorsement names are as spelled on the campaign site; a search snippet spells the former Logansport chief's surname differently (unverified).
- Search snippets of a paywalled Pharos-Tribune article say he held a primary-night watch party at his Walton coffee shop; not read.
- No fundraising reports found.
