# U.S. Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the U.S. House of Representatives write and vote on federal laws, including the federal budget and taxes. Each represents one congressional district for a two-year term; Indiana has nine [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None
- **Voting members:** 435 [1]
- **Indiana seats:** 9 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces bills and resolutions, offers amendments, and votes on legislation; federal laws must pass both the House and the Senate [1].
- Serves on committees, which draft legislation in their subject areas and oversee federal agencies and programs [1].
- Must be at least 25, a U.S. citizen for at least seven years, and live in the state they represent [1].

## Current officeholder: Rudy Yakym (Republican)

Rudy Yakym, of Granger, has represented Indiana's 2nd District since winning the 2022 election held after the death of Rep. Jackie Walorski, and won re-election in 2024 [3]. He serves on the House Ways and Means Committee and previously served on the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee [4].

- Committees: House Ways and Means in his current term; Transportation and Infrastructure in his first term, where his office says he was vice chair for the aviation authorization bill [4].
- May 22, 2025: Voted yes as the House passed H.R. 1, the budget reconciliation bill titled the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, 215-214 [5].
- July 3, 2025: Voted yes on the motion to agree to the Senate's version of H.R. 1, which passed 218-214 [6]. His office says he helped write the bill's tax portion on the Ways and Means Committee [4].
- September 19, 2025: Voted yes on the stopgap funding bill H.R. 5371 (Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2026), which passed 217-212 [7]; on November 12, 2025, he voted yes to agree to the Senate-amended version, which passed 222-209 [8].
- November 18, 2025: Voted yes on the Epstein Files Transparency Act (H.R. 4405), which passed 427-1 [9].
- December 9, 2025: The House passed his bill H.R. 573, the Studying NEPA's Impact on Projects Act, by voice vote; it was sent to the Senate Environment and Public Works Committee [10].
- January 22, 2026: Voted yes on the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (H.R. 7148), which passed 341-88 [11]; on February 3, 2026, he voted yes to agree to the Senate's amendments, which passed 217-214 [12].
- 2026: Voted no on several resolutions under the War Powers Resolution directing removal of U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran, including H.Con.Res. 38 (failed 212-219, March 5), H.Con.Res. 86 (passed 215-208, June 3) and H.Con.Res. 93 (passed 220-204, September 15) [13, 14, 15].
- April 29, 2026: The Ways and Means Committee voted 41-0 to advance his bill H.R. 7655, the Support for Expectant and Parenting Foster Youth Act; it was placed on the House calendar on May 11, 2026 [16].
- April 30, 2026: Voted yes on the farm bill, H.R. 7567 (Farm, Food, and National Security Act), which passed 224-200 [17].
- July 21, 2026: Voted yes on H.R. 9770, a continuing appropriations bill for fiscal year 2027, which passed 220-205 [18].
- July 22, 2026: Voted yes on the Stop Insider Trading Act (H.R. 7008), which passed 232-198; he said in a statement, "no lawmaker should ever profit from information they get on the job" [19, 20].
- August 6, 2026: Led a letter signed by 168 House Republicans to U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer supporting the administration's approach to the USMCA review and seeking improvements before any extension [21].
- September 16, 2026: Voted yes on the Ratepayer Protection Act (H.R. 9340), which passed 417-3; he said, "Hoosiers and their businesses should not be footing the bill for AI data centers" [22, 23].
- Other sponsored bills in 2025-2026 include the Reduce Bureaucracy to Uplift Families Act (H.R. 2284) and the Improper Payments Transparency Act (H.R. 1771), both referred to committee [24].
- March 2025: Protesters at his Mishawaka office criticized him for not holding town hall meetings; his office responded that "Listening to Hoosiers—their thoughts, concerns, and ideas—is the most essential part of my job" and said he travels to all 11 counties each month [25].

## This race

- The 2nd District extends south from the Michiana area and includes parts of Elkhart, Fulton, Marshall, Miami, Pulaski, St. Joseph, Starke, Wabash, Cass, Kosciusko and LaPorte counties [26].
- Yakym was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [26].
- In the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary, the Associated Press projected Jamee Decio the winner over Shaun Maeyens, according to WNDU's election-night report [27].
- The Libertarian Party of Indiana nominated William Henry of Elkhart for the 2nd District at its March 2026 state convention [28].
- Three candidates are on the November 3, 2026 ballot: Rudy Yakym (Republican), Jamee Decio (Democratic) and William Eric Henry (Libertarian) [2].
- In 2024, Henry debated the Democratic nominee while Yakym declined to participate [29]. No 2026 general-election debate involving all three candidates was found as of October 10, 2026.
- Main differences in stated positions: Yakym cites the 2025 tax law, border security, budget discipline and veterans' care as priorities [3]; Decio emphasizes lowering costs, eliminating tariffs, raising the minimum wage and holding town halls [30, 31]; Henry cites tax reform, veterans' care, privacy, constitutional liberties and federal cannabis reform [32].
- As of June 30, 2026, Federal Election Commission data showed Yakym's campaign had raised about $5.25 million for the 2026 cycle with about $855,000 on hand, and Decio's had raised about $356,000 with about $104,000 on hand; Henry's 2026 committee showed no reported receipts [33, 34, 35].

## Rudy Yakym

Yakym is the Republican incumbent, first elected in 2022, and a member of the House Ways and Means Committee [3, 4]. He is seeking re-election citing the 2025 tax law and priorities including affordability, border security and budget discipline [3].

Campaign website: https://yakym.house.gov

### Background

- Yakym is a fourth-generation Hoosier from South Bend with a business finance degree from Indiana University South Bend and an Executive MBA from the University of Notre Dame [4].
- Before Congress he was director of growth initiatives at Kem Krest [4].
- He lives in Granger with his wife, Sallyann, and their three children [4].

### Path to the ballot

- Yakym filed for re-election in January 2026 [3] and was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [26].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & taxes**

- Yakym cites the Working Families Tax Cuts Act (the 2025 reconciliation law), which he says makes the 2017 tax cuts permanent and eliminates taxes on tips and overtime [3].
- In August 2026 he began a tour of all 11 district counties to hear how the tax law is affecting constituents' paychecks and businesses [36].

**Government spending**

- He lists budget discipline and cutting federal bureaucracy among his priorities [3], and sponsored the Improper Payments Transparency Act and the Reduce Bureaucracy to Uplift Families Act [4].

**Manufacturing & trade**

- He said he supports the administration's "focus on securing improvements before agreeing to an extension" of the USMCA trade agreement (August 6, 2026) [21].

**Energy & environment**

- He said large data center operators "must pay for the power it uses" and supported a bill asking state utility regulators to consider cost standards for very large power users (September 16, 2026) [23].

**Immigration & border**

- He lists border security among his priorities for another term [3].

**Veterans**

- He lists veterans' care and benefits among his priorities [3].

**Ethics**

- He voted for and publicly supported the Stop Insider Trading Act, saying "I promised I'd never use this seat to line my own pockets" (July 22, 2026) [20].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder.
- In announcing his re-election bid (January 28, 2026), Yakym said he is "honored to fight for the hardworking people of Indiana's Second District" [3].
- ABC57 interviewed Yakym in August 2026 about his race against Decio, but its written article did not report his comments [37].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Yakym said he will keep working on affordability, border security, budget discipline, cutting federal bureaucracy, and veterans' care and benefits [3].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- FEC data through June 30, 2026 show total 2025-2026 receipts of about $5,246,000, disbursements of about $5,000,000 and about $855,000 cash on hand [33].

### What we couldn't verify

- No statements by Yakym about Decio or Henry were found in fetched sources.
- No endorsements list was verified.
- A third-party summary (search snippet only) reported Q2 2026 fundraising of $1.1 million; not used.
- Yakym's vote on H.R. 1834 (Breaking the Gridlock Act, January 8, 2026) was recorded as no, but the bill's content was not verified, so it is omitted.

## Jamee Decio

Decio is an Elkhart-born lawyer and screenwriter who won the May 2026 Democratic primary [26, 27]. She lists affordability, support for local industry and protecting democracy as her top priorities [38].

### Background

- Decio was born and raised in Elkhart and is the daughter of businessman and philanthropist Art Decio, who died in 2020 [27].
- She earned a law degree from Notre Dame, practiced law in Elkhart including as a deputy prosecutor, and moved to Los Angeles in her 30s to become a screenwriter [39].
- WANE reported she was a legislative aide for the House Banking Subcommittee on Consumer Affairs [40].
- She adopted a son from foster care and moved back to Elkhart in 2025 [39].

### Path to the ballot

- Decio announced her candidacy in November 2025 [39] and won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary over Shaun Maeyens, according to an AP projection reported by WNDU [27].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & taxes**

- She said in November 2025, "The first priority is lowering the cost of food, utilities, housing and of course healthcare" [38].
- She wants to eliminate tariffs, cut taxes for residents and raise the minimum wage, noting Indiana's minimum wage has not increased in 16 years [30].

**Health care**

- Her campaign centers on lowering family costs, including making health care more affordable and accessible [27].

**Manufacturing & trade**

- She criticizes economic policies she says put local industries and farmers at risk [27].

**Immigration & border**

- She condemns ICE efforts to deport undocumented immigrants who hold jobs and pay taxes and opposes deportation spending while Americans lose Medicaid coverage, according to WFYI's summary of her campaign materials [26].

**Foreign policy**

- At a July 2026 town hall she said, "right now we're in a war that we don't need to be in," and that the money "should be spent on the constituents" [41].

**Government accountability**

- She called for stronger congressional checks and balances and said she was motivated in part by President Trump's actions [38].

### On the current officeholder

- In April 2026 she said, "My opponent does not have town halls," and "People can't ask him questions they should be able to ask" [31]. Yakym's office said in March 2025 that listening to Hoosiers is "the most essential part of my job" and that he travels to all 11 counties each month [25].
- She said Republicans like Yakym called the decision to go to war with Iran bold and decisive, and pointed to higher gas prices [31].
- In May 2026 she said people she has spoken with over the past year say the district lacks good representation [30].

### How they say they'd run the office

- She says she will hold town halls and treat constituents as her bosses [31]; she held her second Elkhart town hall on July 24, 2026 [41].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- FEC data through June 30, 2026 show receipts of about $355,600, disbursements of about $251,100 and about $104,500 cash on hand [34].

### What we couldn't verify

- Campaign website not verified.
- Search snippets (not verified on fetched pages) said she argued Yakym votes with President Trump nearly 95% of the time, backed a middle-class tax cut with higher taxes on billionaires, and supported defunding ICE and a pathway to citizenship; not used.
- District-wide primary vote totals were not verified; a search snippet said she received more than 70%.
- A figure for deportation spending in WFYI's report could not be verified exactly and is omitted.
- No endorsements were verified.

## William Eric Henry

Henry is a U.S. Army veteran from Elkhart and the Libertarian nominee, making his third run for the seat [42, 28, 32]. His platform includes tax reform, veterans' care, privacy protections, constitutional liberties and federal cannabis reform [32].

### Background

- Henry, originally from Indianapolis, served in the U.S. Army from 2005 to 2015 and holds a degree from the U.S. Defense Information School [42].
- He was the Libertarian nominee for this seat in 2022 and 2024 [32].

### Path to the ballot

- The Libertarian Party of Indiana nominated him at its March 2026 state convention [28].

### Positions & plans

**Economy & taxes**

- His 2026 platform includes tax reform [32]. In 2024 he said, "we want less government we want less taxes" [29].

**Veterans**

- Veterans' care is part of his 2026 platform [32].

**Civil liberties**

- His platform includes privacy protections, constitutional liberties and federal cannabis reform [32].

**Government accountability**

- He says his campaign focuses on transparency, accountability and public engagement [32].

### On the current officeholder

- Announcing his 2026 run, Henry said "voters deserve leaders willing to answer tough questions directly"; the report did not say whether he was referring to Yakym [32].

### How they say they'd run the office

- In 2022 he said there are "a lot of nonpartisan issues that aren't being taken care of by the Democrats or the Republicans" [43].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- FEC data for his 2026 committee showed no reported receipts or spending [35].

### What we couldn't verify

- Coverage of his 2026 campaign is thin; no campaign website or detailed 2026 issue statements were verified.
- Search snippets (not verified) described him as a former military journalist deployed to Afghanistan and the Middle East, and gave his 2022 vote share as about 3.2%; not used.
- His 2022 positions (reported by WVPE: free-market approaches to health care and Social Security, ending support for Ukraine, cannabis legalization) were not confirmed as his 2026 positions and are omitted.
- No endorsements were found.
