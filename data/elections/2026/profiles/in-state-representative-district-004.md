# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Ed Soliday (Republican)

Ed Soliday, a Republican from Valparaiso, has served in the Indiana House since 2006 and chairs the House Utilities, Energy and Telecommunications Committee; he also sits on the Roads and Transportation Committee and chairs the 2026 Interim Study Committee on Energy, Utilities and Telecommunications [3]. The House Republican caucus describes him as a retired United Airlines pilot and executive and a former Army attack helicopter pilot [3].

- May 2023: The caucus says a bill he authored creating a comprehensive state energy policy was ceremonially signed into law [3].
- April 2025: Authored HEA 1007, which the caucus says offers incentives including a tax credit for small modular reactor manufacturers, requires large-load customers of at least 150 megawatts to pay 80% of new generation built for them, and limits closing existing plants without replacement capacity; Soliday said it "paves the way for new generation without burdening the ratepayers" [4]. Indiana Public Broadcasting reported that the bill would let a utility pass some small-reactor development costs on to customers even if a plant is never finished [5].
- On April 10, 2025, voted for SB 1, the property-tax and local government finance bill, which passed the House 65–29 [6]. On April 25, 2025, voted for the conference report on HEA 1001, the 2025–2027 state budget, adopted 66–27 [7].
- December 2024: Reappointed chair of the House Utilities, Energy and Telecommunications Committee [3].
- December 5, 2025: Voted for HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57–41 [8].
- January 28, 2026: Voted for HB 1002, the electric utility affordability bill, which passed the House 89–4; he was excused for the Feb. 19 concurrence vote (94–2) [9, 10].
- March 2026: Co-sponsored the law creating the Northwest Indiana Stadium Authority and a financing framework for a potential Chicago Bears stadium, according to the caucus [3].
- August–September 2026: After the August derecho in Northwest Indiana, asked the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission to hire an outside reviewer of utility storm response and asked that his interim committee hold a hearing, saying "Hoosiers deserve to know what worked, what didn't and what needs to improve before the next major storm" [11]. After the committee met Sept. 29, he said "No Hoosier should be left without power for weeks on end" [12].

## This race

- House District 4 covers part of Porter County, including Valparaiso, according to the House Republican caucus [3].
- Soliday was re-elected in 2024 with 57.6% of the vote, according to Wikipedia [13].
- Soliday and Kominakis were unopposed in their May 5, 2026 primaries [13]. Kominakis's campaign reported unofficial primary totals of 4,042 votes for him and 3,388 for Soliday [14]. Gearhart was nominated by the Libertarian Party at its convention [15].
- No debate or forum among the three candidates was found as of October 10, 2026.
- Utility costs are a common theme: Soliday chairs the House utilities committee and authored the 2025 energy law, Kominakis calls for more transparency and public representation at the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission, and Gearhart calls for an open-book audit of NiSource [4, 16, 17].

## Ed Soliday

The Republican incumbent, in the House since 2006 and chair of its utilities committee [3].

### Background

- Retired from United Airlines after more than 35 years as a pilot, flight manager and vice president of safety, quality assurance and security; now an aviation consultant [3].
- Served as a U.S. Army attack helicopter pilot and received the Distinguished Flying Cross, two Bronze Stars and the Purple Heart, according to the caucus [3].
- Received the Sagamore of the Wabash in January 2025; lives in Porter County [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [13].

### Positions & plans

- Said in 2025 that "SMRs are the future of efficient, clean and affordable energy" [4].
- Called for an outside review of utility preparedness after the August 2026 storms [11].

## Ryan Kominakis

Ryan Kominakis, the Democratic nominee from Valparaiso, is a Marine Corps veteran and U.S. Steel Gary Works crane operator and union representative [18, 19].

Campaign website: https://www.ryankominakis.com

### Background

- Enlisted in the Marine Corps after high school and served four deployments, including in Afghanistan, according to his campaign [18]. The Post-Tribune reported deployments from 2007 to 2012 [19].
- Has worked more than a decade at U.S. Steel Gary Works as a crane operator and crew leader and serves as a union representative; member of the United Steelworkers [18].
- Helps lead ceremonies at VFW Post 988; grew up in Union Township and lives in Valparaiso [18].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced his candidacy in October 2025 [19].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary without opposition; his campaign reported 4,042 unofficial votes [14, 13].

### Positions & plans

**Labor & workers**

- Supports repealing Indiana's right-to-work law, improving unemployment benefits, letting injured workers choose their own doctor under workers' compensation, and requiring project labor agreements on public projects [16].

**Property taxes**

- Wants to fix or repeal SEA 1 (2025), which his campaign says cut funding for local governments, schools and first responders [16].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports raising the state minimum wage, addressing corporate price gouging, and expanding access to child care, affordable housing and prescription drugs [16].

**Energy & utilities**

- Calls for more transparency and public representation on the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission, hearings in areas affected by rate changes, and strict conflict-of-interest rules [16].

**Data centers**

- Would require energy-intensive companies such as data centers to be transparent and give local residents and governments a voice in major projects [16].

**Veterans**

- Supports expanded mental health and substance-use treatment, full funding for the Indiana Department of Veterans Affairs, and better training and pay for county veteran service officers [16].

### On the current officeholder

- At his announcement, said incumbents have been "in full support of things like the utility rate hikes" and that "We have local legislators that have authored bills to give tax breaks to those companies," referring to data centers; the Post-Tribune reported he did not name Soliday [19].
- After the primary, said his campaign "outworked and outperformed a twenty-year incumbent" [14].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists labor protections, affordability, responsible growth, utility accountability and veterans as campaign priorities [16].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by Valparaiso City Council President Ellen Kapitan, according to the Post-Tribune [19].

## Travis Gearhart

Travis Gearhart is the Libertarian nominee, a former Hebron Town Council member and former local Republican Party officer [17].

Campaign website: https://travisgearhart.com

### Background

- Raised in Hebron; won a Hebron Town Council seat at age 25, served on a planning commission in Belgium, Wisconsin, and lost a general-election race for Porter County Council at-large, according to his campaign site [17].
- Previously held Republican roles including South Porter County chairman and 1st Congressional District vice chair of the Young Republicans; says he later joined the Libertarian Party [17].
- Co-hosts a podcast called Disenfranchised [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Nominated at the Libertarian Party convention, according to The Ballot Brief [15].

### Positions & plans

**Government & elections**

- Supports term limits and says, "I am pledging here, right now, to only serve 2 terms if elected" [17].

**Energy & utilities**

- Calls for a full, open-book audit of NiSource, citing rising costs and a lockout of Steelworkers union members [17].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports ending Indiana's right-to-work law and phasing out the state income tax [17].

**Public safety**

- Supports legalizing cannabis, citing tax revenue in Illinois and Michigan and police and court resources [17].

**Transportation**

- Opposes new toll roads, favors maintaining existing infrastructure, and would end vehicle emissions testing [17].

**Health care**

- Pledges to back legislation supported by the Coalition of Advanced Practice Registered Nurses [17].

### On the current officeholder

- His campaign site does not mention Soliday [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he will publish campaign finance reports and serve no more than two terms [17].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements were listed on his campaign site [17].

### What we couldn't verify

- Little news coverage of his campaign was found.
- Date and details of the Libertarian convention nomination were not found.
- No fundraising totals found.
