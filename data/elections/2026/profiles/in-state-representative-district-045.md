# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Bruce Borders (Republican)

Bruce Borders, a Jasonville insurance and financial services business owner, has represented House District 45 from 2004 to 2012 and since 2014, and serves as an assistant majority whip [3]. He is not on the November ballot because he lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary to Knox County Commissioner Kellie Streeter, which the Indiana Capital Chronicle called "the sole upset thus far" in that night's Indiana House primaries [4, 5].

- Committees: Vice chair of Local Government; member of Commerce, Small Business and Economic Development and of Insurance; assistant majority whip [3].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, which passed the House 65-29 [6]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [7]. Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [8].
- January 2026: Authored HB 1199 to write into state law that the sex listed on a birth certificate cannot be changed, building on Gov. Mike Braun's Executive Order 25-36; the caucus said the bill was referred to the House Public Health Committee [9]. A similar 2017 Borders bill (HB 1361) was criticized by the LGBT advocacy group Freedom Indiana; Borders responded at the time that he respects "accuracy in all legal records," according to Wikipedia's summary of IndyStar reporting [10].
- Feb. 11, 2026: Attended the White House "Clean Beautiful Coal" event, where a coal truck driver from the Bear Run Mine in his district spoke [11]. After the 2026 session he said it "was about protecting taxpayers and making government more efficient" [12].

## This race

- House District 45 includes all of Greene and Sullivan counties and parts of Daviess, Knox and Vigo counties in southwest Indiana [3, 13].
- Open seat: Incumbent Bruce Borders lost the May 5, 2026 Republican primary. Local results reported by the Greene County Daily World showed Kellie Streeter with 4,938 votes (53.2%) to Borders' 4,337 (46.8%); Wikipedia lists 4,939 to 4,340 [5, 14]. Streeter carried both Greene County (1,997 to 1,594) and Sullivan County (1,790 to 1,443) [5, 15].
- The Indiana Capital Chronicle called it "the sole upset" in that night's Indiana House primaries and noted Streeter had nearly unseated Borders in a three-way 2024 primary [4]. Before the primary, Wikipedia lists endorsements of Borders by President Donald Trump, Gov. Mike Braun, the NFIB Indiana PAC and the NRA Political Victory Fund [14]; Indiana Farm Bureau's AgELECT PAC endorsed Streeter [16].
- Rebecca (Becky) Mayfield was the only Democrat listed in the primary [14].
- Streeter and Borders appeared at a March 19, 2026 Sullivan County Farm Bureau candidate forum [17]. No general-election debate or forum between Streeter and Mayfield was found.
- Key differences: Streeter's platform centers on local control, rural infrastructure funding, rural health care and public safety [18]; Mayfield's centers on public education and opposition to state funding for private schools [19].

## Kellie E. Streeter

Kellie Streeter is a Knox County commissioner and administrator of the Daviess County Health Department who won the 2026 Republican nomination by defeating the incumbent [13, 5]. Her campaign focuses on local control, road and bridge funding, rural health care and public safety [13, 18].

Campaign website: https://www.streeterforindiana.com/

### Background

- Knox County commissioner since being caucused into office in 2016; the Sun-Commercial reports she was the first woman elected to that office [13].
- Administrator of the Daviess County Health Department [13, 20].
- Past president of the Indiana Association of County Commissioners and its current legislative chair, in her own description [13].
- Her campaign site lists service on the Good Samaritan Hospital Board of Governors, the Knox County Economic Development Corporation board and the Indiana Behavioral Health Commission, and says she is vice chair of the Knox County Republican Party [18].
- Lives in Freelandville [13].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for the Republican nomination at the Statehouse in January 2026 [13]; ran in 2024 and, by the campaign's account, came within a few hundred votes [13].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over incumbent Bruce Borders, 53.2% to 46.8%, according to local results [5].

### Positions & plans

**Local control**

- Her campaign site says she wants to protect local decision-making on agriculture, land use, energy (solar, wind, coal and natural gas) and economic development [18].

**Infrastructure**

- Seeks fair, sustainable funding for local roads, bridges and drainage so rural counties do not have to raise taxes, according to her site [18].

**Health care**

- Supports behavioral and public health access through rural hospitals, EMS, provider recruitment and early-intervention programs [18]. As commissioner she led the 2022 launch of a Knox County ambulance service, according to her site [18].

**Property taxes**

- At a March 2026 Farm Bureau forum, she described a vision of preserving agricultural land and natural resources while making the property tax system "more streamlined and equitable," as paraphrased by the Sun-Commercial [17].

**Public safety**

- Lists support for first responders, law enforcement, 911, emergency management and veterans as a priority [18].

### On the current officeholder

- Open seat. Streeter has framed her candidacy around her county-government experience: "In many ways, I have already been advocating for our district at the Statehouse and I'm prepared to be our next state rep." [13]
- She pledged to be proactive and author legislation addressing the district's specific needs, as summarized by the Sun-Commercial [17].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Said her campaign would focus on "protecting local control, improving public safety, securing sustainable road and bridge funding and strengthening access to rural healthcare and ensuring our education system works for all families" [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by Indiana Farm Bureau's AgELECT PAC for the 2026 primary [16]. No endorsements are listed on her campaign site [18].

## Rebecca Dawn (Becky) Mayfield

Becky Mayfield is a Linton-Stockton High School social studies teacher running as a Democrat [21, 19]. Her platform centers on public education, and she opposes state funding for private schools [19].

### Background

- Social studies teacher at Linton-Stockton High School, where she has taught AP U.S. Government [21, 19].
- BallotReady lists her as an owner of Mayfield's Transmission and Mayfield Forge and Fabrication [21].
- In October 2023 she organized an event at which students hosted candidates for Linton city offices, according to the Greene County Daily World [19].
- Lives in Linton [19].

### Path to the ballot

- Was the only Democrat listed in the May 5, 2026 primary [14, 21].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Opposes state money going to private schools: "Those are tax dollars, and tax dollars are meant for public institutions, not private ones." [19]
- Criticized cuts to public school funding she tied to the state budget, property tax changes and voucher expansion; the article cited Indiana Coalition for Public Education projections of SEA 1-related shortfalls for area districts, such as $206,480 for Linton-Stockton [19].

**Personal freedoms**

- Said "people deserve choices" and that government should not dictate personal decisions; supports women's ability to choose the direction of their lives while describing herself as not pro-abortion, and supports marriage equality, as reported by the Greene County Daily World [19].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports a living wage for all Hoosiers [19].

### On the current officeholder

- Open seat. Mayfield has said she would put people ahead of party and cross party lines, and pledged not to run a divisive campaign: "I just don't think the time spent blaming others is productive time." [19]

### How they say they'd run the office

- Her campaign planned more meet-the-candidate events in Daviess, Knox and Vigo counties, plus social media outreach, signs and banners [19].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements were found; BallotReady lists none [21].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign website found; BallotReady links a Facebook page.
- No fundraising data found.
- A search-result snippet said she did not answer the Indiana Capital Chronicle's voter-guide questions on property and gas taxes, redistricting or affordability; the guide could not be opened (unverified).
- No statements on data centers, energy or redistricting were found.
