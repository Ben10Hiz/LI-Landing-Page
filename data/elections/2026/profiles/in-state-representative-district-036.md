# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Kyle Pierce (Republican)

Kyle Pierce, an Anderson Republican, was first elected to House District 36 in November 2022 [3, 4]. He is vice chair of the Financial Institutions Committee and serves on the Elections and Apportionment and Environmental Affairs committees [3]; his authored laws have focused on genetic-data privacy, digital assets and proxy advisory firms [5, 6].

- Committees: Financial Institutions (vice chair); Elections and Apportionment; Environmental Affairs [3].
- Feb. 2024: Spoke in support of SB 135, which set a June 30, 2025 deadline for local governments that had not redistricted after the 2020 census and withholds officials' pay if they miss it; he noted Anderson was being sued for not redistricting, and the bill passed the House 90-0 [7].
- Feb. 2025: The House passed his HB 1643, which the caucus says updates campaign-finance reporting, expands electronic filing and creates an Election Division database for local and school board candidates [8].
- 2025 session: Authored HEA 1521, which requires consumer genetic-testing companies to get consent before using or sharing genetic data and bars sharing it with insurers, data brokers or employers; ceremonially signed Aug. 27, 2025 [5].
- April 10, 2025: Voted yes when the House passed SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, 65-29 [9]. April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the 2025 state budget conference report (HEA 1001), adopted 66-27 [10].
- Aug. 2025: Declined to comment to The Herald Bulletin on mid-decade congressional redistricting [11]. Dec. 5, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41 [12]; the Senate rejected it 31-19 on Dec. 11 [13].
- 2026 session: Authored HEA 1042, requiring retirement plans with self-directed brokerage accounts to offer at least one cryptocurrency option and protecting self-custody of digital assets and crypto mining subject to local zoning, and HEA 1273, adding transparency requirements for proxy advisory firms [6, 14].

## This race

- House District 36 covers part of Madison County, including Anderson [3, 15].
- Pierce has held the seat since 2022, when he defeated a 10-term Democratic incumbent [4].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Pierce was unopposed for the Republican nomination [16]; Kim Townsend won the Democratic nomination with 3,796 votes (87.7%) to 530 for Nouhad Melki II [17, 16].
- The Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee lists Townsend as a Target Races candidate [18].
- Key differences: Pierce's campaign emphasizes lower taxes, cutting regulation, school choice and financial-technology policy [19]; Townsend's emphasizes housing access, health care affordability and public school investment [15]. Pierce voted for the 2025 budget, the 2025 property-tax law and the 2025 redistricting bill [10, 9, 12].
- No debate or forum for this race was found.

## Kyle Pierce

Kyle Pierce is the Republican incumbent, seeking a third term [3]. His legislative work has centered on financial regulation, digital assets and data privacy [5, 6].

Campaign website: https://pierceforindiana.com/

### Background

- Bachelor's degree in political science from Ball State University and a law degree from the IU McKinney School of Law; works as a nonprofit development manager and consultant; serves on the Anderson Paramount Theatre board; lives in Anderson [3].
- BallotReady lists his current job as director of finance at IARCA (2024–present) and earlier work as an Indiana House legislative assistant and in development roles at the Ball State University Foundation and Greater Muncie Habitat for Humanity [20].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary [16].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Says he supports "responsible budgets, lower taxes, and property-tax reforms that provide meaningful relief to homeowners" [19]; voted for SEA 1 (2025) [9].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports a competitive tax environment, affordable energy and fewer regulatory barriers, and says economic-development programs should produce measurable benefits for taxpayers [19].
- Supports "clear and responsible rules" for new financial technologies [19]; said HEA 1042 lets Hoosiers "participate in digital asset markets with clarity and confidence" [14].

**Education**

- Supports school choice, curriculum transparency, apprenticeships, trade schools and work-based learning [19].

**Health care**

- Supports price transparency and competition and improved access to mental and behavioral health services [19].

**Data privacy**

- Said of HEA 1521: "Hoosiers should never have to worry about their most personal information being used against them" [5].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Says he supports "clear election laws, consistent administration, and safeguards that protect election integrity" [19]; voted for HB 1032 (2025) [12].
- Said of HB 1643: "Candidates and voters deserve a system that is straightforward, reliable and safe" [8].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements by Pierce about Townsend were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- His campaign site lists priorities including lowering costs, cutting bureaucracy, creating higher-paying jobs and expanding career and technical education [19].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No 2026 endorsements or fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Search snippets said the Indiana Capital Chronicle voter guide reports Pierce supported mid-decade redistricting and lists his campaign issues; the guide returned 403 and was not read.
- His campaign site's 'Kyle's Record' page was not reviewed.
- No fundraising data found.

## Kimberly (Kim) Townsend

Kim Townsend leads the Anderson Housing Authority and is the Democratic nominee [4]. Her campaign focuses on housing, health care, education and community safety [15].

Campaign website: https://votefortownsend.com/

### Background

- Executive director (her campaign says CEO) of the Housing Authority of the City of Anderson since 2018; previously a City of Anderson project contract specialist (1994–2018), Madison County Youth Center JDAI coordinator and Madison County Community Health Center project coordinator [21, 15].
- Her campaign says the agency had a failing HUD designation when she took over and has since secured more than $4 million in grants [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 87.7% over Nouhad Melki II [17, 16].
- Before the primary, a federal Hatch Act challenge argued she could not run while employed by a federally funded housing authority; IPR reports the challenge was denied, and she says she intends to keep the job if elected [4].

### Positions & plans

**Housing**

- Says "Every family in Madison County deserves a safe, stable place to call home" and pledges to expand housing access and protect residents from displacement [15].

**Health care**

- Says she will "champion policies that make quality care accessible and affordable for all Hoosiers" [15].

**Education**

- Supports investing in schools and educators [15].

**Economy & jobs**

- Supports local businesses, attracting investment and creating good-paying jobs [15]; IPR reports her campaign will focus on affordability and rising housing costs [4].

**Public safety**

- Favors "community investment, youth programs, and collaborative approaches to public safety" [15].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Lists voter engagement as a priority, saying every eligible voter should have access and information to participate [15].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about Pierce were found. After the primary she said many people "want to see something different," per The Herald Bulletin's account [16]; her site frames the race as part of an effort to end the Republican supermajority [15].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says: "I've spent 31 years showing up for this community. Now it's time to take our story — and our needs — to the Statehouse" [15].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Named a Target Races candidate by the Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee [18, 15]. No fundraising figures were found.

### What we couldn't verify

- Search snippets said she told the Indiana Capital Chronicle she opposes mid-decade or partisan redistricting and listed public school funding as a priority; that guide returned 403 and was not read.
- No local news coverage of her positions beyond the primary-night stories was found.
- No fundraising data found.
