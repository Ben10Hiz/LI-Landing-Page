## Current officeholder: Dennis Quakenbush (Republican)

Dennis Quakenbush (Republican) has been Hamilton County sheriff since January 2019 and is term-limited, so he is not on the 2026 ballot [2]. Before taking office he spent 16 years in the sheriff's office, starting as a dispatcher and rising to captain of the Patrol Division [3].

- November 2018: Described as sheriff-elect, set to take office January 1; previously a dispatcher, sergeant and captain of the Patrol Division during 16 years with the office [3].
- November 2024: The county council's adopted 2025 budget included salary adjustments for sheriff's deputies and correction officers and authorized two additional deputy positions [4].
- January 2025: The sheriff's office announced an Immigration Commission to take part in the federal 287(g) program with ICE, led by Chief Deputy John Lowes; Quakenbush said the program was meant "to enhance public safety by identifying and removing criminal illegal aliens from the community." Lowes said it would focus on people held in the county jail who had committed a crime, and that victims and witnesses "don't have to hesitate to call us" [5].
- 2026: Quakenbush endorsed his chief deputy, John Lowes, in the Republican primary for sheriff; Lowes lost to Dustin Dixon [6].

## This race

- Open seat: Sheriff Dennis Quakenbush (Republican) is term-limited [2].
- May 5, 2026 Republican primary: Lt. Dustin Dixon won with more than 62 percent of the vote over Chief Deputy John Lowes (about 37 percent). No Democrat had filed at that point [6].
- July 2, 2026: Charles Hollowell filed as the Democratic candidate at the Hamilton County Clerk's Office, one of four Democrats added to the county ballot [2, 7].
- October 6, 2026: Dixon and Hollowell were scheduled to appear together at The Exchange, a breakfast series of the Westfield and Noblesville chambers of commerce [8]. No account of what was said there was found.

## Dustin K. Dixon

Republican nominee and lieutenant in the Hamilton County Sheriff's Office, where he has worked since 2002; his campaign is organized around "Protection, Technology, and Responsibility" [11, 12].

Campaign website: https://www.dixonforsheriff.com

### Background

- Lieutenant with the Hamilton County Sheriff's Office overseeing day-shift patrol, including animal control and probation security; joined as a merit deputy in 2002 [9].
- According to his campaign site: canine handler 2005–2019, promoted to sergeant in 2010 and lieutenant in 2019, led the Investigations Division from December 2019, and returned to patrol as a shift commander in January 2023 [12].
- Began county work in 2001 as Pretrial Diversion Coordinator in the Hamilton County Prosecutor's Office [9, 12].
- U.S. Army veteran (field artillery forward observer, 1995–1998); bachelor's degree in criminal justice and criminology from Ball State University [12].
- Business partner and client manager at a local security consulting and staffing firm [9].
- Member of Fraternal Order of Police Lodge 103 [9].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed as a Republican candidate in January 2026 [9].
- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary with more than 62 percent of the vote over Chief Deputy John Lowes [6].

### Positions & plans

**Patrol & response**

- Lists "reducing the influx of violent crime" as a public-safety priority [12]; his 2024 announcement described reducing crime through "data-driven strategies, increased training" and up-to-date equipment [11].

**Budget**

- Pledges "responsible use of tax dollars entrusted to the agency" [11, 12].

**Transparency**

- Said he wants an office that "listens, responds, and works transparently with our community" [11].
- Proposes to "enhance the use of technology to increase the efficiency and effectiveness of agency operations" [12].

**School safety**

- His campaign biography says he serves on the Hamilton Heights School Corporation Safety Committee; no school-safety platform item was found [12].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about Sheriff Quakenbush's record were found. His announcement said it is "time to move beyond 'policing as usual' and toward true partnership with our residents" [11].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Partner with "community groups, non-profits and social service agencies" [12].
- Discussed transparency, technology in policing and support for deputies in an April 2026 podcast interview [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Former sheriff and retired Indiana State Police Superintendent Doug Carter is his honorary campaign chairman [9].
- Endorsed by Fishers Mayor Scott Fadness and Noblesville Mayor Chris Jensen [14].
- In his own submitted bio, Dixon says he is backed by every living former Hamilton County sheriff, all four Hamilton County Fraternal Order of Police lodges, and Mayors Jensen, Fadness, Willis and Finkham [15].
- His campaign site lists endorsements including former Hamilton County prosecutors Sonia Leerkamp and Greg Garrison and retired Fishers Police Chief George Kehl [12].
