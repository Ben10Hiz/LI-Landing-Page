# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- The district: Southwestern Consolidated School District of Shelby County is a small K-12 district with its office at 3406 W. 600 S., Shelbyville [24]. It runs Southwestern Elementary, Southwestern Jr./Sr. High School and the Little Spartans Preschool [24]. The junior-senior high school is about 7 miles southwest of Shelbyville and had 280 students in 2023–24, according to federal data cited by Wikipedia [25]. Superintendent Dr. Jamison Wilkins reported a fall 2025 count-day enrollment of 599 students, not counting preschool [26]. In September 2026 he said the district expected record enrollment (at least since 2020), with about 20 more K-12 students than the year before [27]. On March 11, 2026 the board voted 7-0 for a new contract for Wilkins, with a condition that he gets no raise in a year when teachers get none [28].
- The board: there are seven members [29, 30]. They are President Jon Deater, Vice President Blake Newkirk, Secretary Dustin Simpson, John Blue, Daryl Thomas, Derrek Tennell and Brad Stamper [29, 30]. Seats are tied to townships, plus one at-large seat [31, 32]. In 2024, Daryl Thomas defeated incumbent Travis Beck for a Hendricks Township seat, 950 votes to 915 [31, 32]. Jonathan Deater and John Blue won the two Jackson Township seats in an uncontested race [32, 31]. The 2026 ballot has three races: At Large, Hendricks Township and Washington Township [6].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: current members Brad Stamper (running at large) and Derrek Tennell (running in Washington Township) are on the ballot [29, 6]. Tennell first joined the board by appointment in March 2022 [33]. Two members said they will not run again [34, 35]. Blake Newkirk said so at the Aug. 13, 2025 meeting [34], and Dustin Simpson said so at the June 10, 2026 meeting [35]. Doug Swift is the only candidate for the Hendricks Township seat [6]. This guide did not find 2022 results or a district source showing which seat each current member holds.
- Seat count is uncertain [6, 32]. Three of the seven members were elected in 2024, which suggests four seats end this year [6, 32]. The state list, however, shows three races, each 'vote for 1' [6, 32]. If Washington Township has two seats on the ballot, both of its candidates would be elected. This guide did not find a 2026 sample ballot or county candidate list to confirm the number of seats in each race.
- Budget, staffing and property taxes (2025–2026): at an Oct. 29, 2025 hearing on the tentative teacher contract, Wilkins said the deal lowered the starting teacher salary to $46,000 and that staff cuts might come the next year to help cover salary and benefit costs [36]. Newkirk asked residents unhappy about the reduced funding to contact their state legislators [36]. The teachers association president told the board on Nov. 12, 2025 that 'with the changes made by the state, this would be a fiscally challenging year' [37]. On May 13, 2026 the board voted 7-0 to cut four positions: a high school health/PE job, the elementary assistant principal, an elementary teaching job and an instructional assistant [38]. In June it ended one contract through a reduction in force [35].
- Assessed value and the 2027 budget: in September 2026 Wilkins said the district's assessed value was about $389 million, down roughly $18 million to $19 million from the year before [27]. He attributed much of the drop to SEA 1 and changes affecting farmland [27]. No one commented at the budget hearing, and the board was expected to adopt the 2027 budget in October [27]. The 2026 budget was adopted 7-0 on Oct. 15, 2025 [26].
- Facilities (2025–2026): the board paid for a roofing project in 2025 [34, 26]. On Nov. 12, 2025 it approved bids for an HVAC project paid for with GO bonds: $180,000 for general trades, $3,189,000 for HVAC and $314,900 for electrical [37]. The construction manager said the work would be done by the start of the August 2026 school year [37]. Bond project payments went on through summer 2026, including $1,307,328.65 to the HVAC contractor [39, 40].
- Academics (2025–2026): in September 2025 the district set goals of 48% ILEARN proficiency in English and 45% in math [41]. Wilkins said current results were 46.3% and 42.7% [41]. On the redesigned 2026 ILEARN, 33.5% of Southwestern students in grades 3–8 were proficient in both English and math [42]. That was above the statewide rate of 31.5% and second among Shelby County districts [42]. Wilkins said Southwestern Elementary was one of three schools in the state with a 100% IREAD passing rate [43]. The high school received a $50,000 Project Lead The Way Biomedical Sciences startup grant [28].
- Enrollment and school choice (2025–2026): the board approved moving 6th grade to the junior-senior high school starting in 2026–27 [34]. It approved an out-of-district enrollment policy for 2026–27 in March 2026 [28]. In May 2026 it created the Spartan Virtual Academy for grades 6–12, run through Edmentum [38, 44]. Eleven students had enrolled in the online academy by July 2026 [39].
- Policies and planning: the board held community brainstorming nights in April 2025 [45] and approved a strategic plan 7-0 on Aug. 13, 2025 [34]. In May 2026 it approved a stricter high school cellphone rule tied to new state law: phones must be put away all day, including on buses [44]. On May 13, 2026 the board voted 7-0 to continue a partnership with Girls Inc. for its Healthy Sexuality Program and to approve the 'I Decide For Me' curriculum for the high school human sexuality unit [38].
- County tournaments (June 2026): at the June 10, 2026 meeting, members of the public and board members objected to the cancellation of the Shelby County tournaments after Shelby Eastern schools withdrew [35]. Wilkins said the athletic directors from Morristown and Waldron had chosen to withdraw and that no formal rules prevented it [35].
- Party labels: under a recent state law change, school board candidates now declare a party or a 'non-disclosed' status [46]. A Triton Central board president told a December 2025 county meeting that a declared party can decide who fills a seat that becomes vacant mid-term [46]. On the state list, Tennell is labeled Republican and the other three candidates Nonpartisan [6].
- Forums: this guide found no candidate forums, questionnaires or news profiles of the 2026 Southwestern candidates.
