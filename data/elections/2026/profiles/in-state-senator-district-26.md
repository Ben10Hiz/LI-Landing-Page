# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Scott Alexander (Republican)

Scott Alexander, a Muncie real estate appraiser and former Delaware County councilman, was first elected to the Senate in 2022 and is seeking a second term [3, 4]. He serves on the Agriculture, Judiciary, Natural Resources, and Pensions and Labor committees [5].

- Committees: Agriculture; Judiciary; Natural Resources; Pensions and Labor; interim study committees on government and pension management oversight, according to the Senate Republican caucus [3].
- April 15, 2025: Voted yes when the Senate gave final approval (27-22) to SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law [6].
- April 25, 2025: Voted yes on the conference report for the 2025 state budget (HEA 1001), which passed the Senate 39-11 [7].
- Oct. 24, 2025: Announced he would support redrawing Indiana's congressional districts, saying "We cannot sit on the sidelines while the Democrats continue to gerrymander" [8]. Dec. 11, 2025: Voted yes on HB 1032, the redistricting bill, which the Senate defeated 19-31 [9]. WFYI reported that day that Sen. Linda Rogers said Alexander had recently had open-heart surgery [10].
- 2026 session: Authored SEA 144, which updates the state definition of tobacco to include vaping devices and raises the penalty for giving tobacco or e-liquid vaporizers to anyone under 21; it was ceremonially signed May 13, 2026. Alexander said such products "should never make it into the hands of minors" [11].
- 2026 session: The caucus says Alexander supported SB 243, conforming state taxes to federal treatment of tips, overtime and auto-loan interest, and voted for HEA 1002 on utility affordability and reliability [12].

## This race

- Senate District 26 covers all of Delaware and Randolph counties, including Muncie [5].
- In 2022, after redistricting, Alexander won the seat with 61% of the vote over Democrat Melanie Wright, a former state representative [4]. Wright now chairs Dale's campaign committee [13].
- May 5, 2026 primaries: Alexander won the Republican nomination over Katherine (Kat) Nunley-Kritsch, and Andrew Dale was unopposed for the Democratic nomination [14].
- Key differences: Alexander voted for the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) [6], which Dale says should be repaired because it shifts the tax burden onto rural communities [13]. Dale supports a 12-to-18-month moratorium on large data center development [5]; no statement from Alexander on data centers was found. No debate or forum was found.

## Andrew Dale

Andrew Dale is a Muncie executive coach running as a Democrat [5]. He focuses on affordability, public education funding, property-tax changes and rural health care [5, 13].

Campaign website: https://daleforindianasenate.com

### Background

- Executive coach at Harbinger Coach, LLC; age 59; lives in Muncie [5].

### Path to the ballot

- Unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [14].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Calls for repairing SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law, which he warns will harm rural communities through property-tax shifts [13].

**Data centers**

- Supports "a moratorium of 12 to 18 months on large data center development in Indiana" while environmental, zoning and public-health effects are studied [5]; favors incentives for wind and solar over courting data centers [13].

**Energy & utilities**

- Says utility costs are too high and the legislature should take a more active role in regulating them [5, 13].

**Education**

- Wants a law barring unlawful dissolution of school corporations, citing Union School Corporation in Randolph County; opposes the voucher program; says "Keeping church out of school is a must" [13].

**Health care**

- Wants rural hospitals and clinics protected from closure [5, 13].

**Housing**

- Calls for a comprehensive approach to housing, criticizes how Indiana handles tax-sale properties, and says homelessness should not be treated as a criminal matter [13].

**Child care**

- Calls child care an economic issue that needs a lasting fix [13].

**Economy & jobs**

- Proposes growing the education sector in Delaware County and supporting manufacturing, farmers and small businesses in both counties [13].

### On the current officeholder

- No direct statements about Alexander or his record were found; Dale says he wants to be "a custodian of trust rather than a gatekeeper of possibilities" [5].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists affordability, fully funded public education, state taxation and assessments, rural health care and preparing young people for the future as his priorities [5].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Top donors listed by WFYI from Transparency USA data are individuals, including Dale himself; no totals given [5].

### What we couldn't verify

- Ballot Brief and Ballotpedia snippets say he holds degrees from Vincennes University and Ball State University and chairs the Delaware County Democratic Party; not confirmed from a primary source.
- A Ball State Daily News profile was referenced by other sites but could not be located.
- No endorsements or fundraising totals found.

## Scott A. Alexander

Scott Alexander is the incumbent, first elected in 2022, and owns a Muncie real estate appraisal firm [3, 5]. His campaign lists jobs, roads, schools and responsive government as priorities [5].

Campaign website: https://electscottalexander.com

### Background

- Owner and operator of Alexander & Company Real Estate Appraisers; attended Delta High School, Vincennes University and Ball State University [3].
- Was a Delaware County councilman before his 2022 election [4].
- Lives in Muncie [5].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Katherine (Kat) Nunley-Kritsch [14].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Says more is needed so that Hoosiers "are not forced out of homes they own free and clear" over property-tax bills [15].

**Health care**

- Says "Healthcare is too expensive" and backs legislation on cost transparency, prescription costs and hidden fees [15].

**Child care**

- Backs letting employers offer child care as a benefit [15].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Supported mid-decade congressional redistricting in 2025, saying "The time to take action is now" [8].

**Public safety**

- Authored SEA 144 on tobacco and vaping products and minors [11].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements about Dale were found.

### How they say they'd run the office

- Campaign priorities listed by WFYI: job protections, safe roads, dependable schools and responsive government [5].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Top donors listed by WFYI from Transparency USA data: Senate Majority Campaign Committee, Indiana Multi Family Housing PAC, Indiana Merit Construction PAC of ABC, Law PAC of Indiana and Indiana Realtors PAC; no totals given [5].

### What we couldn't verify

- Did not respond to WFYI's 2026 survey; his campaign website could not be read.
- Primary vote totals against Nunley-Kritsch were not found, nor what the primary campaign debated.
- No endorsements or fundraising totals found.
