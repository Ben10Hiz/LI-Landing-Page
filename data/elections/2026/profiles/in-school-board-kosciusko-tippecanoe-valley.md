# Indiana School Board Member — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

A school board is the elected or appointed governing body of a public school corporation, the legal unit that runs a local school district [1, 2]. The board hires and evaluates the superintendent, adopts policies, and approves the annual budget, while the superintendent and staff run schools day to day [3]. Most boards have five or seven members serving staggered four-year terms [3]. Starting in 2026, candidates may list a political party, run as independents, or list no affiliation on the ballot, and there is no primary for these seats [2, 4].

- **Term:** 4 years, with elections staggered so part of the board is chosen every two years [3]
- **Elected:** At the November general election (November 3, 2026, or November 2028 for some seats); depending on the local organization plan, members represent the whole school corporation (at large) or a district within it, and some boards are wholly or partly appointed [2]
- **Ballot label:** Each candidate chooses a party label, 'Independent', or no affiliation, shown next to the name; no primary and no straight-ticket voting for these races [2, 4]
- **Pay:** Set by board resolution: an annual amount of no more than 10% of the lowest starting teacher salary in the school corporation, plus a capped per diem [5]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 632 races in 299 school corporations; 1,039 candidates [6]

### What the office does

- Hires, evaluates and can dismiss the superintendent, and approves the hiring and firing of principals, teachers and other staff recommended by the superintendent [1, 7, 3].
- Adopts the annual budget, appropriates funds and oversees school finances [1, 3].
- Decides whether to ask voters to approve an operating referendum (an extra property tax levy); the board adopts the resolution and the Department of Local Government Finance reviews the ballot language for compliance with state law [8].
- Approves contracts, buys and sells property, and decides on construction, repair and demolition of school buildings [1].
- Bargains with the teachers' union over salary, wages and wage-related benefits such as health insurance and retirement; state law excludes teacher evaluation procedures from those bargaining subjects [9].
- Writes and updates policies and rules for schools, often in a policy handbook, including the student handbook [1, 3].
- Buys curricular materials, provides them to students without charge, and runs programs such as student transportation and school lunch [1].
- Must adopt a process for parents and residents to ask that library material be removed as obscene or harmful to minors, review requests at public meetings and keep a public catalog of library materials [10].
- Hears written appeals of student expulsions, unless the board has voted not to hear them [11].
- Must take official action openly; Indiana's Open Door Law says public agencies' official action is to be conducted openly so the people may be fully informed [12, 13].
- A school board member must have lived in the school corporation (and, for a district seat, the district) for at least one year before the election, and a teacher or other employee of the school corporation may not serve on its board [2].
- What the board does not control: the State Board of Education adopts statewide academic standards [14], the General Assembly sets the state school funding formula in the state budget [15], and voucher (Choice Scholarship) eligibility is set by state law [16].

### Recent changes to the office

- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 287 allowed school board candidates to declare a party affiliation; the Indiana School Boards Association opposed the change, and a supporter quoted by WNDU, a Noblesville school board member, said it adds transparency [17].
- 2026: Candidates choose a party, 'Independent' or no affiliation on their nominating petition, and that choice appears on the ballot; a candidate claiming the Democratic or Republican party must have voted in that party's primary in the two most recent primaries they voted in, or have certification from the county party chair, or can be challenged and removed from the ballot [2].
- July 2026: Chalkbeat reported, citing Indiana School Boards Association data, that 524 of 1,044 candidates chose a party, about 72% of them Republicans [18]; the Indiana Election Division's 2026 guidance and the wording of the law differ on the primary-voting test, a question county election boards decide [19].
- February 2026: Senate Enrolled Act 25 set the order of school board candidates on the ballot, grouped by party, with candidates who list no affiliation placed after the party and independent candidates [20].
- 2025: Senate Enrolled Act 1, a property tax law, limits how much operating revenue districts can raise through taxes and restricts school referendums to statewide general elections in even-numbered years [21, 22].
- 2025: The same law requires districts to share operating revenue with brick-and-mortar charter schools that enroll their students, statewide starting in 2028 [21, 22].
- 2025: The state budget opened Choice Scholarship vouchers to all Indiana families starting in June 2026 [16].
- 2026: The Indianapolis Public Education Corporation, rather than the Indianapolis Public Schools board, now certifies Indianapolis Public Schools referendum questions for the ballot [20].
- 2023: Senate Enrolled Act 391 changed the so-called $1 law so the Indiana Department of Education can order districts that lost at least 10% of enrollment over five years to close schools under 60% capacity and offer them to charter schools [23].
- 2023: House Enrolled Act 1447 required school districts to set up procedures for complaints that library material is obscene or harmful to minors [10].

## This race

- Tippecanoe Valley School Corporation, based in Akron, covers parts of Kosciusko and Fulton counties; its schools are Akron Elementary, Mentone Elementary, Tippecanoe Valley Middle School and Tippecanoe Valley High School [24, 25]. State enrollment counts were 1,712 students in 2025-26, 1,738 in 2024-25 and 1,779 in 2022-23 [26].
- Superintendent: Blaine Conley [24, 27].
- The board has five members, each representing a numbered district tied to specific townships; candidates file in Kosciusko County even when their district is in Fulton County [25]. As of January 2025 the members were David Lash (president), Tom Bauters, Aaron Zolman, Adam Heckaman and Todd Hoffman; Zolman and Heckaman were re-elected in 2024 [28]. In October 2026 Zolman was vice president and Bauters secretary [27].
- Seats on the 2026 ballot: Districts 3, 4 and 5, one seat each, matching the state list [6]. In 2022 these seats were filled by David Lash (District 3, Franklin Township, Kosciusko County), Tom Bauters (District 4, Henry Township, Fulton County) and Todd Hoffman (District 5, New Castle Township, Fulton County) [29].
- District 3 is open because incumbent David Lash is not running again; Shelly E. Engle, Jamie Murphy and Joe Leon Secrist are running [30]. District 4 incumbent Tom Bauters is unopposed [31]. In District 5, incumbent Todd Hoffman is not on the ballot; the state list shows only a declared write-in candidate, Jeffrey L. Brown, Jr. [6, 29]. This guide found no reporting on Hoffman's plans.
- Party labels: Bauters filed as a Republican; the District 3 candidates did not declare a party [32, 6].
- Teacher pay: on Oct. 5, 2026 the board presented a tentative 2026-27 agreement with the Tippecanoe Valley Classroom Teachers' Association giving increases of $1,000 to $1,600 on the salary scale depending on degree and experience, stipends for teachers at the top of the scale, and $200 more for a reading literacy endorsement; base salaries range from $46,000 to $81,500 [27].
- Facilities: a new administration building with a child care center was under construction in 2025 and on track to open in August 2026, according to Conley [33, 34].
- Academics: 95% of Valley students passed the IREAD-3 reading test in 2025, about 8 points above the state average [34].
- Budget and property taxes: Bauters credited the board with getting through local property tax funding reductions [31, 35]. This guide found no reporting on the dollar effect of the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1) on the corporation, and no operating or safety referendum.
- Forums: this guide found no 2026 candidate forum. InkFreeNews published candidate interviews in July and October 2026 [30, 36]. In District 3, Secrist describes the board's job as running the business side of the schools, Engle puts literacy first, and Murphy emphasizes support and open communication with staff [36].

## Jamie Murphy — District 3

First-time candidate for the open District 3 seat (incumbent David Lash is not running); no party declared [35, 30, 6].

### Background

- Graduate of Tippecanoe Valley High School [35].
- Says the run is a way to give back to the community that supported the candidate growing up [36].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed June 17, 2026 without declaring a party; listed as Nonpartisan [32, 6]. First run for the board [35].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Emphasizes fiscal responsibility alongside investing in staff [35].

**Teachers & staff**

- Wants the district to support teachers and to make sure board members can hear from staff without fear of reprimand [36].

**Transparency & community input**

- Focuses on listening to everyone in the school community, saying “every voice matters” [35].
- Wants open communication at all levels of the district [36].

### On the current officeholder

- Did not criticize specific board decisions; said a single board member cannot demand changes [36].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Would handle disagreements on the board professionally through open communication [36].

### What we couldn't verify

- Occupation and residence are not reported; InkFreeNews said in July that Murphy was unavailable for comment for its first article.
- InkFreeNews articles refer to the candidate with different pronouns; this guide does not use either.
- No candidate website or social-media page found.
- No endorsements or campaign-finance reports found.

## Joe Leon Secrist — District 3

Local businessman and first-time candidate for the open District 3 seat (incumbent David Lash is not running); no party declared [30, 36, 6].

### Background

- Local businessman [36].
- Has volunteered in the schools for years, substitute taught at the high school and Burket Education Center, and coached on the football and track staffs [30].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed June 17, 2026 without declaring a party; listed as Nonpartisan [32, 6]. Said he had considered running for years but waited while the seat had a good representative in place [30].

### Positions & plans

**Budget & taxes**

- Views the district as a business, with students as clients, staff as employees to support and hold accountable, and the community as investors; wants the district run responsibly [30].
- Says a school board is mainly an administrative body that runs the business side of the schools rather than one that makes classroom decisions [36].

**Teachers & staff**

- Wants to study staff retention, including whether pay or other factors are the cause [36].

**Facilities**

- Wants a close look at existing facilities and how the community can use them [36].

**Enrollment & school choice**

- Wants to build on enrollment, which he says is gaining more students than it loses [36].

### On the current officeholder

- Said the district “had a good representative in place” in Lash [30]. Says the board should review both what works and what does not [36].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Avoids specific promises before taking office and says leaders need about four years to make changes [36].
- Says members should discuss issues, hear every viewpoint and then vote [36].

### What we couldn't verify

- His business is not named in coverage.
- No candidate website or social-media page found.
- No endorsements or campaign-finance reports found.

## Shelly E. Engle — District 3

Teacher educator and former Tippecanoe Valley teacher running for the open District 3 seat (incumbent David Lash is not running); no party declared [30, 36, 6].

### Background

- Works in teacher education, training future teachers and working with schools across Indiana; says she holds a PhD in reading [30, 36].
- Taught 13 years in the district, six at Akron Elementary and seven at Tippecanoe Valley High School [30].
- Tippecanoe Valley graduate; parent of students in the district [30].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed June 18, 2026 without declaring a party; listed as Nonpartisan [32, 6]. First run for the board [30].

### Positions & plans

**Academics**

- Wants literacy (reading, writing, speaking and listening) and math to be a central focus while keeping existing programs [36].
- Wants graduates to be literate, competent and ready for college, work or the military [36].

**Transparency & community input**

- Plans to listen to students, parents, staff and community members to learn what the schools need [30, 36].

### On the current officeholder

- Did not criticize specific board decisions; says she wants the district to remain a place where “students, teachers, and families can thrive” [30].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Would handle disagreements by listening and questioning evidence before deciding what serves students [36].

### What we couldn't verify

- Her current employer is not named in coverage.
- No candidate website or social-media page found.
- No endorsements or campaign-finance reports found.

## Tom Bauters — District 4

Incumbent and board secretary, unopposed for a third term; listed as a Republican [31, 35, 6].

### Background

- Has served eight years on the board; was board president in 2022, vice president in 2025 and secretary in 2026 [35, 25, 28, 27].
- Moved to the area in 2001; he and his wife started a local business in 2014 [31].
- Served 12 years on the district's steering committee, which reviews educational and career opportunities for students [31].
- Parent of Tippecanoe Valley High School graduates; grandparent of current students [31].
- Lives in Akron (Henry Township, Fulton County) [29].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed June 10, 2026 as a Republican [32, 6].

### Positions & plans

**Facilities**

- Points to campus improvements, especially new band and theater facilities [31].

**Budget & taxes**

- Credits the board with getting through COVID-19 and local property tax funding reductions [31, 35].

### On the current officeholder

- Is a current board member. Points to the board's work through the pandemic and property-tax funding reductions, academic gains and campus facility investments [35, 31].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says he wants to keep serving the students, teachers, administration and community [31].

### What we couldn't verify

- The name of his business is not reported.
- No candidate website, questionnaire or forum appearance found.
- No endorsements or campaign-finance reports found.
