# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Garrett Bascom (Republican)

Garrett Bascom, a Lawrenceburg attorney and former Dearborn-Ohio County deputy prosecutor, has represented House District 68 since November 2024, succeeding Rep. Randy Lyness [3, 4, 5]. He won the seat in 2024 with 79.5% of the vote [6].

- Committees: appointed in December 2024 to the Courts and Criminal Code, Judiciary and Local Government committees [7].
- 2025: Authored HEA 1393, which requires jail officials to notify the county sheriff when there is probable cause to believe an arrestee for a misdemeanor or felony is in the country illegally, and the sheriff to notify federal authorities; it passed the House 67-26 and the legislature sent it to the governor in April 2025 [8, 9]. His January 2026 filing release said five bills he authored or sponsored were signed into law in the prior session [5].
- 2026: Co-authored HB 1099, which would require state and local technology contractors to certify they are not tied to a foreign adversary, add security reviews for some students from adversary countries, and bar agents of those countries from buying or leasing Indiana land; it passed the House in February 2026 [10]. Authored HCR 5 to rename two U.S. 50 bridges in Lawrenceburg for local Vietnam War dead [11].
- Voted yes on SEA 1, the 2025 property-tax law (April 10, 2025) [12]; the 2025 budget conference report, HEA 1001 (April 25, 2025) [13]; and HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill (Dec. 5, 2025) [14].

## This race

- House District 68 includes all of Dearborn, Ohio and Switzerland counties and part of Jefferson County [5]. (It does not include Ripley County, which is in House District 67.)
- Bascom and Hunter Collins were each the only declared candidate in their party's May 5, 2026 primary [6].
- Key differences: Bascom highlights mental-health access, immigration enforcement, the justice system and Second Amendment rights [5]; Collins highlights lowering taxes, infrastructure and full funding for public schools and services [15].
- No debate or candidate forum for this race was found.

## Garrett R. Bascom

First-term Republican incumbent and Lawrenceburg attorney [5, 3].

### Background

- Worked in the Indiana Attorney General's Office, returned in 2021 to serve as a deputy prosecutor for Dearborn-Ohio County, and later started his own law firm [5].
- Holds a bachelor's degree from Mount St. Joseph University and a law degree from the IU McKinney School of Law [4].
- Lives in Lawrenceburg [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Filed for reelection in January 2026 [5]; unopposed in the May 5 Republican primary [6].

### Positions & plans

**Immigration**

- Said HEA 1393 "ensures law enforcement officers are working together to identify bad actors and keep our communities safe" [9].

**Agriculture**

- Said HB 1099 would "prohibit adversarial countries from owning or leasing Hoosier land" [10].

**Public safety**

- Lists a "fair and effective justice system" and protecting Second Amendment rights among his Statehouse priorities [5].

**Health care**

- Lists expanding access to mental health services among his priorities [5].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. In his filing release he said he would be "honored to earn your vote and your continued support"; no statements about his opponent were found [5].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Says his approach is "focused on practical, common-sense solutions that protect families, respect our values, and keep our communities strong" [5].

## Hunter B Collins

Democratic nominee from Aurora; a warehouse lead at Amazon Air and Teamsters Local 89 organizer when he announced in June 2025 [15].

### Background

- An Aurora native who, at announcement, was 27 and worked as a warehouse lead at Amazon Air and an organizer with Teamsters Local 89, involved for two years in the effort to unionize Amazon [15].
- Lives in Aurora [15].

### Path to the ballot

- Announced by the Dearborn County Democratic Party in June 2025 [15]; the only declared Democrat in the May 5, 2026 primary [6].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- His campaign announcement listed lowering taxes as a focus [15].

**Education**

- Called for fully funding public schools and essential services [15].

**Economy & jobs**

- Said: "I've gone up against one of the largest corporations in the world on behalf of working people. Now, I'm bringing that same fight to Indianapolis to stand up for working families in Southeast Indiana" [15].

**Infrastructure**

- Listed restoring infrastructure as a focus [15].
