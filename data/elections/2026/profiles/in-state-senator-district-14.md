# Indiana State Senator — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana Senate write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The Senate has 50 members, each representing one district for a four-year term; about half the seats are elected every two years [1].

- **Term:** 4 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 50 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** 25 districts [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 25 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year, under Article 4 of the Indiana Constitution [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Tyler Johnson (Republican)

Tyler Johnson, an emergency room physician from Leo, was elected in 2022 and is seeking a second term; the Senate Republican caucus lists him as assistant majority caucus chair and a member of the Education and Career Development, Elections, Family and Children Services, Health and Provider Services, and Tax and Fiscal Policy committees. [3]

- 2025: Authored Senate Bill 480 on health insurers' prior authorization rules, which passed the Senate 47–2 on 2025-02-20 and took effect as Senate Enrolled Act 480 on 2025-07-01. [4, 5] The caucus says it sets limits on when insurers can require prior authorization, sets response deadlines and defines medically necessary care; Johnson said, "Reforming Indiana's prior authorization system has been one of my top priorities since being elected to serve in the Senate." [4]
- 2025-10-29: Issued a statement opposing a proposed relocation of a casino to northeast Indiana near Auburn, saying a state study "ignores risks to families, schools and public safety" and calling for an independent review of social, traffic and infrastructure costs. [6] In 2024 he had told a New Haven town hall, "In my legislative career I've not really been for gambling." [7]
- 2025-12-11: Voted for the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which the Senate defeated. [8] In March 2026, President Trump endorsed him among Republican senators who supported redistricting. [8]
- 2023–2026: The caucus lists statements on SEA 480 (2023), which addressed gender transition procedures for minors, and on SCR 17 (2023) condemning physician-assisted suicide, and a 2026 letter he co-signed with Rep. J.D. Prescott urging federal action on the abortion drug mifepristone. [3]

## This race

- Senate District 14 covers portions of Allen and DeKalb counties in northeast Indiana. [3]
- Johnson is the incumbent; Democrat Blaine Sefton, a union electrician, is a first-time candidate. [9] Both were their parties' nominees after the May 5, 2026 primary. [1]
- Key differences in stated positions: Sefton's platform calls for repealing Senate Bill 76 (immigration enforcement), a moratorium on new data centers, and ending the private school voucher system; Johnson's record emphasizes health insurance prior authorization changes and opposition to a casino relocation in the district. [10, 4, 6]

## Blaine Sefton

Blaine Sefton is a union electrician and community activist running for office for the first time. [9]

Campaign website: https://www.blaineforindiana.org/

### Background

- Works as a union electrician and has been active with the Democratic Socialists of Fort Wayne and Indivisible Indiana, according to WBOI. [9]
- His campaign site says he is a member of the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers and previously worked as a case manager for the Indiana Family and Social Services Administration and at the Social Security Office of Disability Adjudication. [11]
- Describes himself as "a Worker, a Democratic Socialist." [12]

### Path to the ballot

- Filed just before the filing deadline at the start of 2026. [9] Listed as the Democratic nominee after the May 5, 2026 primary. [1]

### Positions & plans

**Economy & jobs**

- Campaign platform calls for raising the minimum wage to $20.30 by 2030, supporting unionization and repealing Indiana's right-to-work law. [10]
- Proposes raising the corporate tax rate to 7.5%, incentives for small and local businesses, and a one-time 5% wealth tax on individuals worth $100 million or more. [10]

**Housing**

- Would bar companies and private equity firms from buying single-family homes, increase landlord accountability for repairs, and overhaul zoning rules. [10]

**Health care**

- Proposes a state "Medicaid-for-all" plan, requiring employers to cover medical expenses tied to their insurance plans, and reviewing nonprofit status of hospitals when patients are left with bills. [10]

**Data centers**

- Calls for a moratorium on new data center construction, restrictions on existing centers' noise, light, water use and emissions, and an end to tax breaks for tech companies. [10]

**Energy & utilities**

- Supports rules, modeled on Oregon laws, making large energy users pay full cost and capping rate increases, plus building renewable generation. [10]

**Education**

- Would end the private school voucher system and strengthen teachers' unions' bargaining over class size and working conditions. [10]

**Immigration & civil rights**

- Calls for repealing Senate Bill 76 and ending state cooperation with ICE, allowing sanctuary cities, repealing the abortion ban, and strengthening anti-discrimination laws. [10]

**Marijuana**

- Supports legalizing marijuana and decriminalizing low-level, nonviolent offenses. [10]

### On the current officeholder

- Told WBOI, "It is the responsibility of the government to work for everyday people," and said he wants to hold accountable systems he feels no longer serve people; he did not name Johnson in the material reviewed. [9]

### How they say they'd run the office

- Platform areas listed on his site: housing, health care, workers, child care, rights, data centers, taxes, roads and transit, public schools, utilities and marijuana. [10]
- Child care: cap child care costs, enact paid parental leave, and create incentives for child care workers. [10]

### Endorsements & fundraising

- His campaign site lists endorsements from the Indiana AFL-CIO, MadVoters and the Fort Wayne Democratic Socialists of America. [13]

## Tyler Johnson

Tyler Johnson is the incumbent senator, an emergency room physician from Leo first elected in 2022. [3]

### Background

- Emergency room physician; the caucus lists Leo High School and the University of Saint Francis in his education. [3]
- Serves as assistant majority caucus chair. [3]

### Path to the ballot

- Renominated in the May 5, 2026 Republican primary; Trump endorsed him in March 2026, and the Capital Chronicle described him as among senators unopposed or heavy favorites for renomination. [8, 1]

### Positions & plans

**Health care**

- Has said, "Too often, prior authorization stands between patients and access to the care their doctor recommends," and that SEA 480 "restores the original intent of prior authorization." [5]
- In April 2025 issued a statement supporting the "Make Indiana Healthy Again" movement, per the caucus. [3]

**Gambling**

- Opposes relocating a casino to northeast Indiana near Auburn. [6]

**Elections & redistricting**

- Voted for the 2025 mid-decade congressional redistricting bill. [8]

**Roads**

- The caucus lists his announcements of Community Crossings road grants for the district, including about $6.1 million in March 2025 and more than $1.4 million in July 2026. [3]

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No statements about Blaine Sefton were found. [3]

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by President Trump in March 2026. [8]
