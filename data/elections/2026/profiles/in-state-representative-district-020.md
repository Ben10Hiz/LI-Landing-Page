# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Jim Pressel (Republican)

Jim Pressel, a Republican from Rolling Prairie who owns a residential home building and remodeling company, has represented House District 20 since 2016 and chairs the House Roads and Transportation Committee, according to the House Republican caucus [3].

- Committee assignments listed by the House Republican caucus: Roads and Transportation (chair); Utilities, Energy and Telecommunications; and Elections and Apportionment [3].
- April 2025: voted yes when the House passed Senate Bill 1, the property-tax bill, 65–29 on third reading; a caucus release quoted him saying "Our goal is to unburden Hoosiers from the weight of increasing property taxes" [4, 5].
- April 2025: voted yes on the conference committee report for the 2025–2027 state budget (HEA 1001), which the House adopted 66–27 [6].
- December 5, 2025: voted yes on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting map, which passed the House 57–41 [7, 8].
- 2026 session: authored House Bill 1200 (HEA 1200), which, as described by the caucus, limits commercial driver's licenses for nondomiciled drivers to certain visa holders, requires English proficiency, revoked CDLs held by nondomiciled drivers without approved work visas as of April 1, 2026, and allows civil penalties of up to $50,000 for businesses hiring unauthorized commercial drivers; Gov. Mike Braun ceremonially signed it in April 2026 [9].
- The House version of HB 1200 also included billboard advertising limits, rear-camera rules for vehicles and changes to driver's license testing, according to the caucus [10].
- February 2026: wrote to the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission asking it to review NIPSCO's residential rates, saying "Hoosiers in our community are struggling with high utility bills and we want to see solutions" [11].
- June 2026: appointed chair of the Interim Study Committee on Roads and Transportation, which is studying topics including digital driver's licenses [12].
- September 2026: after an interim committee hearing on utilities' response to August storms in Northwest Indiana, said it is "dangerous and unacceptable for any Hoosier to go two weeks without electricity" and that he is ready to take legislative action if needed [13].

## This race

- House District 20 includes portions of LaPorte and Starke counties in northwest Indiana [3]. Pressel is seeking re-election; it is not an open seat [2].
- In the May 5, 2026 Republican primary, Pressel won 3,238 votes (62.2%) to 1,972 (37.9%) for Juanita Haney; in the Democratic primary, Laura Liskey won 1,350 votes (52.7%) to 1,212 (47.3%) for Alicia Firanek, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [14].
- ABC57 reported that Pressel had the backing of President Trump and Gov. Mike Braun in the primary, and that Haney focused on regulating data centers, utility prices and local taxes [15].
- Liskey and Firanek took part in a La Porte County League of Women Voters candidate forum on March 19, 2026, before the primary [16]. No general-election debate or forum between Pressel and Liskey was found as of October 10, 2026.
- Pressel's recent public record emphasizes roads, commercial driver licensing and utility costs [3, 11]; no published issue positions from Liskey were found, so key policy differences could not be documented.

## Jim Pressel

Jim Pressel, the Republican incumbent from Rolling Prairie, has served in the House since 2016 and chairs the Roads and Transportation Committee [3].

### Background

- Owns Pressel Enterprises Inc., a residential home building and remodeling company, and served as 2016 president of the Indiana Builders Association, according to the caucus [3].
- Helped start the building trades program at New Prairie High School in 2003 and still serves on its board, per the caucus [3].
- Lives in Rolling Prairie [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Republican primary over Juanita Haney, 62.2% to 37.9% [14, 15].

### Positions & plans

**Energy & utilities**

- Asked the IURC to examine NIPSCO's investments and expenses over the past decade, make bills clearer, and take short-term steps to stabilize rates [11].
- Said he is ready to pursue legislation if needed so utilities can respond effectively to severe weather [13].

**Transportation & public safety**

- Said of his CDL law: "Indiana is refusing to allow illegal drivers to continue to endanger Hoosier motorists" [9].
- Said "Maintaining quality infrastructure and road safety is always a priority" [12].

**Property taxes**

- Supported the 2025 property-tax law, SEA 1, which the caucus says provides about $1.3 billion in homeowner savings over three years [5, 4].

### On the current officeholder

- Is the current officeholder. No public statements by Pressel about Liskey were found; ABC57 reported he did not respond to its requests for comment before the primary [15].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Chairs the 2026 interim study committee on roads and transportation, which is studying digital driver's licenses and the naming of roads and bridges for fallen Hoosiers [12].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- iVoterGuide lists endorsements from the NRA Political Victory Fund, NFIB, Indiana Right to Life, Gov. Mike Braun, the Indiana State Police Alliance and Indiana Farm Bureau AgELECT [17].
- iVoterGuide lists 2026 contributions from groups including Hoosiers for Quality Education, the National Shooting Sports Foundation, the state Republican Party and several building-trades unions, without amounts [17].

### What we couldn't verify

- No current campaign website was found; jimpressel.com appears to be an older site from his first campaign.
- Pressel did not answer the iVoterGuide questionnaire.
- No campaign-finance totals from the Indiana campaign finance portal were reviewed.
- A caucus listing shows a September 2026 call for allowing untaxed off-road diesel in farm semi-trucks through harvest; the release itself was not read.

## Laura Liskey

Laura Liskey is the Democratic nominee; she won a close May 2026 primary, and little public information about her background or positions was found [14].

### Background

- No biographical information was found in her BallotReady or iVoterGuide profiles [18, 19].

### Path to the ballot

- Won the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary with 52.7% over Alicia Firanek, according to Wikipedia's tabulation [14].

### On the current officeholder

- No public statements by Liskey about Pressel or his record were found.

### Endorsements & fundraising
