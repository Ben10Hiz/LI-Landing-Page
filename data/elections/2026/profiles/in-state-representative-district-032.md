# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Victoria Garcia Wilburn (Democratic)

Victoria Garcia Wilburn, a Fishers Democrat and Indiana University associate professor of occupational therapy, was first elected in 2022; the House Democratic caucus lists her as ranking minority member of the Judiciary Committee and a member of the Employment, Labor and Pensions and Public Health committees [3, 4].

- April 2025: Voted no on Senate Bill 1, the 2025 property-tax law, saying Hamilton County stood to lose $132.98 million and that local income taxes could rise; also voted no on the 2025 state budget (HEA 1001), citing public-school, public-health and pre-K funding, while saying she supported its cigarette-tax increase [5, 6].
- December 5, 2025: Voted no on House Bill 1032, the mid-decade congressional redistricting bill, which passed the House 57-41; she said she voted no because of "an outpouring of calls and emails from constituents" opposing it [7, 8].
- 2026 session: Authored House Bill 1036, requiring Department of Child Services caseworkers to see a child in person before closing a case; the bill passed both chambers unanimously and was signed into law, according to her office [9, 10].
- 2026: Her request for an interim study of guardian ad litem and court-appointed special advocate programs was approved, and in August she called on FSSA to pause procedural Medicaid terminations in counties hit by flooding [11, 12].

## This race

- House District 32 covers parts of Fishers, Carmel and a small part of Indianapolis, crossing Hamilton and Marion counties, according to Garcia Wilburn's campaign[13].
- Garcia Wilburn won the seat in 2022 and was re-elected in 2024 with 52.1% of the vote, according to Wikipedia [14, 15].
- No Republican filed for the May 2026 primary; on June 26, 2026, Hamilton County Republican precinct committee members chose Paul Nix by unanimous vote in a caucus to fill the ballot vacancy [16].
- Property taxes are a point of difference: Garcia Wilburn voted against the 2025 property-tax law (SEA 1), saying it would cut local services and raise local income taxes, while Nix calls the law's $300 credit inadequate and supports a proposal to replace property taxes with a 7% tax on services [5, 17].

## Paul G. Nix

Paul Nix, the Republican nominee, is a Fishers insurance agent picked by a party caucus in June 2026 who is running mainly on eliminating property taxes[17].

Campaign website: https://nix4indiana.com/

### Background

- Moved to Fishers from Fort Wayne in 1990 and lives in Delaware Township [17].

### Path to the ballot

- Was chosen on June 26, 2026 by a unanimous vote of Hamilton County Republican precinct committee members at a caucus held to fill the vacancy left when no Republican filed for the primary [16].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- His campaign says Hoosiers are "being taxed out of homes they already own" and calls for "real reform" rather than "a $300 credit that disappears from local increases" [17].
- Supports House Bill 1288, a proposal by Rep. J.D. Prescott to abolish property taxes and replace them with a 7% tax on services; in a letter to The Reporter he wrote that state fiscal estimates showed it would raise more revenue than property taxes [18, 17].

**Budget**

- Says he would keep a balanced budget and "find real savings in programs like Medicaid and Medicare without cutting the quality of care" [17].

**Education**

- Says parents should have the final say in what children are taught; opposes CRT, DEI and "gender ideology" in schools and opposes male athletes competing in girls' sports [17].
- In his letter, suggested Hamilton Southeastern Schools, which he said has 1,500 fewer students than six years ago, could rent out empty classrooms "instead of a referendum" [18].

**Public safety**

- Supports Indiana's constitutional carry law [17].

**Immigration**

- Says he supports legal immigration "100 percent" [17].

**Abortion**

- Opposes taxpayer funding of abortion and says Indiana should do more to protect the unborn [17].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Nix about Garcia Wilburn's record were found; his campaign says District 32 "can be won" and that Indiana under Republican leadership is "the envy of our neighboring States" [17].
- At the caucus, he said a candidate who waits until Medicare age to run is "not the typical career politician" [16].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Lists cutting property taxes as his top priority, saying "no Hoosier should ever be evicted from a home they own because they cannot pay the property taxes" [17].

## Victoria Garcia Wilburn

Rep. Victoria Garcia Wilburn, the Democratic incumbent seeking a third term, is an occupational therapist and Indiana University faculty member who has focused on health care, child welfare and public safety training [3].

Campaign website: https://www.wilburnforindiana.com/

### Background

- Associate professor of occupational therapy at Indiana University-Indianapolis; earned a bachelor's in occupational therapy from Boston University and master's and doctoral degrees in health science from the University of Indianapolis [4, 3].
- Has served on the boards of the American Occupational Therapy Association and Hope Academy Recovery High School, according to the caucus [3].
- Lives in Fishers [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Was unopposed in the May 5, 2026 Democratic primary [19, 14].

### Positions & plans

**Property taxes**

- Voted against 2025's SEA 1, saying, "Senate Bill 1 in its current form is just not workable for House District 32," and citing potential local income-tax increases and cuts to schools and public safety [5].

**Education**

- Opposed the 2025 budget in part because it moves to universal private school vouchers in 2027 and, she said, did not give special education enough of an increase [6].
- Held an education town hall in Fishers in September 2026 [20].

**Health care**

- Authored 2026 bills to let patients pick a physical or occupational therapist as a primary care provider (HB 1016) and to require the insurance commissioner to consider affordability when reviewing premium increases (HB 1037) [21].
- Has criticized FSSA over Medicaid disenrollments, saying, "FSSA is doing too much too fast" [22].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Opposed mid-decade redistricting, saying in October 2025 that the state should focus on energy bills, property taxes and health care costs instead [23, 8].

**Public safety**

- Her campaign says she supports first responders and "common sense gun laws"; the caucus says her first-term law (Public Law 139) provides mental health and resiliency training to public safety professionals [13, 3].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Her campaign says she is running to strengthen public schools with attention to special education, improve mental and behavioral health infrastructure and advocate for gun safety laws [13].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- Endorsed by the OneZone Business Action Committee in March 2026 [19].
- Joined NewDEAL, a national network of Democratic state and local officials, in July 2026 [4].

### What we couldn't verify

- No campaign finance totals were found.
- One local blog described her as a physical therapist; her caucus bio says occupational therapist.
- Reported only by a local blog (LarryInFishers) and not included: Is the current officeholder. No public statements by Garcia Wilburn about her challenger were found.
