# Indiana State Representative — 2026 race profile

_Researched 2026-10-10 · status: draft._ Pending editorial review.

## About the office

Members of the Indiana House of Representatives write and vote on state laws as part of the Indiana General Assembly. The House has 100 members, each representing one district for a two-year term [1].

- **Term:** 2 years [1]
- **Term limit:** None [1]
- **Seats:** 100 [1]
- **On the 2026 ballot:** All 100 [2]

### What the office does

- Introduces and votes on state legislation as one of the two chambers of the Indiana General Assembly [1].
- Must be at least 21 years old when taking office and have lived in the district for at least a year [1].

### Recent changes to the office


## Current officeholder: Chris Campbell (Democratic)

Rep. Chris Campbell (D-West Lafayette), an audiologist first elected in 2018, is ranking Democrat on the House Insurance Committee and serves on Ways and Means and Government and Regulatory Reform, according to the House Democratic caucus [3]. Campbell won 64.1% in 2024, per Wikipedia's compilation [4].

- April 25, 2025: Voted against the final state budget (HEA 1001), saying "Purdue's funding is getting cut, and they're receiving no funding for capital projects" and "The bad in the budget outweighs the good" [5].
- December 5, 2025: Voted no on HB 1032, the congressional redistricting bill [6]; said "Every resident in Indiana, from the dunes to the Ohio River, is hurt by this map" and objected that Purdue's area would not have "a unified voice in Congress" [7].
- 2026 session votes recorded by BillTrack50 include no votes on SB 76 (immigration matters), SB 285 (housing matters), SB 1 (human services matters) and HB 1343 (public safety matters), and yes votes on HB 1177 (child care assistance) and SB 91 (syringe exchange program) [6].
- July 2026: Testified against proposed state child care rule changes, saying "These rule changes are not viable solutions to Indiana's childcare crisis" and calling for predictable, sustainable provider funding [8].

## This race

- House District 26 includes portions of Tippecanoe County, and Campbell is from West Lafayette, according to the House Democratic caucus [3].

## Chris Campbell

Campbell is a four-term incumbent and Lafayette audiologist [3].

### Background

- Works as an audiologist in Lafayette [3].
- Board and volunteer service listed by the caucus includes the Indiana board of the American Red Cross, Lafayette Transitional Housing and the Greater Lafayette League of Women Voters; past service includes the West Lafayette Library Board and Redevelopment Commission [3].
- Lives in West Lafayette [3].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed by Wikipedia's compilation as the only declared Democratic candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Education**

- Criticized the 2025 budget's K-12 funding, saying schools receive a small state increase but lose more in property tax revenue, and objected to cuts to Purdue's state funding [5].

**Health care**

- Said "The cuts for local public health initiatives are concerning" in voting against the 2025 budget [5].

**Elections & redistricting**

- Opposed the 2025 congressional redistricting plan and voted no on HB 1032 [7, 6].

**Economy & jobs**

- Called for "predictable, sustainable funding" for child care providers rather than lowered staff qualifications [8].

## Magdalaine Davis

Davis, who goes by Maggie, is a Purdue University student and researcher making her first run for office [10, 11].

Campaign website: https://www.electmagdalainedavis.com

### Background

- Studied political science at Purdue and is continuing studies in sociology there; her research centers on sexual violence and human rights [10, 11].
- Says she decided to run after experiencing sexual assault and navigating the process that followed: "Running for office became a way for me to turn that experience into public service" [10].
- Describes herself as a member of the West Lafayette community [10].

### Path to the ballot

- Listed by Wikipedia's compilation as the only declared Republican candidate in the May 5, 2026 primary [4].

### Positions & plans

**Elections & redistricting**

- Calls the mid-decade redistricting push "a hard no" and supports independent commissions to draw maps after each census [9].
- In the iVoterGuide questionnaire, opposed voting without photo ID and opposed abolishing the Electoral College [11].

**Data centers**

- Supports a moratorium on AI data centers in Indiana and more transparency on public incentives to companies [9].

**Public safety**

- Supports a statewide ban on Flock automated license plate readers and AI-powered mass surveillance systems [9].
- Lists sexual violence prevention and survivor support as her first legislative priority, including access to care "regardless of where they live or their ability to pay" [9, 11].
- Opposes allowing teachers to carry guns at school, per her questionnaire [11].

**Education**

- Says vouchers should be "limited to public school to public school choice" and opposes taxpayer-funded vouchers for private or charter schools [9].

**Health care**

- Says pregnancy and abortion decisions should be made by "a woman in consultation with her healthcare provider, not politicians" [9].
- Supports lowering health care and prescription costs and expanding mental health services [11].

**Economy & jobs**

- Lists affordability (housing, child care, utilities, food) as a priority and says she will examine whether Indiana's minimum wage reflects working Hoosiers' costs [9].

**Energy & utilities**

- Supports "stronger protections for Indiana's waterways and agricultural land" and wants residents to have "a voice when major developments are proposed," citing SK Hynix [9].

### On the current officeholder

- No statements by Davis about Campbell's record were found. She says "people deserve a government that listens, responds, and takes their concerns seriously" [10].

### How they say they'd run the office

- Pledges monthly town halls with a live stream and to be honest about "where I stand, how I vote, and why" [9].

### Endorsements & fundraising

- No endorsements or fundraising totals were found; her site is paid for by the Committee to Elect Maggie Davis [12].

- No news coverage of her candidacy was found.
- Occupation beyond student/researcher and community roles are not stated on her site or questionnaire.
